# Anthom — Thadou-Kuki communities, Manipur

## Current evidence status

**Canonical record:** `MNP-001-ANT`  
**Community:** Thadou-Kuki  
**Primary source:** SRC-068  
**Confidence:** Named beverage and source-specific preparation documented; village-level coverage unresolved.

Hoikhokim, Ruth Laldinthar, Hoikhovah Haokip and Nenei Lhungdim (2019) describe **Anthom** as a fermented rice beverage associated with Thadou-Kuki communities in Manipur. The article reports use of sticky/glutinous rice, including black or white varieties, and a starter called **Chol**. It describes a preparation process and discusses cultural and ethnomedicinal claims. Direct article PDF: https://ijcrt.org/papers/IJCRT1135364.pdf

A later article by Hoikhokim and Laldinthar (2023), “A Study On The Preparation Technique Of Fermented Rice Beverages By The Thadou-Kuki Tribe Of Manipur,” also describes Thadou-Kuki fermented rice beverages. It may help cross-check terminology and methods, but exact product-name equivalence should be verified against the full text: https://doi.org/10.53555/sfs.v10i3.3381

## Preparation described in the 2019 source

1. Sticky rice is cooked without making it overly soft or mushy.
2. Cooked rice is spread on a flat woven bamboo basket called **Go dal** and allowed to cool.
3. **Chol** starter is sprinkled over and mixed with the cooled rice.
4. The mixture is transferred to a pot; the article describes placing charcoal over the rice.
5. The pot is covered and tied/sealed, then kept in sunlight or a warm kitchen location.
6. After a reported **3–7 days**, fermentation produces a soft, semi-solid rice mass and a semi-liquid top layer.
7. The source calls the separated liquid layer **aning** and describes collecting it in a separate container.

These details are source-specific; they should not be presented as a universal process for every Thadou-Kuki household.

## Naming and interpretation cautions

- **Anthom** is the named fermented rice product in the source.
- **Aning** is described as the separated liquid fraction. There is not yet enough evidence to create it as a separate canonical beverage.
- **Chol** is the reported starter name. Its ingredients, microbial composition, preparation and locality-level variations need further documentation.
- The accessible article metadata does not establish the exact villages surveyed or current production frequency.
- Reported uses for urinary problems, menstrual difficulties, post-partum weakness, kidney stones and other conditions are ethnographic claims, not clinical evidence of efficacy or safety.

## Research priorities

1. Identify the fieldwork villages and district-level sampling frame.
2. Compare the 2019 and 2023 descriptions to see whether they describe the same product and whether additional named forms exist.
3. Document local terminology for the solid fermented mass, the liquid aning, and the Chol starter.
4. Seek community-authorized sources and avoid generalizing a single account to all households.
5. Keep cultural claims attributed to their source and distinguish historical reports from present-day practice.
