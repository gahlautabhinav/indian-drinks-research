# Nyishi Opo rice wine — Arunachal Pradesh

## Current evidence status

**Canonical record:** `ARP-001-OPO`  
**Community:** Nyishi  
**Primary source:** SRC-067  
**Confidence:** Community-level beverage documented; village and product-variant boundaries unresolved.

Kumari, Ama, Jyoti Koch and Debbarma (2025) published “Biochemical and physicochemical characterization of traditional rice wine of Nyishi tribe of Arunachal Pradesh, India” in *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 24(7):615–626. The article identifies **Opo** as a traditional starter culture used for Nyishi rice wine and characterizes samples labelled **Tisar, Kecha and Adak**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.56042/ijtk.v24i7.13884

## What the source supports

- Opo is described as a traditional starter culture passed across generations.
- The study reports biochemical and physicochemical measurements of collected rice-wine samples.
- Tisar, Kecha and Adak are labels used for the varieties/samples discussed by the paper; the accessible abstract does not establish whether these are separate beverage products, village variants, rice varieties, or sample labels.
- The accessible article metadata and abstract do not name the production villages or show a village-by-village distribution.

## What must not be inferred

Laboratory findings about minerals, phenolics, flavonoids or chemical compounds do not establish clinical health benefits, safety, or a universal recipe. Do not assign the study to specific Nyishi villages without evidence from the full methods or other sources. Do not create separate canonical records for Tisar, Kecha and Adak until their local meanings and product boundaries are confirmed.

## Research priorities

1. Obtain and inspect the full methods and sample-origin details.
2. Identify villages and the community-preferred meanings of Tisar, Kecha and Adak.
3. Document Opo starter preparation and storage from a primary or community-authorized source.
4. Record cultural contexts only when the source names the practice and locality.
5. Respect knowledge-holder consent and any restrictions on publishing household-specific starter knowledge.
