# SRC-054 addendum — Sarma (2017): historical Dimasa rice-beer label “zou”

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026  
**Source:** Rabindranath Sarma (2017), “Rice Beer and tribes of Assam,” *IOSR Journal of Humanities and Social Science* 22(7), Version 2, pp. 13–16. DOI: 10.9790/0837-2207021316.  
**Full text:** https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jhss/papers/Vol.%2022%20Issue7/Version-2/C2207021316.pdf

## Finding

On printed page 14, the article states that the Dimasa-Kachari call their rice beer “zou.” The sentence is explicitly quoted from Bordoloi, Sharma Thakur and Saikia (1987), *Tribes of Assam*, Part I, p. 49: it says the beverage is home-brewed and used for household consumption and entertaining guests.

This gives us a traceable older printed reference for the form **zou**, but the 2017 article is the accessible intermediary. The 1987 book is the underlying cited source and should be inspected directly before treating the quotation as a verified transcription of the original page.

## Method and locality limits

Sarma says the article combines library work and interviews. Its methods list Tinsukia–Dibrugarh among district-level field areas for Deori, Sonowal Kachari and Dimasa. However, the Dimasa “zou” sentence on p. 14 is not presented as a result of those interviews; it is explicitly attributed to the 1987 book. Therefore:
- do not map Dimasa Zou to Tinsukia or Dibrugarh on the basis of the methods list;
- do not interpret the district list as a product-specific producer sample;
- do not infer current prevalence from the historical quotation.

## Cross-source spelling boundary

The older cited form **zou** is not automatically identical to:
- **Ju**, one of the four names listed by Bose et al. (2018, SRC-338);
- **Joo**, used by Daulagajau (2015) for Dimasa rice beer;
- **Judima**, the specific rice beverage documented in the Judima studies;
- **Juhara/Juharo/Juharao**, the names associated in some sources with a stronger/distilled product.

These forms may be related linguistically or represent broad versus specific categories, but the retrieved sources do not demonstrate the relationship. Preserve the exact spellings and source context; no canonical merge is justified.

## Archive decision

1. Keep SRC-054 as a secondary synthesis that quotes an older ethnographic source.
2. Add “zou” as a source-attested historical Dimasa rice-beer label, with Bordoloi et al. (1987, p. 49) marked as the cited underlying source and not yet directly inspected.
3. Do not add Tinsukia or Dibrugarh production-locality links.
4. Do not merge Zou with Ju, Joo, Judima or Juharo.
5. Prioritise retrieval of Bordoloi et al. (1987), *Tribes of Assam*, Part I, and Bordoloi (1984), *The Dimasa Kacharis of Assam*, for direct page-level verification.
