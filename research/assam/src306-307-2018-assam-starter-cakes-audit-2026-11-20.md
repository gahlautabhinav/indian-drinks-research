# 2018 Assam dry-starter evidence audit — SRC-306 and SRC-307

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Focus:** Emao/Humao and related Assam amylolytic starters

## 1. Sources recovered

### SRC-306 — Anupma et al. (2018)

Anu Anupma, Pooja Pradhan, Shankar Prasad Sha and Jyoti Prakash Tamang, “Traditional skill of ethnic people of the Eastern Himalayas and North East India in preserving microbiota as dry amylolytic starters,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 17(1), January 2018, pp. 184–190.

Full PDF: https://dspace.cus.ac.in/jspui/bitstream/1/5998/1/IJTK-Marcha%202018.pdf

The paper states that documentation ran for 12 months, December 2015–November 2016. The authors used structured questionnaires, interviewed people involved in starter preparation, observed preparation procedures, collected plant samples and report identification through the Botanical Survey of India, Sikkim Circle. Assam locations are printed as “Kokrajhar, Jorhat, Sivsagar, and Moran district of Assam.” The syntax does not clearly specify which are districts and which are localities; Moran is retained exactly as printed and is not converted into a district. Critically, the article does not map each starter recipe or respondent to an individual Assam site.

#### Emao/Humao account

The paper distinguishes the terms by community: it calls **Emao** the Bodo starter and says Dimasa call it **Humao**. It describes the starter as a ball-like amylolytic inoculum used to ferment glutinous rice for alcoholic beverages including zu/jou/Judima. The recipe it reports is:

1. Soak glutinous rice (*Oryza sativa*) for about 2–3 hours.
2. Add banana leaves, a plant named locally “lwkwna”, leaves of *Scoparia dulcis* (printed as dong-phang-rakhep), and dry bark of *Albizia myriophylla*.
3. Grind the ingredients in a wooden mortar and pestle; the apparatus is called gaihen and ual.
4. Sieve the powder in a bamboo sieve called sandri; add a little water to form thick dough.
5. Form small round cakes, dust with powdered old Emao/Humao inoculum, cover with rice straw and ferment for 2–3 days.
6. Sun-dry the cakes.

The extracted page break separates the beginning and end of the paragraph, but the sequence is recoverable across printed pp. 186–187. No precise old-starter percentage is supplied for this recipe.

**Important limitation:** The paper does not provide a plant voucher number or botanical identity for “lwkwna” in the accessible text. Although the authors state that plant samples were collected and identified, the local term-to-voucher crosswalk is not supplied here. Do not silently equate lwkwna with any other Dimasa/Bodo plant name.

#### Other Assam starter variants

- **Xaaz pitha:** attributed to the Ahom community. Local rice is soaked 10–12 hours; dried leaves and stems of wild herbs are added; the mixture is ground, made into dough, formed into oval balls and placed on fern leaves. The fresh cakes are then dried in sun or over a fireplace; the text says they become hard after about five days. The exact herb taxa and field locality are not specified in the recipe passage.
- **Modor pitha:** attributed to a Kachari community and used to ferment rice into alcoholic beverages such as “xaaz pani” (as printed). Glutinous rice is soaked 2–3 hours, mixed with dried leaves, stems and roots of plants, ground, moistened and formed into small cakes. Old starter powder is added; cakes are placed on rice straw, covered with ferns and fermented 2–5 days until they swell and harden.

These are starter-culture identities and preparation accounts, not new finished-beverage records. Preserve the author's labels and do not conflate Xaaz pitha, Modor pitha and Emao/Humao.

### SRC-307 — Sha et al. (2018)

S. P. Sha, M. V. Suryavanshi, K. Jani, A. Sharma, Y. Shouche and J. P. Tamang, “Diversity of Yeasts and Molds by Culture-Dependent and Culture-Independent Methods for Mycobiome Surveillance of Traditionally Prepared Dried Starters for the Production of Indian Alcoholic Beverages,” *Frontiers in Microbiology* 9 (2018), article 2237. DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2018.02237.

Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2018.02237/full

The study analysed 40 starter samples across eight Northeast states, with five samples per starter except Marcha (ten). The five Assam samples were Humao. The article does not identify the Assam district, village, household, producer or ethnic community from which those five Humao samples came.

**Humao-specific results reported in the article:**

- Average yeast population: **6.834 ± 0.14 log CFU/g** (Table 1; five Humao samples).
- Table 4 lists four representative Humao isolates:
  - AS:Y3 — *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*, KY587126
  - AS:Y5 — *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*, KY587127
  - AS:Y7 — *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*, KY587128
  - AS:Y4 — *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*, KY605162

These are four representative molecularly identified isolates, not the entire microbial community and not necessarily four independent producer batches. The paper reports 386 yeast isolates and 202 PCR-DGGE bands across all eight starter types. Global species percentages in the abstract/results are across the full starter set; they must not be copied into a Humao-specific microbial profile.

The study used culture-dependent isolation and phenotypic tests, Biolog identification, ITS-region sequencing, and culture-independent PCR-DGGE targeting the D1/D2 region of 26S rRNA. The authors discuss methodological limits of DGGE, including inability to distinguish viable from non-viable cells and difficulties interpreting multi-band profiles. This is starter microbiology, not finished Judima beverage microbiology or a safety certification.

## 2. Cross-source interpretation

1. **Emao/Humao:** SRC-306 supplies an ethnographic/recipe account and explicitly distinguishes Bodo Emao from Dimasa Humao. Treat this as an author-reported terminology relationship, not proof that every community uses one identical formulation.
2. **Humao sample origin:** SRC-307 independently analyses five Humao samples from Assam, but the sample metadata do not establish whether they are Bodo Emao, Dimasa Humao or another Assam practice. Do not attach the Sha et al. microbiology to a particular community or to Dima Hasao.
3. **Botanical variation:** SRC-306 lists banana leaves, “lwkwna”, *Scoparia dulcis* leaves and *Albizia myriophylla* bark in the Emao/Humao account. Earlier Judima studies in the archive document other Humao/Umhu plant combinations and locality-specific recipes. Do not collapse these into a universal ingredient list.
4. **Finished beverage distinction:** Humao/Emao is a starter culture. Judima, Jou/Zu and other fermented drinks are separate finished products. Microbial findings from a dried starter do not establish the microbiology, alcohol content or safety of the resulting beverage.
5. **Geography:** The 2018 ethnobotany paper lists Assam field locations but does not allocate Emao, Humao, Xaaz pitha or Modor pitha to each location. The 2018 mycobiome paper only states Assam for Humao. No new place rows are justified.

- Added SRC-306 and SRC-307 to `sources/source_register.csv`.
- Added both sources to the evidence trail for ASM-001-JUD, with an explicit warning that the 2018 microbiology is from dried Humao starter samples, not finished Judima.
- Expanded ASM-GAP-002 to capture the Emao/Humao terminology, unresolved “lwkwna” plant identity, missing recipe-to-site crosswalk, and unlocated five-sample Humao microbiology cohort.
- No new beverage, plant, method or locality records were created from these sources in this pass.

## 4. Next evidence priorities

1. Recover Anupma et al.'s original field schedule, informant count, plant voucher catalogue and the exact local identity of “lwkwna”.
2. Clarify which of the four Assam locations were districts, towns or village-level sites, and which starter products were documented at each.
3. Recover Sha et al.'s supplementary Data Sheets S1/S2 and determine whether DGGE band-level Humao assignments can be extracted reliably.
4. Seek sample-source clarification from the 2018 study authors: district, community, producer, and whether the five samples represent five makers, batches or repeat samples.
5. Obtain community review before normalising Emao/Humao, Xaaz pitha or Modor pitha terminology.

**Conclusion:** SRC-306 is useful primary ethnographic documentation of several Assam starter preparations; SRC-307 supplies the first direct Humao starter mycobiome values located in this research pass. Neither source supports assigning the five microbiology samples to Dimasa/Dima Hasao or treating the recipe as universal.
