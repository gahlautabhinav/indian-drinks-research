# SRC-146 Gogoi, Dutta & Mondal (2013) — full-text audit

**Citation:** Gogoi, B., Dutta, M. & Mondal, P. (2013). “Various Ethno Medicinal Plants used in the Preparation of Apong, a Traditional Beverage use by Mising Tribe of upper Assam.” Journal of Applied Pharmaceutical Science 3(4 Suppl 1):S85–S88. DOI 10.7324/JAPS.2013.34.S16.

## Field scope and source character

The article calls itself a review, but its abstract and methods report active field survey in three Sivasagar villages during September–December 2012: “afola gaon”, “disang mugh” and “borhula par” (spellings preserved as printed). Mising women were visited; nearby fields and forests were visited and plant samples collected. The article says samples were identified with local floras and made into herbarium specimens deposited at the Department of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Dibrugarh University. It does not report informant count, village-by-village plant allocation, voucher accession numbers or a precise sampling protocol. Do not silently normalize the village spellings or create duplicate locality rows without matching the existing locality dataset.

## Plant inventory

The article states that 39 species were recorded. A visual transcription of Table 1 shows 37 numbered entries: 1–14, 16–28 and 30–39. Serial numbers 15 and 29 are absent, and the article does not explain whether two entries were omitted from the printed table or the numbering is erroneous. The 37 visible entries are transcribed verbatim in research/assam/apong-39-plants-src146.csv. This file is a source transcription, not a validated taxonomic dataset. Missing serials are not filled or guessed.

The table includes plants and plant parts with vernacular names, families and ethnomedicinal-use descriptions. Some printed forms/family labels and part/use pairings are suspect (examples include “Capcicum annum”, “Ptridium aquilinum”, “Swernia chirata”, “Drymeria cordata”, “Melothrea heterophylla”; one row has no family printed). Preserve source spellings. The article's ethnomedicinal descriptions are community/traditional-use claims and cited literature, not evidence of clinical efficacy or proof that the finished alcoholic beverage treats disease.

## Preparation account and attribution

The article's “Method of preparation of Apong” says plant leaves are cleaned, used fresh or sun-dried, ground separately from soaked rice, mixed with water, shaped into oval cakes about 6 × 3 cm and sun-dried. It describes an earthen fermentor fumigated over a fireplace, cooled boiled rice, one powdered Apop pitha per 1 kg rice, banana leaves or bhilongoni (Cyclosorus exlensa) covering, at least five days, then water addition and filtration. Crucially, the method paragraph ends with a citation to Das et al. (2012). Treat it as a method reproduced from/citing the 2012 survey, not as an independent protocol newly established by the 2012 field visits.

## Reconciliation with SRC-145 and SRC-144

- SRC-145 (Handique & Deka 2016) reports 17 Apop plants and explicitly labels its Mising beverage method Nogin Apong. It cites Gogoi et al. (2013) as a reference. The 17-name list is a subset/related inventory, not proof that the two papers independently observed the same complete list.
- SRC-146 reports 39 species, but only 37 visible numbered entries. Its method paragraph cites Das et al. (2012). The paper's title/topic and table do not identify this method as the Poro ash-added variant.
- SRC-144 (Basumatary et al. 2023) places a 1:45, 4–5-day/8–9-day process summary under Poro Apong, while SRC-145's primary text labels the near-identical summer/winter method Nogin Apong. This is a material attribution discrepancy. Do not transfer SRC-145/SRC-146 Nogin process details to Poro without Pawe & Gogoi (2013) full text or another explicit primary source.
- SRC-136 (Kardong et al. 2012) remains a distinct Dibrugarh Po:ro Apong/Saimod account: 100 g Epop per 3 kg rice, about 20 days at 30–35°C and Ta:suk extraction.

## Repository decisions

Register SRC-146 and its visible 37-row table transcription. Do not add beverage rows or new place rows from this source in this pass: the three printed village names need matching against existing rows and exact current spelling evidence; the paper is historical. Update ASM-GAP-060 and keep it Open. Next: compare SRC-146's 37 visible taxa with SRC-145's 17 and SRC-005/006/007/136 inventories at exact-string, likely-variant and genus-only levels; obtain Pawe & Gogoi (2013) full text to settle Poro-specific claims.
