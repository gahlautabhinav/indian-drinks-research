# SRC-197 / SRC-225 — Handique early field-survey and thesis-author trail audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-14  
**Scope:** Recover evidence that can advance the Charaideo / SRC-011 sample-locality problem, especially A1 “Bharalisuk” and A2 “Konwergaon”.

## 1. SRC-197 — Handique & Deka (2016), earlier nine-village field survey

Full text: https://www.allsubjectjournal.com/assets/archives/2016/vol3issue1/3-1-88.2.pdf

The article reports a field survey in **nine villages** from **November 2013 to February 2014**. It says the villages were densely populated by Ahom, Mising and Deori communities and that approximately 20–30 people from each community, mostly women, were interviewed. The researchers observed starter-culture and beverage preparation and collected plant samples with local assistance.

The acknowledgements identify seven key informants, including:
- Mrs. Putoli Konwer
- Mrs. Promila Bharali
- Mrs. Budhbori Gogoi
- Mr. Himanjal Deori
- Mrs. Rukmini Mili
- Mrs. Nirmala Deori
- Mrs. Dipali Mili

### What this changes

This is a useful earlier field-network lead. The names **Putoli Konwer** and **Promila Bharali** may be relevant to follow-up retrieval because the 2017 sample names include “Konwergaon” and “Bharalisuk”. However, the article does not name any of its nine villages, does not map an informant to a village, and does not link any informant to the later October 2015 A1/A2 sample collection. A surname/name resemblance is not locality evidence.

The 2013–2014 field survey is a separate study from the six-site October 2015 sample collection in SRC-011. Do not merge the two field schedules or infer that the 2016 paper's nine villages contain the six 2015 sample sites.

## 7. SRC-227 — Handique et al. (2020), follow-on antioxidant/phenolic study

Full text (author-uploaded): https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339117011_Antioxidant_Properties_and_Phenolic_Contents_of_Traditional_Rice-Based_Alcoholic_Beverages_of_Assam_India

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40009-020-00903-5

The methods state that freshly prepared samples were collected from **six different villages in Sivasagar district in September 2015**. The sample groups are:
- S1 and S2 — Deori Sujen
- A1 and A2 — Mising Apong
- X1 and X2 — Ahom Xaj

The article reports DPPH free-radical inhibition, equivalent vitamin-C antioxidant activity, total phenolic content and RP-HPLC identification of six phenolic compounds. It gives a second analytical view of the same broad three-community/six-village research programme associated with SRC-011.

### Critical reconciliation limits

1. **Month discrepancy:** SRC-011 says its six villages were sampled in **October 2015**; SRC-227 says the antioxidant/phenolic samples were collected in **September 2015**. The papers may describe separate collection rounds, or one month may be a reporting error. Without the thesis, notebooks or author clarification, do not claim that the physical samples are identical.
2. **Sample-code collision:** SRC-227 uses **A1/A2 for Mising Apong** and **X1/X2 for Ahom Xaj**. SRC-011 uses A1/A2 for the Ahom Xajpani sample pair. The labels are paper-specific and must never be merged by code alone.
3. **No village names:** SRC-227 again says six villages but does not name them or map its codes to localities. It does not identify Bharalisuk or Konwergaon and does not independently strengthen the Mahmora alias hypothesis.
4. **No new place rows:** This article is analytical evidence for beverage types and sample design, not village-level locality evidence.

### Decision and next action

Register SRC-227 and attach it to ASM-GAP-076 as related analytical research. Keep the gap High/Open. A2 Mahmora Konwar Gaon / Census code 293217 remains a high-plausibility candidate, not a confirmed alias; A1 Bharalisuk remains unresolved. Request the thesis and original September/October 2015 field and laboratory accession records, asking the authors to explain whether these were separate sampling rounds and to provide a crosswalk of sample labels to village names/coordinates.

## 8. Earlier Ahom Xaj-pani analytical evidence — SRC-105 (2014)

The earlier SRC-105 study by Das, Khawas, Miyaji and Deka (2014), *HPLC and GC-MS analyses of organic acids, carbohydrates, amino acids and volatile aromatic compounds in some varieties of rice beer from northeast India*, includes one Ahom Xaj-pani sample, code **AsB4**, with collection locality **Sibsagar, Assam**. The broader nine-sample study collected samples from four Northeast states during November 2010–February 2011. Table 1 reports the AsB4 alcohol value as 4.12 ± 0.01% and pH as 4.39 ± 0.01; these are sample-specific analytical values.

This is an independent, earlier district-level Xaj-pani record. It does not provide a named village or household, and cannot be used to identify A1 Bharalisuk or A2 Konwergaon from SRC-011. SRC-105's sample code AsB4 is unrelated to SRC-011's A1/A2 labels and SRC-227's S/A/X labels. Do not join samples across papers based on beverage/community alone.

The 2023 review by Bhattacharya and Deka cites Handique's thesis with the page range **10–70** (SRC-203 and SRC-226 retrieval trail). This is a precise page-range lead but not a transcription of the thesis or evidence that those pages contain sample-site names. The review's 2017 thesis-submission citation and the university's 2018 award approval are not inherently inconsistent: submission and award are different events.

The 2021 *Toxicology Reports* article by Deka, Handique and Deka studies Bodo Jou varieties (Joubidwi, Joufinai and Jougwran), not the Ahom–Mising–Deori six-village research series. It provides no Charaideo site evidence and is excluded from this locality crosswalk.

**Decision:** Keep ASM-GAP-076 High/Open. SRC-105 supports historical Sibsagar-district Ahom Xaj-pani evidence only. A1 Bharalisuk remains unresolved; A2 Mahmora Konwar Gaon / Census code 293217 remains a high-plausibility candidate, not a confirmed alias. No new Charaideo beverage-specific place row is justified.
