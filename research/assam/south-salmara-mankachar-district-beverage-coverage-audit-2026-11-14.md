# South Salmara-Mankachar district beverage-coverage audit — 14 November 2026

## Decision

**No South Salmara-Mankachar beverage-specific place row is added.** The district remains at zero place rows. This is a locality-evidence gap, not evidence that no traditional beverage is prepared or consumed there.

## 1. Strong Rabha Jonga lead — SRC-187 (2018)

The Assam Institute of Research for Tribals and Scheduled Castes report *Exploring the Traditional Health Practices among the Rabhas of Goalpara district* was retrieved in full. It is valuable because it explicitly names Rabha traditional rice beer as **Jonga** and describes household consumption and traditional beliefs associated with it.

Its methods, however, define the field geography precisely:
- Goalpara district: 19 villages, selected across Balijana, Krishnai, Kuchdhowa, Lakhipur, Matia and Rangjuli blocks;
- Kamrup (Rural): 6 villages;
- Baksa: 5 villages in Tamulpur block;
- 300 households in total; fieldwork May–August 2018.

The village table names the 19 Goalpara sites (including Dighali, Maju Borgom, Thabalpara, Siluk Part 2, Chituk, Habanggiri, Karanchakona, Nowapara Rabha Gaon, Mandalgram, Chatabari, Kanyakuchi, Choto Damal, Bamundanga, Kalyanpur, Dariduri, Beldangpara, Kantapara, Chatabari (Baida) and Dahikata), plus the Kamrup and Baksa sites. **No Dhubri or South Salmara-Mankachar field site is listed.**

The report's Jonga household finding is presented in aggregate; it does not allocate the Jonga observation or household counts by district or named village. It separately notes that a Rabha Koch section is found in Dhubri. That is a population-distribution statement, not evidence that Jonga was documented among those Dhubri households. It cannot be transferred to the present South Salmara-Mankachar district.

The report is a traditional-health study, not a beverage process inventory. It does not provide a district-resolved starter-culture or production dataset for South Salmara-Mankachar.

## 2. Administrative boundary and current district context

### SRC-188 — Official district profile

The Government of Assam district profile lists two revenue circles (South Salmara, part; Mankachar), 152 revenue villages and three development blocks. It supplies the current administrative frame against which future village-level evidence should be checked.

### SRC-189 — Official district survey report

The district's draft District Survey Report states South Salmara-Mankachar was created by bifurcating old Dhubri district in 2016. It describes Goalpariya (Deshee) speech and a mixed cultural history that includes Koch, Rajbongshi, Mech, Jogi and Rabha ancestry/community references.

This is useful context but is not a beverage study. Language, ancestry and broad community descriptions cannot establish that a particular beverage is made in a named village. The district's Goalpariya identity should not be treated as proof of a specific alcoholic beverage tradition.

### Boundary implication

Historical sources labelled only “Dhubri district” require a village-level crosswalk to the 2016 South Salmara-Mankachar boundary. A Dhubri-wide statement cannot be reassigned to South Salmara-Mankachar without the original village, circle/block and a current revenue-village match.

## 3. Related Rabha/Jonga sources screened

- **SRC-017 (Deka & Sarma 2010):** direct Jonga-mod/Kecha-mod field study at Dhupdhora, Goalpara district. This is a Goalpara locality, not a South Salmara-Mankachar site.
- **SRC-049 (NIPCCD 2022):** documents Rabha Kesa mod/Jonga mod process and starter terminology without a named district/locality; community/process context only.
- **SRC-102 (2021):** Choko study uses five unnamed Goalpara villages; no current South Salmara-Mankachar locality can be established.
- **SRC-124 (2026):** Rabha fieldwork districts include Goalpara and Kamrup, not South Salmara-Mankachar.
- **SRC-187 (2018):** documents Jonga in an aggregate Rabha household sample but actual fieldwork districts are Goalpara, Kamrup Rural and Baksa.

No source reviewed jointly identifies a named South Salmara-Mankachar village, a traditional beverage and a household/producer or preparation observation.

## 4. Search scope and archive actions

Targeted searches screened South Salmara-Mankachar, South Salmara, Mankachar and Hatsingimari with Jonga, Jonga-mod, Choko, Kesa mod, rice beer, traditional alcoholic beverage, Rabha, Koch, Goalpariya and Deshee terms. Official district history/profile/DSR material and the full 2018 Rabha field report were checked. Statewide beverage reviews and southern Assam rice-beer studies were excluded from district attribution where their fieldwork was elsewhere.

Archive changes:
- Registered **SRC-187** — 2018 Rabha traditional-health report and its actual three-district field geography.
- Registered **SRC-188** — official South Salmara-Mankachar district profile.
- Registered **SRC-189** — official South Salmara-Mankachar District Survey Report.
- Created **ASM-GAP-080** for direct present-district Jonga/other beverage evidence and pre-2016 Dhubri locality crosswalks.
- Updated the district coverage matrix, search log, district-community coverage notes and README.
- No new beverage identity or place row was created.
