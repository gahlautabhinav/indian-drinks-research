# Hojai district beverage-coverage audit — 14 November 2026

## Decision

**No Hojai beverage-specific place row is added.** Hojai remains at zero place rows in the district matrix. This is a geographic-evidence decision, not a claim that Hojai has no traditional beverage practices.

## New primary-source screening

### SRC-182 — Sarma & Konwar (2024), Tiwa Zu

The full paper, *A Study on Zu, a Traditional Beverage of the Tiwa Community of Assam*, was retrieved from the publisher's journal site (LM Journal of Multidisciplinary Research, Vol. I, No. 1, March 2024, pp. 60–68). The article reports a survey of five villages and focal conversations with 80 elderly people in total. Its methods explicitly assign:

- Rahadhala — Nagaon district;
- Ahatguri Bilpara, Kohiguri, Lothabori and Rahadhala (Goxaibori) — Morigaon district.

It does **not** name a Hojai village. The journal is published by Lanka Mahavidyalaya, whose contact address is Lanka Town, Hojai; the article authors' affiliation is Kalibabor College, Nagaon. Neither institutional address is evidence that the fieldwork took place in Hojai.

The article documents Zu and Bakhor preparation and describes source-specific product distinctions: filtered/diluted Kesa Zu and a jaggery-treated, distilled form called Poka mod/Fatika mod. It reports 20 plant species for Zu cake at study level, but supplies no village-by-plant allocation and no village-wise respondent counts. These data are not imported into a Hojai beverage or starter inventory. The paper's medicinal statements are community-reported beliefs, not clinical findings.

### Existing Tiwa sources rechecked

- **SRC-036 (Goswami 2020):** full text reports 2019 fieldwork in Boaropujia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat across Nagaon and Morigaon districts. It does not name a Hojai site. Its methods give the district pair for the study, but do not supply a complete village-to-district allocation in the reviewed passage. Keep its locality strings source-scoped; do not relocate them to Hojai.
- **SRC-037 (Duarah et al. 2023):** selected Tiwa villages are described as being in Nagaon district; the reviewed source does not name a Hojai village.
- **SRC-078:** the separate Plains-Tiwa village leads in Morigaon remain distinct; no Hojai locality is identified.

The fact that Hojai was historically part of Nagaon does not mean every source-era Nagaon study locality is now inside Hojai. A named village and a reliable boundary crosswalk are required.

## Administrative chronology and boundary caution

**SRC-183**, the Hojai District Disaster Management Plan 2024–2025, says Hojai was a subdivision under Nagaon from 15 August 1983; it records district declaration in 2015, merger back with Nagaon on 31 December 2022, and re-declaration as an administrative district on 7 September 2023. It lists Hojai, Lanka and Doboka revenue circles.

The current Hojai district website separately says Hojai was carved out of Nagaon on 15 August 2016. These official sources use differing chronology wording. The archive retains the exact statements and dates in their source contexts rather than inventing a harmonized legal timeline. Neither source provides a crosswalk from the Tiwa study villages to present Hojai.

The searches reviewed here did not locate a source that jointly identifies a named current-Hojai village, a community, and a traditional beverage with preparation or producer evidence.
