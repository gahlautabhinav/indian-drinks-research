# SRC-143 — Comparative biochemical analysis of Assam indigenous rice beverages (2015): full-text audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-04  
**Source:** Arjun, J. (2015). “Comparative biochemical analysis of certain indigenous rice beverages of tribes of Assam with some foreign liquor.” *Bioscience, Biotechnology Research Communications* 8(2):138–144.  
**Archive ID:** SRC-143  
**Related gap:** ASM-GAP-064

## 1. Retrieval and source identity

The complete seven-page article PDF was retrieved from the journal publisher:
https://bbrc.in/bbrc/papers/pdf%20files/Volume%208%20-%20No%202%20-%202015/8.pdf

The article is a distinct 2015 comparative biochemical study by J. Arjun. It is not the same paper as SRC-138 (Arjun, Verma & Prasad, 2014), although it shares the lead author and overlaps in Judima analysis.

## 2. Field scope and method

The paper states that fieldwork was undertaken in North Cachar Hills (identified in the text as Dima Hasao) and Karbi Anglong. It describes:
- structured questionnaires;
- visits to homes, workplaces and beverage-selling markets;
- face-to-face interviews in local languages;
- questions on preparation of Judima, Jumai, Horo and Poro.

The paper does **not** name the field villages, report the number of households/informants, identify how many beverage samples or batches were collected, or allocate each beverage sample to a named locality/community with sufficient clarity. The abstract's sequence linking Poro, Horo, Judima and Jumai to Mising, Karbi, Dimasa and Boro respectively is not a reliable explicit crosswalk; the methods name only Karbi, Mising and Dimasa people in the stated field districts, while the results compare four beverages. No new locality records or definitive beverage-community mapping are created from this paper.

## 3. Preparation details reported

The paper describes a two-stage process:
1. preparation of rice cakes/starter;
2. preparation of rice beer.

The starter description includes rice and *Acacia pennata* bark. It also mentions *Piper betle* leaves, *Buddleja asiatica* leaves and *Hedyotis scandens* leaves/twigs as variation ingredients. It describes glutinous rice flour softened by soaking, grinding with bark in a mortar/pestle called “remin”, addition of water, cake formation (reported radius 5–7 cm and mass 80–100 g), sprinkling old cake powder over new cakes (15–20 g), holding new cakes for at least 24 hours and sun-drying.

For beverage preparation, cooked rice is cooled, mixed with powdered rice-and-bark mixture, and kept in a large container covered with jute gunny bags. The text says slightly yellowish, creamy-white or white liquid appears after 5–7 days, followed by dilution and filtration.

**Caveat:** The article generalizes the process across four beverages and does not demonstrate that every ingredient, vessel, cake dimension or timing applies identically to every community or household. One passage says plant leaves (including *Acacia pennata* leaves) were collected, while the preparation description emphasizes *Acacia pennata* bark. Preserve this plant-part conflict; do not silently normalize it.

## 4. Plant screening table

The paper presents four plant taxa in its phytochemical screening:
- *Acacia pennata*
- *Buddleja asiatica*
- *Hedyotis scandens*
- *Piper betle*

It reports screening categories including protein, carbohydrate, phenols, tannins, flavonoids, saponins, glycosides, steroids, terpenoids and alkaloids. The paper states plant determinations were repeated three times and analyzed using one-way ANOVA with a Tukey post-hoc test. That triplicate statement appears in the plant-study methods; it should **not** be transferred to the beverage biochemical table, for which sample count and replicate design are not clearly reported.

These four plants are not assigned with adequate clarity to each beverage or each specific locality. Do not treat them as a verified universal Judima starter inventory or attach them to a village-specific recipe.

The table's pH heading is poorly rendered in the extracted text; the value 4.2 is preserved as printed and should be checked against a visual page image before structured numeric reuse.

### Internal discrepancies

The discussion (printed page 142) reports:
- Judima alcohol: **30%**, versus **39%** in the table.
- Horo alcohol: **30%**, versus **36%** in the table.
- Poro alcohol: 22.88% in both table and discussion.
- Jumai alcohol: 40.1% in the table and discussion.

The discrepancy is not resolved by the article. Both Judima values (39% table; 30% discussion) and both Horo values (36% table; 30% discussion) must remain visible in the archive. Do not average them or choose one without author/lab clarification.

The paper reports no clear beverage sample count, batch-level data, standard deviations or explicit beverage-assay replication design. The values are therefore single-source reported observations, not population estimates, current product specifications or safe-consumption guidance.

## 6. Methodological quality and interpretation

- Alcohol estimation is described using a chromic-acid reaction after distillation and spectrophotometric reading at 600 nm.
- Total carbohydrate is described using the anthrone method with glucose standard.
- Protein is described using the Folin–phenol/Lowry method.
- Turbidity is described spectrophotometrically.
- The free-amino-acid method is not adequately detailed in the accessible methods text.
- The comparison includes heterogeneous commercial beverages and locally prepared products; the paper does not provide enough sampling and analytical detail to support broad nutritional superiority claims.
- The paper's claims about therapeutic or health benefits are not clinical evidence and are not adopted in the archive.

## 7. Comparison with SRC-138 and household Judima studies

- SRC-138 (2014) is a separate preparation/biochemical study whose complete publisher PDF remains inaccessible in this pass. Its abstract reports 4–5 days; SRC-143 reports 5–7 days for its generalized preparation description. Do not collapse these into one standardized fermentation period.
- SRC-001 documents a Dhansiri household case and a source-specific Humao process; SRC-002 documents a Dibarai survey and a diagram-level process; SRC-003 documents a Tereh household case. These remain distinct evidence units.
- SRC-143 does not provide the locality-specific detail needed to override or merge those household records.

## 8. Repository decision and next action

- Register SRC-143 as a distinct full-text primary study.
- Open ASM-GAP-064 (High / Open) for sample allocation, method and data-discrepancy resolution.
- Do not add new beverage or locality records from this article.
- Next: seek author/lab clarification for the alcohol conflicts, beverage sample count, sample-to-community allocation, exact plant parts and raw analytical records; visually verify the table headings before importing numeric data.

**Conclusion:** SRC-143 adds a useful comparative method and a set of source-reported measurements, but its missing sample design and internal alcohol conflicts materially limit quantitative reuse.
