# SRC-114 — HRLC-MS study of Mising Apong starter cake E'pob

## Citation
Borah, Manashjyoti; Chavan, Sumedh; Shekade, Pratiksha; Dhabe, Arvind. (2024). “HRLC-MS analysis of Apong starter culture (E'pob): The starter cake used for the preparation of traditional rice beer of the Mising tribe of Assam.” *Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry* 13(2):810–813. https://doi.org/10.22271/phyto.2024.v13.i2f.14928

## Research question and method
The paper investigates chemical constituents in E'pob, a homemade starter cake used to ferment cooked rice for Mising Apong, using high-resolution liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (HRLC-MS). The accessible publisher page identifies the paper, authors, citation, DOI and abstract. The linked PDF endpoint presented an access-verification page during this audit, so detailed tables and compound identifications could not be independently checked from the accessible text.

## Evidence that can be recorded
- The paper treats E'pob as a mixed plant-part and rice-flour starter cake used in traditional Apong preparation.
- Its stated analytical target is the starter material itself, not a controlled trial of the finished beverage.
- The article's abstract repeats claims about medicinal/therapeutic properties associated with the beverage and herbs; those claims are not demonstrated clinical outcomes merely by chemical profiling.

## Limitations and interpretation rules
- Do not state that an identified chemical in a starter proves a health benefit, bioavailability, effective dose, safety, or presence at the same concentration in finished Apong.
- Do not infer microbial species, fermentation performance, or finished-drink alcohol content from HRLC-MS alone.
- Compound-level findings are intentionally not transcribed here because the article PDF could not be inspected in this audit. Obtain and review the full text before using individual compound names, peak assignments, or quantitative claims.
- No named village, field sample count, or beverage-subtype distribution is established by the accessible abstract.
- This study complements but does not replace ethnobotanical field studies of E'pob composition and locality-specific practices.

## Follow-up: indexed full-text extraction and analytical caution (2026-10-24)

A search-indexed copy of the article PDF exposes the methods and tables even though the publisher's PDF endpoint remains behind an access-verification page. The paper states that one E'pob sample was collected in **Lakhimpur district on 15 August 2023**. It reports drying and grinding the starter cake, extracting 25 g by Soxhlet with ethanol, petroleum ether and chloroform, and sending extracts for QTOF HRLC-MS analysis. Its tables list **34 compound annotations** across positive and negative ion modes.

This is a single starter-cake sample, not a representative survey of Mising starter cultures or finished Apong. The article's tables include names of well-known pharmaceutical substances (for example, mitoxantrone, irinotecan, carbenicillin and sulfinpyrazone). The accessible report does not establish that these are confirmed, naturally occurring constituents of E'pob using authentic reference standards or orthogonal confirmation. Treat them as the authors' tentative spectral annotations, not verified ingredients, evidence of drug contamination, or proof of biological activity. The paper's therapeutic conclusions are not supported by clinical testing of E'pob or Apong.

For Atlas use, record the Lakhimpur district as the **sample origin reported by this analytical paper**, not a village or a general production locality. Do not create a village-level row from this sample. Further verification would require raw spectra, library-match scores, blanks/controls, authentic standards, and author clarification on compound identification.
