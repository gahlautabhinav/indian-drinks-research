**Source:** Talukdar, S.; Jyethi, D. S.; Sarma, B.; et al. (2026), “Cultural ecology and conservation of traditional beverage practices among selected communities of Assam, Northeast India,” *Discover Food* 6, article 376. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s44187-026-01101-w  
**Repository source ID:** SRC-124

## Scope and extraction rule

The article's Table 1 explicitly labels a subset of plant entries as “Bodo” or shared between Bodo and another community. This crosswalk records those table-level labels only. The table does **not** contain a beverage-specific column and therefore does not establish that each listed plant is used in Amao, Joufinai, Joubidwi, or any particular Jou variant. The study covers Bodo informants in Kokrajhar and Baksa districts, but does not provide a named village for each plant record in the extracted table.

## Cross-check against SRC-123 Amao-related descriptions

SRC-123 is the more directly relevant Bodo-specific methodology paper and describes Amao starter ingredients/plant parts with vernacular names including Anaros (pineapple), Kanthal (jackfruit), Dongphangrakeb (*Scoparia dulcis*), Thalir (*Musa balbisiana*), Mwkwna (*Clerodendrum viscosum*), and sometimes Kuser (*Saccharum officinarum*) leaves. The cross-source comparison supports only these cautious observations:

- **Potential taxon-level overlaps:** SRC-124 includes *Ananas comosus*, *Scoparia dulcis*, and *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, which may be relevant to compare with SRC-123's Anaros, Dongphangrakeb and Kanthal descriptions. The SRC-124 rows do not say these are Amao ingredients, and *Artocarpus heterophyllus* is listed as a beverage-preparation leaf in a pooled table, not specifically as a starter-cake ingredient.
- **Do not equate banana entries:** SRC-124 lists *Musa paradisiaca* and SRC-123 reports *Musa balbisiana* (Thalir). They are different scientific-name strings; no synonymy or specimen-level identity should be assumed from shared vernacular “banana” context.
- **Plant part and role must remain distinct:** SRC-124 reports pineapple “tender leaf” and *Scoparia dulcis* “aerial part”; SRC-123's Amao account includes a plant-part-specific preparation description. A shared species name alone does not establish the same part, processing stage, or function.
- **No canonical inventory changes:** none of these rows should be appended to the canonical Amao ingredient list solely from SRC-124. Preserve as comparative leads pending a table-level product mapping, voucher crosswalk and/or author/community clarification.

## Quality flags retained

1. The paper reports 41 species but its prose life-form counts (17 herbs + 13 shrubs + 9 trees + 4 climbers + 4 ferns) sum to 47. The prior SRC-124 quantitative audit and ASM-GAP-041/046 remain open.
2. FL is defined as a percentage using (Ip/Iu) × 100, but the table reports values such as 3.78. These cannot be interpreted as percentage values without clarification.
3. Voucher code ST/EES/2024/015 appears for both *Drymaria diandra* and *Pteridium aquilinum* in the table. Preserve the discrepancy; do not infer which identifier is correct.
4. The table's general beverage terminology (angkur/Bakhar) is not sufficient to merge those labels with SRC-123's Amao terminology.

## Outcome

This is a source-faithful attribution crosswalk, not a corrected botanical inventory. It improves discoverability of the article's Bodo-tagged rows while keeping beverage identity, localities, taxonomic identity and starter-culture membership unresolved where the source does not provide the necessary link.
