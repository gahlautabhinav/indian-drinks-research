# SRC-136 — Po:ro Apong / Saimod full-text audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-04  
**Source:** Kardong, D.; Deori, K.; Sood, K.; Yadav, R.N.S.; Bora, T.C.; Gogoi, B.K. (2012). “Evaluation of Nutritional and Biochemical aspects of Po:ro apong (Saimod) — A home made alcoholic rice beverage of Mising tribe of Assam, India.” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 11(3):499–504.  
**Archive ID:** SRC-136  
**Related gap:** ASM-GAP-060

## 1. Retrieval status and evidence scope

Full article text retrieved from the author-uploaded ResearchGate copy. The paper reports that consumable Po:ro Apong samples were collected under sterile conditions from local Mising households in Dibrugarh district, Upper Assam. It does not name villages or provide a household-level sampling frame. It is a historical study, not a current production census.

## 2. Preparation process reported

### Epop starter
- Glutinous rice is preferred, soaked for about two hours and ground.
- It is mixed with semi-dried powdered leaves or whole plant/plant parts.
- The mixture is kneaded into an oval form.
- Microbial consortium from a previous batch is transferred/inoculated into the new starter.

### Po:ro Apong
- About **100 g powdered Epop** is described as sufficient for **3 kg cooked rice**.
- Warm cooked rice is coated with ash powder from rice husk and rice straw.
- The rice-ash mixture is mixed with Epop and placed in a clean, dry container, preferably an earthen pot.
- The vessel is plugged with dry straw or fresh fern leaves (*Pteridium* spp.).
- Fermentation is reported for about **20 days at 30–35°C**.
- The ripe mash is called **Po:ro arouk** (also printed as aruk in one passage).
- Extraction uses warm water poured over mash held in a bamboo cone called **Ta:suk**, internally wrapped with banana or Canna leaves. The liquid collected below is the drink.
- The remaining mash is described as fodder for pigs and cattle.

The article also reports traditional sensory/odour observations at different fermentation stages. These are recorded as ethnographic observations, not as validated food-safety indicators.

## 3. Epop plant inventory

The article states that at least 16 herbs are used and provides a 16-entry Table 1. All 16 entries, local names, scientific names as printed, growth forms, plant parts and reported community interpretations have been transcribed to:

**research/assam/epop-16-plants-src136.csv**

Important controls:
- The source contains likely botanical spelling and taxonomy errors (for example, “Centala acciatica”, “Hydrocotyl rotundifolia”, “Zanthoxylum hemiltonianum” and “Naravelia feylavica”).
- These forms are preserved verbatim in the source transcription. No silent correction has been made.
- Table 1 reports local interpretations/uses. These are not clinical evidence of medicinal efficacy.
- The article itself says the plant count may vary from place to place within the Mising community. Do not treat this 16-entry list as a universal Epop recipe.

The paper reports microbial load as:
- Epop starter: 549 × 10^7 cfu/gm.
- Ripe mash: 414 × 10^7 cfu/gm.
- Finished product: 8,700 ± 6.332 cfu/mm^3.

The starter/mash values use cfu/gm and plate-count methods; the finished-liquid value is reported per mm^3 and determined using a haemocytometer. These counts are **not directly comparable** without accounting for different sample matrices, methods and units.

## 5. Methods and limitations

- Carbohydrate: Anthrone method with D-glucose standard.
- Protein: Folin–Phenol method.
- Reducing sugar: DNS reagent.
- Total amino acids: Ninhydrin reagent with lysine standard.
- pH: digital pH meter.
- Titratable acidity: titration against 0.1N NaOH, expressed as lactic acid.
- Ethanol: chromic acid method after distillation, expressed as % v/v.
- Microbial load: dilution plate technique for Epop and ripe mash; haemocytometer for finished product.

The article's own table is based on three samples, and the authors describe sample selection as products with similar taste and texture. It is not a representative survey of all Mising producers. The 7.52–18.5% ethanol range is study-specific and must not be generalized to all Po:ro Apong or used as safety advice.

## 6. Cross-source reconciliation priorities

Compare the 16 taxa and starter process against:
- SRC-005 (Sivasagar ÉPOP study);
- SRC-006 (Lakhimpur Mising starter survey);
- SRC-007 (Majuli Epop study);
- SRC-084 and other later Epop inventories.

Do not collapse differing inventories into a single master recipe. Retain source-specific counts, spelling, plant-part claims and locality. Pawe & Gogoi (2013), SRC-135, remains bibliography-only and requires its own full-text retrieval. The two 2011 Kardong studies (SRC-139 and SRC-140) also remain full-text retrieval targets.

**Conclusion:** SRC-136 is a substantial primary source for Mising Po:ro Apong. It documents a distinct ash-assisted process, a 20-day fermentation description, bamboo Ta:suk extraction, a 16-entry Epop inventory and biochemical measurements. Its district-level locality and three-sample design must remain explicit.
