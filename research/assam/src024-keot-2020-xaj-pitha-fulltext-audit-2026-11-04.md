# SRC-024 — Keot et al. (2020) Xaj-pitha microbial quality and defined-starter study: full-text audit

**Citation:** Keot, J., Bora, S. S., Kangabam, R. D., & Barooah, M. (2020). “Assessment of microbial quality and health risks associated with traditional rice wine starter Xaj-pitha of Assam, India: a step towards defined and controlled fermentation.” *3 Biotech* 10, 64. DOI: 10.1007/s13205-020-2059-z. Published online 23 January 2020; issue February 2020.

**Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6977804/

## Field sampling design

- Target: Ahom Xaj-pitha starter cakes, not a representative survey of all Assam rice beverages.
- Six Upper Brahmaputra Valley districts: Sivasagar, Jorhat, Dibrugarh, Tinsukia, Dhemaji and Lakhimpur.
- Random cluster design: two clusters per district, 12 clusters total; five Xaj-pitha samples per cluster; 60 samples total.
- Named clusters (as printed): Tinsukia — Margherita, Sadiya; Dibrugarh — Moran, Naharkatiya; Sivasagar — Bokota, Sonari; Jorhat — Titabar, Nimati; Lakhimpur — Bihpuria, Dhakuakhana; Dhemaji — Bordoloni, Silapathar.
- Samples were transported on ice and immediately used for microbial analysis.
- For microbial diversity analysis, five samples from each cluster were combined equally to create one composite per cluster (12 composites; two per district). The metagenomic analysis used one representative composite from Titabar subdivision, Jorhat district. It is not a metagenomic profile of all 60 samples.

## Culture-based and metagenomic findings

The authors report high fungal, yeast, lactic acid bacterial and aerobic bacterial counts in starter samples. Table 1 provides district/cluster-level average CFU counts for fungi, yeasts, LAB, aerobic mesophiles and enterobacteria. Across district averages, reported ranges are:
- fungi: 2.4–3.7 log10 CFU/g;
- yeasts: 7.5–8.4 log10 CFU/g;
- LAB: 7.8–8.7 log10 CFU/g;
- aerobic mesophiles: 1.5–2.7 log10 CFU/g;
- enterobacteria: 0.33–1.3 log10 CFU/g.

The paper's fermentation dynamics experiment used a composite starter sample with gelatinized glutinous Bora rice. It reports yeast and LAB counts increasing over seven days, with highest counts on days four and five; enterobacteria were reported to disappear by day four in that experimental fermentation. The paper reports pH decreasing from 6.0 to 3.3 and temperature remaining around 25–30 °C.

The single Titabar/Jorhat metagenomic composite yielded sequence-based taxonomic assignments including *Rhizopus delemar*, *Mucor circinelloides*, *Lactobacillus plantarum*, *Meyerozyma guilliermondii*, *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia*, *Wickerhamomyces ciferrii* and *Lactobacillus brevis*. These are findings from one composite sample and must not be generalized to all 60 samples or all Xaj-pitha.

## Isolate screening and defined mixed culture

The paper reports:
- 22 morphologically different mould strains isolated; 13 showed extracellular amylase production; four were selected for further work: *Penicillium* sp. ABTSJ23, *Rhizopus oryzae* ABTSJ63, *Mucor guilliermondii* ABTSJ72 and *Amylomyces rouxii* ABTSJ82.
- Eight yeast isolates selected: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* ABTY1S, ABTY1J, ADJ1 and ADJ5; *Wickerhamomyces anomalus* ADJ2; *Saccharomycopsis malanga* ADJ3 and ADJ6; *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* ADJ4.
- The paper reports two yeast strains with biogenic-amine production ability: *S. cerevisiae* ABTY1S decarboxylated lysine HCl and ADJ1 decarboxylated tyramine HCl.
- All tested mould cultures were negative for aflatoxin production in the reported test; this is not a blanket screen for all mycotoxins or all starter samples.
- The selected mixed culture used *Amylomyces rouxii* ABT82 as fungal partner, *S. fibuligera* ADJ4 and *S. cerevisiae* ABT-Y1J as yeast partners. The abstract reports ethanol production up to 10.11% v/v and 9.88% v/v for selected yeast strains; the mixed culture results are reported separately in the abstract (glucose 10.91% w/v, ethanol 7.5% w/v and liquid 51.0% w/v).

## Evidence boundaries and interpretation

1. This is a study of Ahom Xaj-pitha and experimental defined cultures. It does not establish a universal recipe or microbial profile for all Ahom households or other Assam communities.
2. The 60 starter cakes are not 60 independent metagenomic profiles: five samples were pooled per cluster, and WGS was performed on one representative Titabar/Jorhat composite.
3. The paper's potential-health-risk discussion is an interpretation of organisms detected in starter culture. It is not an epidemiological study, clinical diagnosis, finished-beverage exposure assessment or evidence that all Xaj is unsafe.
4. Aflatoxin-negative mould results do not mean all isolates, all mycotoxins or finished beverage batches were tested and cleared.
5. The selected defined consortium is an experimental candidate, not evidence that it is adopted by households or a validated universal safe starter.
6. Keep sample locality, isolate identity and beverage process separate. The article does not establish current production in every sampled cluster.

## Atlas actions

- Retain SRC-024 as the source record; this is a full-text audit, not a new source.
- Use the 12 named cluster leads in ASM-GAP-021 only as historic Xaj-pitha sampling sites, not as proof of current Xaj production.
- The audit does not add new beverage, village or product rows.
- Compare with SRC-023 (2016 Titabar/Jorhat metagenomics) and SRC-108 (2019–20 multi-starter mycoflora) without merging datasets. The studies differ in sampling frame, starter scope and analytical design.

## Next verification

Seek the sample register, isolate-level source mapping and supplementary methods; verify strain accessions and experimental replicate details; ask authors whether the selected mixed consortium was evaluated in any household-scale or follow-up study.
