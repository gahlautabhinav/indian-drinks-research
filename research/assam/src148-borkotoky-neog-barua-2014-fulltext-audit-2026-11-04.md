# SRC-148 — Borkotoky, Neog & Barua (2014): full-text audit

## Bibliographic record and access

Babita Phukan Borkotoky, Mridula Neog and Iswar Chandra Barua, “Mishing’s Apong – An Intangible Cultural Heritage of Assam.” Reproduced in *The Exalted Edition / IJHEPS*, January 2014, Issue 7, article beginning at printed page 59 and continuing through pp. 59–63 in the digital issue. The issue displays ISSN 2320-2955. Accessible digital reproduction: https://fliphtml5.com/ntyj/brxv/

The article is accessible as text within the FlipHTML5 issue. This is a digital reproduction, not a publisher-hosted journal archive. Bibliographic details should be cross-checked against an original issue before treating the journal metadata as fully authenticated.

## Field geography and collection

- The authors report extensive and repeated survey in Mising-dominated areas of Jorhat district, Assam.
- Prepared Epop/Epop pitha starter and Apong samples were collected mostly from five named villages: Sirum, Budubari, Boloma, Bhomoraguri and Arunamukh.
- Plant collection took place in April, May and June of 2011 and 2012.
- The authors say voucher specimens were prepared, identified using local floras and herbarium references, and preserved in the Herbarium of Kakojan College, Jorhat.
- No informant count, household count, village-specific allocation of taxa, or voucher accession numbers are supplied in the accessible text.

## Starter-culture account

The paper says women were generally involved in preparing Epop and Apong. Plant materials/parts were sun-dried for at least 2–3 days and ground in a wooden grinder. The greenish powder was mixed with water-soaked rice and a few old Epop cakes, then ground further. Water was added and spherical/ovoid cakes 2–5 cm in diameter were formed. Cakes were held on a bamboo frame about 1.5 m above the kitchen fireplace for about three days, then sun-dried another 2–3 days. The authors say the dried cakes could be stored for about two months.

These are the article's reported observations; they should not be merged into a universal Epop recipe.

## Separate beverage processes

### Nogin Apong

The paper describes tightly cooked fresh rice, mixed with powdered starter at about 4–8 Epop cakes per kg rice and kept in clean pots, usually earthen, plugged with rice straw or fern leaves. It reports that Nogin Apong accumulated after 3–4 days.

### Po:ro Apong / Saimod

The authors describe ash from specially burnt rice husk with hay mixed into cooked rice before starter addition, until the rice became black. The mixture was held in pots for 15–20 days. The fermented mixture was transferred to a conical bamboo frame lined with banana leaves; drinking water was poured through it and the filtrate collected in earthen pots.

This is a distinct field account from other reported Po:ro processes (including SRC-136 and SRC-147). Preserve the difference rather than normalising times or ratios.

## Microbiology and biochemical results

The paper reports three species of *Saccharomyces*, and one species each of *Mucor* and *Rhizopus*, isolated from the starter culture. It says no growth of Enterobacteriaceae or lactic acid bacteria was observed under the culture conditions used. The reported total viable yeast/mould count is 118 × 10^7 per ml of original culture sample.

The authors report 5% alcohol in both Nogin and Po:ro Apong; Nogin is reported to have higher carbohydrate, protein and fat content and a higher calorific value than Po:ro, which the authors associate with the absence of a filtration step. They report approximately 11 free amino acids in both varieties. These are source-specific results and must not be treated as universal product values or health evidence. Culture-negative results are limited to the media and incubation conditions used; they do not prove that bacteria are absent from the beverage ecosystem.

## Interpretation and research gaps

This source adds a named Jorhat collection footprint and detailed comparative Nogin/Po:ro process descriptions, plus culture-based microbiological and biochemical results. It does not supply sample-size information, taxon-to-village mapping, voucher accession numbers, or a reliable machine-readable 51-plant inventory. Do not infer clinical benefits from the paper's broad health language.

The paper's own 5% alcohol result differs from the 7.52–18.5% v/v range reported in SRC-136. This should be presented as between-study variation, not an error to reconcile without comparable sample definitions and analytical protocols.

## Interpretation cautions

- The abstract reports 51 starter-plant species, but the accessible text layer does not reliably expose Table 1. No species-level inventory has been added.
- The reported 118 × 10^7 viable yeast/mould cells per ml is attributed to the original culture sample; it is not a count for every finished beverage or a universal starter specification.
- The absence of Enterobacteriaceae and lactic-acid bacteria is a negative result under the paper’s stated selective media and incubation conditions, not proof that such organisms never occur in Epop or Apong.
- The paper’s 5% alcohol figure is source-specific and should not be averaged with other studies.
- Claims about medicinal importance, health and welfare are not clinical evidence.
