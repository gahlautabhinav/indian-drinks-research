# Bodo Jou community heritage pages: source and claim audit (SRC-119)

**Audit date:** 2026-10-24  
**Purpose:** Capture the process and cultural claims published by Bodoland GI Products and Handloom Centre Trust while separating them from the official GI registry record and from scientific or legal product specifications.

## Source identity and authority

SRC-119 is the Bodoland GI Heritage website, a community-heritage and promotion resource operated by Bodoland GI Products and Handloom Centre Trust. The site's About page says the Trust was established on 25 March 2025 and describes the Bodo Culture and Heritage Preservation Board (BCHPB) as having spearheaded GI registration. The Trust says it does not own the GI registrations. This is useful institutional/contextual evidence, but it is not itself the official GI Registry and its product pages are not substitutes for the underlying Statements of Case.

- Jou Gwran page: https://bodolandgihandloomcentre.org/gi-heritage/bodo-jou-gwran
- Jou Gishi page: https://bodolandgihandloomcentre.org/gi-heritage/bodo-jou-gishi
- Maibra Jou Bidwi page: the site's museum lists the product, but the direct page was not found in this pass.
- Trust/About page: https://bodolandgihandloomcentre.org/about
- Official registry applications: https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/968 ; /969 ; /970

## Registry-number correction

The heritage pages use the certificate numbers as “GI” numbers and repeat them as application numbers. The official IP India registry distinguishes these fields:

| Product | Official application no. | Official certificate no. | Heritage page label |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bodo Jou Gwran | 968 | 648 | “GI 648” and “Application No. 648” |
| Bodo Jou Gishi | 969 | 649 | “GI 649” and “Application No. 649” |
| Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi | 970 | 650 | Museum label “GI 650” |

For archive identifiers and citations, use the official application numbers 968–970 and certificate numbers 648–650 in their correct fields. The website's label is a display/metadata inconsistency, not evidence that the registered products themselves are invalid.

## Source-reported process claims

### Bodo Jou Gishi

The page describes cooked rice cooled under banana leaves, powdered Amao starter mixed by hand, an initial 1–2 day period, transfer to an earthen pot or bucket with a conical bamboo sieve called “Janta,” sealing, and harvesting a golden-yellow liquid after 5–6 days. It reports pH ~4.21 and alcohol around 5–6.4%.

These are **SRC-119 claims**, not values verified against the official Statement of Case or a retrievable analytical paper in this pass. The displayed starter ratio (“1:1 kg of rice”) is ambiguous as written and should not be silently reinterpreted. The page's health/medicinal assertions are cultural-belief statements, not clinical evidence.

### Bodo Jou Gwran

The page describes Amao mixed with cooked rice; a 3–4 day fermentation to “jumai”; storage for 3–5 days; dilution with water into “Afri”; a 2–3 hour hold; and distillation in a three-layer pot arrangement for 2–4 hours, reportedly repeated two to four times. It names the residue “Afri Katla.”

The same page reports alcohol around 16.11%, but also states that more repeated distillation results in lower concentration. That wording is internally unclear without knowing whether it refers to the particular apparatus, collection fractions, dilution, or a translation issue. Do not present it as a general distillation principle or as a verified GI specification. The measurement method, sample identity, sample count, and source for the percentage remain unverified.

### Maibra Jou Bidwi

The site's museum summary says it is brewed from a special glutinous rice called “Maibra,” and associates it with festivals, religious rituals and welcome-drink use. A secondary 2024 news summary says half-cooked rice (“mairong”), less water and Amao are used, but this is not a substitute for the registered case statement. The direct product page and primary specification were not retrieved in this pass.

## Geography and cultural claims

The site describes the products as belonging to the Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR), while the official registry's geographical-area field reads “Assam.” These are not necessarily contradictory: one is a regional heritage description, the other a broad registry field. Neither alone defines the legally delimited production boundary. Do not expand the place register from this page alone.

The pages describe marriage, ritual, social and welcome-drink contexts. Preserve them as source-attributed cultural descriptions; do not assume every Bodo household follows the same practice or that every described use applies identically to all three products.

## Decisions for the research archive

1. Add SRC-119 as a secondary institutional/heritage source.
2. Use its process descriptions as leads for primary-source comparison, not as legal GI specifications.
3. Correctly distinguish official application numbers 968–970 from certificate numbers 648–650.
4. Keep Jou Gwran, Jou Gishi and Maibra Jou Bidwi as separate registered records; no synonymy inferred.
5. Keep ASM-GAP-026 open until readable official Statements of Case, Journal 191 entries and amended maps/producer lists are transcribed and compared.
6. Do not add locality rows, infer active production from authorised-user addresses, or repeat health claims as clinical findings.


## Current-domain and product-page recheck (2026-11-13)

The Trust's current Bodoland GI Heritage site is now served at `bodolandgi.abacusai.app`. Its About page identifies the same Bodoland GI Products and Handloom Centre Trust and the same Trust Deed number (2025/IGR085/4/11) recorded on the earlier domain, so this is treated as a migrated/current version of SRC-119, not a separate independent source. The earlier `bodolandgihandloomcentre.org` pages remain discoverable and contain substantially similar content.

### Updated page-level process claims

- **Jou Gishi:** the current page reports cooked rice cooled under banana leaves, powdered Amao mixed by hand with the ambiguous wording “1:1 kg of rice”, 1–2 days before transfer to an earthen pot/bucket with a conical bamboo sieve (Maldang/Janta), then 5–6 days until golden-yellow liquid is harvested. It repeats pH ~4.21 and 5–6.4% alcohol without a measurement protocol or sample design.
- **Jou Gwran:** the current page reports 3–4 days to form jumai, 3–5 days preservation, dilution into Afri, 2–3 hours holding, and three-layer pot distillation for 2–4 hours repeated two to four times. It repeats ~16.11% alcohol and says more repeated distillation lowers concentration. This remains internally unclear and is not a general distillation principle or a GI specification.
- **Maibra Jou Bidwi:** the new current page adds a longer maturation claim: half-cooked Maibra rice, Amao, 1–2 days initial stage, 5–7 days until a dark brownish-red juice forms, but harvest only after 3 months to a year or more. Keep the 5–7-day fermentation/formation stage distinct from the claimed later maturation/storage period. The 1:1 kg wording is ambiguous as written; do not reinterpret it as a ratio.

These are Trust-published heritage descriptions, not the registered Statements of Case and not independent analytical evidence. The pages' health/medicinal claims remain cultural/promotional statements, not clinical evidence. No method row or beverage canonical field was changed on this basis.

### Holder / applicant discrepancy

The current Trust About page says GI registrations are held by five community associations and specifically lists the **Bodo Traditional Brewer's Association** as the holder association for Maibra Jou Bidwi, Jou Gishi and Jou Gwran. By contrast, the official IP India application-detail pages for 968–970 continue to show **Bodo Geographical Indication (G.I) Board** in the applicant field. These fields may describe different procedural roles or a later transfer, but the current evidence does not settle that. Do not replace the official registry applicant field with the Trust's holder statement. Request the registration certificates, current Part A register entry, or an official confirmation of the registered proprietor/association.

The Trust pages continue to label certificate numbers 648, 649 and 650 as “Application No.”; official IP India records show application IDs 968, 969 and 970 and certificates 648, 649 and 650. Treat the current site's “Verification Status: VERIFIED” as its own display label, not independent verification.

No new locality, producer-village, GI boundary or canonical ingredient claim was added. The pages describe BTR/Assam at broad scale only.
