# SRC-171 — Sarma & Devi (2016): Nogin Epop plant inventory and cross-study reconciliation

**Research date:** 2026-11-12  
**Source ID:** SRC-171 (new; checked source register before assignment)  
**Citation:** Jintu Sarma & Ashalata Devi (2016), “Role of dicot angiosperms in the livelihood of Mishing community in Sonitpur district, Assam, India,” *Tropical Plant Research* 3(3):662–672. DOI: 10.22271/tpr.2016.v3.i3.087.

## 1. Field scope and botanical workflow

The study reports fieldwork in **17 Mising villages in Sonitpur district during 2013–2015** and interviews with **194 villagers (149 male, 45 female)**. The villages listed are Dharikati, Khonamukh, Kathani, Rangajan, Rongajan Miri, Baligaon, Sotaimiri, Toupamiri, Bamunipam, Bordikorai, Sikomgaon, Silenighat, Morikhuti, Bokagaon, Kekokoli, Tinighoria and Gudamghat.

Herbarium specimens were prepared and identified using Gauhati University and Botanical Survey of India, Shillong herbarium comparisons, literature and taxonomic consultation. The paper says analyzed herbarium material was submitted to the Tezpur University Herbarium (TUH) for preservation. It does not give specimen accession numbers or a plant-to-village matrix for the Epop taxa.

The full source-faithful table transcription is in research/assam/src171-sarma-devi-2016-nogin-epop-11-taxon-inventory.csv. Blank vernacular/use cells and the *Costus* plant-part conflict are retained rather than filled by inference.

The authors state that Mising households use both dicots and monocots in Nogin Epop preparation; the 11 taxa are the dicot subset recorded in this study, not a complete all-plant Nogin recipe.

## 3. Reconciliation against SRC-148 and SRC-170

Three source-specific inventories now need to remain distinct:

- **SRC-148 (Borkotoky et al., 2014 issue):** reports 51 plant species across 31 families used in Mising Epop starter culture. It describes both Nogin and Po:ro processes but does not allocate the 51 taxa to either variant in the accessible article text. The article's Table 1 is not exposed as machine-readable text in the retrieved digital copy; the full 51-row inventory still requires page-image/high-resolution table transcription. The paper says voucher specimens were prepared and identifications were checked against local floras, Assam Agricultural University Weed Herbarium and BSI Kanjilal Herbarium, but gives no accession numbers in the accessible methods.
- **SRC-170 (Pawe & Gogoi, 2013):** explicitly Poro Apob and reports 28 taxon-level entries; its table has 27 visible serials because two *Thelypteris* names share row 25 and serial 26 is absent. It is not a generic Epop list.
- **SRC-171 (Sarma & Devi, 2016):** explicitly Nogin/Nogjin Epop and reports 11 dicot taxa only, from the 17-village Sonitpur field survey. It does not claim these are the full mixed dicot/monocot recipe.

### Candidate cross-source spelling/identity checks

- SRC-171 *Costus speciosus* versus SRC-170 printed “Coctus speciosa”: likely a spelling/taxonomic-name correspondence, but keep source strings unchanged until a botanical authority crosswalk verifies the accepted name.
- SRC-171 *Zanthoxylum nitidum* versus SRC-170 printed “Zenthoxylum nitidum”: likely a typographic difference, but keep both source strings and mark as candidate match pending authority verification.
- No other identity merges are asserted. Similar vernacular labels or broad family/genus resemblance are not sufficient to merge taxa.
- The SRC-148 51-taxon list cannot yet be compared systematically because its Table 1 remains unreadable in the retrieved text version. Do not claim that the 2016 list is a confirmed subset of the 2014 list.

## 4. Evidence and data-quality limits

- SRC-171's 17 villages and 194 respondents describe the broad ethnobotanical survey; the paper does not provide Epop-specific respondent counts or village-by-taxon allocation.
- TUH herbarium submission is reported, but specimen numbers and a publicly traceable voucher catalogue were not recovered.
- Table 3 has an internal *Costus* part-used/use-text mismatch; preserve both fields.
- The article's general ethnomedicinal content is not evidence of clinical efficacy or safety for ingesting any plant.
- The paper's 11 species should not be treated as the complete Nogin starter composition, because it explicitly describes a dicot-only subset and notes monocots are also used.
- No plant abundance, recipe proportions, frequency-of-use ranking or microbial function is established by the 11-row table.
