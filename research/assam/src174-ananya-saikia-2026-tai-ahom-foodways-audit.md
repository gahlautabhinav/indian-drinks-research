## Source-faithful beverage evidence

### Names and cultural use

- The abstract identifies **luk lao** as rice beer and a food tradition important in Tai-Ahom rituals, festivals and social life (printed pp. 159–160).
- The article separately says “rice wine named xaj” is common among Ahom villages in Upper Assam, then describes **Luk-Lao** as a holy drink used in religious ceremonies and offered to ancestors (printed pp. 167–168).
- The article does not explain whether Xaj is a synonym, a related preparation, a stage, or a distinct beverage from Luk-Lao. Preserve both source strings; do not merge the names.
- It reports ritual importance but provides no specific ritual date, village, household count or independent community-language validation.

### Starter-cake terminology and ingredients

The article calls the starter **XajorPitha**. It names three ingredients with scientific names: Tezmui (*Zanthoxylum nitidum*), Manimuni (*Centella asiatica*) and Kopou dhekia (*Lygodium microphyllum*). It also lists “soft leaves of pineapple and sugarcane”, Pani Madhuri, bon jaluk, phutkola, jetulipoka and huwalota without scientific names. These are source-reported strings only. No voucher specimens, plant parts for each ingredient, collection localities, quantities or taxonomic determinations are supplied. Do not assign species to the vernacular-only names or merge XajorPitha with Vekur pitha / Xaj-pitha without further evidence.

The starter-making account says herbs are gathered, washed and sun-dried, then mixed with freshly pounded rice flour and shaped into cakes. Cakes are placed in a bamboo sieve called “saloni” (the text later spells it “saloli”), covered with bihlongoni, hay and banana leaves for three days, then dried on a chula. This is a narrative account, not a controlled protocol; the exact plant identity of bihlongoni is not established in this article.

### Beverage preparation — two conflicting accounts

1. One passage says mixed rice is half-boiled, cooled, inoculated with yeast cake, fermented in an earthen pot and served after **5–10 days**. Water is then added to the fermented rice and the liquid poured for service (printed p. 168).
2. A later passage says Bora rice is cooked and cooled on banana leaves, the dry cakes are crushed and mixed into the rice, then placed in a closed earthen pot called **koloh**. It states that Lao is ready after **3–5 days**, after which water is mixed into the fermented mass and it is filtered (printed pp. 168–169).

The article does not explain whether these are two different household methods, two phases, or an internal inconsistency. Preserve both time ranges separately. Do not select one as canonical or treat the duration as a food-safety guarantee.

## Evidence quality and archive decisions

- **Value:** a recent full-text qualitative article that directly names Luk-Lao, reports its ritual role, introduces XajorPitha terminology and gives a narrative starter/process description.
- **Limitations:** no named locality, field dates, informant count, voucher register, laboratory measurements, reproducible recipe quantities or explicit Xaj/Luk-Lao distinction.
- The article's use of “Xaj” and “Luk-Lao” in the same discussion reinforces that the naming problem remains open; it does not resolve it.
- The XajorPitha name is not automatically equivalent to Vekur pitha, Haaj-pitha or Xaj-pitha in other sources.
- No canonical beverage record, plant record, village row, or beverage alias was created or merged from SRC-174 alone.

## Next evidence needed

1. Obtain Tai-Ahom language/community confirmation of the distinction, if any, between Luk-Lao, Xaj, Nam-Lao, Laopani, Sajpani and Xajpani/Kolohpani.
2. Request author clarification of field locality, field dates, number of participants, and whether the 5–10-day and 3–5-day passages describe different methods.
3. Ask for the source or voucher evidence behind XajorPitha's plant list, especially the vernacular-only ingredients and bihlongoni.
4. Compare XajorPitha against Vekur pitha and the 2007/2012/2013 Ahom starter descriptions without assuming synonymy.
