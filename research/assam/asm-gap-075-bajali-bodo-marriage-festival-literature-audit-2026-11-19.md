# ASM-GAP-075 — Bajali Bodo Jou: historical marriage and festival literature follow-up

**Audit date:** 2026-11-19  
**Scope:** Follow up the two Bajali-focused Gauhati University dissertation leads identified in the previous locality audit. Determine whether accessible evidence links Jou to Bajali specifically.

## Result

**The two dissertations remain unrecovered. ASM-GAP-075 stays Open. No village-level beverage-production or consumption row is justified.**

A significant contextual source has been recovered: Kameswar Brahma's *Aspects of Social Customs of the Bodos* (1989), hosted as a digitised book by the Government of India's Tribal Research repository. It describes jau/jumai as rice beer in Bodo marriage and funeral customs, including storage and serving equipment. It does not identify Bajali as a field site. Its geographic comparisons name other areas/districts (including Kamrup, Darrang, Goalpara and Kokrajhar), not Bajali. It is therefore a Bodo-wide/historical comparative source, not Bajali locality evidence.

A 2021 article by Alaka Basumatary also describes Jou being prepared and served at a traditional Bodo marriage ceremony (the Juli Afad element under the sainashali marriage pavilion). However, the article's accessible text does not identify Bajali or a named study village; no field-site method was located in the accessible article text. Treat it as a contemporary general Bodo marriage-custom comparator, not Bajali field evidence.

## The two dissertation leads

The page reports five chapters:

2. Cultural and social convergence of Boros and non-Boros in the area

This is an especially relevant acquisition lead because Chapter III is explicitly about major folk festivals in the Bajali area. But the thesis itself, catalogue record, abstract and chapter text were not retrieved. We cannot state that it mentions Jou, identify a festival-specific beverage practice, or name any village from the thesis.

### SRC-299 — Mangroo Prasad (2002)

Bibliographic form recorded by the same annotated bibliography page:
*Marriage Customs of the Bodos (with Special Reference to Bajali Sub-division)*. Gauhati University Library.

The page reports six chapters covering introduction, Bodo marriage system, stages of marriage, marriage ceremony, marriage songs and conclusion. It says the work addresses marriage types, bride selection, selection of month/date and associated songs. The thesis itself, official catalogue record, abstract and chapter text were not retrieved. It is a direct and promising Bajali-focused acquisition lead, but no claim about Jou in its pages can be made yet.

**Bibliographic caution:** The accessible page is a user-space WikiEducator annotated bibliography, not Gauhati University's official catalogue. It supplies a library location claim and chapter summaries but no call number, department, degree, supervisor, accession number or stable institutional record. Both thesis entries remain provisional until confirmed against Gauhati University Library's catalogue or physical holdings.

## Recovered comparative source — SRC-297

Kameswar Brahma, *Aspects of Social Customs of the Bodos* (1989), digitised by the Tribal Research Institute, Assam, and hosted by the Government of India's Tribal Research repository.

Relevant text:
- The marriage chapter describes jau/jumai (rice beer) being carried to the bride's home, served to villagers and supplied in abundance during the marriage feast.
- It records large earthen storage pitchers called Dabkha or Maldanga; a wooden vessel Doongshu; a bamboo mixing stick Khadou; a bamboo strainer jantha; a server/distributor called jau rangra; and a banana-bark cup called thona.
- It says rice beer was prepared ahead of the ceremony and describes mixing with water and filtering before serving.
- The same book notes that some Bodo groups following Brahma Dharma had discouraged rice beer in marriage ceremonies, with tea replacing it in the account's contemporary period. Preserve this as the author's time- and group-specific observation, not a universal present-day statement.
- The book also mentions rice beer in funeral and seasonal/religious contexts.

The book's author says it arose from extensive research fieldwork and informant conversations, but the accessible book does not identify a Bajali field site. The marriage chapter's explicit district-level comparisons refer to Kamrup, Darrang, Goalpara and Kokrajhar; no Bajali attribution was located. Do not use this book to fill Bajali's locality gap.

## Additional comparator — SRC-300

Alaka Basumatary (2021), “Corrosion of Tribal Social Customs Owing to Acculturation: A Study of Bodos in India,” *Turkish Online Journal of Qualitative Inquiry* 12(5):3270–3287.

The article describes the marriage pavilion (sainashali), two soil ovens and preparation/serving of jOu (rice beer) as Juli Afad. It also describes post-marriage rice-beer customs. The accessible paper's text does not identify Bajali or name the villages where the described customs were observed. No respondent count or field-site schedule was located in the accessible text. This source is contextual only.

## Locality and evidence decisions

1. The two dissertation titles explicitly identify Bajali sub-division, but their contents have not been inspected. Their titles alone do not establish a specific beverage practice.
2. The recovered 1989 and 2021 marriage accounts support the general proposition that Jou/jau has a place in Bodo marriage customs. Neither is a Bajali-specific field observation.
3. Do not create a beverage-place row for Chenglimari, Duramari, Mwithabari, Jamguri, Bharegaon, Sonapur, Kathalmurighat, Doloigaon, Bamakhata or Muguria based on this evidence.
4. Do not infer that every Bodo marriage or festival in Bajali currently includes Jou. Brahma's account itself records change and variation.
5. Do not treat Jou/Jou bishi/Joubidwi/Joufinai/Jou Gishi/Jou Gwran as interchangeable names. These sources generally use jau/jou/rice beer and do not resolve the specific product identity or preparation type.
