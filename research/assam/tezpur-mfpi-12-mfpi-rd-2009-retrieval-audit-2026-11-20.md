# Tezpur University MoFPI rice-beer project retrieval audit
**Review date:** 20 November 2026  
**Project:** Quality Improvement of Traditional Method of Rice Beer Production by the Tribal People of Northeast India  
**Project reference:** 12/MFPI/R&D/2009  
**Principal Investigator:** Prof. S. C. Deka  
**Current gap:** ASM-GAP-063

## Executive finding
The project is verified through Tezpur University and Government of India records, and its reported technical outputs can now be stated more precisely. The original scientific final report and its detailed annexure remain unrecovered. No field-site or village-level beverage record is justified by the materials inspected in this audit.

## 1. Institutional project record — SRC-246
Tezpur University's official Project Completion Reports archive lists the project under September 2020, with reference 12/MFPI/R&D/2009 dated 26 February 2010 and Prof. S. C. Deka as Principal Investigator. The accessible catalogue page exposes the project title, reference and PI but does not expose a downloadable file link in the retrieved text. Catalogue listing is not equivalent to retrieval of the report.

## 2. MoFPI completed-project summary — SRC-247
The official MoFPI completed-project PDF, Project Summary 5, reports:
- Implementing organization: Department of Food Engineering & Technology, Tezpur University, Assam.
- Project duration: two years.
- Total funding received: Rs. 77.548 lakh.
- Outputs: new kinds of starter cakes; a new rice-beer preparation methodology; improvement/standardization of production technology in comparison with traditional technology for each tribe.
- Laboratory preparation: standardized quality rice beer achieved an overall sensory acceptability score of 7.43 on a nine-point hedonic scale, reported as higher than other samples.
- Commercialization: considered possible after scale-up/process validation; the summary separately says pilot-scale studies and toxicity studies remained necessary.
- Publications: one research publication, one review article and three paper presentations.
- Patent: one in process.

The summary is an administrative outcome abstract. It does not state the sampled communities, villages, informant/household counts, sampling dates, sample-to-community/locality crosswalk, starter plant taxa, voucher numbers or laboratory sample identifiers. The phrase “for each tribe” is not a list of tribes and cannot be expanded into a community roster without the underlying report.

## 3. Independent grant-release record — SRC-302
A separate official MoFPI grant-release table for 2009–10 lists the same project at Tezpur University, Napaam, Assam. It reports:
- Approved grant-in-aid: Rs. 78.685 lakh.
- First instalment released: Rs. 39.342 lakh.

This differs from SRC-247's reported total funding received of Rs. 77.548 lakh. Both values are retained exactly as reported. The available records do not explain whether the difference reflects sanctioned versus received amounts, accounting revisions, or another basis; no reconciliation is asserted.

## 4. Project objectives in the later government inventory — SRC-142
The TERI/MDoNER 2022 study inventory identifies the project as completed in 2012 and describes four objectives: standardization of traditional rice-beer preparation methods; biochemical characterization of plants used as starter materials; microbiological and biochemical characterization of rice beer collected from different tribal belts; and evaluation of tapioca roots as a substitute substrate. This is a secondary government inventory and does not replace the original report or its field annexures.

## 5. What remains unknown
The sources inspected do not resolve:
- Which communities/tribes were sampled, and whether every community named in associated publications was included in this specific project.
- Which states, districts, villages or households supplied samples.
- Whether starter-plant lists are community-specific, site-specific or aggregated.
- Botanical voucher identifiers, herbarium repositories and specimen-to-taxon links.
- Sample identifiers linking household beverages, starter cakes and laboratory batches.
- Which results came from traditional household preparations versus standardized laboratory preparations.
- The detailed methods, analytical tables and appendices in the original scientific report.

## 6. Retrieval plan
1. Request the signed final report and all enclosures from Tezpur University's Department of Food Engineering & Technology / project records office, explicitly asking for **Enclosure II (the detailed scientific report)**, field schedule, sample register, starter-plant tables and voucher details.
2. Ask MoFPI for the submitted final report and annexures under project reference 12/MFPI/R&D/2009.
3. Cross-reference the one research publication, one review article and three presentations listed in SRC-247, but do not assume every output derives from the same sample cohort or carries identical locality metadata.
4. Keep all village-level place records unchanged until a primary source provides direct sample-to-site evidence.

## 7. Research decision
ASM-GAP-063 remains **High / Open**. SRC-246, SRC-247, SRC-302 and SRC-142 improve project provenance and clarify the reported technical outputs, but no new beverage-place row is added. Laboratory standardization, sensory ranking and commercialisation potential must remain distinct from traditional household practice and community-specific locality evidence.
