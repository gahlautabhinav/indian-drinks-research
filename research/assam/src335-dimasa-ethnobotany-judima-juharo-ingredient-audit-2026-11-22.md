# SRC-335 — Dimasa ethnobotany paper: Judima/Juharo ingredient and locality-boundary audit

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026  
**Source:** Dipika Parbo and Awadhesh Kumar (2021), “Ethnobotany: The Ethnicity of the Dimasa Kachari, Dima Hasao, Assam, India,” *Journal of Plant Science and Research* 8(2), citation page 207.  
**Source URL:** https://www.opensciencepublications.com/fulltextarticles/JPSR-2349-2805-8-206.pdf

## 1. Why this source was added

This paper is a new, independently retrieved Dimasa ethnobotany source. It contains an explicit statement that *Albizia myriophylla* (local name printed as “themra”) is used in preparation of local liquors, specifically rice wine Judima and distilled liquor Juharo. This is relevant to the archive's Humao ingredient comparison, because several Judima accounts instead identify *Acacia pennata* as Thembra/Thempra. The names and taxa must not be treated as interchangeable.

## 2. What the paper actually documents

- The study concerns Dimasa Kachari ethnobotanical knowledge in Dima Hasao district.
- Fieldwork is described as running from August 2015 to May 2017, across 18 villages situated within Maibang, Langting, Diyungbra and Umrangso.
- The methodology says each of the four study sites was visited four times a year and that interviews were conducted in local languages.
- The methods report 84 interviewees in the Results section; the Abstract instead says 62 respondents. This internal sample-size discrepancy is unresolved and must be retained.
- The paper says plant specimens were prepared as herbaria and submitted to the Department of Forestry, North Eastern Regional Institute of Science and Technology (NERIST), Arunachal Pradesh. It also says identifications were made with expert help from prepared herbaria or field photographs.
- The discussion explicitly states that *Albizia myriophylla*, local name “themra,” is used for local liquors, rice wine (Judima) and distilled liquor (Juharo), and notes that the plant is rare and sold by interior villagers.
- The process section gives a short Judima account: bark is dried and powdered, mixed with rice powder to make biscuits, sun-dried, and a portion of biscuit is mixed with cooled cooked rice before closed-container fermentation. The account also mentions onion and chilli being kept on top of the container cover.

## 3. Evidence boundaries

### Locality

The paper's 18-village field frame is an ethnobotanical survey frame, not a Judima producer census. The statement about *Albizia myriophylla* is not allocated to a named village, respondent, household or product batch. Do not add any of the 18 ethnobotanical villages as Judima/Juharo production localities on this evidence alone.

The study-site towns (Maibang, Langting, Diyungbra and Umrangso) are not equivalent to beverage-production locations. Table 1 is cited for village names and respondent distribution, but the beverage-use passage does not identify which village(s) supplied the “themra” or Judima/Juharo information.

### Botanical identity and terminology

- Preserve the paper's name as printed: *Albizia myriophylla*; preserve its local spelling “themra.”
- Other Judima sources in this archive report *Acacia pennata* as Thembra/Thempra in Humao preparation. These are different botanical names and cannot be merged because the vernacular spellings resemble one another.
- The present paper does not demonstrate that *Albizia myriophylla* is the sole or universal Judima starter ingredient, nor does it resolve whether this is a local variant, a separate starter tradition, a plant-name confusion, or a taxonomic misidentification.
- The article says herbaria were submitted to NERIST and refers to voucher numbers in its data-analysis description. The accessible text reviewed here does not establish the exact voucher number, specimen label, collector, taxon-to-voucher mapping or current custody for the *Albizia myriophylla* record. Do not describe this particular taxon determination as voucher-verified until the table and herbarium record are checked.
- The paper's description does not use the starter name Humao in the quoted Judima process passage. Do not automatically assign this *Albizia* account to the Humao/Umhu starter without corroboration.

### Beverage distinction

The paper explicitly distinguishes Judima (rice wine) from Juharo (distilled liquor) in its discussion. Preserve the two as separate beverage records/process stages. Do not treat the mention of *Albizia myriophylla* as proof that the plant is used in distillation itself; the source says it is used in preparation of both local liquors, but its process description is for Judima and does not separately document the Juharo distillation method.

### Sample-size discrepancy

The abstract states 62 respondents, while the Results section states 84 interviewees. This difference is material to any use of survey scale and must remain unresolved pending author clarification or source correction. Do not silently choose one count.

## 4. Comparison with existing Judima evidence

| Source | Place / field frame | Starter ingredient reported | Evidentiary limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| SRC-001, Sarmah, Pegu & Sanong (2022) | Dhansiri; February 2022; one multigenerational brewing household, with 100 local interviewees | *Acacia pennata* bark, Thempra, in Humao | One household's documented practice; not a district-wide recipe |
| SRC-002, Jamal, Upadhyay & Bajaj (2022) | Dibarai; November 2019; about 50 snowball-sampled respondents | Diagram-level account identifies *Acacia pennata* / Thembra | Cultural-association survey, not a representative producer or recipe census |
| SRC-003, Daulagupu & Kikon (2023) | Tereh; December 2022; direct household ethnography | Mature Thembra bark in the household's Humao practice | One household case |
| SRC-335, Parbo & Kumar (2021) | 18-village Dimasa ethnobotany survey across four town areas; no product-specific village allocation | *Albizia myriophylla* (“themra”) stated for Judima and Juharo | Broad ethnobotany survey; no beverage-specific village, household, batch or taxon-voucher crosswalk established in reviewed text |

This is not enough to conclude that one of the two botanical names is wrong. The correct archive treatment is a **documented source-level ingredient divergence requiring taxon/voucher and community-terminology follow-up**.

## 5. Retrieval questions

1. Obtain the paper's complete Table 1 and Table 3 at image resolution, including the 18 village names, taxon rows, plant parts, use descriptions and voucher numbers.
2. Ask NERIST Department of Forestry whether the *Albizia myriophylla* voucher(s) from Dipika Parbo and Awadhesh Kumar's 2015–2017 Dima Hasao survey were deposited, and request accession number, collector number, label locality, determination history and image.
3. Ask the authors which village/respondent supplied the “themra” Judima/Juharo information, whether the same plant was used for both products, and whether it was bark or another part in each case.
4. Ask the authors to clarify the 62-versus-84 respondent discrepancy.
5. Seek Dimasa-language/community confirmation of “themra” versus “Thembra/Thempra,” keeping each plant name and household context separate until resolved.

No contact has been made.

## 6. Archive decision

- Register the paper as SRC-335.
- Record the *Albizia myriophylla* / “themra” claim as a source-specific ingredient report, not as a replacement for *Acacia pennata*.
- Do not add any of the 18 ethnobotanical villages to the Judima or Juharo production map.
- Do not change the canonical Judima recipe, Humao crosswalk, plant occurrence tables or existing locality records.
- Keep the Humao ingredient question open. This source adds a meaningful divergence, not a resolution.

## 7. Original article re-check and NERIST retrieval route — 22 November 2026

A second pass against the publisher's full HTML text clarifies the voucher situation:

- The methods explicitly state that herbaria of collected plant species were prepared and submitted to the Department of Forestry, NERIST, Arunachal Pradesh.
- The Data Analysis section says the study tabulated voucher numbers of vouchered specimens alongside ethnobotanical use, interviewee information and study areas.
- The HTML text extraction does not expose the contents of Table 1, Table 2 or Table 3 as readable table rows. In particular, it does not expose the *Albizia myriophylla* voucher number or its specimen-level locality. This is a table-image/text extraction gap, not evidence that the paper omitted all voucher numbers.
- The paper's abstract says 62 respondents; its Results section says 84 interviewees. Both statements remain recorded pending clarification; do not assume one is a typographical error or infer the reason.

### Revised archive status

The paper does report herbarium submission and says voucher numbers were tabulated. The unresolved question is narrower and more precise: which voucher number corresponds to the *Albizia myriophylla* “themra” record, and can that number be linked to an extant NERIST sheet with a readable locality label? Until verified, retain the botanical claim as author-reported identification with a published voucher trail not yet reconciled to an accessible accession record.
