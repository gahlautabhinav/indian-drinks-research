# SRC-115 — Mising ÉPOP starter-culture field study, Sivasagar

## Citation
Mili, R., & Sundriyal, R. C. (2023). “ÉPOP (traditional starter culture): a complex composition of plant resources prepared by the Misings of Assam, Northeast India.” *Journal of Ethnic Foods*, 10, article 47. Published 6 December 2023. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2

## Study design and geography
This primary ethnobotanical field study focused on Mising ÉPOP starter-culture preparation in Sivasagar district. The researchers surveyed 15 randomly selected villages and then selected Majorbari and Ligiribari as two representative villages for in-depth work. Knowledgeable women were interviewed and observed during ingredient collection and starter-cake preparation. The paper says plant specimens were identified with assistance from taxonomists at the Botanical Survey of India, Arunachal Station, and by comparison with regional herbaria/floras. The paper's methods do not provide a public sample-by-sample mapping of every taxon to one village; do not attribute every listed species to both villages separately.

## Main findings
- The authors recorded 31 plant species across 22 families used for ÉPOP starter-culture preparation (27 angiosperms and four pteridophytes).
- Collection sources were reported as forest (35.53%), fallow land (23.68%), agricultural fields (21.05%) and homestead gardens (19.74%).
- Leaves were the most frequently used plant part (69.23%); young shoots 20.51%; whole plants 10.26%.
- The reported process includes collecting plant parts before flowering, washing and sun-drying them for 3–4 days, pounding them, soaking glutinous rice for 2–3 hours and crushing it with a traditional rice picker called KI-PAR. The article reports a plant-ingredient-to-soaked-rice ratio of 1:5, shaping cakes generally around 20–25 g, applying ground prior starter to inoculate them, and drying the cakes in sun and over fire for at least seven days. The article reports storage up to six months in a cool, dry place, preferably an earthen container (KI-LING). These are source-specific observations, not universal production instructions or food-safety validation.
- The authors discuss Poro Apong and Nogin/Noggin Apong. In their framing, Poro includes ash from rice husks or bran mixed with cooked rice; Nogin uses rice without that ash addition. The article's introduction characterizes Poro as distilled, while the later discussion describes the difference mainly by ingredients. Compare this with other primary sources that describe Ta:suk extraction/filtration. Do not assume the terms filtration and distillation are interchangeable or that the article documents every stage for each subtype.
- The article describes cultural restrictions, knowledge transmission and women’s role in plant collection and starter preparation. These are findings from its field context and should not be generalized to all Mising households or assumed to be unchanged today.

## Taxonomic and health-claim cautions
The plant inventory is valuable ethnobotanical evidence, but it is not a clinical study. Reported traditional medicinal uses are not proof of efficacy, dosage, safety or bioavailability. Preserve each taxon as the source prints it until a controlled taxonomic crosswalk is completed; the paper's scientific-name spellings and accepted names may differ. Avoid treating the number of species in this study as a trend against studies with different geographic or sampling designs.

## Atlas updates and cross-study links
- Existing place records ASM-PL-004 (Majorbari) and ASM-PL-005 (Ligiribari) already identify these as in-depth sites; update their evidence/source fields to include SRC-115 rather than duplicating place records.
- Existing ASM-GAP-045 already tracks comparison of Sivasagar and Majuli ÉPOP inventories. SRC-115 strengthens its Sivasagar side; retain the gap until the full Majuli list is harmonized taxon by taxon.
- Compare with SRC-084 (Borah 2026, Majuli study; 48 species across three villages not named in the reviewed text), SRC-114 (Borah et al. 2024 HRLC-MS analysis of one E’pob sample from Lakhimpur district), SRC-008 (Gogoi et al. 2013) and SRC-089 (Pegu et al. 2013).
- No new beverage record is needed: this source deepens evidence for existing Mising ÉPOP/Apong records. No new village record is needed because Majorbari and Ligiribari are already registered.
