# Barpeta district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**District:** Barpeta, Assam  
**Outcome:** No new beverage-specific place record accepted; district remains an evidence gap.

## Search scope

Targeted searches covered:
- Barpeta + Haria/Hariya/Haari, rice beer, Apong, Zu and traditional alcoholic beverage;
- Barpeta + Adivasi/Tea Tribe, Bodo and Rabha beverage traditions;
- Barpeta district academic and government sources;
- current official Tea & Ex-Tea Tribes Welfare Directorate tea-garden directory.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. District-specific ethnobotany — excluded from beverage register

Bhattacharjya, Akhtar, Deka and Bharadwaj (2023), “An ethnobotanical survey on phytomedicines based on traditional knowledge in the Barpeta district, Assam, India,” *Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine* 14(4), 100763. DOI: 10.1016/j.jaim.2023.100763. Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10371788/

The paper reports fieldwork in Barpeta during 2021–2022 with 30 traditional healers. Its stated scope is plant use for human ailments. It is useful as district-specific ethnobotanical context, but it does not establish beverage preparation, a beverage name, producer, or beverage locality. It is therefore not used to create a beverage place row or ingredient association.

### 2. Adivasi Haria tradition — existing statewide evidence, not Barpeta evidence

Tanti et al. (2010), already registered as SRC-096, documents Adivasi starter culture Dabai and liquor Haria in Assam in a multi-community/multi-state study. The accessible table identifies community and state, not a Barpeta village or district-specific sample. It cannot be used to infer Barpeta production.

The 2013 review “Ethno medicinal value of various plants used in the preparation of traditional rice beer by different tribes of Assam, India” also describes Haria among Assam Adivasi communities, but the field locations acknowledged in the accessible text are elsewhere (including Kokrajhar, Tinsukia, Goalpara, Golaghat, Dibrugarh, Dhemaji and Karbi Anglong). No Barpeta field site is named. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dit.2013.09.002

### 3. Official tea-garden directory — contextual negative evidence only

The Assam Directorate of Tea Tribes and Adivasi Welfare's current “List of Tea Garden at Assam” page displays Barpeta with “Nil” under tea gardens. https://ttwd.assam.gov.in/frontimpotentdata/list-of-tea-garden-at-assam

This means the official directory does not list a tea estate in Barpeta. It does **not** prove that Adivasi households or Haria traditions are absent from the district; community presence and beverage practices may extend beyond tea-estate settings. No beverage inference is made from this listing.

## Decision

- Keep Barpeta at zero beverage-specific place rows.
- Do not create a generic “Barpeta — Adivasi Haria” row from statewide evidence.
- Do not import medicinal plants from the 2023 phytomedicine survey into starter-culture ingredients.
- Retain Barpeta as an open geographic verification task, not as a district with no beverage tradition.

## Next research route

Seek Barpeta-specific community/household evidence, including local Adivasi settlements and Bodo/Rabha communities, and ask for village-level attribution when a source is statewide. Check historical administrative boundaries separately: Bajali was formerly part of Barpeta, so a source-era Barpeta label must be cross-walked against the village rather than copied into the current district.
