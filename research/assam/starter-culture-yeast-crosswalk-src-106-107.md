# Assam starter-culture yeast literature crosswalk (SRC-106–SRC-107)

**Research date:** 2026-10-23  
**Purpose:** Capture two microbial-literature leads surfaced by the Assam rice-beverage systematic-review/reference audit. This is a source crosswalk, not a new beverage census.

## 1. SRC-106 — Nath, Parasar & Sarma (2021)

**Citation:** Nath, B. J., Parasar, D. P., & Sarma, H. K. (2021). “Linking the Diversity of Yeasts Inherent in Starter Cultures to Quorum Sensing Mechanism in Ethnic Fermented Alcoholic Beverages of Northeast India.” *Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems*, 5, 678045. https://doi.org/10.3389/fsufs.2021.678045

**Source type and scope:** Open-access review of yeast diversity and yeast–yeast interactions in ethnic starter cultures and fermented alcoholic beverages across Northeast India. The authors explicitly summarize earlier Assam work on Bodo, Karbi, Rabha, Mising, Ahom and Kachari starter materials.

### Assam-relevant statements retained as review-level leads

- Bodo starter material **emao/amao** is summarized as containing *Pichia burtonii*, *Pichia anomala* (now often classified as *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*), and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*.
- Karbi starter material **thap** is summarized as dominated by *S. cerevisiae*.
- Ahom **xajar pitha** is summarized as containing *Candida tropicalis* and *Candida glabrata*; the review notes that *Saccharomyces* isolates were not retrieved in the cited work.
- Mising **apong kusure/apop pitha** is summarized as showing *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* dominance, with *W. anomalus* also noted in the table.
- Rabha **bakhor** is discussed with reference to *Meyerozyma caribbica* and earlier studies.
- Kachari **modor pitha** is summarized as containing *S. cerevisiae*, *W. anomalus*, and *C. glabrata*.

These are statements reported by a secondary review and point back to cited primary studies (including Parasar et al. 2017 and Tanti et al. 2010); they should not be silently promoted to independent primary evidence or assumed to describe every household, locality, or current starter batch. Taxonomic names and spelling variants require reconciliation against the original papers before being normalized in the project database.

### What this source does not establish

- It does not provide a complete, current village-level distribution of each Assam starter or finished beverage.
- Starter-culture identity is not interchangeable with finished-beverage identity; a shared starter or microbial species does not prove that two drink names are synonyms.
- Yeast presence or proposed quorum-sensing effects do not establish clinical health benefits, safety, or a standard alcohol concentration.
- The authors explicitly describe important research gaps in yeast interactions and volatile profiles; speculative mechanisms in the review are not the same as demonstrated effects in every traditional fermentation.

## 2. SRC-107 — Nath et al. (2020)

**Citation:** Nath, B. J., Verma, E., Sarma, H. K., Mishra, A. K., Tanti, B., & Jha, D. K. (2020). “Evaluation of Basic Fermentation Parameters and Effective Combinations of Predominant Yeasts from Traditional Starter Materials of Indigenous Communities from Northeast India.” Publisher record: https://dl.bhu.ac.in/bhuir/entities/article/13f44b10-79a7-4300-b833-c9d6ad617ff2

**Accessible evidence:** The university repository record’s abstract says that yeasts from starter materials associated with four Northeast Indian indigenous communities were characterized and cultured individually and in combinations in synthetic minimal media containing glucose or starch. It names *S. cerevisiae*, *W. anomalus*, *C. glabrata*, and *C. tropicalis*, and reports different relative growth patterns in glucose versus starch.

**Relevance and limits:** This is an experimental microbiology study that may help explain starter-community function, but the accessible abstract does not identify the four communities, specific Assam beverage-to-starter pairings, field localities, or a direct mapping from the laboratory combinations to finished-drink sensory properties. Do not attribute all four yeasts or the experiment to Assam alone without the full article and exact sample metadata. Results in synthetic media are not a recipe, proof of household fermentation performance, alcohol specification, or safety assessment.

## 3. Cross-source implications for the Atlas

1. Treat **starter culture** as a separate entity/field from the finished drink; retain community-specific names and source-specific spellings.
2. Link claims to the underlying primary study wherever it can be retrieved. SRC-106 is a review and SRC-107’s repository abstract is not enough to resolve every community/sample.
3. Do not create new canonical beverage records or village records from these microbial findings alone. They strengthen the research trail for existing Bodo, Karbi, Ahom, Mising, Rabha and Kachari dossiers but do not add verified production localities.
4. Next verification target: retrieve the full text and sample table for Nath et al. (2020), then crosswalk the four source communities and starter materials to primary studies already indexed in the source register. Separately compare SRC-106’s cited primary studies against the archive before adding any further source records.
