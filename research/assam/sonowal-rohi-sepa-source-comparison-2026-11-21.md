# Sonowal Kachari Rohi–Sepa: SRC-044 / SRC-045 comparative source audit

**Audit date:** 21 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Reconcile the plant inventories, field frames, beverage process and analytical evidence in two Sonowal Kachari studies without treating their differences as a single universal recipe.

## 1. Sources and study frames

### SRC-044 — Ripunjoy Sonowal (2023)
“Traditional Rice Beer Fermentation Technology of the Sonowal Kachari Tribe of Assam, India,” *Antrocom Online Journal of Anthropology* 19(1):605–613.  
Full text: https://antrocom.net/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/sonowal-rice-beer-fermentation-sonowal-kachari-assam.pdf

The paper reports fieldwork in three Dibrugarh district villages: Boiragimath Kachari gaon, Niz-Manncota and Dainijan. Selected informants who regularly prepare Rohi were interviewed and the process was observed. It does not report the number of informants, household/batch count, village-to-plant allocation or voucher accession numbers. Botanical identification is described as consultation of standard local-flora literature (Dutta 1985).

### SRC-045 — Bhattacharya & Chandra Deka (2026)
“Preparation, physicochemical analysis and antioxidant properties of Rohi and Sepa: traditional rice beers of the Sonowal Kacharis, Assam,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 25(9):914–921. DOI: 10.56042/ijtk.v25i9.23169.  
Official full text: https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/68641/1/IJTK%20Vol.25%289%29%20914-921.pdf

The paper reports visits during June 2020–March 2021 to four Dibrugarh villages: Chaharikota, Teporguri, Bhoralibari and Meslou. It collected information from aged and experienced villagers, but does not report informant counts, household/batch counts or village-to-sample allocation. It acknowledges Rajen Mili of Dhemaji and Khelanta Sonowal of Chaharikota, but this does not establish the origin of each analytical sample. The dataset is available from the corresponding author on reasonable request and is not publicly accessible due to privacy/ethics.

These are distinct field frames; do not merge the seven village names into one survey or infer that every village supplied every sample.

## 3. Starter and beverage terminology

SRC-044 calls the starter **Mod pitha**, also **Saul pitha**. It calls the beverage **Rohi**, and additionally lists **Jhul mod** and **Madhopurukhor rokh** without proving exact equivalence or whether these are local names, forms or serving distinctions.

SRC-045 lists Assamese starter names collectively as **mod pitha**, **saoul pitha**, **vekur pitha** and **Bakhar**. This collective wording does not establish that all four are exact synonyms across Assam's different communities. Do not use it to collapse Ahom Vekur pitha, Deori Mod pitha and Sonowal Mod pitha into one community-independent canonical starter.

## 4. Process comparison: distinct documented methods

### SRC-044 (2023)
- Starter dough is made from soaked local rice, plant parts and 2–3 old starter cakes.
- Cakes are dried on paddy straw/banana leaves and covered with straw and Bihlongoni fern.
- Drying takes about two weeks above the kitchen fireplace.
- Rohi uses steamed Bora/glutinous rice; the article gives 1 kg rice to 1 starter cake.
- Fermentation occurs in an earthen Koloh, at least five days at room temperature and about one week in winter.
- Filtration through Sakoni yields Rohi.

### SRC-045 (2026)
- Starter plant parts are ground with pre-soaked rice, rolled into spheres, covered with *Cyclosorus dentatus* leaves and dried in a dark room for about two weeks.
- Cakes are then wrapped in *Casearia aculeata* leaves, put in a polythene bag and hung from the roof.
- Rice is half-cooked (Aaroi), cooled, mixed with powdered starter, placed in an aluminium Handi with water and sealed with banana leaves.
- Fermentation is reported as 4–5 days in summer and 8–10 days in winter.
- A bamboo Khorahi lined with perforated banana leaves separates the first yellowish filtrate, Rohi.
- Water is added to the residual fermented mash and it is pressed through cotton cloth to produce whitish Sepa.

The papers may describe different household protocols. The 2026 method's aluminium Handi and sequential Rohi/Sepa extraction should not overwrite the 2023 Koloh method.

## 5. SRC-045 analytical results and limits

For home-brewed sticky-rice samples, SRC-045 reports:
- Rohi ethanol: 6.14 ± 0.28% v/v
- Sepa ethanol: 5.36 ± 0.22% v/v
- Rohi pH: 3.57 ± 0.05
- Sepa pH: 2.92 ± 0.12

For lab-brewed samples it reports Rohi ethanol 7.04 ± 0.28% v/v and Sepa 6.72 ± 0.22% v/v. These are results for the study's analysed samples, not universal values for every Sonowal household or batch.

The paper reports triplicate determinations and antioxidant/phenolic assays. Such in-vitro assays do not establish clinical health benefits or make alcohol consumption medically beneficial. The paper does not report microbial community sequencing, pathogen screening, mycotoxin testing or a full food-safety assessment. It notes visible microbial growth during extended storage; its reported storage durations are not safe-storage guidance.

## 6. Archive changes and safeguards

- Retain ASM-019-ROH as the canonical Sonowal Kachari Rohi record.
- Keep SRC-044 and SRC-045 as separate source records with separate village frames and process descriptions.
- Record 2023's ten non-rice taxa plus rice and 2026's four non-rice taxa plus rice separately.
- Do not infer a 14-plant recipe, village-level plant allocation or temporal loss of plant knowledge.
- Add ASM-GAP-138 for source-lineage and voucher reconciliation.
- No new beverage or village rows are justified by this comparison.

## 7. Priority follow-up

1. Obtain SRC-045's underlying dataset and ask the authors which plants were personally collected/observed in the four villages versus compiled from references 18–19.
2. Recover voucher accession numbers, collection labels and plant photographs for both studies.
3. Compare the exact four SRC-045 plant taxa against references 18 and 19 to establish whether the table is derivative.
4. Request informant counts and village-to-batch mapping from both author teams.
5. Verify “Ptridium aquilinum” in SRC-044 against the original plant material.
6. Ask Sonowal Kachari knowledge holders whether Rohi, Jhul mod and Madhopurukhor rokh are synonyms or distinct forms.

**Conclusion:** The two papers establish separate Dibrugarh field frames and distinct reported starter lists. The 2026 article adds analytical measurements and a sequential Rohi/Sepa extraction description, but its plant table's cited literature lineage is unresolved. The archive should preserve both datasets independently until original provenance and voucher evidence are recovered.
