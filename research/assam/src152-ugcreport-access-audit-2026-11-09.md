# SRC-152 UGC project report — accessible-copy audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-09  
**Project:** Devid Kardong, “Isolation and Biochemical Characterization of Potential Amylolytic and Alcohol Producing Yeast Strains from Yeast Cake (Epope) Used in the Preparation of Po:ro (Sai-mod), a Traditional Alcoholic Rice Beverage of Mising Tribe of Assam, India.” UGC Minor Research Project F. No. 32-617/2006(SR).  
**Official institutional report:** https://www.dibru.ac.in/pdf/Devid-Kardong-Report.pdf  
**Additional copy examined:** https://www.scribd.com/document/1042837999/Devid-Kardong-Report

## 1. Document identity and chronology

The five-page Scribd-hosted reproduction identifies Devid Kardong, Department of Life Sciences, Dibrugarh University, as principal investigator. It states the reporting period as 1 April 2007 to 31 March 2009 and gives the project title above. The administrative form says the project was completed, two bound copies of the final report were enclosed, and publication work was being prepared. It also lists presentations in 2007 and 2008.

This provides a useful chronology for the later Epop-related publication trail, but does not establish that SRC-139 or SRC-140 is a direct publication of any particular experiment in this project. Those articles remain separate sources pending their full texts.

## 2. Summary-level findings visible in the copy

The Annexure I summary says:
- Three Epop starter-culture samples were collected from “different places”; the places are not named in the accessible summary.
- Seven pure isolates with amylolytic properties were recovered, described collectively as bacteria and fungus/yeast.
- Six different isolates with alcohol-fermenting properties were recovered.
- Partial characterization was reported for amylolytic isolates AY-3 (also printed “amy-3”), AY-1(19) and AY-4 (“Big cell”), and for alcohol-fermenting isolate FY-10.
- The report proposes AY-3, AY-4 and FY-10 as a suitable consortium for solid-state fermentation of Saimod/Po:ro.

These are summary-level statements, not independently reproducible results. The accessible copy contains no species-level taxonomy, named sample locations, isolate accession numbers, raw measurements, full culture conditions, detailed enzyme assays, or sample-by-sample results.

## 3. What this copy does not resolve

The Scribd page identifies itself as a five-page document. It does not expose the technical annexures referenced in the administrative form. Consequently it cannot support a full microbiological reconstruction or an enzyme-activity comparison. The official Dibrugarh University-hosted report remains the preferred institutional record, but the accessible institutional copy previously examined is itself only a short project summary.

The summary's “seven pure isolates” and “six different isolates” are counts for two functional screens; do not assume the groups are disjoint unless the technical report confirms it. Likewise, the three proposed consortium codes are functional isolate labels, not organism names.

## 4. Archive decision

- Retain this material under existing SRC-152; do not create a duplicate source ID.
- Treat the Scribd copy as a secondary-hosted reproduction of an administrative summary, not as a university-hosted full technical report.
- Keep the three sample places unspecified.
- Keep AY-3, AY-1(19), AY-4 and FY-10 as isolate codes; do not infer species names.
- Keep SRC-139 and SRC-140 as separate 2011 publication records with unresolved full texts.
- No new fermentation time-series, enzyme values, taxonomic identifications or production localities are added.
