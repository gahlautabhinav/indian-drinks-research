# SRC-084 field-method metadata audit

Borah (2026) reports a field survey in three randomly selected villages in Majuli from April to October 2024 and data from 28 informants aged 40–70, with emphasis on older women involved in Apong preparation. The accessible paper text does not name the three villages. Therefore the 48-plant inventory is attributable to a three-village study in Majuli district, not to named villages, and no village-level production records should be created from it.

The paper reports both Po:ro Apong and Nogin Apong, and says the study documents plants used in E'pob starter cake preparation. Its abstract identifies Oryza sativa (RFC 1.0, UV 1.6) and Dryopteris filix-mas (RFC 1.0, UV 1.5) as high-index entries. The source table includes apparent spellings 'Guilandian bonduc' and 'Termalia arjuna'; Kew accepts Guilandina bonduc and Terminalia arjuna, respectively. The reported family fields Fabaceae and Combretaceae align with those likely intended names, strengthening a spelling-error interpretation, but not proving specimen identity.

Source: Borah, P. (2026), International Journal of Advanced Research 14(4), 1361–1368. https://doi.org/10.21474/IJAR01/23298. Kew references: https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:114706-2 and https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:170962-1.

Decision: retain source strings; keep normalized-name candidates in the audit layer only. Do not infer village names. Request article author clarification and voucher/accession details. ASM-GAP-045 remains open.
