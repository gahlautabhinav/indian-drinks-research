# SRC-098 — Chakrabarty, Sharma & Tamang (2014): access and evidence-level correction

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Source ID:** SRC-098  
**Citation:** Jayasree Chakrabarty, G. D. Sharma & Jyoti Prakash Tamang, “Traditional technology and product characterization of some lesser-known ethnic fermented foods and beverages of North Cachar Hills District of Assam,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 13(4) (October 2014), 706–715.

## 1. Retrieval status

The article is confirmed in the official NIScPR Online Periodical Repository's October 2014, volume 13(4) collection, which lists the authors and page range 706–715. The repository item has handle `123456789/29511`, but opening the item endpoint returned a 502 gateway error in this audit.

The PDF currently linked in the source register is hosted on a JNU faculty page. Inspection shows that it is an issue-level compilation of article abstracts, not the full 10-page paper. The target article appears at printed pages 706–715 and the compilation reproduces its abstract at lines 589–604. It does not expose the full article body, data tables, methods or references.

Therefore SRC-098 must be described as **abstract-level evidence** until the complete paper is recovered. The previous source-register description overstated the level of access and has been corrected.

## 2. What the abstract actually establishes

The authors state that the study selected five distinct product groups from North Cachar Hills:

1. Wet fermented bamboo-shoot product Tuaithur
2. Dry fermented bamboo-shoot product Tuairoi
3. Fermented pork/boar meat product Honoehingrain
4. Dry mixed amylolytic starter Humao (explicitly described as a non-food starter)
5. Fermented alcoholic beverage Judima

The abstract says traditional knowledge was documented through observation and interviews with producers and consumers, including production, consumption, socio-economic and ethnic value.

It reports **21 samples in total across Tuaithur, Tuairoi, Honoehingrain, Humao and Judima**. The total must not be misreported as 21 Judima samples. The abstract does not give the number allocated to Judima, whether samples represent independent households or batches, or whether replicate measurements were pooled.

The abstract reports microbial populations in the range (10^4) to (10^9) CFU/g across the sampled product groups. It lists bacteria (including Lactobacillus, Enterococcus, Leuconostoc, Pediococcus, Lactococcus, Bacillus, Staphylococcus and Micrococcus), yeasts (Debaryomyces hansenii and Saccharomyces cerevisiae) and moulds (Mucor and Rhizopus). This is a combined list for the five product groups. Without the tables, do not assign every listed organism to Judima or Humao individually.

The abstract further states that Judima showed higher mineral contents, but gives no mineral names, numerical values, sample basis or analytical method in the accessible text. It does not support a quantitative nutritional comparison or health claim.

## 3. Research and archive decisions

- Corrected the SRC-098 source-register access description to say that only the abstract compilation is currently accessible.
- Added ASM-GAP-126 to track recovery of the full article, sample allocation, field locality, analytical methods and numerical tables.
- Do not add a new beverage row: Judima is already represented by ASM-001-JUD.
- Do not create new place rows: the abstract gives no named villages or sample-to-locality mapping.
- Keep Humao as a starter culture, not a beverage.
- Do not attribute the combined microbial list or all 21 samples to Judima.
- Do not add numeric mineral claims or safety/health conclusions from the abstract alone.

## 4. Retrieval actions

1. Retrieve the NIScPR item bitstream for handle 123456789/29511 when the repository endpoint is available.
2. Request pages 706–715 from the journal/publisher or corresponding author.
3. Obtain the sample allocation table and identify the number of Judima and Humao samples.
4. Recover interview sites, collection dates, household/batch identifiers and consent/provenance notes.
5. Extract the full microbial and nutritional tables and laboratory methods before any product-specific dataset updates.

**Conclusion:** The abstract confirms that Judima and Humao were studied in a five-product investigation, but it is not sufficient to reconstruct Judima-specific sample counts, microbiology, nutritional composition or field geography.
