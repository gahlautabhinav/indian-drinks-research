# Mising Apong starter microbiology: sample-provenance and isolate crosswalk

**Research date:** 2026-11-12  
**Scope:** SRC-148, SRC-162, SRC-164, SRC-167 and linked follow-up isolate publications. This audit separates starter-cake evidence from finished-beverage evidence and distinguishes direct source linkage from inference.

## Executive result

The literature does **not** currently support a unified, strain-resolved microbial profile for Po:ro Apong. The studies have different sample frames and biological materials:

- SRC-148 reports a pooled Apong/Epop starter-culture microbiology result while discussing both Nogin and Po:ro preparation. It does not publish a starter sample-to-beverage subtype allocation. Its detected yeasts and moulds therefore cannot be labelled Po:ro-specific or Nogin-specific.
- SRC-164 is a Mising-specific starter-cake yeast study from Dhemaji district, but its beverage subtype is not named as Po:ro or Nogin. It reports six initial Mising isolate codes and selects two for molecular identification. One of the reported selected codes is inconsistent with the six-code list.
- Nath et al. (2019; follow-up paper, not a new source collection) explicitly states its isolate table is reproduced from Parasar et al. (2017). It independently provides the accession for MIB4X: **KM603602**, identified as *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*. It does not represent a second field sample.
- Parasar et al. (2020) characterizes *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* isolate MIC5X and reports DDBJ accession **LC011412**. It is follow-on isolate characterization, not an independent household survey.
- SRC-162 is a separate 2019 Assam Agricultural University collection of starter cultures labelled xaj, apong and suzen. Its sample-level community, district and subtype allocation is not published in the accessible paper. It cannot be treated as a Mising-only dataset.
- SRC-167 is explicitly about Po:ro Apong/Saimod and uses a household-made starter (HMS) to derive a three-genus laboratory consortium. The article does not give the source isolates' laboratory codes, accessions or taxonomic resolution beyond genus. No linkage to MIB4X, MIC5X or SRC-148 organisms is established.

**Bottom line:** only SRC-167 is explicitly Po:ro-specific at the experimental beverage level, but its microbial consortium is genus-level and cannot be strain-matched to the other papers. The Mising yeast isolates from SRC-164 are starter-cake evidence with no published Po:ro/Nogin subtype attribution.

## 2. SRC-164: isolate-code and accession reconciliation

The 2017 Parasar et al. paper says six Mising isolates were initially present in the collection: **MIE1X, MIB4X, MID3X, MID3.1X, MID4Y and MID4.2X**. In the same selection passage, it says MIB4X and MIC5X were selected for molecular identification. **MIC5X does not appear in the preceding six-code list**, while MIB4X appears in both the selected pair and the initial list.

Two independent follow-up publications resolve the identities/accessions of the two names used in the selected pair:
- Nath et al. (2019), Table 1, explicitly labels its data as reproduced from Parasar et al. (2017), and lists MIB4X as Mishing *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*, accession **KM603602**.
- Parasar et al. (2020) identifies MIC5X as *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* and gives DDBJ accession **LC011412**.

This corroborates the identities/accessions, but it does **not** repair the original six-code discrepancy. Possible explanations (typographical error in the six-code list, code transcription error, or another unreported isolate) cannot be selected without author clarification or the original isolate register. Keep the discrepancy open.

The 2017 article also contains a narrative contradiction: its abstract/results say *W. anomalus* occurs across the communities except Ahom and identify MIB4X as *W. anomalus*, while one discussion sentence claims absence of a specific fermenting yeast in Mising starters. The isolate table reproduced in Nath et al. supports MIB4X = *W. anomalus*. Retain the original discussion contradiction as a source-quality issue, not a reason to erase the isolate record.

## 3. SRC-148: why its taxa cannot be assigned to Po:ro

SRC-148 describes both Nogin and Po:ro preparation, including different process stages and durations. The microbiology section, however, says organisms were isolated from “starter culture of Apong” and reports three *Saccharomyces* species plus one *Mucor* and one *Rhizopus*. It does not say which Epop cakes came from households making Nogin, which came from Po:ro, or whether the same starter was used for both forms.

Therefore:
- Retain the five reported taxa at the source's starter-culture level.
- Do not place them exclusively under Po:ro or Nogin.
- The paper's “no growth” report for Enterobacteriaceae and LAB is limited to its media and incubation conditions; it is not evidence that bacteria are absent from Apong generally.
- The 118 × 10^7 viable yeast/mould count is a source-specific count from original culture sample; it is not a subtype-resolved count or a modern molecular abundance estimate.

## 4. SRC-162: community and beverage subtype cannot be cross-mapped

Saikia et al. (2019) states that starter cakes called xaj, apong and suzen were collected from Ahom, Mising and Deori communities. Its abstract reports 12 starters from six districts; the methods say seven districts. The accessible methods do not give a sample register linking each of the 12 cakes to a community, district and starter name. Its results include district-level detections of *S. cerevisiae*, *S. fibuligera* and *Candida* spp., but these cannot be assigned to Mising Apong merely because the study includes Mising participants. The term “apong” is not resolved into Po:ro and Nogin.

This is a separate collection from SRC-164. The 2019 Nath et al. IAA paper must not be confused with this Saikia et al. 2019 study: Nath et al. explicitly reused isolates from Parasar et al. (2017), whereas Saikia et al. collected a separate mixed-community starter set.

## 5. SRC-167: Po:ro-specific, but no strain bridge

SRC-167 is the clearest subtype-specific experimental evidence: it explicitly evaluates Po:ro Apong/Saimod. Its household-made starter (HMS) is used as the source for a selected consortium of *Mucor* sp., *Saccharomycopsis* sp. and *Saccharomyces* sp. However, the article gives no codes or accession numbers for those isolates. Similarity between genus names in SRC-148 or other Assam starter papers is not enough to establish identity.

No cross-study isolate equivalence is asserted:
- SRC-148 *Mucor* / *Rhizopus* / *Saccharomyces* isolates are not linked to SRC-167 isolates.
- SRC-164 MIB4X / MIC5X yeasts are not linked to SRC-167's *Saccharomyces* or mould isolates.
- SRC-152's AY/FY codes are not identified as any of these organisms.

## 6. Follow-up requests that would actually close the gaps

1. **Parasar et al. / Gauhati University:** request the original Mising starter sample register, six-isolate inventory, the Table 1 coordinates at legible resolution, isolate code notebooks and the explanation for MIC5X appearing in the selected pair but not the initial list.
2. **SRC-148 authors / Kakojan College:** request the sample-wise Epop register, number of starter cakes and households, which samples were used for microbiology, and whether the isolates came from Po:ro, Nogin or both. Request the full species names and culture-deposit details for the five reported fungi/yeasts.
3. **Saikia et al. / Assam Agricultural University:** request the 12-sample register with sample code, community, district, starter name, village, and isolate code; ask for the 28-versus-32 yeast count and six-versus-seven district discrepancies to be clarified.
4. **Kardong et al. / Dibrugarh University:** request SRC-167 isolate codes, identification methods, raw culture records and any link to the UGC SRC-152 strains.
5. For every supplied isolate record, capture accession number, sequence database, sequence length, taxonomic method, source material, community, district/village, collection date, and whether the sample is a starter cake, fermenting mash or finished beverage.

## Archive decision

This pass improves sample and isolate provenance without inventing a unified microbial profile. No new source IDs, beverage localities, microbial taxa or canonical recipes are created. The central unresolved gap is the lack of subtype-resolved sample registers and the original SRC-167 isolate identifiers.
