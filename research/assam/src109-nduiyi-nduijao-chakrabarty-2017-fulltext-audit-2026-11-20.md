# SRC-109 full-text audit — Nduiyi starter and Nduijao beverage
**Review date:** 20 November 2026  
**Citation:** Jayasree Chakrabarty (2017), “Identification of Yeast Strain Isolated from Nduiyi of Dima Hasao District of Assam, India,” *International Journal of Current Microbiology and Applied Sciences* 6(3):587–594. DOI: 10.20546/ijcmas.2017.603.068.  
**Beverage ID:** ASM-026-NDJ  
**Related gap:** ASM-GAP-122

## Executive finding
The full paper gives materially better geographic and methodological detail than the previous abstract-level archive note. It distinguishes Nduiyi (the starter) from Nduijao (the finished sweet-sour alcoholic beverage), reports five localities associated with starter production by rural women, and gives sample-level collection points for both starter and finished beverage. It does not provide a complete Nduijao brewing recipe or a current production census.

## 1. Terminology and community
The author describes Nduiyi as a traditional mixed amylolytic starter used by Jeme Naga people in Dima Hasao to produce Nduijao. Keep the starter and finished beverage as separate data concepts. Do not equate these names with Zeme Naga Nduizou, Zao/Zou or Ndhuihi/Nduihi without independent linguistic/community evidence.

## 2. Starter ingredients and preparation
The paper describes:
- Sticky rice soaked for 9–10 hours at room temperature.
- Rice crushed with either bark of *Albizia myriophylla* or leaves of *Saccharum officinarum*.
- Addition of 2–3% previously prepared Nduiyi as mother culture.
- Water added to make a paste, then kneaded into flat rounded/oval cakes.
- Cakes held for 1–3 days at 25–30°C, sun-dried for 2–3 days, then stored in a dry place for more than one year.
- Reported cake dimensions: 1.1–10.1 cm diameter; mass 15–25 g.

These are the author's reported starter-making details, not a full recipe for brewing Nduijao. The paper provides no complete finished-beverage rice/starter/water ratio, fermentation vessel, fermentation duration or filtration/extraction sequence. No botanical voucher or specimen accession is supplied for the two starter plant materials in this article.

## 3. Geographic evidence — separate production list from sample table
### Localities named in results prose as Nduiyi production localities
The article says rural women prepare Nduiyi in:
1. Lodi
2. Asalu
3. Nutan Gunjung
4. Boro Haflong
5. Lisong Basti

The paper does not give sample counts for Asalu, Nutan Gunjung or Boro Haflong. This is a source-reported production-locality list, not a complete distribution map or proof of current prevalence.

The paper defines n as number of samples and reports means ± SD. The table therefore contains four Nduiyi starter samples and four Nduijao beverage samples, not four samples total across both products. Haflong Bazaar is a market/sample collection locality and must not be interpreted as a village-wide production estimate. The table spells the finished-beverage site “Laisong Basti”; results prose spells the production locality “Lisong Basti”. Treat these as unresolved variants until an authoritative crosswalk or community confirmation is obtained.

## 4. Microbial identification and beverage measurements
- Starter isolates DHN:Y1 and DHN:Y2 were identified as *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*.
- Starter isolates DHN:Y3 and DHN:Y4 were identified as *Candida glabrata*.
- The author reports average yeast population around 10^8 log CFU/g in Nduiyi samples; Table 1 provides locality-specific counts.
- For Nduijao, the paper reports pH 4.3 ± 0.1, titratable acidity 0.44 ± 0.01 (unit not stated in the prose) and alcohol 4–6%.
- The paper states moulds were not recovered from the finished beverage samples in this study.

Do not turn the reported alcohol range or pH into universal product specifications or food-safety guidance. The author’s health/tonic and safety statements are not clinical evidence.

## 5. Social and economic observations
The paper reports home production of Nduiyi and sale in local periodical markets by rural women, and describes starter offerings in religious festivals. It includes a photograph captioned as a woman selling Nduijao in Haflong bazaar. These are observations reported in this 2017 study, not evidence of current prevalence, universal gender roles or current market conditions.

3. Seek community-authorized current confirmation of Nduiyi production and the complete Nduijao preparation sequence.

## 8. Archive decision
SRC-109 is full-text audited. ASM-026-NDJ remains a distinct provisional beverage record with partial geographic coverage. ASM-GAP-122 remains High/Open. The evidence supports district-level and named-locality documentation, but not a complete current distribution map or a complete brewing protocol.
