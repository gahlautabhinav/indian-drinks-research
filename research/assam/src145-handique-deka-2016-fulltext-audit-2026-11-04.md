# SRC-145 Handique & Deka (2016) — full-text audit and Mising process attribution

**Audit date:** 2026-11-04. **Primary source:** Handique, P. & Deka, D. C. (2016). “Methodology of rice beer preparation by some ethnic communities residing in Sivasagar District of Assam, India: A survey.” International Journal of Multidisciplinary Research and Development 3(1):337–340.

## Study design and geographic resolution

The authors report a field survey in nine villages densely populated by Ahom, Mising and Deori communities in Sivasagar district, conducted November 2013–February 2014. They interviewed about 20–30 people from each community, mostly women, and observed preparation of starter cultures and rice beverages. The article does not name the nine villages, provide respondent totals by village, or allocate each beverage/starter inventory to individual villages. Therefore this source supports a multi-village Sivasagar study record, not nine named place rows.

The authors say plant samples were collected and scientific names confirmed from literature and with help from Gauhati University’s Department of Botany. No herbarium voucher identifiers or deposition details are provided in the article.

## Mising starter: Apop

The Mising subsection lists 17 plant names used in Apop: Centella asiatica; Hydrocotyle sibthorpioides; Oldenlandia corymbosa; Clerodendrum viscosum; Saccharum officinarum; Ipoemea sp.; Cyclosorus exlensa; Scoparia dulcis; Drymeria cordata; Capsicum annuum; Ananas comosus; Lygodium flexuosum; Zanthoxylum hamiltonianum; Piper nigrum; Pteridium aquilinum; Phogocanthus thyrsiflorus; and Piper longum. These spellings are transcribed as printed; do not silently normalize them. The paper’s reference 16 is Gogoi, Dutta & Mondal (2013), “Various Ethno Medicinal Plants used in the Preparation of Apong…”, Journal of Applied Pharmaceutical Science 3(4):85–88. This indicates that the 17-name list is associated with that distinct 2013 Apong plant paper, not evidence of the complete plant table in Pawe & Gogoi (2013).

The authors describe plant leaves sun-dried for 6–7 days, powdered and mixed with Bao or Sali rice powder at approximately 1:50 w/w, then formed into 11–16 g spheres. Rukji leaves are used to cover the spheres while drying; finished Apop is stored in a dry place, traditionally over kitchen fire heat.

## Mising beverage process — explicitly Nogin Apong

The section heading explicitly calls the beverage “Apong (Nogin Apong)”. The paper reports:
- Summer: cooked Sali rice cooled on a Dola; powdered Apop mixed at approximately 1:45 w/w; mixture placed in a dry earthen Koloh, mouth covered with straw and Rukji leaves; Koloh hung 3–4 feet above fire for 4–5 days; mixture then mixed with water at 2 litres per 5 kg and filtered through cloth. Filtrate is Apong; residue Aaruk is used as feed for pig, fish and cattle.
- Winter: warm cooked rice mixed with powdered Apop; fermentation takes 8–9 days.

**Critical distinction:** this primary source does not describe ash from rice husk/straw in the Mising Nogin Apong method. Its detailed 1:45 / 4–5-day / 8–9-day account must not be relabelled as Poro Apong. This is a key correction to the way SRC-144 (2023 review) places a very similar process summary under its Poro Apong heading. The review appears to combine or misattribute distinct descriptions; keep the original paper's explicit beverage label authoritative for this source.

The paper states that the beverages of all three communities are consumed without distillation. That general statement is not a substitute for beverage-specific process documentation and does not resolve the Poro Apong descriptions in other sources.

## Comparison with other Mising sources

- SRC-136 (Kardong et al. 2012) documents Dibrugarh household Po:ro Apong/Saimod: 100 g powdered Epop per 3 kg cooked rice, about 20 days at 30–35°C, ripe mash Po:ro arouk and warm-water extraction through Ta:suk. This is not the same method as SRC-145's Nogin Apong.
- SRC-005 (Mili & Sundriyal 2023) distinguishes Poro and Nogin and reports ash addition to Poro; its introduction uses distillation wording. Preserve this account separately rather than importing SRC-145's Nogin timings.
- SRC-144's review text presents 1:45, 4–5-day summer and 8–9-day winter details in the Poro Apong subsection. The corresponding 2016 primary article explicitly labels them Nogin Apong. Treat this as a review attribution problem pending examination of Pawe & Gogoi (2013) and the original cited source material.

## Repository decisions

- Register SRC-145 as a primary historical field survey.
- Update SRC-144 to flag the Poro/Nogin attribution issue.
- Do not add nine village rows because the villages are unnamed.
- Do not create a new beverage row: the source documents the already registered Mising Nogin Apong form.
- Update ASM-GAP-060 with this source-level correction and keep it Open. Next: retrieve Gogoi, Dutta & Mondal (2013) and Pawe & Gogoi (2013) original texts; compare the 17-name list and determine whether any Poro-specific method is directly described in the latter.
