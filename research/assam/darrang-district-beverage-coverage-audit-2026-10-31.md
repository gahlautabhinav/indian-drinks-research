# Darrang district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for historical and contemporary beverage evidence explicitly associated with Darrang district, including Kachari/Bodo and Rabha traditions.  
**Decision:** Add one historical district-level evidence lead for Kachari rice beer called Zu/Záu. Do not treat this as evidence of current production, village-level prevalence, or equivalence with the modern canonical Bodo beverage records.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Endle (1911), *The Kacháris* — direct historical district evidence

Sidney Endle's ethnographic monograph states that Kacharis of Darrang habitually consumed rice beer identified as **Zu/Záu**. The text describes it as prepared when wanted for immediate consumption and gives a preparation description involving rice and plant materials. This is a direct historical association between Darrang, the Kachari community, and a named rice beverage. The book was published in 1911 under the orders of the Government of Eastern Bengal and Assam. [Project Gutenberg full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/50008/50008-h/50008-h.htm).

**Limitations:** This is a colonial-era ethnographic account. It does not establish present-day practice, prevalence, or a specific current village. Historical ethnonyms and administrative boundaries must be handled cautiously. The source's Kachari label is retained rather than silently normalised to a contemporary community identity.

### 2. Historical Assam report — corroborating geographic context

A historical settlement report excerpt describes Kacharis in the northern part of Darrang, particularly the Mangaldai subdivision, as consuming substantial quantities of rice beer. The excerpt supports the district/subdivision context but does not clearly name a beverage variant or give a modern village-level production record. [Digitised report excerpt](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/23555/GIPE-008729.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3).

**Limitations:** The available excerpt is not sufficient to establish the report's complete bibliographic metadata or the precise relationship between its historical subdivision boundaries and current administrative units. It is used as corroboration only, not as the sole basis for the beverage identity.

### 3. Rabha Jonga mod / Kecha mod — regional association only

A 2013 ethnobotanical article describes Jonga mod/Kecha mod as a Rabha rice beer and says Rabhas were concentrated in Goalpara, Kamrup and Darrang. However, the study's stated field locality for the relevant plant table is Dhupdhora in Goalpara district. A separate 2010 primary paper is explicitly about Rabha rice beer in Goalpara district. These sources do not establish that Jonga mod was observed or produced in a named Darrang locality. [2013 article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0975761913000720); [2010 primary paper](https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9773/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20459-462.pdf).

**Decision:** Do not add a Darrang Jonga mod production row or attach the Rabha drink to Darrang's place register on the basis of population distribution alone.

## Archive treatment

- Add ASM-PL-108 as a historical, district-level Kachari rice-beer evidence lead.
- Preserve the source wording Zu/Záu and community wording Kachari.
- Leave Beverage_IDs blank: the archive's modern Bodo Jou and other Zu/Zou records have unresolved name and product boundaries; no synonymy is assumed.
- Do not invent a village. Record the historical district/subdivision context in the locality field and caveat.
- Update Darrang's matrix from zero rows to one historical district-level lead, not a current-production locality.
- Keep the district open for contemporary village-level verification.

## Remaining research questions

1. Can contemporary Darrang ethnography or community-held sources identify present-day Kachari/Bodo rice-beverage terminology and localities?
2. Can the historical Mangaldai subdivision references be crosswalked to named villages using the original report and administrative maps?
3. Is Endle's Zu/Záu linguistically or culturally continuous with any currently documented Bodo beverage form? This requires community/primary-source confirmation, not inference from similar names.

**Conclusion:** Darrang is no longer an empty archive row: a historical district-level beverage tradition is documented. It is not yet a verified contemporary production locality.
