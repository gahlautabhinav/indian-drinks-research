# Bodo Jou process and locality cross-check: SRC-066 vs GI heritage summaries

**Audit date:** 2026-10-24  
**Scope:** One targeted follow-up after the Journal 191 PDF retrieval attempt. Reconcile the accessible 2025 peer-reviewed Bodo beverage methodology article with the current Bodoland GI Heritage exhibits and official GI registry metadata. This is not a substitute for the product-specific GI Statements of Case.

## Sources reviewed

1. Deka, A. K., Basumatary, S., & Deka, D. C. (2025). “Methodology of ethnic cereal-based and fruit-based beverages of Bodo community of Assam.” *Journal of Ethnic Foods*, 12, article 23. ## New findings from the 2025 peer-reviewed article

- The article reports fieldwork in four named villages around Kokrajhar district headquarters: **Amlaiguri** (25 km north), **Gendrabili** (5 km south), **“Jalpaiguri”** (10 km east, exactly as printed), and **Banshigaon** (2 km west). It reports 50 interviewees (35 male, 15 female), with information collected in April–July 2022 and October–November 2023 (Methods, lines around 102–104 on the publisher page).
- The accessible article text does **not** assign each individual beverage subtype to each of the four villages. Therefore, these four locations are evidence of the article's overall survey frame, not four confirmed production locations for every Jou subtype.
- The article distinguishes **Joubidwi** as the direct rice-fermentation product from **Joufinai** as a stored beverage. Its detailed process account says stored Joufinai can be kept for months; the article also describes **Kanthal-Jougwran** (jackfruit-based), **Kola-Jougwran** (banana-based), and fruit beverages including **Kamrenga-bidwi** (starfruit) and **Amlai-bidwi** (gooseberry). It describes a traditional distillation setup for Jougwran (publisher page, Results and Discussion).
- The paper's general background also describes rice-derived Jougwran, but the detailed process section explicitly includes fruit-based Jougwran. The word **Jougwran alone is not sufficient to infer substrate**. Preserve the substrate/process qualifiers when recording a source claim.

The current IP India pages confirm three separate registered entries: 968 Bodo Jou Gwran, 969 Bodo Jou Gishi, and 970 Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi; each page lists Journal 191 (available 31 May 2024), and each gives the registry's geographical-area field as Assam. The registry's area field must not be converted into a claim of uniform production in every Assam district. See the individual official pages linked above.

## Geography handling

- **Amlaiguri, Gendrabili, Banshigaon:** retain as named survey localities associated with the overall SRC-066 fieldwork frame. Do not yet create beverage-specific production rows unless the archive's place schema can clearly label the association as *study-area / fieldwork locality* rather than confirmed product production.
- **“Jalpaiguri”:** preserve the exact printed spelling in the source note and flag it for verification. Do not silently change it to another place name based only on directional plausibility.
- Do not infer the exact locality of Jou Gishi, Maibra Jou Bidwi or any specific Jougwran substrate from the four-village study frame without an explicit source-level mapping.

## Gap and next action

**ASM-GAP-026 remains Open.** The source article improves the comparative process picture but does not replace the GI Journal 191 / Statements of Case / maps / producer lists. The next step is to compare the product-specific Maibra Jou Bidwi heritage exhibit and the source-level terminology in SRC-029/SRC-066, then return to official document retrieval only if a new access route is available. No canonical beverage, locality or synonym record is added by this memo.
