# SRC-342 — Nativers' Institute of Bodology (2026): Dimasa lexicography and archive project status

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026  
**Publisher:** Nativers' Institute of Bodology (NIB), Kokrajhar, Assam.  
**Project page:** https://www.nibodology.org/blogs/multilingual-dictionary-bodo-dimasa-kokborok-english  
**Archive project page:** https://www.nibodology.org/blogs/bodo-and-dimasa-heritage-digital-archive-project

## 1. New lexicographic lead

NIB's 6 November 2026 project page announces a comparative **Bodo–Dimasa–Kokborok–English multilingual dictionary** undertaken by its School of Bodo Language, Literature and Translation Studies. The description says it aims to compile shared vocabulary, cognate roots and semantic equivalents, with English glosses, phonemic transcriptions and grammatical categorisation.

This is an institutional project announcement, not a published dictionary or a list of verified lexical entries. The page gives no entry-level content, fieldwork locations, named Dimasa contributors, dialect coverage, or publication/delivery date. It therefore does not yet resolve Judi, Jugap, Juharo, Judima, Ju, Zou, Jumai or Laosa. Do not use the proposed cognate/semantic comparison as evidence that similarly named beverages are synonyms.

## 2. Archive project continuity

NIB's separate 6 November 2026 page describes the Bodo and Dimasa Heritage Digital Archive as a long-term, participatory, open-access Mukurtu project in collaboration with the University of North Texas. It says the archive is intended to preserve Bodo and Dimasa oral lore, cultural traditions, rituals, artefacts and material history, with community control and academic accessibility.

The page is a current institutional project description. It does not report current item counts or name any beverage entries. The live archive root and /digital-heritage route timed out in this retrieval session (25 November 2026); this is an access limitation, not evidence that any particular entry is absent.

## 3. Relevance to the Dimasa beverage audit

The 2024 archive papers already provide a strong path for further work:
- Longmailai & Sinha (2024), *Vāk Manthan* 9(II), says the Dimasa archive had 256 Cultural Heritage items, 54 oral narratives (50 from Cachar and 4 from Dima Hasao), 256 Dimasa-English dictionary words and 3 blogs at the reported snapshot. It documents the broad term **ju** in the Hawar laothai pre-marriage ritual and rice wine at the Baidima harvest celebration, without beverage production locality or recipe.
- Longmailai (2024), *Sampratyaya* 1, Special Issue 2, reports 255 cultural-category items, 310 Dimasa dictionary headwords and 3 blogs at its reported snapshot. It explicitly distinguishes Judi (rice wine), Jugap (rice beer) and Juharo (a rice drink compared to white rum), and says Humao is used as starter for these.
- The *Sampratyaya* paper lists Singha's **Dimasa Word Book: A Classified Vocabulary** (2010) under the archive's “Scholarly Works” category. This confirms that the book is represented as a scholarly resource in the archive; it does not expose its individual word-book entries or establish that the beverage terms occur in it.

The different 2024 counts (256 vs 255 cultural items; 256 vs 310 dictionary words) are retained as separate source-reported snapshots. The papers may reflect different update dates or counting definitions; the evidence retrieved does not explain the difference. Do not average, reconcile or overwrite either figure.

## 4. Lexical source access status

Catalogues identify Kh. Dhiren Singha's *Dimasa Word Book: A Classified Vocabulary* as a 121-page English–Dimasa (Roman script) dictionary published by DVS Publishers, Guwahati, in 2010; ISBN 978-81-86307-33-5. The bibliographic record lists only Tokyo University of Foreign Studies Library as a holding institution in the NII catalogue result retrieved. No searchable scan or preview of the actual entries was found in this retrieval pass.

Therefore, no claim is made about whether Judi/Jugap/Juharo appear as headwords or how the book defines them. The book remains a high-priority source for direct inspection.

## 5. Archive decisions

1. Register the NIB 2026 project announcement as SRC-342, explicitly classified as a project lead, not lexical evidence.
2. Do not create beverage entities, recipes, synonyms or locality records from the dictionary project's announcement.
3. Preserve the 2024 archive counts separately as reported in SRC-339 and SRC-341; do not force a reconciliation.
4. Preserve the distinction between the 2010 Word Book being listed in the archive's Scholarly Works category and the archive's own Heritage Dictionary headwords.
5. Continue seeking direct access to the Word Book and item-level archive entries; any extraction should preserve source attribution, dialect, date, cultural protocol and reuse restrictions.
