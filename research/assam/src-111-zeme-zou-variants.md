# SRC-111 — Zeme Zou beverage variants and the Nduizou/Nduijao question

**Citation:** Atungbou, N. (2020). “Food practice for physical and spiritual well-being of the Zeme, the tribe in Northeast India.” *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 7, article 41. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42779-020-00074-0

## Main finding: Zou is described as a category with distinct forms
Atungbou identifies three forms under the Zeme local beverage category **Zou**: **Tekuizou**, **Tamzou/Lingtakzou**, and **Nduizou**. The paper also uses the spelling **Tamzau** in one passage, and calls Nduizou **Herazou** (a sacred drink). These names should be preserved as source-specific forms, not flattened into a single generic recipe.

### Tekuizou / Zousang
- Made using rice and paddy; the paddy is soaked until it germinates, then sprouts and rice are pounded together.
- The account mentions a gourd or wooden pot, hot water followed by cold water, and sealing for about a week; filtration/fermentation is described as generally taking about five days.
- The author describes Tekuizou as common, offered to guests as hospitality, and suitable for a broad range of people in the reported cultural context.
- A separate passage refers to distilling and drying the germinated paddy before pounding. This is a source-specific process account; do not generalize it to every Zou preparation.

### Tamzou / Lingtakzou (also Tamzau in the text)
- Identified as a corn/maize beverage made from pounded maize powder.
- The paper describes boiling the maize, spreading it on a bamboo tray, adding yeast/sprout seeds, fermenting in a basket for about a week, and extracting liquid in a bamboo tube called *tepin* with water.
- The source associates this form with festivals and describes it as a drink for older people in its reported cultural context.

### Nduizou / Herazou
- Described as an important sacred drink used on special occasions, including rituals and marital negotiation.
- The paper describes rice, fresh sugarcane leaves and a starter called **nduihi**. The starter is made from soaked rice and chopped sugarcane leaves, shaped into small cakes and dried; cooked rice is cooled on a bamboo tray, mixed with starter, and covered with banana leaves. The paper describes a liquid called **chiangne** as the first drip and assigns it special cultural restrictions.
- A separate paragraph calls Nduizou “herazou” and says it is reserved for special occasions. Cultural restrictions and reported practices are recorded as the author's ethnographic account, not universal rules for all Zeme households.

## Critical crosswalk: Nduizou is not yet proven to be Nduijao
Chakrabarty (2017; SRC-109) documents **Nduijao** and a starter spelled **Nduiyi** among the Jeme Naga in Dima Hasao. Atungbou (2020; SRC-111) documents **Nduizou/Herazou** and a starter spelled **nduihi** among the Zeme. The similar names and starter descriptions make this a high-value cross-check, but neither paper explicitly says the names are synonyms or that the starter forms are identical. Keep ASM-026-NDJ and the new Nduizou record separate pending community-led or author clarification.

Likewise, the 2024 metagenomic paper (SRC-110) calls the Zeme beverage “Zao” in prose but “Zou” in Table 1 and names starter “Ndhuihi.” Atungbou’s article supports Zou as a category, but does not by itself resolve the 2024 paper's internal spelling inconsistency or prove that all Zou variants correspond to the 2024 sample.

## Atlas decisions
- Registered SRC-111.
- Add provisional separate records for Tekuizou, Tamzou/Lingtakzou and Nduizou/Herazou, linked to SRC-111; do not treat them as independently sampled products or claim village-level production.
- Keep Nduizou separate from Nduijao and keep Nduihi separate from Nduiyi/Ndhuihi until stronger evidence resolves identity.
- Cultural uses are documented ethnographic claims; traditional health framing is not clinical evidence.

## Follow-up
Seek community-authored or community-reviewed material, clarify the Nduizou/Nduijao and nduihi/Nduiyi relationship, and identify the specific Zeme locality/sample in SRC-110 if available.
