# Rohi and Sepa — Sonowal Kachari, Assam

This research batch records two names separately because a 2026 peer-reviewed paper explicitly treats **Rohi** and **Sepa** as traditional Sonowal Kachari rice beers. The accessible abstract does not fully explain the technical distinction, so Sepa's recipe is deliberately left unresolved rather than copied from Rohi.

## Record A — Rohi

**ID:** `ASM-019-ROH`  
**Community:** Sonowal Kachari  
**Field-study locality:** Jokai area, Dibrugarh district (exact village not specified in accessible text)  
**Starter:** Mod pitha / Saul pitha / Saoul pitha

### Documented preparation

Sonowal (2023) reports glutinous rice as the principal substrate. Plant parts are collected and washed; the article describes drying them on a bamboo sieve (Saloni), soaking rice overnight, grinding rice and plant materials in a traditional foot-operated wooden grinder (Dheki), and adding a small quantity of older starter. Starter cakes are shaped and dried.

Cooked/glutinous rice is cooled and mixed with starter, then fermented in a heat-sterilized earthen pot. The reported duration is generally 4–5 days, about a week in winter. The fermented mass is strained using a Sakoni net to produce the beverage. Preserve the exact wording and botanical list from the article when extending this record; starter ingredients should not be universalized across localities.

### Cultural context

The 2023 participatory study reports Rohi in festivals, sacrificial rituals, deity offerings, ceremonies and everyday social life. It describes fermentation technology as knowledge held by women in its study context. These are source-specific findings, not universal claims about every household.

### Names

The 2023 article reports Rohi, Jhul mod and Madhopurukhor rokh. Their exact synonymy across villages is not yet established.

## Record B — Sepa

**ID:** `ASM-020-SEP`  
**Community:** Sonowal Kachari  
**Starter reported:** Mod pitha  
**Locality:** Not established from the accessible abstract

Bhattacharya and Chandra Deka (2026) explicitly name Sepa alongside Rohi and report that both are rice-based fermented alcoholic beverages using Mod pitha. The paper examines physicochemical parameters and antioxidant properties. The repository abstract does not provide Sepa-specific ingredients, equipment, fermentation duration, sample villages, or its exact distinction from Rohi. This dossier does not invent those details.

The article's antioxidant analysis is not proof of clinical benefit. Any medicinal claims require separate evidence.

## Geographic confidence and limitations

Rohi has a field-study locality at the Jokai area of Dibrugarh from the 2023 article. The accessible text does not give an exact village name. The 2026 paper's repository abstract does not establish Sepa's sampling locality. Do not use the broader Sonowal Kachari distribution as proof of current brewing in every district.

## Research lead — Black Rohi Modh (provisional variant)

**Provisional ID:** `ASM-021-BRM`  
**Reported sample locality:** Pukia, Dhemaji district (2026 study)  
**Community label in source:** Kachari; exact subcommunity not established in the reviewed passage  
**Sample designation:** Black Rohi Modh (BR), distinguished from Rohi Modh (RH)

Das, Medhi and Tamang (2026) separately analyzed a household batch labelled Black Rohi Modh and a batch labelled Rohi Modh. Their sample metadata places both at Pukia, Dhemaji district; the study describes the Black Rohi Modh sample as a black-rice beverage, while Rohi Modh is described as using Bora chaul/glutinous rice and an aaroi starter. The article describes a four-day fermentation for Rohi Modh, but the Black Rohi Modh-specific starter and fermentation details should not be inferred from that description without checking the full sample-specific methods.

This archive currently treats Black Rohi Modh as a **provisional substrate-associated variant record**, not as proof of an entirely separate community tradition. The paper cites Sonowal (2023), whose fieldwork on Sonowal Kachari Rohi was in the Jokai area of Dibrugarh. However, the 2026 study's broad “Kachari” label and Pukia sample location do not, on their own, establish the producer's precise subcommunity or whether Black Rohi Modh is locally understood as a distinct beverage name. That boundary requires local confirmation.

The 2026 study analyzed one authentic household batch per beverage, with technical replicates. Its chemical and microbial findings are valuable sample-level evidence, not a population-wide survey. Antioxidant assay results and predicted metabolic pathways should not be presented as proof of clinical health benefits.
