# SRC-095 audit — systematic review on traditional fermented rice beverages of Assam

**Audit date:** 2026-10-23  
**Source:** Barman, P.; Roy, K.; Sahu, R. (2026). “Systematic review on traditional fermented rice beverages of Assam.” *Next Research* 4, article 101241. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nexres.2025.101241  
**Purpose:** Assess what this review can contribute to the Assam beverage archive without treating a secondary synthesis as primary field evidence.

## 1. What the accessible publisher preview establishes

The publisher-indexed article preview describes a PRISMA-guided literature-selection process and says 42 studies on fermented beverages were retained. The selection arithmetic is internally consistent:

- Initial search results: 2,997
- Duplicates removed: 512
- Excluded for relevance/inclusion criteria: 2,204
- Excluded after title/abstract screening: 233
- Excluded after full-text screening: 6
- Reported final included set: 42

The article frames its synthesis around rice substrates, starter cakes, microbial communities, fermentation processes, plant ingredients, cultural significance and potential bioactive compounds. It names communities including Deori, Mising, Ahom, Dimasa, Boro/Bodo, Karbi, Rabha and Sonowal Kachari in its discussion of Assam traditions.

## 2. What this does—and does not—add to the archive

**Useful as a discovery and synthesis source**
- Provides a recent consolidated reference trail for older beverage, starter-culture and microbiology literature.
- Signals broad subject coverage that can be used to identify missing primary papers and check whether current dossiers have missed older references.
- Its stated future-research priorities include beverage safety, preservation and long-term health effects—topics that should remain open research questions rather than claims of established benefit.

**Not primary evidence for locality-level production**
- The review does not establish a current village-by-village production map in the accessible preview.
- A community name or beverage name in the review cannot be used to infer that every member or village produces it.
- The headline “approximately 70 medicinal plants” is a review-level summary; it must not be treated as a validated, universal starter recipe or a beverage-specific plant count without tracing the underlying primary sources.
- Traditional medicinal use, in-vitro activity, metabolite detection and clinical benefit are different evidence categories. The review's broad pharmacological framing is not evidence that consuming a traditional alcoholic beverage treats disease or is safe.

## 3. Review-method audit

The reported screening arithmetic reconciles to 42 studies. However, the accessible material does not provide a verified row-by-row extraction of all 42 included studies, a fully checked included-study bibliography, or enough data to independently reproduce the screening. The article says PRISMA was used as a methodological template; this should not be upgraded to a claim that every PRISMA reporting item was independently verified.

The publisher-indexed page exposes a long reference list (166 references in the visible publisher record), while the ResearchGate record reports 153 references. This may reflect platform metadata or reference parsing rather than an error in the article; preserve the discrepancy as unresolved until the publisher PDF/reference list can be compared directly.

The publisher page also includes references to general fermented foods, plants, and health studies outside Assam. A paper appearing in the reference list is not necessarily one of the 42 included Assam beverage studies. Do not equate total references with included studies.

## 4. Cross-check against current project records

The archive already contains primary or focused research records for, among others:
- Mising Apong/ÉPOP and locality-specific starter inventories (SRC-005 onward; ASM-GAP-006 and ASM-GAP-045).
- Deori Sujen and starter-name variation (SRC-009 to SRC-012).
- Karbi Hor-alank/Hor-arak process distinction (SRC-013 onward).
- Rabha Jonga-mod/Choko and source-specific Phap inventories (SRC-017 onward; SRC-102 and SRC-103).
- Tai-Ahom Xajpani naming and starter terminology (SRC-021 onward).
- Bodo Jou forms, starter variation and GI evidence limits (SRC-027 onward; SRC-066).
- Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu and separate Hill-Tiwa Chu comparison (SRC-036 to SRC-038, SRC-078, SRC-092, SRC-100, SRC-101).
- Dimasa Judima and related North Cachar Hills fermentation research (SRC-001 onward; SRC-097 and SRC-098).
- Sonowal Kachari Rohi and related terminology questions (SRC-044 to SRC-046).

This overlap supports using SRC-095 as a **bibliographic cross-check**, not as a basis to add duplicate beverage records or place rows. The next valuable step is to obtain the complete included-study list and map each Assam-relevant primary study to existing source IDs and dossiers. Only genuinely missing primary sources should be added as new source records.

## 5. Decisions

1. Keep SRC-095 as a secondary review source.
2. Do not add or merge canonical beverage records from the review's broad community list alone.
3. Do not add locality rows from district/community mentions in the review.
4. Keep health claims qualified as reported traditional beliefs or preclinical findings unless supported by appropriate direct evidence.
5. Leave the 42-study included set and 166-versus-153 reference count unresolved pending a directly checked publisher full text.
6. Keep the related research gap open; next action is a primary-source crosswalk, not another broad beverage-name expansion.
