# Primary-source follow-up: Deori Sujen (2007) and Rabha rice beer (2010)

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Compare two primary Assam ethnobotanical papers with the existing SRC-315 (Bhuyan & Baishya 2013) review-derived records and improve the canonical beverage records without duplicating drinks or inventing village-level provenance.

## 1. Deori Sujen — SRC-009

**Citation:** Chaya Deori, Samim Sofika Begum & A. A. Mao (2007), “Ethnobotany of Sujen—a local rice beer of Deori tribe of Assam,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 6(1):121–125.  
**Full text:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242292561_Ethnobotany_of_SujenA_local_rice_beer_of_Deori_tribe_of_Assam

### Field sites and method
The study reports fieldwork during 2003–2004 in three villages:
- Sunpura Deori gaon, Sadiya, Tinsukia district
- Kololuwah, Dibrugarh district
- Rajabari, Sivasagar district (paper spelling “Sibsagarh”)

The authors interviewed villagers who regularly prepared Sujen and observed the complete preparation process. They consulted the Regional Herbarium, Assam, Botanical Survey of India, Eastern Circle, Shillong, for plant identification. The paper does not report respondent counts, household counts, village-specific plant matrices or voucher numbers.

### Starter: Mod pitha
The paper reports 32 plant taxa in the starter inventory. Twenty are called essential; the remainder are said to add flavour. The authors also report historical use of more than 100 plants, but say only a smaller selection was being used at the time of their study. Do not interpret the historical >100 as a measured current inventory.

The documented method:
1. Cleaned leaves, fronds, bark, roots and bulbs are sun-dried for a day.
2. About 3–5 kg of rice (Saol) is soaked for around two hours.
3. Rice and dried plant parts are ground together in a Dheki and repeatedly sieved.
4. Two or three old Mod pitha cakes are added as inoculum.
5. Water is added to form sticky paste; cakes approximately 2–3 cm diameter and 1 cm thick are shaped.
6. Cakes are laid on clean paddy straw in a Kula, covered with straw and dried above the kitchen fireplace on a Dhua sang for a couple of weeks until hard.
7. Unused cakes are stored in a small earthen Tekele covered with straw; the paper reports storage for 2–3 months.

### Brewing Sujen
- The paper lists rice (preferably glutinous), iron Kerahi, bamboo spoon Heta, bamboo Dola, Koloh earthen pot, rice husk (Tuh), iron stand Siya, Sakoni conical sieve and banana leaves among the equipment/materials.
- About 4–5 kg rice is cooked with rice husk and water, stirred to avoid burning.
- Cooked rice is cooled on banana leaves; two or three powdered Mod pitha cakes are mixed into the warm rice.
- The vessel is smoked/dried using rice husk over fire; the inoculated rice mass is transferred to the Koloh.
- Bihlongoni fronds and banana leaves are used to cover the mixture; the vessel is closed and fermented.
- The paper reports 3–4 days in summer and about one week in winter.
- Whitish froth called Meya is filtered through a Sakoni; the filtrate is Sujen.
- Fresh Sujen is described as sweet; reported storage is 2–3 weeks in summer and up to one month in winter. These are source-reported observations, not food-safety advice.

### Interpretation
This is a primary source with a named three-village study frame and observed methods. It strengthens ASM-004-SUJ but does not justify allocating individual plant taxa to each village. Mod pitha is a starter, not a synonym for Sujen.

## 2. Rabha rice beer — SRC-017

**Citation:** Dipali Deka & Gajen Chandra Sarma (2010), “Traditionally used herbs in the preparation of rice-beer by the Rabha tribe of Goalpara district, Assam,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 9(3):459–462.  
**Full text:** https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9773/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20459-462.pdf

### Field provenance
The authors interviewed older, experienced Rabha men and women in different villages of Goalpara district and prepared herbarium specimens. However, they do not name the villages, give survey dates or informant counts, publish a village-to-plant matrix, or provide specimen voucher numbers. The article identifies three Rabha sub-tribe/language labels in its plant table: Rangdaniya, Maitori and Pati.

Do not equate these unnamed SRC-017 villages with Dhupdhora, the locality reported by SRC-315 (Bhuyan & Baishya 2013). That is a separate source-specific locality account.

### Beverage and starter terminology
- Starter cake: **bakhor**, **surachi** or **phap**.
- Fermented drink: **choko** or **jonga-mod**.
- Distilled liquor after a second fermentation: **fotika**.
- Boro-rice choko: **sachotia mod**.
- Rice and beer consumed together without separating solids/liquid: **juguli**.

These names describe different process forms or preparations and should not be collapsed into one beverage alias list without local confirmation.

### Ten taxa in the starter-plant table
1. *Ananas comosus* — Anaros (Pati) — tender leaves described in medicinal-use column, but Table 1 plant part should be checked against printed page; extracted table does not clearly specify the starter part.
2. *Artocarpus heterophyllus* — Pan-chung (Rongdani) — leaves
3. *Calotropis gigantea* — Akhomhang (Rongdani) — leaves
4. *Capsicum frutescens* — Jhaluk (Maitori) — fruits
5. “*Cleodendrum viscosum*” (printed spelling) — Holitita (Pati) — leaves
6. *Dennstaedtia scabra* — Bisalhkia (Maitori) — fronds
7. *Ochthochloa coracana* — Kuchibun (Pati) — leaves
8. *Plumbago indica* — Agiachit (Rongdani) — roots
9. *Saccharum officinarum* — Kurchi (Rongdani) — leaves
10. *Scoparia dulcis* — Phap jibra (Rongdani) — shoots

Preserve the paper's printed botanical spellings, including “Cleodendrum” and “Ochthochloa”, pending botanical verification.

### Process
- The paper describes starter paste made with wet rice, the plant materials and a considerable quantity of old starter cake. Round/flat globules of approximately 50 g are formed and dried on straw over a broad bamboo sieve. *Heteropogon contortus* is scattered on the sieve and sun-dried; the text describes it as a drying-bed material, not one of the ten listed ingredient taxa.
- For choko/phap, tightly cooked rice is cooled on a bamboo mat. The authors report about two cakes for rice cooked from 2 kg fresh rice.
- Rice and starter are placed inside an earthen pitcher called **jonga**, around a bamboo-net cylinder called **janthi**. The mouth is sealed with warmed *Musa balbisiana* leaf and the vessel is kept in a dark place.
- Fermentation is reported as 4–5 days in summer and 7–8 days in winter. The liquid stored in janthi is collected using a perforated dried *Lagenaria siceraria* shell.
- The fermented rice-beer is again fermented with water and starter for 3–4 days, then distilled in three stacked earthen or metal pitcher-like pots (*hadi/luduki*). The strong distilled drink is called **fotika**.

### Keep ingredients separate from equipment and ritual materials
Do not count the following as starter ingredients:
- *Heteropogon contortus*: drying-bed substrate.
- *Musa balbisiana*: vessel seal.
- *Ricinus communis* leaf and charcoal: placed on lid as protection against evil spirits (author-reported ritual).
- *Lagenaria siceraria*: collection scoop.
- *Corchorus capsularis* fibre and mud: seals joints in the distillation apparatus.

The paper's medicinal claims and claims that beverages cure ailments are ethnographic beliefs, not clinical evidence or safety validation.

## 3. Cross-check with SRC-315 (Bhuyan & Baishya 2013)

SRC-315 is a later multi-community paper with a Rabha account from Dhupdhora, Goalpara, and a starter terminology/process account. It should be compared with SRC-017 but not treated as an independent confirmation of every plant or every field locality.

- SRC-017's ten taxa are an explicitly enumerated Rabha starter-plant list.
- SRC-315's account is a separate locality-specific description; do not retroactively assign SRC-017's ten taxa to Dhupdhora.
- Any matching taxa across the papers are corroborated reports, but independence of the informants and underlying field data is not established.
- SRC-017 calls the drink choko/jonga-mod and the distilled liquor fotika. Do not merge fotika with the fermented beverage record unless a separate product record exists and its identity is verified.
