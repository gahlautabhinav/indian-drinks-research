# Sujen — Deori community, Assam research dossier

- **Archive ID:** ASM-004-SUJ
- **State:** Assam
- **Community:** Deori
- **Type:** Traditional fermented rice beverage
- **Starter culture:** Mod pitha; Chuzae-pitha is reported for the Deori starter in a 2010 Dhemaji study
- **Evidence status:** Core identity and historical village-level ethnobotanical documentation are well established; present-day distribution and process need updated verification.

## Evidence-backed summary

Sujen is a traditional rice-based alcoholic beverage associated with the Deori community of Assam. The 2007 paper by Deori, Begum and Mao describes ethnobotanical fieldwork conducted during 2003–2004 in three villages: Sunpura Deori gaon (Sadiya, Tinsukia district), Kololuwah (Dibrugarh district) and Rajabari (Sivasagar district). It reports 32 plant species used in the preparation of the Mod pitha starter in that study.

A separate 2010 study by Baruah, Gohain and Sonowal documents Deori starter culture in Borbam Deori gaon, Bordoloni block, Dhemaji district, and uses the name Chuzae-pitha for the starter. It reports 15 plant species for its sampled Deori starter-culture practice. These inventories differ because the studies cover different locations and methods; neither should be treated as a universal Deori recipe.

## Starter culture and process

Older ethnobotanical accounts describe rice and locally collected plant parts being processed into starter cakes, with previously prepared starter material used to carry forward the culture. The beverage is then prepared by combining rice and starter, fermenting the mixture in a covered vessel, and straining the resulting liquid. Bamboo, wooden and earthen equipment are described in historical accounts, but exact equipment and terminology should be attributed to each source.

The 2007 study reports fermentation taking approximately 3–4 days in summer and about a week in winter in its observed context. It also records seasonal differences in keeping time. These are historical observations from that study, not universal process standards or food-safety recommendations.

## Locality evidence

- **Sunpura Deori gaon, Sadiya (Tinsukia district):** named in the 2007 three-village ethnobotanical study.
- **Kololuwah (Dibrugarh district):** named in the same study.
- **Rajabari (Sivasagar district):** named in the same study.
- **Borbam Deori gaon, Bordoloni block (Dhemaji district):** named in the 2010 starter-culture study.

These are historical research localities. They do not prove that every household in these villages currently prepares Sujen or that these are the only production areas.

## Cultural context

The 2007 study describes Sujen as significant in Deori festivals and celebrations, and reports use in some birth and marriage ceremonies and historical hospitality practices. Later surveys also describe its importance as a welcome drink. These reports should remain source- and locality-specific until contemporary community sources verify current practice.

## Research gaps

1. Verify current Sujen preparation in the four named historical research localities.
2. Compare Mod pitha and Chuzae-pitha terminology and plant inventories by village.
3. Separate historical fermentation and storage observations from current practice.
4. Establish whether starter preparation seasons reported in later surveys are followed across districts or only in sampled areas.
5. Expand village coverage with recent, consent-based sources and community review.

## Editorial rule

Preserve source dates and study boundaries. Do not merge different starter names or plant inventories without evidence, and do not treat reported ethnomedicinal uses as proof of clinical efficacy.
