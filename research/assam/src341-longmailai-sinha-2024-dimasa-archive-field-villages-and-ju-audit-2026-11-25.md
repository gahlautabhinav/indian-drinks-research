# SRC-341 — Longmailai & Sinha (2024): Dimasa oral-narrative archive, field villages and beverage evidence boundary

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026  
**Citation:** Monali Longmailai and Rajkumari Monimala Sinha (2024), “Documenting Dimasa Oral Narratives and Digital Archiving,” *Vāk Manthan* 9(II).  
**Full text:** https://selindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/01_Dimasa-oral-narratives.pdf  
**Archive:** https://bododimasaarchive.org/

## 1. Why this source matters

This article provides a detailed account of the Dimasa section of the Bodo and Dimasa Heritage Digital Archive (BDA), its fieldwork and its item documentation model. It is distinct from Longmailai's August 2024 *Sampratyaya* paper (SRC-339), although both concern the same community-led archive.

The article says the Dimasa archive team collected material in 15 Cachar villages in 2022 and gives their names. It describes oral narratives, village histories, songs, rituals and other cultural heritage, and reports that the archive included 256 items at the time of writing, of which 50 were oral narratives from Cachar. It also explains that the archive uses Mukurtu and has categories, communities and cultural protocols.

## 2. Field villages — archive collection, not beverage production

The 15 villages listed are:

1. Rongpur
2. Dholai
3. Sheorartal
4. Kumacherra
5. Kanakpur
6. Hawarma
7. Joypur
8. Khaspur
9. Khauthai
10. Raidilung
11. Madra
12. Bijoypur
13. Lodi
14. Lodi Kachari
15. Borbond

The paper says material collected across these villages includes tools and instruments, flora and fauna, folk songs and other categories. It does **not** say that Judi, Jugap, Juharo, Judima or any other rice beverage was produced in each village. These are archive fieldwork localities, not beverage producer localities. Do not create beverage production edges for any of the 15 villages.

## 3. Beverage evidence in this paper

In its discussion of Hawar Dimasa traditions, the article mentions rice beer **ju** in the **laothai** pre-marriage ceremony, alongside betel leaf and betel nut. This is useful independent cultural/ritual context for a broad Dimasa/Hawar rice-beer label. It does not provide a recipe, starter, fermentation duration, alcohol strength, named producer or specific village where the drink was brewed.

The paper also describes Baidima dance and says rice wine is prepared and consumed with meat and sticky rice during the harvest celebration. This is a broad cultural description; it does not name the drink Judi/Judima/Juharo, and does not provide an item-level producer locality. Do not map it to a named village or equate the term with the more specific categories in SRC-339.

## 4. Archive metadata model — important for future extraction

The paper explains that the archive's categories include cultural heritage items, with metadata and text fields. It describes oral-narrative item documentation through:
- summary;
- description;
- cultural narrative and translation;
- audio/video/image or other media;
- source and contributor details;
- creator;
- location;
- original date (date recorded);
- upload and modification dates.

It also explains that the Heritage Dictionary supports headwords, alternate spellings, translations, audio recordings, sources, phonological transcription, definitions, example sentences, additional media and related content. Cultural protocol is a distinct archive component concerning community ownership/permissions.

These fields are precisely what the beverage archive should be checked for: source/contributor, dialect or speech variety, location, recorded date, alternate spellings, audio, and links between dictionary entries and cultural heritage items. The article does not expose an item-level Judi/Jugap/Juharo dictionary entry or its metadata.

## 5. Access and verification attempt (25 November 2026)

The live archive root and its /digital-heritage route timed out when opened through the available web retrieval path during this audit. Search indexing did not return a direct Judi/Jugap/Juharo archive item. This is an access/retrieval limitation, **not** evidence that the items are absent from the archive.

The paper itself gives examples of direct item routes and archive sections. It identifies the archive as open access but also describes community cultural protocols and ownership. Future retrieval should respect any item-level protocol, attribution, watermarking and reuse terms; public visibility does not automatically mean unrestricted reuse.

## 6. Cross-source reconciliation

- SRC-339 (Longmailai 2024, *Sampratyaya*) gives the explicit taxonomy Judi (rice wine), Jugap (rice beer), Juharo (rice drink compared to white rum), and associates Humao starter cake with these names.
- SRC-341 uses the broader term **ju** in a Hawar pre-marriage ritual context; it does not call it Judi or Judima.
- SRC-340 documents Judi in birth and marriage ritual contexts in Dima Hasao, but its four villages are ritual-study sites, not production localities.
- SRC-338 lists Ju, Jumai/Judi, Juhara and Laosa, without separate processes.
- These sources establish a rich terminology and cultural-use record but do not yet establish synonymy among Ju, Judi, Judima, Zou, Jumai, Jugap or Juharo.

## 7. Archive decisions

1. Register this article as SRC-341.
2. Preserve the 15 villages as **Dimasa archive fieldwork locations only**, not beverage production localities.
3. Record the term **ju** as used in the Hawar laothai pre-marriage ritual context, with no recipe or village assignment.
4. Record the Baidima harvest celebration rice-wine mention as a cultural-use lead only; do not infer beverage identity or production location.
5. Keep SRC-339's Judi/Jugap/Juharo taxonomy separate from this broad ju label.
6. Revisit the live archive for item-level entries when accessible, prioritising the Food category and Heritage Dictionary. Extract only item metadata that is publicly visible and compatible with the relevant cultural protocol.
