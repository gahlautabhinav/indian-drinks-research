# SRC-154 — Duarah (2023) Nutrition Society of India antimicrobial abstract

**Source:** Radali Duarah, with Prof. Mridula Saikia Barooah named as co-presenter/supervisor in the abstract. “Antimicrobial analysis of medicinal plants used in traditional rice beer starter culture of Assam against five human pathogenic bacteria.” Abstract ID **PP-2023-0069**, in *55th Annual National Conference of the Nutrition Society of India: Programme, Proceedings & Abstracts*, 25–26 November 2023, ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad. Proceedings PDF, printed page 162 (PDF extraction lines 10093–10125).

**Publisher/source:** Nutrition Society of India conference proceedings hosted at https://www.nsi-aicon.com/pdf/SOVENIR_BOOK_55TH_ANNUAL_CONFERENCE_OF_NSI_2023_Version1.pdf

## What the abstract reports

- Six medicinal plants used in traditional rice-beer starter culture in Assam were screened against five named bacterial targets: *Salmonella typhi*, *Escherichia coli*, *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Shigella* and *Serratia* (the abstract does not specify the *Serratia* species in this conference version).
- Ethanolic and methanolic extracts were tested by agar-disc-diffusion method.
- The result paragraph reports the following *E. coli* inhibition-zone values: ethanolic “Cucurmin longa” leaves 25 mm, *Polygonum hydropiper* 23 mm, *Drymaria cordata* 23 mm, and *Psidium guajava* leaves 24 mm; methanolic *Polygonum hydropiper* 18 mm and *Vitex negundo* 24 mm.
- The conclusion says activity was observed against the selected pathogens except *Shigella*.
- The abstract does not name all six screened plants in one complete list, does not provide sample sizes/replicates, extract concentrations, controls, detailed protocol, statistical analysis or organism strain identifiers.

## Relationship to SRC-151 and the AAU thesis abstract

The same researcher, Radali Duarah, is associated with:
- SRC-151, the 2023 IJPSR ethnobotanical survey reporting 36 Mising starter plants and a six-community survey;
- the AAU *Post Graduate Thesis 2020–21* compendium abstract, “Bioactivity of medicinal plants used in traditional rice beer starter cultures of Assam,” pages 350–351, which reports a 12-plant laboratory-screening set and more extensive proximate, ABTS/DPPH, phytochemical, FTIR and antimicrobial results.

The conference abstract is thematically and authorship-linked to the thesis research programme. However, the accessible proceedings do not state whether it is a subset, revised analysis or independent experiment from the thesis. Do not count it as an independent study or combine its values with the thesis abstract without full methods and dataset comparison.

The conference abstract spells “Cucurmin longa”; the thesis abstract lists *Curcuma longa*. Preserve the conference spelling as printed and flag the likely transcription discrepancy; do not silently correct the source. Its list of six screened plants is incomplete in the abstract's results paragraph, so no sixth identity is inferred.

## Evidence boundary

These are in-vitro plant-extract screening results, not assays of finished Apong, starter cakes or consumed beverages. They do not establish beverage antimicrobial activity, human therapeutic efficacy, food safety or clinical benefit. Zone-of-inhibition values cannot be compared reliably across studies without matching extract concentrations, methods, strains and controls.

## Retrieval gaps

Obtain the full thesis and laboratory appendices, including plant sample identity/vouchers, extraction concentrations, bacterial strain identifiers, controls, replicate counts, raw zone measurements and statistical analysis. Ask the Nutrition Society of India / presenter for a full paper or poster if one exists. Retain ASM-GAP-067 Open / High until full thesis and voucher records are obtained.
