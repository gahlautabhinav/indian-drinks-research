## Locality finding

The paper describes research in remote villages and reports a multi-community, multi-state study. However, the accessible comparative table identifies community/state and starter/beverage associations rather than a verifiable village-by-community list for these Assam entries. **No village-level place rows should be created from SRC-096 alone.** The study is historical (fieldwork reported for 2008–2009), and should not be used as a present-day prevalence census.

The methods report 440 participants, of whom 242 supplied information across all methods, and state that prior informed consent was obtained. The paper reports 23 plant species used in starter preparation across 11 communities. Those study-wide counts must not be represented as Assam-only counts.

## Cautions and follow-up

- “Adivasi” is too broad to safely map to a particular ethnolinguistic community or district without additional evidence.
- Zu is reported for both Dimasa and Bodo in this paper; that shared name alone does not prove the beverages are identical.
- Nagin-Apong and Poro-Apong are described in relation to preparation/substrate handling; do not automatically split or merge canonical beverage records.
- The Karbi beverage terminology needs corroboration before mapping Arak and Hor Alank as synonyms.
- Plant identifications and historical community labels should be checked against the article tables and contemporary ethnobotanical nomenclature before taxonomic or community-level conclusions.

**Outcome:** improves source-to-claim traceability, but adds no locality rows and no new canonical beverage IDs.
