# Hajang beverages: Jatha Kasa and Ruhi

**Record type:** Preliminary research lead  
**State:** Assam  
**Community label in available sources:** Hajang / Hajong (retain each source's spelling)  
**Canonical beverage records created:** None  
**Status:** Names found in secondary and heritage sources; identity and process require primary-source verification.

## Executive finding

An Assam tourism publication lists **Jatha Kasa** and **Ruhi** as beverages consumed by Hajang people. The publication offers a broad statement about rice beers across Assam, but it does not provide a Hajang-specific recipe, starter culture, fermentation duration, named village, or a clear distinction between the two names. This is a discovery lead, not sufficient evidence for two canonical beverage records.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Assam Tourism, *Aporup Assam* (2022 edition)

The publication's food/cultural section lists: “the Hajang consume JATHA KASA and RUHI.” The surrounding paragraph generalizes that traditional rice beers are made by treating rice, collecting herbs, making offerings, and filtering the beer. That general description is not a documented method for either Hajang beverage individually.

Source PDF: https://publications.assamtourism.gov.in/publications/files/Aporup_Assam_2022.pdf

**Evidence strength:** Low-to-moderate for the fact that these names are reported in a public heritage publication; low for preparation, alias relationships, current practice, and exact geography.

### 2. JETIR review/article (2019)

A 2019 article on traditional alcoholic drinks in Assam lists “Hajong – Ruhi, Ghamani” among community-associated beverage names. It does not, in the accessible passage, provide a recipe or establish whether Ghamani is a synonym, a distinct beverage, or a spelling variant of another name.

Source PDF: https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR1907E44.pdf

**Evidence strength:** Low. Use as a source lead only; the article's methodology and original sources need checking before relying on its classification.

### 3. Contextual ethnobotanical literature

Research on traditional rice beer and starter cakes in Assam documents diverse community-specific fermentation practices, but the sources found in this pass do not directly establish a Hajang-specific starter or process. For example, Deka and Sarma (2010) study Rabha rice-beer starter plants in Goalpara, where Hajang communities also live; that is not evidence for Hajang recipes and must not be transferred across communities.

Deka, D., & Sarma, G. C. (2010). “Traditionally used herbs in the preparation of rice-beer by the Rabha tribe of Goalpara district, Assam.” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge*, 9(3), 459–462. https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9773/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20459-462.pdf

## Questions for further research

1. Locate the primary ethnographic, linguistic, or community-authored source behind the names Jatha Kasa and Ruhi.
2. Check whether *Jatha Kasa* is a single compound name or two separate terms; preserve the source spelling until confirmed.
3. Determine whether Ruhi and Ghamani are synonyms, separate beverages, or source/transliteration variants.
4. Identify ingredients, starter-culture name, preparation process, fermentation and/or distillation status for each beverage separately.
5. Verify localities and community-preferred spellings using Hajang/Hajong community and language sources; do not infer a production village from district-level community presence.
6. Establish ritual, hospitality, or seasonal uses only from sources that explicitly describe Hajang practice.

## Archive decision

Do not add canonical beverage records yet. The available evidence establishes a useful research lead, but not a reproducible preparation dossier or defensible classification. Keep the three names in the gap register and return to this lead when primary sources or community-verified material become available.
