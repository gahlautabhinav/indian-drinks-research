# Ahom–Bodo beverage and starter naming crosswalk

**Audit date:** 2026-10-23  
**Scope:** Cross-source terminology audit for Tai-Ahom Xajpani-family terms and Bodo Jou-family names. This is a controlled crosswalk, not a claim that every similar name is a synonym.

## Decision summary

| Naming family | Terms encountered | Current interpretation | Archive action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tai-Ahom finished beverage | Xajpani / Xaj pani / Xaj-pani | Orthographic/spacing variants strongly overlap in source usage. | Retain as spellings under ASM-008-XAJ, while preserving each source's exact wording. |
| Tai-Ahom finished beverage | Koloh pani | Used alongside Xaj pani in the 2012 survey and in later literature. The sources support an association, but not a full linguistic proof of universal equivalence. | Keep as a documented related name in ASM-008-XAJ with a caveat; do not silently claim universal synonymy. |
| Tai-Ahom finished beverage | Haj pani / Haaj | Used in Ahom sections of some reviews. The relationship to Xajpani/Koloh pani is plausible in the literature but needs Tai-Ahom language/community confirmation. | Keep as a source-attested naming variant/lead, not a fully resolved synonym. |
| Tai-Ahom finished beverage | Sajpani / Laopani / Lao-pani | Saikia et al. (2007) report Sajpani (Tai) / Laopani (Assamese) in a rural Tai-Ahom context; other cultural sources list Luk-Lau / Nam-Lau / Lao-pani. The overlap in names does not establish equivalence with Xajpani. | Keep as a related but unresolved name cluster; do not merge Luk-Lau or Nam-Lau into ASM-008-XAJ without language/source confirmation. |
| Tai-Ahom starter | Vekur pitha, Xaj-pitha / Xajor pitha, Haaj-pitha, Ghai pitha | These are starter-related terms, not beverage names. Ghai pitha is described in a review as a parent/previous starter used to seed another starter. | Keep in the starter/process layer; never add them as beverage aliases. |
| Bodo umbrella tradition | Jou / Jou bishi / Jou-Jumai wording | Jou is used broadly; the sources also use more specific product names and sometimes use Jumai for rice or a process intermediate. | Keep the umbrella concept separate from product records; do not assume Jumai always means the finished beverage. |
| Bodo registered product | Jou Gishi | Official GI name; heritage source describes a fermented rice beverage. | Keep ASM-010-JGI separate; exact legal product boundary still requires the Statement of Case. |
| Bodo registered product | Maibra Jou Bidwi | Official GI name; Maibra is described in field literature as a special rice associated with this product. | Keep ASM-011-JBD separate; do not equate every use of Joubidwi with the GI-defined product without specification comparison. |
| Bodo stored beverage | Joufinai / older finai | 2025 study describes Joufinai as a stored beverage and distinguishes it from direct-fermented Joubidwi; 2014 paper lists finai among Jou types. | Keep Joufinai distinct in the surveyed context; exact relationship to older “finai” remains unresolved. |
| Bodo distilled product | Jou Gwran / Jougwran | Registered GI name and field-study term for a distilled form; published sources also describe fruit-based variants. | Keep ASM-013-JGW distinct from fermented Jou products; preserve substrate variants rather than assume one recipe. |
| Bodo starter culture | Amao / Emao / Angkur | Sources report overlapping starter terminology, but usage and exact equivalence can vary by study/community. | Treat as starter names, not finished-drink aliases; retain Amao/Emao as documented related names and Angkur as a term requiring local confirmation. |

## Evidence basis and caveats

- Tai-Ahom dossier tai-ahom-xajpani.md records Xaj pani/Koloh pani in Das, Deka and Miyaji (2012), Haj pani/Koloh pani in Bhuyan and Baishya (2013), and Sajpani (Tai)/Laopani (Assamese) in Saikia, Tag and Das (2007). It also records the historical Khon Ming manuscript catalogue's ritual use of Luk Lau, but the manuscript's ingredients and its identity relative to Xajpani are not established.
- Bodo dossier bodo-jou.md distinguishes broad Jou usage, the GI-registered product names, stored Joufinai, distilled Jou Gwran, and starter names. The 2014 and 2025 field sources do not by themselves settle the exact legal scope of the GI names.
- Official GI registry pages confirm that Jou Gishi (application 969/certificate 649), Jou Gwran (968/648), and Maibra Jou Bidwi (970/650) are registered names. Registration establishes legal name/status, not universal synonymy among folk terms.
- The GI registry also lists Authorized User addresses. These are useful leads for future provenance review, but an authorized-user address is not automatically a verified brewing/production locality. Do not create village production rows from those addresses alone.

## No-merge rules

1. Never merge starter names into beverage records.
2. Never merge a fermented beverage and its distilled product.
3. Do not merge Luk-Lau/Nam-Lau with Xajpani solely on name similarity or shared cultural context.
4. Do not treat the GI product name as proof that all older folk spellings are equivalent.
5. Do not infer village-level production from GI applicant or authorized-user addresses.
6. When a source uses “Jumai,” inspect its local definition in context before assigning it to a finished beverage.

## Next evidence needed

- A qualified Tai-Ahom language reading of the Khon Ming manuscript passage and community confirmation of Xajpani, Haj/Haaj, Sajpani/Laopani and Luk-Lau/Nam-Lau.
- Text extraction of the official GI Statements of Case for all three Bodo products, compared line-by-line with the 2014 and 2025 field literature.
- Locality-specific community confirmation for Amao/Emao/Angkur and older finai/Joufinai terminology.

**Outcome:** controlled naming relationships documented; no beverage IDs merged and no new locality/production claims added.
