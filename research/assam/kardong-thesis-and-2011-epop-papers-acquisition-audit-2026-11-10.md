# Kardong thesis and 2011 Epop papers — acquisition audit

**Research date:** 2026-11-10  
**Scope:** Locate the 2011 Dibrugarh University thesis and two journal papers already registered as SRC-139 and SRC-140. This is an access/provenance audit, not a reconstruction of missing experimental results.

## 1. Thesis identity confirmed; full text not located

A CSIR-NEIST institutional publication report lists Dr Devid Kardong's PhD as awarded in 2011 by Dibrugarh University, under Dr. T. C. Bora, with the title printed as “Studies on Microbiological and Biochemical aspects of Chaimod(PO:RO): A Traditional Alcoholic Beverage of Mishing Tribes of Assam.”

This confirms the thesis bibliographic identity and award year. It does not expose the thesis abstract, contents, dataset or PDF. Preserve “Chaimod” from this record; do not silently replace it with “Saimod”.

Exact-title and author searches in the available public web index did not locate a thesis record or downloadable full text. Dibrugarh University's own thesis discovery pages direct researchers to Shodhganga, IndCat, KrishiKosh and other repositories. These are acquisition routes, not evidence that the thesis is deposited in any one of them.

## 2. 2011 papers: institutional bibliography confirms citation, not contents

The Dibrugarh University research-publication volume lists:

- **SRC-139:** D. Kardong, “Studies on the Changes of certain Parameters during fermentation of Saimod using Epop: The Traditional Starter for Preparation of Alcoholic beverage by Mishing Tribe of Assam, India,” *Journal of Assam Science Society* 52(2):1–6, ISSN 0587-1921 (2011).
- **SRC-140:** Devid Kardong, Kaushal Sood, R. N. S. Yadav and T. C. Bora, “Amyolytic activity of mold isolated from Epop used by the Mising tribe of Assam, India in preparation of alcoholic Rice beer,” *Bulletin of Life Sciences*, Vol. XVI, ISSN 0971-5746 (2011).

The institutional record spells the second title “Amyolytic”; retain it as bibliographic transcription. Exact-title searches did not recover either full article. No parameters, timepoints, isolate names, enzyme values, assay methods or numerical results are added here.

If full-text supply is restricted, request catalogue identifiers, holding status, scan/document-delivery procedure and permission requirements.

## 4. Evidence boundary retained

- SRC-152 remains the only directly accessible Kardong project report from 2007–2009. Its available five-page version confirms three Epop samples from unnamed places, seven amylolytic isolates, six alcohol-fermenting isolates and candidate strain codes AY-3/amy-3, AY-1(19), AY-4 and FY-10. It is not a substitute for the thesis or either 2011 article.
- A superficially similar 2017 amylolytic-fungi paper is about Bodo *amou* and Deori *perok-kushi* starter cakes. It is not Mising Epop evidence and is excluded from SRC-139/SRC-140 interpretation.
- Until primary texts are obtained, no longitudinal fermentation curve, Mising mould taxonomy or enzyme-activity value can be extracted from SRC-139 or SRC-140.
- No new source IDs, village records, plant records or microbial taxa are created by this acquisition audit.

## 6. Additional catalogue and issue-date checks — 2026-11-10

A second targeted search pass checked exact titles, alternate title wording, ISSN, volume/issue references, the institutional thesis discovery pages and the older Dibrugarh University publication PDF.

### What was newly corroborated
- The university's 2016–2015 assessment-period research-publication volume again lists SRC-140 as Kardong, Sood, Yadav and Bora, “Amyolytic activity of mold isolated from Epop used by the Mising tribe of Assam, India in preparation of alcoholic Rice beer,” *Bulletin of Life Sciences*, Vol. XVI, ISSN 0971-5746, 2011.
- The same volume lists SRC-139 as D. Kardong, “Studies on the Changes of certain Parameters during fermentation of Saimod using Epop,” *Journal of Assam Science Society* 52(2), pp. 1–6, ISSN 0587-1921, 2011.
- The CSIR-NEIST PhD-award list independently confirms Dr Devid Kardong's Dibrugarh PhD in 2011, the title printed as “Studies on Microbiological and Biochemical aspects of Chaimod(PO:RO): A Traditional Alcoholic Beverage of Mishing Tribes of Assam,” and guide Dr T. C. Bora.
- Dibrugarh University's current global thesis page links to NDLTD and OATD; its Indian thesis page links to Shodhganga, IndCat and other repositories. These are discovery routes, not evidence that the thesis is digitised or indexed there.

### Bibliographic discrepancy to retain
A separate institutional profile/search result describes *Journal of Assam Science Society* Vol. 52(2) as an issue edited in 2012. The university research-publication volume lists Kardong's SRC-139 paper in 52(2) as 2011. Until the physical issue masthead or article itself is checked, retain the issue/year conflict as unresolved rather than silently changing SRC-139's registered year.

### Search outcome and access boundary
No publisher-hosted or institutional full text for SRC-139, SRC-140 or the PhD thesis was recovered in this pass. The legacy university report URL printed immediately before the SRC-140 bibliography row in the institutional PDF was tested as a lead but did not resolve through the browser route; the PDF layout does not establish that this generic report URL is a download link for SRC-140, so it is not treated as evidence of article contents. No taxa, enzyme values, fermentation timepoints, sample locations or methods are inferred from the titles.

The current Dibrugarh University library page identifies the Lakshminath Bezbaroa Library as the central information resource centre and publishes the university's general contact route. The practical next step remains a targeted catalogue/document-delivery request for: (1) *Journal of Assam Science Society* 52(2), article pp. 1–6; (2) *Bulletin of Life Sciences* Vol. XVI, 2011, Kardong et al. article; and (3) Devid Kardong's 2011 PhD thesis. Request catalogue record identifiers, holdings, scan/document-delivery procedure and any access conditions.
