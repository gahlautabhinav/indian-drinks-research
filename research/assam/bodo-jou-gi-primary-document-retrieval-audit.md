## Interpretation controls

1. Use 968, 969 and 970 as application IDs. Numbers 648, 649 and 650 are certificate numbers and also appear as serial numbers in the consolidated register; do not label them application IDs.
2. Do not equate the registry’s applicant address with a production locality.
3. Do not infer a complete production boundary from the broad “Assam” field. The official map and case statement must be examined.
4. Do not promote community heritage accounts, ethnographic papers, or lab studies into the GI specification. They may be cross-checks only after primary specifications are extracted.
5. Do not merge Jou Gwran, Jou Gishi and Maibra Jou Bidwi processes, starter cultures, ingredients or naming conventions without product-specific evidence.
6. Keep reported alcohol, pH, starter ratios and distillation claims separate from GI requirements unless the official specification or an appropriately sourced study directly supports the claim.

## Outstanding extraction checklist

For each of applications 968, 969 and 970, obtain and review:
- full Statement of Case, including product definition and claimed link to origin;
- original and amended maps, noting any changes;
- producer / authorised-user list and its date;
- GI Journal entry and registration certificate;
- any amendment that affects the product description, origin boundary or producer eligibility.

Then create a field-by-field crosswalk: official name; product type; raw materials; starter; fermentation/distillation steps; permitted variation; measurable quality characteristics; origin boundary; producer eligibility; evidence citations; discrepancies against SRC-119, SRC-122 and SRC-123.

## Decision

**ASM-GAP-026 stays Open.** The current step improves the traceable inventory of official documents and confirms the registration metadata, but does not satisfy the primary-specification gate. No canonical beverage ingredients, village-level production records, or technical specifications are added on the strength of registry metadata alone.
