# SRC-001 — Sarmah, Pegu & Sanong (2022): Dhansiri Judima recipe and plant-variation audit

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026  
**Canonical source ID:** SRC-001 (the source register already contained this article; no duplicate ID is created)

## Citation
Puja Sarmah, Bhaba Kumar Pegu and James Sanong (2022), “Documentation of Traditional Method of Preparation and Characterization of Judima, a Traditional Rice Beer of Dimasa Tribe of Assam and its Comparison with Old Preserved Judima,” *Biosciences Biotechnology Research Asia* 19(4), pp. 1133–1138. DOI: 10.13005/bbra/3062.
Full text: https://www.biotech-asia.org/download/43690

## Dhansiri fieldwork and sample provenance
- Study area: Dima Hasao district.
- Dhansiri village was selected from the district's reported 695 villages.
- Fieldwork: February 2022; semi-structured local-language face-to-face interviews with 100 local people (65 women, 35 men; ages 28–67), with consent reported.
- Fresh and old preserved Judima were collected from **one family in Dhansiri**, described by the authors as a multigenerational and locally renowned preparer.
- The family is unnamed. The paper does not enumerate producer households or establish village-wide prevalence. The 100 interviewees are not 100 brewers.

## What the article says about the core Humao recipe
The method section describes Humao as rice plus bark of Thempra / *Acacia pennata*. Bark is sun-dried, cut and powdered; soaked rice is ground using a rimin; bark powder and rice are mixed with a little water into dough; cakes are made (reported radius 5–7 cm and weight 80–100 g); 15–20 g of old rice cake is sprinkled over a new cake; cakes are sun-dried for 4–5 days and stored in a cool place.

For the beverage, Bairing or Bora rice is preferred. Cooked rice is cooled on bamboo mats, mixed with crushed Humao (one large cake for about 5 kg rice), placed in earthen or bamboo khulu vessels covered with jute bags, and left for 5–7 days. The article says seasonal conditions affect fermentation time; the liquid is collected, strained and may be diluted.

This is one Dhansiri-linked account, not a universal specification.

## Separate plant-variation statement in the same article
The article separately says its field study found plant parts used as “common growth supplements” in starter-culture preparation, naming:
- *Piper betle*
- *Acacia pennata*
- *Buddleja asiatica*
- *Hedyotis scandens*

It further specifies *Buddleja asiatica* leaves, *Hedyotis scandens* leaves/twigs, and *Acacia pennata* leaves and bark. This broader field statement must not be collapsed into the core Humao cake recipe above. The paper's wording does not provide a household-by-household matrix showing which of these additions were used in the particular Dhansiri sample family.

## Cross-source reconciliation

### SRC-098 — Arjun et al. (2014)
Arjun et al. reports 100 interviewees in North Cachar Hills (79 women, 21 men, ages 30–60), a starter called Umhu/Humao made from rice and Thempra (*Acacia pennata*) bark, with optional flavour/variation plants *Piper betle*, *Buddleja asiatica* and *Hedyotis scandens*. It reports collection of plant material from different North Cachar Hills locations and vouchers JA-1 to JA-4 deposited at Lumding College. The four voucher labels are not mapped to a household, Judima batch or village in the accessible text. This source is a strong comparator for the Dhansiri article's four-species field list, but does not make the Dhansiri household's exact plant combination independently certain.

### SRC-002 — Jamal et al. (2022), Dibarai survey
Its preparation flowchart shows Thembra (*Acacia pennata*) bark and rice, but the authors identify the flowchart as adapted from Arjun et al. (2014). It is not a direct observation of the approximately 50 Dibarai respondents preparing Humao. Do not treat the diagram as a separate Dibarai household recipe.

### SRC-020 — Loying et al. (2024) review
The review's Judima section describes Humao/Umhu with *Acacia pennata*, *Hedyotis diffusa* and *Solanum ferox*. This differs from the primary Arjun et al. account, which names *Hedyotis scandens* and does not list *Solanum ferox* in the same variation-plant list. The review is secondary and does not assign these plants to a named village or household.

Kew's Plants of the World Online treats *Hedyotis scandens* as a synonym of the accepted name *Dimetia scandens*, while *Hedyotis diffusa* is treated as a synonym of *Scleromitrion diffusum*. These are not the same accepted species. Therefore the two published names must not be silently normalised into one plant. Community vernacular names, specimens and original author identifications are still needed.

### SRC-306 — Anupma et al. (2018), Emao/Humao
The article states that Emao is prepared by the Bodo community and Dimasa call it Humao. However, the detailed recipe under the Emao/Humao heading describes Emao preparation using glutinous rice, banana leaves, locally named lwkwna, *Scoparia dulcis* and *Albizia myriophylla* bark, with old Emao as inoculum. This multi-community synthesis does not establish that this exact botanical combination is used by Dimasa households making Judima. Its Assam field locations (Kokrajhar, Jorhat, Sivasagar and Moran) are not allocated to the Humao recipe, and “lwkwna” remains unresolved.

### SRC-049 — NIPCCD government report
The report documents two Dimasa entries:
- Maiju hanger Judima (red rice), using red rice maiju hanger mairung and Xumai.
- Judima (white rice), using sticky maijumairung plus non-sticky rice and Xumai.

Both entries describe thembra powder mixed with rice flour at 1:15, kneaded with water, covered with powdered yeast cake and sun-dried for 4–5 days. Cooked rice is mixed with powdered Xumai and fermented in a dark room for three days; the fermented rice is called Zukap, and the liquor extracted from it is Judima.

The report's general methodology used interviews with women heads of household selected through local workers/leaders and photographs of foods/processes. It documents 89 foods across eight Assam tribes, but does not give a village, district, respondent identity or sample allocation for the two Judima entries. Preserve Xumai and Zukap as the report's spellings; do not equate Xumai with Humao solely because both are starter cakes, or merge its Zukap with similarly spelled terms in unrelated process accounts.

## Archive decisions
1. Keep SRC-001 as the canonical record for Sarmah et al. (2022); do not create duplicate source IDs.
2. Keep Dhansiri as a field-survey and beverage-sampling/preparation locality; do not add a named producer or producer count.
3. Preserve the core recipe and additional plant-variation statement as separate claims.
4. Preserve all source-specific terms (Humao, Umhu, Emao, Xumai, Zukap) until Dimasa-speaker validation.
5. Keep ASM-GAP-002 Open. Priority evidence: original field schedules and plant vouchers, taxonomic determination sheets, source-to-village/sample mapping, and community review of local starter terminology and plant names.
