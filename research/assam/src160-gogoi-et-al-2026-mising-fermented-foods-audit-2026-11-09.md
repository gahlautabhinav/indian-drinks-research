# SRC-160 — 2026 Mising fermented foods and beverages field study audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-09  
**Source:** Gogoi, P.; Konwar, S.; Das, S.; Das, D.; Doimari, R.; Sonar, T.; Doley, S. (2026). “Ethnobotanical documentation of medicinal plants and traditional fermented foods of the Mishing community in Jorhat district of Assam, India.” *Ethnobotany Research and Applications* 34:75, pp. 1–31. DOI: https://doi.org/10.32859/era.34.75.1-31  
**Related archive IDs:** SRC-148, SRC-151, SRC-153; ASM-003-POR; ASM-002-NOG; ASM-GAP-067; new ASM-GAP-069.

## 1. Study design and geographic resolution

The article reports:
- field surveys in **10 villages in Jorhat district** between March 2024 and 2025;
- **50 key informants**;
- semi-structured questionnaires, individual interviews and focus-group discussions;
- collection and taxonomic identification of plants using regional floras and online nomenclatural resources.

The accessible article text does not provide the ten village names, a village-by-informant register, beverage-specific informant counts, or a plant-by-village occurrence matrix. It therefore supports a Jorhat-district field-study lead, not ten village-level beverage production records.

### Voucher limitation

The authors explicitly state that they did not collect or deposit voucher specimens because of technical/logistical constraints. No herbarium accession numbers or herbarium acronyms are supplied. The botanical identifications cannot be independently rechecked against deposited vouchers from this study.

## 2. Beverage-specific information

The paper documents two Mising rice-based beverages:
- Po:ro Apong
- Nogin Apong

It describes Apop/Apopitha as the starter cake. The text also uses “opo” or “sajpani” in the Po:ro subsection. Preserve these as the authors' spellings/terminology; do not silently normalize them to Epop/E’pob or treat “sajpani” as a beverage name.

**Important allocation caveat:** the conclusion says that 20 plant species were used to prepare starter cultures for fermented beverages “such as Po:ro and Nogin Apong” collectively. The text does not supply a separate, fully cross-tabulated plant list for each beverage or identify which plants were used by which household. Although the 20 names appear in the Po:ro subsection, they must not be interpreted as exclusive to Po:ro, as universal across all Jorhat Mising households, or as a village-by-village inventory.

The article's broader medicinal-plant inventory (94 species) is not the same thing as the beverage-starter inventory. Do not import all 94 medicinal taxa into the Apop starter list.

## 3. Process account and comparison with earlier Mising studies

The article describes Po:ro preparation using partially burnt paddy husk and dried paddy-straw ash mixed with boiled rice and powdered starter cake. It describes fermentation in a vessel and subsequent water extraction/filtration through a bamboo basket.

This is broadly consistent with the ash-plus-water-extraction family of accounts in:
- SRC-148 (Borkotoky, Neog & Barua; five Jorhat villages, 2011–2012 collection; 51 plants claimed);
- SRC-147 (Pegu et al. 2013; Mising communities in Dhemaji/Majuli and East Siang; Ta:suk/Korai terminology);
- SRC-084 and other Mising process sources, which must remain separately attributed.

The 2026 paper's process is not a complete controlled recipe: exact rice/starter/ash proportions, fermentation duration, temperature, batch count and village-specific variation are not adequately established in the accessible text. It should not be used to overwrite SRC-148's 15–20 day account or SRC-147's seasonal timings.

For Nogin Apong, the paper describes cooked rice mixed with powdered Apop, fermentation in an earthen pot, addition of water, stirring and sieving. It reports a usual 7–10 day period. Treat this as the authors' account, not a universal Mising schedule.

## 4. Claims not to promote into evidence

- The article does not report laboratory microbiology, metabolomics, alcohol concentration or biochemical testing of the beverage batches.
- Traditional medicinal/health claims and claims about nutritional or probiotic potential are not clinical evidence and were not tested in this study.
- The article's broad medicinal-plant dataset cannot be treated as a measured composition or health effect of finished Apong.

## 5. Cross-study comparison and research significance

This source gives a useful contemporary fieldwork layer (2024–2025) that complements older Jorhat evidence:
- SRC-148 reports five named collection villages and 51 starter-plant species, with vouchers said to be held at Kakojan College Herbarium but no accession numbers; its Table 1 remains unreliably exposed in the digital text.
- SRC-151 reports 36 distinct numbered Mising starter entries in a 2023 survey, while its broader survey total is 51 plants across six communities.
- SRC-153 reports 31 ÉPOP taxa from 15 selected Sivasagar villages, but the full village frame, voucher accessions and village-by-taxon matrix remain unavailable.
- SRC-160 reports 20 starter plants in the beverage discussion, ten unnamed Jorhat villages and 50 informants, but explicitly has no voucher deposit and no plant-by-village or beverage-by-household crosswalk.

The totals **51, 36, 31 and 20 are not directly comparable prevalence estimates**. They differ in geography, study design, date, scope and likely plant-selection criteria. They must not be added together or treated as a single canonical Mising starter inventory. The 20 taxa in SRC-160 may overlap heavily with prior lists; a taxonomic synonym and plant-part reconciliation is required before counting unique taxa.

## 6. Next evidence requests

1. Obtain the ten village names and fieldwork sampling register.
2. Request the 50-informant beverage-specific matrix and clarify which informants/households prepared Po:ro, Nogin or both.
3. Request the raw starter-plant list with local names, plant parts, voucher/photographic evidence and taxonomic determinations.
4. Clarify the use of “opo”, “sajpani”, “Apop” and “Apopitha” with the authors and Mising knowledge holders.
5. Request batch-level process notes for rice, ash, starter proportions, fermentation duration and water extraction.
6. Crosswalk the 20 reported taxa against SRC-148's claimed 51, SRC-151's 36, and SRC-153's 31, preserving author spellings and uncertainty.

**Archive decision:** no new place rows or plant rows are created from this article at this stage. The district-level study lead and a dedicated open gap are sufficient until the missing allocation and identity data are obtained.
