# SRC-287 Sharma & Pegu (2011) — full-text audit

**Citation:** Sharma, U. K. & Pegu, S. (2011). “Ethnobotany of religious and supernatural beliefs of the Mising tribes of Assam with special reference to the 'Dobur Uie'.” *Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine* 7, article 16. DOI: [10.1186/1746-4269-7-16](https://doi.org/10.1186/1746-4269-7-16).  
**Full text:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1746-4269-7-16  
**Accessed:** 2026-11-17.

## 1. Research scope and method

The article studies plants associated with Mising Dobur Uie ritual and reports 30 plants in 23 families. The map and methods identify eight Assam districts: Sonitpur, Lakhimpur, Dhemaji, Dibrugarh, Tinsukia, Sibsagar, Jorhat and Golaghat. The authors say the selected areas followed mopping-up surveys in 2009–2010.

Methods describe repeated field trips, interviews with hamlet headmen, Mibus, healers and elderly people, field identification of plants with informants, and herbarium voucher preparation/deposition at Dhemaji College Herbarium (DCH). The authors specifically state that Mising women were interviewed about plant species used in Apong preparation in different areas.

**Geographic limit:** The article does not provide a village-by-village sampling frame or plant-to-village allocation. The inclusion of historical Sibsagar district is not enough to assign any observation to present-day Charaideo, Ramnagar, Gumutha or Balikhuti. Preserve this as Assam-wide / multi-district Mising evidence only.

## 2. Apong and Dobur Uie ritual

The article describes Apong as a rice beer offered to spirits, sprinkled on the Dobur Uie altar and ritual participants, and collected from households with rice, wild vegetables, chicken and pork as Ajenge dues. It states that Apong is prepared in every household and that about 10–15 plants are used in Apong preparation “now a days,” varying by place and plant availability; it contrasts this with a historical figure of 50 plants. Treat these as the authors' reported community-level statements, not a measured prevalence estimate or a universal recipe.

**Important correction:** *Acorus calamus* appears in the article's Dobur Uie plant inventory, but its entry describes ritual-site use and medicinal uses; it does **not** identify it as an Apong-preparation ingredient. The explicit Dhemaji Apong-preparation tuber entry is *Asparagus racemosus*. Do not conflate these taxa.

The article also describes rice straw being burned to produce “Chhai Mod” (ash liquor) and used to filter Apong. This is a separate beverage/process note; it does not establish that Chhai Mod is Apong or that it is produced in Charaideo.

## 4. Keep the two plant datasets separate

- **Dobur Uie ritual inventory:** 30 species in 23 families, including plants used at the altar, in ritual structures, as offerings, and in related ritual foods.
- **Apong-preparation list:** the 16 taxa above are those whose individual entries explicitly mention Apong preparation in the article. Some taxa overlap with ritual use, but the two categories are not interchangeable.
- The article's “Local status” field is defined with reference to availability of plants used in Dobur Uie in the local research areas. Do not automatically treat those labels as Apong ingredient availability or assign them to a named district/village.

## 5. Research use for Charaideo / Ramnagar

This source is valuable for:
1. Establishing Mising Apong's reported ritual role in Dobur Uie.
2. Identifying a multi-district set of plant taxa that the authors say are used in Apong preparation.
3. Directing future questions to Mising women and local knowledge holders about plant selection, parts, seasonality and local variation.

It does **not** establish:
- Apong production or ritual use in Ramnagar, Gumutha, Balikhuti or any other named Charaideo locality;
- which of the 16 taxa are used in Charaideo;
- a single standardized Apong formula or starter-cake composition;
- a plant-to-village sampling map;
- present-day prevalence or continuity of any practice.

No beverage record, Charaideo place row, plant allocation or starter-culture record is created from this paper. Keep ASM-GAP-120 open pending locality-resolved evidence for Ramnagar.
