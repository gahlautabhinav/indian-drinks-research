# Assam Apong microbiology crosswalk — starter cultures vs finished beverages

**Research date:** 2026-11-09  
**Scope:** Mising Apop/Apong starter microbiology and comparative Assam culture studies. This is a literature crosswalk, not a microbial census.

## Executive findings

The available studies support at least three distinct evidence layers:

1. **Mising Apop/Apong starter cake:** a Mising-specific study reports *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* as dominant in collected Mising fermentative cakes from three named Dhemaji localities (Kaitong, Phatiabam and Jiadhal). The isolate MIC5X is linked to DDBJ accession LC011412. This is culture/isolate evidence from starter material, not a finding about finished Apong liquid.
2. **Multi-community Assam starter cultures:** Saikia et al. (2019) cultured yeasts from starter materials labelled xaj, apong and suzen. The paper reports *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* and *Candida* spp.; its tables give district-level occurrence for selected isolates, but do not provide a reliable sample-to-community/starter-to-district crosswalk.
3. **Later culture plus molecular characterization:** Saikia et al. (2024; existing SRC-108) studied nine starter inocula labelled Xaaj pitha, Apong and Suzen/mod pitha, isolated 16 yeast strains and two filamentous fungi, and sequenced representative yeast strains. It does not allocate the nine samples or taxa to individual communities, beverage subtypes or villages.

Separately, Das et al. (2019; SRC-087) studied **finished rice-beer samples**, including Poro Apong RB01 and Nogin Apong RB02/RB03. Their 16S rDNA sequencing characterizes bacterial DNA in the beverages, not starter-cake yeasts or moulds. These datasets must not be merged.

## 1. Mising-specific starter-cake evidence — Parasar et al. (2020) follow-on isolate characterization

**Citation:** Parasar, D. P., Ramakrishnan, E., Kabilan, S., Kotoky, J. & Sarma, H. K. (2020). “Characterization of β-Cryptoxanthin and Other Carotenoid Derivatives from *Rhodotorula taiwanensis*, A Novel Yeast Isolated from Traditional Starter Culture of Assam.” *Chemistry & Biodiversity* 17(12), e2000198. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202000198. PMID: 33094905.

**Sample and locality details recoverable from the author-shared full-text preview:**
- Starter cakes were collected from densely populated Mising areas in Dhemaji district.
- Table 1 names Kaitong (27.39°N, 94.57°E), Phatiabam (27.39°N, 94.55°E) and Jiadhal (27.45°N, 94.50°E).
- The paper describes *R. taiwanensis* as dominant in all collected Mising starter cakes.
- One isolate, laboratory code MIC5X, was identified by ITS rDNA sequencing; DDBJ accession LC011412; approximate sequence length 141 bp as printed in the table.
- The 2020 article is a follow-on characterization of a yeast isolate, not an independent finished-beverage survey. It investigates pigments from the isolate (including β-carotene, β-cryptoxanthin, torulene and torularhodin).

**Evidence boundary:** The article supports occurrence of the isolate in Mising starter cakes sampled at these localities. It does not show that *R. taiwanensis* is dominant in finished Nogin or Po:ro Apong, that every household uses it, or that the three villages represent all of Dhemaji. The 2021 Frontiers review attributes the earlier community comparison to Parasar et al. (2017); the 2020 pigment paper is linked to the same isolate LC011412 and should not be double-counted as an independent field survey.

## 2. Assam multi-community starter-culture yeasts — Saikia et al. (2019)

**Citation:** Saikia, B., Ali, M. S., Nath, P. D. & Dey, P. C. (2019). “Exploring the Yeast Diversity of Common Alcoholic Beverages of Assam.” *International Journal of Current Microbiology and Applied Sciences* 8(5):1519–1527. DOI: https://doi.org/10.20546/ijcmas.2019.805.175.

**Methods and results:**
- Starter materials labelled xaj, apong and suzen were collected from Ahom, Mising and Deori communities.
- The abstract reports 32 yeasts from 12 starter cultures and six districts; the body says samples came from seven districts (adding Karbi Anglong), and the results state 28 yeasts identified. Preserve these internal count/scope discrepancies rather than choosing one figure.
- Culture-dependent isolation used PDA with streptomycin, followed by YEPD purification and morphological/micromorphological identification. No molecular sequencing is reported for the isolates in this article.
- The paper identifies *S. cerevisiae* as dominant across samples; *S. fibuligera* is reported in Jorhat, Golaghat and Sivasagar; *Candida tropicalis* is identified in Sivasagar, with other *Candida* spp. recorded in Jorhat, Sivasagar and Dhemaji.
- The article's locality tables are organized by district, not by community or starter type. It is therefore unsafe to claim, for example, that every district-level isolate listed was recovered specifically from Mising Apop rather than another community's starter.

**Evidence boundary:** Useful comparative Assam starter-culture evidence; not a Mising-only microbial profile. The study does not characterize finished beverage samples or provide village names.

## 3. Assam starter-culture fungi and molecularly characterized yeast — Saikia et al. (2024), existing SRC-108

**Citation:** Saikia, B., Ali, M. S., Gogoi, S. H. & Nath, P. D. (2024). “Isolation and Characterization of the Mycofloral Diversity in Traditional Assamese Alcoholic Fermentation from India.” *Asian Journal of Dairy and Food Research* 43(1):104–110. DOI: https://doi.org/10.18805/ajdfr.DR-1829.

**Methods and results:**
- Nine starter inoculum samples were collected in 2019–20; the paper names Xaaj pitha, Apong and Suzen/mod pitha. The abstract lists seven districts, whereas the body says six districts. No sample-by-district or sample-by-community matrix is published in the accessible article.
- Starter cakes were ground and serially diluted; yeast extract peptone dextrose (YEPD), potato dextrose agar (PDA) and V8 agar supplemented with streptomycin were used at 25±1°C for 48 hours. This is culture-dependent isolation, with bacterial growth deliberately suppressed.
- Sixteen yeast strains: nine *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, three *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera*, and four *Candida tropicalis*. The article says *S. cerevisiae* was dominant in all nine samples.
- Two filamentous fungi were identified as *Aspergillus oryzae* and *A. niger*. The paper describes *A. oryzae* confirmation by the National Centre for Fungal Taxonomy (NCFT), ID 2001.17.
- Representative yeast strains were sequenced using ITS primers ITS1/ITS4. NCBI accessions: MK110643 (*S. cerevisiae*), MK110642 (*S. fibuligera*), MK110644 (*C. tropicalis*).
- The article's abstract says 16 yeast isolates and two fungi “from rice beer and starter cultures”, but its methods/results describe the 16 yeast strains as isolated from nine starter cultures. Retain this wording ambiguity.

**Evidence boundary:** The species are associated with the pooled set of Assam starter inocula, not proven for every Mising Apop sample or for finished Apong. Because the article used media with streptomycin to suppress bacteria, it cannot be used to infer absence of lactic acid bacteria.

## 4. Finished Apong beverage microbiota — Das et al. (2019), existing SRC-087

**Citation:** Das, S., Deb, D., Adak, A. & Khan, M. R. (2019). “Exploring the microbiota and metabolites of traditional rice beer varieties of Assam and their functionalities.” *3 Biotech* 9:174. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-019-1702-z.

**Sample design and findings:**
- 27 finished-beverage samples from nine villages were collected across Mising, Ahom and Bodo communities; triplicate samples from each location were pooled into nine composite samples.
- Mising samples: RB01 = Poro Apong; RB02 and RB03 = Nogin Apong.
- 16S rDNA next-generation sequencing characterized bacterial communities in finished beverages. Eighteen core bacterial genera were reported, with lactic acid bacteria dominant overall; *Lactobacillus* predominated in most samples, while *Weissella* dominated RB03 (Nogin) and *Pediococcus* dominated one Xaaj sample.
- The paper also reported bacterial genera such as *Paracoccus*, *Enterococcus*, *Olivibacter* and *Cellulosimicrobium* in RB01 (Poro Apong), and *Vibrio* and *Pseudomonas* among detected genera in Nogin samples. DNA detection is not equivalent to viable-cell enumeration or a pathogen-risk diagnosis.

**Evidence boundary:** These are finished-drink bacterial DNA profiles, not starter-cake fungal/yeast profiles. Sample-to-village identities remain unresolved because the supplementary sample table has not been recovered. Do not infer that the starter cake contains the same bacterial profile or that all detections represent viable organisms.

## 5. Cross-study interpretation

| Material / stage | Evidence type | Organisms or signal | What can be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mising starter cakes, Dhemaji | Culture/isolate + ITS identification | *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* (MIC5X; LC011412) | Reported in Mising starter cakes sampled at Kaitong, Phatiabam and Jiadhal |
| Multi-community Assam starter cakes (2019) | Culture-dependent morphology | *S. cerevisiae*, *S. fibuligera*, *Candida* spp. | Yeasts found across a mixed set of xaj/apong/suzen starters; community-level attribution incomplete |
| Assam starter cakes (2024) | Culture-dependent + representative ITS sequencing | *S. cerevisiae*, *S. fibuligera*, *C. tropicalis*, *A. oryzae*, *A. niger* | Species associated with the study's nine starter inocula; sample-level attribution unavailable |
| Finished Apong (2019) | 16S rDNA amplicon sequencing | LAB-dominated bacterial profiles | Bacterial DNA profile of three Mising finished-beverage composites (one Poro, two Nogin) |

### Important limitations
- Culture-dependent studies recover organisms able to grow under the selected media, temperature and incubation conditions; they do not capture the full microbial community.
- 16S sequencing targets bacterial DNA and does not characterize yeasts or moulds. DNA detection alone does not establish viability.
- ITS isolate sequencing confirms selected representative cultures; it is not shotgun metagenomics of the entire starter community.
- Starter cake and finished beverage are different substrates/stages. Do not merge their organism lists.
- No reviewed study provides a longitudinal stage-by-stage microbial succession profile for Mising Apong.
- The 2024 article's six/seven-district and nine-sample design discrepancies, and the 2019 article's 28/32 isolate and six/seven-district discrepancies, remain unresolved.
- Do not convert detections of *Candida tropicalis*, *Vibrio* or *Pseudomonas* into a food-safety verdict without organism-level viability, strain, toxin/pathogenicity and exposure evidence.

## 7. Primary six-community study — Parasar, Sarma & Kotoky (2017), new SRC-164

**Citation:** Parasar, D. P., Sarma, H. K. & Kotoky, J. (2017). “Exploring the Genealogy and Phenomic Divergences of Indigenous Domesticated Yeasts Cultivated by Six Ethnic Communities of Assam, India.” *Journal of Biological Sciences* 17(2):91–105. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3923/jbs.2017.91.105. Full text: https://scialert.net/fulltext/?doi=jbs.2017.91.105.

**Sampling and methods verified in publisher full text:**
- Starter cakes collected in six districts: Sibsagar, Dhemaji, Karbi-Anglong, Goalpara, Kokrajhar and Kamrup.
- Communities: Ahom (Sibsagar), Mising (Dhemaji), Karbi (Karbi Anglong), Rabha (Goalpara), Bodo (Kokrajhar) and Kachari (Kamrup).
- Cakes shade-dried for two days; 1 g homogenized in 10 mL distilled water, diluted 10⁻³ and plated on YPD agar with chloramphenicol and chlorotetracycline; incubated at 30°C. Cultures were subcultured five times.
- 64 isolates reported from the six-community starter collection; 31 selected for molecular identification, yielding nine species across seven genera. The 31 were distributed as Bodo 10, Rabha 6, Karbi 6, Kachari 4, Ahom 3 and Mising 2.
- Molecular identification used ITS1/ITS4 primers and sequencing; article says accessions are in Tables 2 and 3. The article's detailed table images are not machine-readable in the accessible text extraction, so this pass does not transcribe accession-to-isolate mapping beyond the MIC5X record independently linked to LC011412 in the 2020 follow-on article.

**Mising-specific result:** the two Mising isolates selected for molecular identification are MIB4X and MIC5X. Figure legend identifies MIC5X as *Rhodotorula taiwanensis*; the paper's text identifies MIB4X as *Wickerhamomyces anomalus*. Thus the primary paper supports both species in the Mising starter-cake sample set. This corrects the oversimplified “Rhodotorula only” summary sometimes repeated in secondary literature.

**Important internal inconsistency:** the abstract says *W. anomalus* dominated starter cultures across all six communities except Ahom; the results section says it occurred across all six except Ahom; but one discussion sentence says “absence of specific fermenting yeast” in Mising starters. The isolate mapping in the results identifies MIB4X as *W. anomalus*, so the discussion sentence appears inconsistent with the paper's own results. Preserve this as a source contradiction rather than deleting either statement.

**Evidence limits:** the paper studies starter cakes, not finished Apong. It uses selective media that inhibit LAB, so it cannot characterize the full starter microbiome or establish absence of bacteria. It reports district-level community attribution, but the table images need high-resolution inspection before a specific village can be assigned to MIC5X or MIB4X. The 64 cultured isolates and 31 molecularly identified isolates are not interchangeable denominators.

## 9. SRC-165 — LAB isolates from Assam rice beer and starter materials (Das et al., 2019)

**Primary source:** Arup Jyoti Das, Manas Jyoti Das, Tatsuro Miyaji & Sankar Chandra Deka, “Growth and metabolic characterization of four lactic acid bacteria species isolated from rice beer prepared in Assam, India,” *Access Microbiology* 1(4):e000028 (published 29 May 2019), DOI 10.1099/acmi.0.000028; PMID 32974521; PMCID PMC7470291.

### Sampling and isolate table

The methods say rice beer and starter cakes were collected from households of different tribal communities in Assam, in replicates, transported refrigerated and stored at 4°C. The article does not provide village/district sampling locations or household-level provenance. LAB were cultured on MRS agar with CaCO3 and bromocresol purple using a double-layer technique; identification used 16S rRNA sequencing.

Table 1 explicitly reports the following Mising rows:

| Source label | Community | Isolate | 16S accession |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apong | Mising | *Lactobacillus casei* TEZU262 | KR781613 |
| Apop-pitha | Mising | *Pediococcus pentosaceus* TEZU481 | KT273331 |
| Apop-pitha | Mising | *Pediococcus pentosaceus* TEZU482 | KT273330 |
| Apop-pitha | Mising | *Pediococcus pentosaceus* TEZU486 | KT273328 |

The remaining Table 1 entries document LAB from Dimasa (Umhu, Judima), Karbi (Thap, Hor-alank), Deori (Perok-kushi, Sujen), Ahom (Mod-pitha, Xajpani) and Bodo (Amou, Jou-bishi). All 14 table rows and accessions should be retained as article-level culture records, not converted to village-level production records.

### Internal discrepancy to preserve

The results prose states three *L. casei*, one *L. pentosus*, one *L. plantarum* and eight *P. pentosaceus* strains (13 total). Table 1 actually lists 14 isolate rows and four *L. casei* entries: TEZU309, TEZU468, TEZU262 and TEZU374. Do not silently harmonize the prose to the table. The table's combined heading “Rice beer/starter cake name” also does not make the biological material type unambiguous for every row.

### Interpretation limits

The work is culture-dependent and targets LAB; it is not a comprehensive microbiome survey. The Mising *L. casei* and *P. pentosaceus* detections are tied to the paper's labels “Apong” and “Apop-pitha,” respectively, but no named village, district, household, fermentation stage or precise source material is supplied for each isolate. Do not equate Apop-pitha starter cake with finished Apong. The paper's acid/bile tolerance, antibiosis and antioxidant assays are in-vitro strain characterizations, not clinical evidence of probiotic efficacy, treatment benefit, or safety in consumers.

### Cross-source relevance

This study complements SRC-087, which used 16S rDNA sequencing on pooled finished beverages, and SRC-162/SRC-164, which examine yeast isolates from starter materials. Its Mising LAB strains are not direct confirmation of the bacterial profile in SRC-087's RB01–RB03 composites because the sources, samples and methods differ. Keep the evidence layers separate: culture-isolated LAB from this paper; bacterial DNA profiles from SRC-087; and starter-cake yeast isolates from SRC-162/164.


## 10. Starter-cake bacterial evidence check — 2026-11-09

### Finding

A targeted search for bacterial-community studies of Mising Apop-pitha / E’pob did not identify a publicly accessible primary study that comprehensively profiles bacteria in the Mising starter cake itself. This is a bounded literature-search result, not proof that no such work exists.

The strongest directly relevant bacterial evidence remains SRC-165 (Das et al., 2019): culture-dependent LAB isolates associated in Table 1 with Mising labels Apong and Apop-pitha. The Apop-pitha isolates are *Pediococcus pentosaceus* TEZU481, TEZU482 and TEZU486 (accessions KT273331, KT273330 and KT273328); the Apong isolate is *Lactobacillus casei* TEZU262 (KR781613). The article does not supply locality/household-level provenance or clearly separate the biological material type for every row under its combined “rice beer/starter cake name” heading.

SRC-164 (Parasar et al., 2017) provides culture-dependent yeast evidence from Mising starter cakes, including *Rhodotorula taiwanensis* MIC5X and *Wickerhamomyces anomalus* MIB4X. It is not a bacterial survey. SRC-087 (Das et al., 2019) provides 16S rDNA bacterial profiles from pooled finished-beverage samples RB01–RB03 (Poro/Nogin Apong), not from E’pob starter cake. SRC-087's S1/S2 supplementary tables remain inaccessible in the reviewed public route.

### Excluded near-matches

- The 2016 whole-genome-shotgun metagenomics study of Xaj-pitha (PMC4947050) is explicitly an Ahom starter-cake study. Its LAB and other bacterial taxa cannot be attributed to Mising E’pob.
- The 2019 microbial-quality study of Xaj-pitha (PMC6977804) concerns Ahom starter and fermentation, not Mising starter.
- The 2024 E’pob HRLC-MS article (SRC-114) profiles chemical/spectral annotations in a starter-cake sample; it does not identify bacterial taxa.
- The 2021 yeast review (SRC-106) summarizes starter-cake yeasts and identifies Mising apong kusure/apop pitha with *R. taiwanensis* and *W. anomalus*; it is secondary yeast evidence, not bacterial community data.

### Evidence-layer decision

Keep three datasets distinct:
1. **Starter cake:** Mising yeast isolates (SRC-164) and LAB isolate rows associated with Apop-pitha (SRC-165); no comprehensive bacterial starter-cake microbiome established.
2. **Fermentation stage:** no Mising Apong stage-resolved bacterial time series located in this pass.
3. **Finished beverage:** pooled Mising Poro/Nogin 16S profiles (SRC-087) and culture-dependent Apong-associated LAB isolate row (SRC-165), with distinct sampling designs and no direct sample linkage.

Do not infer that the Apop-pitha *P. pentosaceus* strains are present in every starter, that they dominate starter cakes, or that finished Apong inherits the same measured community. Next useful primary-source targets are the original sample provenance/isolate records for SRC-165 and any unpublished thesis/project on Apong-Pitha microbial composition; search leads alone must not be promoted to source records without a retrievable primary document.


## 11. Mising Apong biochemical composition — primary evidence review

### SRC-087: Das et al. (2019), finished-beverage comparison

The 2019 *3 Biotech* study analysed nine pooled location composites from 27 finished-beverage samples. Mising samples were RB01 (Poro Apong), RB02 and RB03 (Nogin Apong). The sample-specific table reports:

| Measure | RB01 Poro | RB02 Nogin | RB03 Nogin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alcohol (% v/v) | 10.67 ± 1.02 | 9.99 ± 0.40 | 10.37 ± 0.57 |
| DPPH scavenging (mg ascorbic-acid equivalent/ml) | 3.01 ± 0.18 | 3.47 ± 0.06 | 3.17 ± 0.08 |
| ABTS scavenging (mg ascorbic-acid equivalent/ml) | 2.04 ± 0.06 | 3.23 ± 0.03 | 1.80 ± 0.03 |
| Total phenolics (mg gallic-acid equivalent/ml) | 2.99 ± 1.05 | 2.80 ± 0.21 | 2.07 ± 0.25 |

These are reported means ± SEM in the paper. The table's significance-letter annotations must be consulted before making pairwise statistical claims. The study pooled three samples per location, so these values represent composite samples, not independent household-level variation.

GC–MS Table 3 lists detected compounds across the rice-beer sample set, including saccharides (e.g., glucose, fructose, maltose, trehalose), organic acids (e.g., lactic, acetic, butanoic, propanoic and gluconic acids), amino acids (e.g., glycine, proline, serine, alanine, valine, leucine and glutamic acid), and other compounds. The accessible table presents a combined list; it does not provide a sample-by-sample concentration matrix in the extracted text. Therefore do not claim that every listed metabolite occurs in every Poro or Nogin sample, or that one subtype has a greater concentration without the full quantitative data.

The article reports 18 bacterial genera and says LAB form the dominant group overall (about 90% abundance). This is an overall statement across the nine composites, not a Poro-vs-Nogin-specific quantitative ranking. Functional pathways were inferred using PICRUSt from 16S data; these are predictions, not direct metabolite flux measurements.

### SRC-166: Kumari et al. (2024), Sai Mod/Poro Apong and Rohi

Publisher page: *Systems Microbiology and Biomanufacturing* 4(1):56–65, DOI 10.1007/s43393-023-00188-x; online 14 October 2024. The publisher labels the item “Review,” although its abstract describes a current study and reports measured physicochemical and analytical results. The full text was not accessible in this pass.

The abstract reports pH 3.72 ± 0.22 and alcohol 35% v/v for Rohi; pH 3.86 ± 0.43 and alcohol 26% v/v for Sai Mod. It also reports moisture, volatile acidity, total soluble sugar/protein, crude fat, DPPH, phenolics, flavonoids and mineral measurements. The abstract's ordering is explicit: first Rohi, then Sai Mod for the paired values in its first sentence; the second sentence reports the same order for values “for Sai and Rohi mods respectively,” which creates an apparent ordering ambiguity. Do not assign the paired values beyond the explicit sequence without checking the full paper's tables. The reported 26–35% v/v alcohol values are substantially higher than SRC-087's approximately 10% for Apong; this difference may reflect sample/process definition or analytical/reporting issues and must not be reconciled by assumption. The abstract also prints total soluble sugar and protein in µg/ml, units that require full-text verification.

### Cross-study cautions and archive decision

- SRC-087 is the best sample-labelled quantitative comparison currently available for Poro and Nogin finished beverages.
- SRC-166 is a relevant additional source, but its abstract alone is insufficient to harmonize values, assign all measurements securely, or establish comparability.
- Do not merge Sai Mod with every Poro Apong process or equate Rohi with Mising Nogin.
- Do not infer clinical antioxidant benefit from in-vitro DPPH/ABTS or total-phenolic assays.
- No stage-resolved fermentation chemistry or time-series metabolomics was identified in this search pass.


## 12. Po:ro Apong fermentation process and experimental evidence

### SRC-136 — Kardong et al. (2012): household process and stage observations

The article reports Mising household samples from Dibrugarh district. Its process description is unusually specific: approximately 100 g powdered Epop per 3 kg cooked rice; rice is coated with rice-husk/straw ash; fermentation in a clean, dry container (preferably earthen pot) plugged with dry straw or fresh fern leaves for about 20 days at approximately 30–35°C. The fermented solid mash is called Po:ro arouk and is extracted by adding warm water over the mash in a cone-shaped bamboo Ta:suk lined with banana or Canna leaves.

The authors describe a mild fruity-sweet aroma by days 2–3 and a stronger fruity/pungent odour around days 8–10 as traditional sensory cues. These are reported observations, not validated chemical markers or proof of a universal maturation schedule.

The paper reports three finished samples with similar taste/texture and finished-product pH 4.06 ± 0.1, titratable acidity 0.53% lactic acid, reducing sugars 3.33 ± 0.2 mg/ml, free amino acids 2.43 ± 0.12 mg/ml, total carbohydrate 46.62 ± 4.3 mg/ml, protein 1.05 ± 0.06 mg/ml and alcohol 7.52–18.5% v/v. Microbial load is reported separately for Epop, ripe mash and finished filtrate, but methods/units differ between solid starter/mash and liquid product. These are not a controlled fermentation time-series and should not be used to calculate stage survival or growth rates.

### SRC-167 — Kardong, Pegu & Ghose Guha (2017): controlled starter comparison

This primary experimental paper compares traditional home-made starter (HMS) against laboratory-made starter (LMS) and a selected microbial consortium for Po:ro Apong. The consortium contains two amylolytic fungi, *Mucor* sp. and *Saccharomycopsis* sp., plus an alcohol-fermenting *Saccharomyces* sp., all isolated from HMS.

At the 144-hour endpoint, the HMS mash showed:
- pH 4.78
- reducing sugar 17 ± 0.12 mg/g
- titratable acidity 0.22 ± 0.01% lactic acid
- ethanol 3.1 ± 0.02 ml/100 g fermented mash

The paper reports lower ethanol and acidity in LMSP/MC than HMS, while bacterial colony counts increased in LMSP/MC and fell in HMS. It also notes that the laboratory consortium digested rice starch more visibly but produced a less desirable/faulty odour, whereas HMS mash had a fruity smell. The authors interpret this as evidence that a small selected consortium did not reproduce the functional performance of the traditional mixed starter.

The study's endpoint is 144 hours (six days), not the approximately 20-day household fermentation described in SRC-136. Do not compare the six-day mash ethanol directly with SRC-136 finished-drink alcohol. The experimental substrate, assay units and fermentation endpoint differ.

**Method caveats:** the article reports 14 herbs in results while its abstract mentions 13; it does not disclose named villages or household sample count, and does not give a detailed multi-timepoint trajectory. It is a preliminary laboratory comparison, not a controlled trial isolating individual plant effects. It supports the importance of the traditional starter as a complex inoculum but does not prove which individual microbes/herbs cause the observed effect.

### Cross-source synthesis: why reported times differ

The sources document distinct process descriptions, not one standardized recipe:
- SRC-136 (2012): Po:ro mash about 20 days at 30–35°C.
- SRC-147 (2013): Po:ro 6–8 days warm / 12–15 days cold; separate Nogin process.
- SRC-148 (2014/2015 issue-date discrepancy): Po:ro 15–20 days; Nogin 3–4 days in its reported Jorhat sample.
- SRC-167 (2017): controlled laboratory comparison assessed at 144 hours; not a household finished-product schedule.
- SRC-153 (2023) and SRC-160 (2026): further source-specific descriptions; no universal timing should be inferred.
- SRC-161 (2026 Majuli): reports its own 10–15-day summer process accounts.

The differences may reflect locality, season, rice/starter preparation, vessel, desired product, and how authors define fermentation completion versus extraction. The evidence does not justify choosing one value as universally correct.

### Evidence gap

No Mising Apong study located in this pass provides a robust, replicated time-course sampling of the same fermentation batches at multiple points (e.g., day 0, 2, 4, 6, 10, 15, 20) with matched pH, sugars, ethanol, organic acids and microbial community measurements. This remains a high-value research gap.
