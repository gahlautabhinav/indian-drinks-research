# Tamulpur district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for traditional beverage evidence explicitly associated with present-day Tamulpur district, especially Bodo rice-beer traditions and older Baksa/Bodoland administrative labels.  
**Decision:** No qualifying Tamulpur place row added. The district remains an evidence gap, not a district with no beverage tradition.

## Sources and findings reviewed

### 1. Bodo beverage methods study (2025)

Deka, Basumatary and Deka (2025) document Bodo cereal- and fruit-based beverages, including Joubidwi, Joufinai and fruit-based Jougwran. The study's stated field survey is in rural localities around Kokrajhar district headquarters; the named villages are Amlaiguri, Gendrabili, Jalpaiguri and Banshigaon. It does not identify Tamulpur as a field district or any named Tamulpur village. [Full article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-025-00286-2).

**Decision:** Do not extend the Kokrajhar fieldwork to Tamulpur based on shared Bodo identity or Bodoland regional association.

### 2. Bodo beverage literature and regional summaries

The 2013 ethnobotanical review describes Jou bishi and identifies Kokrajhar as its study district for the relevant plant table. [Article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0975761913000720).

The 2024 review of North-East Indian rice beverages describes Jou bishi as a Bodo beverage associated with the Bodoland region, but the accessible section does not identify a Tamulpur village or sample. [Article record](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/7769743).

The 2025 Bodo fermentation article describes Jou as part of Bodo food culture but does not provide Tamulpur field sites in the accessible record. [Article page](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijrias/view/traditional-fermentation-practices-in-bodo-cuisine-indigenous-knowledge-health-and-cultural-identity).

**Decision:** Regional and community-level descriptions are useful context, but do not establish Tamulpur-specific production.

### 3. Other Assam-wide beverage research

Saikia and Bori (2020) discuss several Assam community beverage traditions without a Tamulpur locality allocation in the accessible abstract. [Article page](https://archives.palarch.nl/index.php/jae/article/view/3776).

Borah et al. (2021) report a 32-village, six-district ethnobotanical survey, but the reviewed record does not establish Tamulpur as one of the study districts or map any sample to a present-day Tamulpur locality. [Article record](https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451).

## Administrative and historical caution

Tamulpur is a relatively recent district and older records may use Baksa or Bodoland Territorial Region administrative labels. However, regional inclusion alone cannot identify which villages fall within present-day Tamulpur, nor can it demonstrate that a beverage was prepared there. Any historical source must be crosswalked at village level before a place row is created.

## Archive treatment

- No place row added.
- No beverage record or source-register entry added from regional Bodo summaries alone.
- Matrix remains at zero place rows.
- Keep the district open for present-day village mapping and community-led verification.

## Remaining research questions

1. Which historical Baksa/BTR beverage-study villages now fall within present-day Tamulpur?
2. Are there local Bodo-language sources or oral histories documenting Jou or related drinks in named Tamulpur villages?
3. Can current village-level production be distinguished from broad community presence or regional beverage identity?

**Conclusion:** No source reviewed in this pass establishes a traditional beverage locality in present-day Tamulpur. This is a bounded literature-search result, not evidence that Tamulpur communities lack beverage traditions.
