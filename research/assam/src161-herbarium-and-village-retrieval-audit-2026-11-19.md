# SRC-161 — Jengraimukh College herbarium and Majuli village retrieval audit

**Audit date:** 19 November 2026  
**Study:** Borah, Porismita (2026), “Documentation of ethnobotanical plants used in the preparation of Apong, a traditionally prepared rice beer by the Mishing tribal community of Majuli district,” *International Journal of Advanced Research* 14(04):1361–1368. DOI: 10.21474/IJAR01/23298.  
**Canonical archive source:** SRC-161  
**Related gap:** ASM-GAP-070

## New evidence recovered

The primary article's Methods section states that fieldwork took place in **three randomly selected Majuli villages, April–October 2024**, using semi-structured interviews, informal discussions and transect field walks. It reports **28 informants aged 40–70**, with special emphasis on elderly women involved in Apong preparation. The article does not name the three villages, give informant counts by village, or publish household/batch sampling details.

Plant material was collected with local informants from forests, agricultural fields and home gardens. The article then explicitly says that herbarium sheets were prepared and submitted to the **herbarium of Jengraimukh College**. No accession numbers, collector numbers, label images, sheet IDs or public catalogue are provided. This is a stronger retrieval lead than the previously recorded generic absence of accession numbers: a named institutional repository is stated, but custody and traceability still require confirmation.

The college's official 2024–25 prospectus lists Porishmita Borah as Assistant Professor and Head of the Department of Botany (SRC-293). This supports the institution as a practical acquisition route. It does not prove that all 48 records have a deposited sheet, that the sheets remain in custody, or that the herbarium has a searchable catalogue. No contact has been made.

## Repeated Zanthoxylum record pair

The paper reports 48 numbered inventory rows. Two rows print the same botanical name but different local labels and quantitative scores:

| Table row | Scientific name as printed | Vernacular name | Part | RFC | UV |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 20 | *Zanthoxylum oxyphyllum* Edgew. | Prothoma | Leaves | 0.7 | 0.6 |
| 33 | *Zanthoxylum oxyphyllum* Edgew. | Mejenga | Leaves | 0.9 | 1.1 |

Kew POWO accepts *Zanthoxylum oxyphyllum* Edgew. in Rutaceae and includes Assam in its native distribution. This supports the name as regionally plausible but cannot authenticate the collected plants. The authors define RFC as FC/N and report 28 informants; the distinct RFC/UV values show that Prothoma and Mejenga were tabulated as separate response/use records. They do not disclose whether these labels refer to separate local forms, separate specimens, separate vernacular categories for one taxon, or a duplicated taxonomic determination. Preserve both rows. Do not merge them, create two species entities, or count 48 unique scientific-name strings.

**Current correct representation:** 48 source-table records; 47 unique printed scientific-name strings; one repeated-binomial pair requiring voucher and raw-data reconciliation. This is not a correction of the paper's own reported total of 48 ethnobotanical species; it is a transparent description of the printed table's lexical structure.

## Scope limitations

The inventory concerns plants incorporated into E’pob starter-cake preparation. It is not a separate Po:ro and Nogin ingredient matrix. No taxon is assigned to a particular one of the three villages, household, informant or habitat source. The 28 informants' ages and total count do not establish community-wide prevalence. RFC/UV are study-sample metrics, not laboratory measures of fermentation function or beverage quality.

Other taxonomic flags already documented in the SRC-161 taxonomic review remain open for specimen-level verification: “Tinospora Miers” lacks a species epithet; *Achasma loroglossum* is a synonym of *Etlingera loroglossa* under POWO but the article assigns the family Scytosiphonaceae; and the printed forms “Paderia”, “Guilandian” and “Termalia” require nomenclatural checking. Raw source strings remain untouched.

## Targeted acquisition request (not sent)

1. Names and official village identifiers for the three April–October 2024 study sites.
2. Informant count per village, with an anonymised plant/vernacular-name response matrix for all 28 informants.
3. Jengraimukh College herbarium accession/catalogue numbers, collector numbers, collection dates and sheet images for the study material.
4. Specific sheet/field records underlying rows 20 and 33 (Prothoma and Mejenga), including determination notes and whether they represent separate collections or one taxon under two names.
5. Plant-by-village and plant-by-household occurrence data, including collection habitat (forest, agricultural field or home garden).
6. Confirmation whether the 48 E’pob records are used across both Apong types or whether any taxa are variant-specific; do not assume a split in the absence of a matrix.

## Archive decision

ASM-GAP-070 remains **Open**. The named college herbarium submission is now a concrete retrieval path, but no accession-level evidence or village identities have been recovered. Do not add village production records, split Po:ro/Nogin taxa, or collapse the Prothoma/Mejenga records before documentary review.
