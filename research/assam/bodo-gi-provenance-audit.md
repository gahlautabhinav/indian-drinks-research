**Reviewed:** 2026-10-22  
**Scope:** Official Intellectual Property India public registry metadata for GI applications 968, 969 and 970. This is a registry/provenance audit, not a full extraction of the statements of case.

The registry pages report the applicant as Bodo Geographical Indication (G.I) Board, applicant address in Tengapara, Kokrajhar, BTR, Assam; filing date 29 August 2022; Class 32; goods classified as manufactured goods; geographical area listed as Assam; certificate date 1 October 2024; and validity through 28 August 2032.

These official records support the **registered product names and registration metadata**. They do not, by themselves, prove that a related spelling or a broader community term is synonymous with the registered product, nor do they establish current production in every Assam village.

## Authorized-user addresses: research leads only

The public registry pages display authorized-user addresses. The pages reviewed list these place names:

- **Deborbill / Amteka Bazar / Amguri** — appears in the authorized-user details for all three registrations.
- **West Dangarkutti / Dotoma / Dotma** — appears in the authorized-user details for all three registrations.
- **Batabari** — appears in the authorized-user details for all three registrations.
- **Palashbari / Polashbari** — appears in the reviewed details for application 970 (Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi).

These are official **authorized-user address leads**, not verified production localities. The public page does not establish that the person currently produces the product at the listed address, that the place is the only production location, or that every registered product is produced at every shared address. Do not convert these addresses into confirmed village-level production claims without corroborating evidence.

## Statement-of-case extraction remains open

The official pages list downloadable documents named “Statement of Case” for each application, but the text of those PDFs has not yet been extracted in this pass. That remains the highest-value next step for the Bodo GI records:

1. Extract each statement of case and any official map/specification.
2. Record the exact definition, ingredients/substrate, preparation boundary and geographical-area wording for each registered product.
3. Compare those definitions with SRC-081 (Deka et al., 2025), which discusses Joubidwi, stored Joufinai and fruit-based Jougwran variants.
4. Update beverage records only where the registered specification supports a correction.
5. Keep authorized-user addresses and actual production observations in separate evidence categories.

## Repository interpretation

The existing beverage records already identify the official registrations and caution against treating applicant addresses as proof of universal production. This audit adds the observed authorized-user address leads while retaining that limitation. No canonical beverage record or village-level production claim is added from addresses alone.

The Statement of Case appears as item 24 for applications 968 and 969, and item 23 for application 970. The registry HTML makes the existence and labels of these documents verifiable, but the document links are rendered as generic PDF controls. In this research pass, the web retrieval layer did not return the PDF body for direct extraction. Therefore, this audit **does not claim to have read** the Statements of Case or producer lists, and it does not yet extract their product definitions, production steps, historical claims, village lists, or producer counts.

## Cross-check against a Bodo GI heritage exhibit

A community-facing GI heritage exhibit was located and added to the source register as **SRC-083**. It provides useful product-level descriptions for all three GI beverages, but it is secondary explanatory material; it is not a substitute for the registry's Statement of Case.

| Beverage | Description reported by SRC-083 | Research interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Bodo Jou Gishi (GI 649) | Described as a fermented rice beverage; cooked rice is cooled, mixed with powdered Amao starter, and fermented in a vessel with a bamboo sieve before liquid is collected | Supports treating Jou Gishi as a finished fermented beverage rather than a distilled spirit. Exact parameters and specification still need primary-document verification. |
| Bodo Jou Gwran (GI 648) | Described as a distilled rice liquor produced from fermented cooked rice (jumai), with Amao starter and a multi-vessel distillation arrangement | Supports the key fermented-versus-distilled distinction. Do not treat reported alcohol concentration as a stable specification without checking the underlying study and methods. |
| Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi (GI 650) | Described as a traditional welcome drink made from a special rice called Maibra and fermented with Amao; cultural use and plant materials are also discussed | Supports retaining the Maibra-specific name as its own record. The relationship between the product name, rice variety and legal specification remains to be checked in the Statement of Case. |

SRC-083's pages also report ritual, marriage and hospitality contexts. These are valuable leads, but the archive should preserve the distinction between (a) a heritage exhibit's narrative, (b) peer-reviewed fieldwork, and (c) the legally registered product specification. The source has been cross-referenced to the three corresponding beverage records without treating its descriptions as definitive legal boundaries.

### Conflicts and cautions to preserve

- The official registry currently displays the three products under application numbers 968, 969 and 970, certificate numbers 648, 649 and 650 respectively. Some secondary pages inconsistently label an application number as the GI number; keep the official registry's application/certificate fields separate.
- A secondary source's alcohol percentage, health benefit or “strongest” ranking should not be presented as universal without checking the study design, sample and analytical method.
- The 2025 Bodo field study (SRC-081) discusses both fermented and fruit-based beverages and a distillate called Jougwran. Its four named survey localities are survey scope, not proof that each product is made in each locality.
- The GI geography shown in registry metadata is “Assam”. This alone does not establish uniform production throughout the state; the Statement of Case, map and producer list remain the key outstanding documents.


## Authorized-user address review — 2026-10-23

The current official registry detail pages expose Authorized User rows for all three registrations. Examples of locality strings in those public records include Deborbill / Amteka Bazar / Amguri, West Dangarkutti / Dotoma, Batabari, and Palashbari / Polashbari (the latter appears in the application 970 record). These strings are recorded here as **registry-address leads only**.

An Authorized User address may be a person's declared address and is not automatically a production site, a surveyed village, or evidence that every GI product is brewed there. Therefore, this pass does not create or upgrade beverage-place rows from those addresses. If used in a later geography pass, first inspect the GI Statement of Case, the producer-list document, and any map/area description; distinguish a legal GI area from individual authorized-user addresses and from directly observed production localities.
