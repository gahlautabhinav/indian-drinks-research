# Mech Kachari beverage names: Haaz, Photika and Jau — Assam research note

**Status:** Provisional research lead; no canonical beverage entries created.
**Community label in the lead source:** Mech Kachari.
**Geographic confidence:** District-level regional leads only; no named village or confirmed current production locality.
**Key caution:** Do not assume the names Haaz, Photika and Jau are synonyms, or that Haaz pitha, Zu and Bakhar are starter names paired one-to-one with those products.

## What the sources actually say

### 1. Secondary review lists the names, but does not resolve pairings

Bhattacharya and Deka's 2023 review lists Mech Kachari with starter-culture terms **Haaz pitha, Zu and Bakhar**, beverage names **Haaz, Photika and Jau**, and the districts **Udalguri, Kokrajhar and Goalpara** as major inhabited districts. The table cites earlier works, but the reviewed table does not specify which starter is used for which beverage, whether the three beverage names describe distinct processes, or which district contains which product. The district list is not village-level evidence of production. [SRC-062](https://www.foodandnutritionjournal.org/volume11number2/biochemical-indices-and-consumption-pattern-of-traditional-alcoholic-beverages-by-tribal-communities-of-north-east-india-a-review/)

### 2. A separate source documents Jau/Jumai and Photika among Deoris

Dutta's 2014 socio-cultural account describes **Jau/Jumai** as a Deori rice beer and **Photika** as a distilled liquor prepared from Jau/Jumai. It also reports social hospitality and ritual offerings in a Deori context. The passage names no village and has limited methodological detail, so it is a cross-check rather than a definitive community-exclusive classification. However, it raises a real attribution question: a later review lists Jau and Photika under Mech Kachari too. This could reflect shared regional terminology, overlapping use, citation carry-over or a review-table error; current evidence does not settle which. [SRC-063](https://globusedujournal.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/JJ14palash.pdf)

### 3. What is not established

- Whether **Haaz** is one product, an umbrella label, or a name used across more than one community.
- Whether **Photika** is consistently a distilled product in the Mech Kachari context, as it is described in the Deori account.
- Whether **Jau** among Mech Kachari refers to the same product/process as Deori Jau/Jumai.
- Which of **Haaz pitha, Zu and Bakhar** is used with each finished beverage.
- Which villages currently make each product, and whether the district list in the review describes community residence, source study areas, or beverage-production sites.
- Community-preferred spellings and meanings in the relevant language(s).

## Research decision

Keep this as a **naming-and-attribution investigation**, not a new canonical beverage record. Do not import the Deori preparation description into a Mech Kachari record or assign the three listed districts as confirmed production sites. First trace references 33 and 56 in the 2023 review to their original sources; then seek Mech Kachari-authored or community-verified sources and locality-specific interviews.

### Citation-trail audit: the review's references 33 and 56

The 2023 review's Mech Kachari row cites its references **33** and **56**. Reference 33 is Sarma (2017), *Rice Beer and Tribes of Assam*; it is a broad review/article with district-level discussion, including a general passage on Mech/Jau, rather than a village-specific production survey. Reference 56 is Senapati and Gurumayum (2016), *Medicinal Plants Used in Traditional Alcoholic Beverage Preparation by Tribes of Assam*. Its abstract names Dimasa, Boro, Rabha, Mising, Karbi, Ahom, Deori and Sonowal Kachari, but does **not** name Mech Kachari. Therefore reference 56 does not independently substantiate the Mech Kachari row on the evidence checked so far; the citation may be broad, indirect or mismatched. [SRC-054](https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jhss/papers/Vol.%2022%20Issue7/Version-2/C2207021316.pdf) [SRC-064](https://rjpbcs.com/pdf/2016_7%285%29/%5B128%5D.pdf)

The table's district list is explicitly headed “major inhabited districts” for each community, not a list of confirmed villages producing each named beverage. Do not convert Udalguri, Kokrajhar and Goalpara into village-level production claims. The current evidence remains a **regional naming lead**, not a verified Mech Kachari product specification.

## Research pass — 2026-10-22: primary-source trail for Mech Kachari Jau

### What the 2017 paper actually supports

Sarma (2017), *Rice Beer and tribes of Assam*, explicitly says the Meches' home-brewed rice beer is called **jau** and reports it in socio-religious festivals and hospitality. Its methods section says primary material for the Mech community was collected in **Goalpara district**. However, the article's Mech passage cites Bordoloi (1991) and does not provide a recipe, starter-culture name, ingredient list, fermentation duration, named village or distinction between fermented and distilled products. The district-level fieldwork statement does not establish which specific Mech beverage labels were observed in Goalpara. [SRC-054]

This provides a second, broad source trail for the name **Jau** in a Mech/Mech Kachari context, but it does not resolve the 2023 review's listing of **Haaz, Photika and Jau** alongside **Haaz pitha, Zu and Bakhar**. The review's references do not map starter names to individual beverages. A separate Deori account's Jau/Jumai and Photika description must not be imported into the Mech Kachari context without independent evidence. [SRC-062; SRC-063]

### Updated assessment

- **Jau as a Mech-associated rice-beer name:** documented at a broad historical/ethnographic level.
- **Haaz and Photika as distinct Mech Kachari beverages:** not independently established by the sources reviewed.
- **Zu / Haaz pitha / Bakhar pairing with a particular finished drink:** unresolved.
- **Current production villages and present-day practice:** unresolved.
- **Canonical records:** do not add Haaz, Photika or a second Jau record yet; preserve the discrepancy and seek community-preferred terminology and locality-specific primary evidence.

The next useful step is to trace the cited older ethnographic sources behind the review, then corroborate terminology and product boundaries through Mech Kachari community or language sources.


## Research pass — 2026-10-22: detailed Mech Jou preparation source

### Stronger source found
Ajita Sarkar's 2011 University of North Bengal doctoral thesis, *Ethnobotanical Studies of Sub-Himalayan Duars in West Bengal and Assam with Particular Reference to the Tribe Mech*, contains a dedicated chapter, “Preparation of Jou [Rice Beer]” (chapter 7, section 7.7, printed pages 124–126). The institutional repository identifies it as a thesis and names Sarkar as author and A. P. Das as advisor. [SRC-073]

The chapter explicitly identifies **Jou** as Mech rice beer and documents a source-specific method:
- It lists rice (*Oryza sativa*, called Mairong in the passage) and five plants in the starter mixture: *Plumbago zeylanica* (Emao), *Scoparia dulcis* (Rakhep), *Clerodendrum viscosum* (Lakhna), *Artocarpus heterophyllus* (Kanthal) and *Dryopteris filix-mas* (Saldaokhumai).
- It describes pounding the rice and plant materials, making a paste and forming flat round starter cakes. The cakes are kept in straw under a jute covering; the thesis reports fungal growth after roughly two days, then sun-drying.
- For the beverage, it describes cooking 2 kg rice, cooling it on a bamboo plate, mixing in 200 g powdered starter cake, and fermenting in a metal container. The thesis reports 7–8 days in winter and 4–5 days in summer, followed by straining the liquid.
- The thesis associates Jou with social occasions and describes rice beer used in a Mech festival context elsewhere in the work.

These are historical ethnographic details from the thesis, not a current universal recipe or food-safety recommendation. The thesis title covers the Sub-Himalayan Duars in West Bengal and Assam, but the Jou chapter does not identify an exact Assam village; treat its locality as **Mech community evidence with geographic scope not precise enough to establish current Assam production**.

### What this changes—and what it does not
- **Jou/Jau:** stronger preparation-level evidence exists for a Mech-associated rice beer than the earlier review and broad Sarma (2017) article alone supplied.
- **Starter names:** the thesis calls its starter **Emao**. It does not establish that Emao is identical to the review's **Haaz pitha**, **Zu** or **Bakhar**.
- **Haaz and Photika:** still not independently described as Mech preparations by the sources reviewed. The separate Deori account of Photika must not be transferred to Mech Kachari practice.
- **Assam locality/current practice:** still unresolved. Do not create extra canonical records for Haaz or Photika on the strength of the review table.

### Next evidence target
Trace the thesis's underlying sources and look for Mech Kachari community or language documentation in named Assam localities. Keep the thesis as a valuable cross-regional Mech source while the Assam-specific geography remains open.


## Research pass — 2026-10-22: historical comparison for Emao and Photika

### Newly checked historical source
Sidney Endle's *The Kacháris* (1911) provides an older comparative account of rice beer in the Kachári Duars. Endle describes a starter called **emáo**, rice-beer called **Zu/Záu**, and a distilled spirit called **phatiká** made from that rice beer. He describes starter cakes made with rice and plant materials, fermentation in an earthen vessel, and serving rice beer at marriages, funerals and harvest festivities. [SRC-074]

The book's rice-beer chapter specifically describes **Kacháris of Darrang**; its introduction discusses the northern Bodo/Kachari branch and notes that in Assam these people were called Kacharis and in Bengal Meches. This is historically valuable regional context, but it is **not a village-specific Mech Kachari study** and the chapter does not establish that every Mech community used the exact same names or process. It therefore cannot independently confirm the modern review's Mech Kachari label “Photika” or equate it definitively with the older spelling “phatiká.”

### Updated interpretation
- **Jou/Jau:** the strongest Mech-associated preparation evidence remains Sarkar's 2011 thesis, supplemented by Sarma (2017)'s citation to Bordoloi (1991).
- **Emao:** attested in both Sarkar's Mech-focused thesis and Endle's historical Kachari account, but plant recipes differ by source and must remain source-specific.
- **Photika / phatiká:** a plausible spelling relationship now has historical comparative support: Endle calls *phatiká* a distilled spirit made from rice beer. However, direct Mech-specific confirmation of the exact name and product boundary is still needed.
- **Haaz / Haaz pitha / Zu / Bakhar:** no source reviewed establishes the pairings in the 2023 table. Do not treat the list as a reliable one-to-one starter/product mapping.
- **Geography:** Endle's chapter is Darrang/Kachari Duars context, while the later Mech fieldwork lead is Goalpara. Neither alone establishes current village-level production of Haaz or Photika.

### Next evidence target
Compare Bordoloi (1991) Part III's Mech chapter around the rice-beer passage (Sarma cites p. 90) with Endle and Sarkar; preserve historical source geography and spelling exactly. Seek community/language confirmation before creating canonical Haaz or Photika entries.
