# SRC-061 locality and claim-level audit

**Source:** Borah et al. (2021), “Compendium of plants used for preparation of traditional alcoholic beverages by four major ethnic communities of Assam, northeast India,” *Biodiversitas* 22(4): 2019–2031. DOI: https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451  
**Accessed:** 2026-10-23  
**Purpose:** Extract the geographic scope that is actually stated in the paper and prevent a study-area map from being misread as beverage-by-district production evidence.

## 1. What the paper explicitly establishes

The Methods section names six Assam study districts: **Baksa, Biswanath, Golaghat, Lakhimpur, Sivasagar (spelled “Sibasagar” in the paper), and Sonitpur**. Field investigations covered **32 villages from October 2019 to March 2020** and involved **202 respondents**, selected through purposive stratified sampling. The paper describes ethnobotanical research with four communities: Ahom, Bodo, Karbi, and Mising.

The article reports 129 plant species across 107 genera and 54 families associated with traditional alcoholic-beverage preparation among the communities studied. Its summary table associates:
- Ahom — Haaj/Laupani; starter names Haajpitha/Vekurpitha.
- Bodo — Jou/Bishi; starter names Amao/Angkur.
- Karbi — Hor/Hor-alank; starter Thap.
- Mising — Poro apong/Sai mod; starter Apoppitha.

These are **study-wide community/b​​everage associations**. The main-text methods and summary table do not assign each beverage/community pair to each of the six districts.

**Village-level limitation:** The reviewed article text says 32 villages were covered and Figure 1 maps the six study districts, but the accessible text does not provide a district-by-village list that can be transcribed reliably. We must not invent village names from the map or infer them from author affiliations, specimen codes, or other studies.

## 3. Process claims that must remain scoped

- The paper treats Po:ro apong as a distinct Mising preparation involving paddy-husk/straw ash and a described extraction/filtration step. Preserve that as a source-specific account, not as a universal recipe.
- A separate section describes distillation observed among three studied communities but not Ahom. The article does not explicitly establish that this generic distillation description is a later stage of Po:ro apong. Do not merge the two procedures without a direct source statement.
- Plant-use findings are from the study's sampled communities and localities. They should not be represented as an exhaustive inventory for every district or every household.

## 4. Evidence decision

**Accepted:** Six-district study footprint; 32-village / 202-respondent sample description; four-community study scope; the paper's named community/b​​everage associations at study-wide scope.

**Not accepted:** Any specific village name not explicitly recoverable from the paper; a claim that all four communities were sampled in every district; a claim that every listed beverage was produced in every study district; assignment of SRC-061's generic distillation process to Po:ro apong.

## 5. Next actions

1. If a reliable supplementary file or author-provided locality schedule becomes available, extract the 32 village names and the district/community pairing verbatim, with page/table references.
2. Until then, keep SRC-061 as study-area evidence for the six districts, not product-specific production evidence.
3. For Biswanath and Sonitpur, seek district-specific primary research that identifies a named community and beverage together before adding a Beverage_ID to the place row.
4. Use the same discipline when auditing existing SRC-061-linked districts: do not upgrade a pre-existing place row's evidence tier solely because it falls inside the study's district list.
