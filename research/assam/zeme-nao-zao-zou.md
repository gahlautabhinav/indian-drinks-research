# Zao / Zou — Zeme Naga, Assam

**Record:** `ASM-017-ZAO`  
**Classification:** fermented rice-based alcoholic beverage tradition; related named preparations may include distinct filtered or distilled forms  
**Research status:** documented in ethnographic and food-science literature; village-level coverage and subtype boundaries remain open

## Summary

Zao (also written Zou in some sources) is a rice-based alcoholic beverage tradition associated with the Zeme Naga community. Southern Assam food-science studies document Zao among the community's traditional beverages and identify a starter called Nduihi/Ndhuihi, prepared from rice and bark of a locally named plant, Nduihi gei. The botanical identity of Nduihi gei is not established in the reviewed sources.

The name must be handled carefully. Atungbou (2020) distinguishes several named Zeme beverages, including **Tekuizou/Zousang**, **Tamzou/Lingtakzou**, and **Nduizou**. That account describes different substrates and processes, and says Nduizou/herazou is associated with special occasions. The 2026 southern Assam paper uses “Zao” and “NduiZou” as names for the Zeme rice beverage. These sources do not justify flattening every named preparation into one recipe or treating every term as a perfect synonym.

## Names and distinctions

- **Zao / Zou:** spelling differs across publications.
- **NduiZou / Nduizou:** named in the 2026 and 2020 accounts, but its exact relationship to the broader category Zao/Zou needs community-specific reconciliation.
- **Tekuizou / Zousang:** Atungbou describes a preparation involving soaked rice and paddy that germinate, are dried and pounded, then processed in a gourd or wooden pot.
- **Tamzou / Lingtakzou:** Atungbou describes a maize-based beverage, not simply another name for the generic rice-based Zao record.
- **Nduihi / Ndhuihi:** starter names in the 2026 and 2024 sources; spelling variation and the plant's identity require confirmation.
- **Do not conflate with Hmar Zu.** Similar spelling does not establish the same beverage or process.

## Generic Zao preparation reported in the 2026 account

1. Rice is cooked and spread on a bamboo tray to cool.
2. A starter cake called Nduihi is prepared using soaked rice and ground bark from a locally named plant, Nduihi gei; the cake is sun-dried.
3. The dried starter is mixed with cooled cooked rice.
4. The mixture is placed in a clay pot covered with banana leaves and left to ferment.

A 2024 paper's summary table gives a 3–5-day fermentation duration for Zeme Zou, citing Atungbou (2020). This should not be treated as a universal duration for all named preparations.

## Related processing variation

Atungbou (2020) describes Nduizou as cooked rice mixed with nduihi starter and wrapped in a banana-leaf-lined basket. Liquid drips through as the fermentation and filtration mature; the account calls this liquid *chiangne*. The liquid can be consumed with water or soaked and distilled through the filter basket in a repeated process. This is a subtype-specific description and is **not** evidence that every Zao/Zou preparation is distilled.

The same source describes Nduizou/herazou as a sacred drink used on special occasions. The exact product boundaries and contemporary practice require further study.

## Geography and evidence

The 2024 microbiome study and the 2026 cultural/technical article include Zeme Naga beverages in a southern Assam study context that covers Cachar, Hailakandi and Karimganj (renamed Sribhumi). They do not, in the reviewed passages, identify the precise village for each Zao sample. Atungbou (2020) and Longkumer (2016) offer broader ethnographic and cultural detail, but the archive does not infer village-level production without explicit evidence.

## Cultural context

Published accounts describe Zao/Zou as socially and religiously meaningful. Atungbou (2020) reports use in contexts including rituals, hearings, feasts, festivals and marital negotiations, and describes Nduizou/herazou as reserved for special occasions. Longkumer (2016) examines how zao participates in changing debates around Zeme religion and culture, including encounters with Christianity. These accounts should not be read as a single universal belief or practice shared by all Zeme households.

## Evidence cautions

- **Supported:** Zao/Zou is a Zeme Naga beverage tradition; rice and Nduihi/Ndhuihi starter are reported in southern Assam studies; several related names and processes are described in ethnographic literature.
- **Unresolved:** exact relation between Zao/Zou and Nduizou; preferred spelling by locality; botanical identity of Nduihi gei; village-specific recipes and current production.
- A fermented sample being studied before distillation does not establish the full range of products made in a community.
- Cultural and ethnomedical claims are not clinical evidence.
- Respect community terminology, religious diversity and any restrictions on traditional knowledge.

## Open research tasks

1. Compare the full text of Atungbou (2020), Longkumer (2016), and the 2024/2026 southern Assam studies.
2. Verify which terms are category names, local variants, or distinct beverage preparations with Zeme knowledge holders.
3. Identify the botanical species called Nduihi gei only when reliable evidence supports the identification.
4. Locate the villages associated with each research sample; do not infer them from district-level maps.
5. Create separate beverage records for named subtypes only when a distinct product boundary is supported.
