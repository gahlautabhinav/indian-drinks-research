# SRC-339 and SRC-340 — Dimasa Judi, Jugap and Juharo: community archive and ritual-use cross-check

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026

This audit compares two distinct sources that illuminate the Dimasa names without proving that all spellings are synonyms or that every community uses the same taxonomy.

## SRC-339 — Longmailai (2024): community archive account and three beverage categories

**Citation:** Monali Longmailai (cited in the article's own recommended citation as “Longmaila, Monali”) (2024), “Creating a Digital Archive for Dimasa Cultural Heritages and Identity Preservation,” *Sampratyaya* 1, Special Issue (The Northeast) No. 2, pp. 70–83. DOI: 10.21276/smprt.202408.1sne2.a7.  
**Full text:** https://sampratyaya.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Article-7.Dr_.Monali.pdf

### Archive and fieldwork context

The paper describes the Bodo and Dimasa Heritage Digital Archive as a community-based platform, with community members acting as curators/co-administrators and community heritage information. It reports Dimasa community outreach and recording across 14–15 villages in Cachar district in 2022, alongside online uploads beginning in 2021. It also notes later Bushu festival documentation in Haflong in 2024.

The paper's food section gives the following lexical distinction:
- **judi** — rice wine;
- **jugap** — rice beer;
- **juharo** — a rice drink said to resemble white rum.

It says **Humao** is a yeast cake specially prepared for making all three. This is a valuable Dimasa-language/cultural distinction, but the paper does not give separate preparation protocols, alcohol measurements, distillation apparatus, producer names or item-level locality metadata for these three drinks. It therefore cannot establish whether the difference between Judi and Jugap is process, strength, consistency, serving stage, dialect or a combination.

### Limits

- The 14–15 Cachar villages are the overall heritage archive fieldwork frame; the paper does not say that the Judi/Jugap/Juharo description was collected in each or any named village.
- Do not create beverage production locality edges from this general fieldwork statement.
- Do not infer that all three products use identical rice, proportions or fermentation times merely because the same starter cake is named.
- “Juharo resembles white rum” is an analogy in the paper, not a laboratory finding or proof of a specific distillation method.
- Preserve the article's spelling “Humao”; do not automatically merge it with NIPCCD's “Xumai” or every “Hamao/Umhu” form.

## SRC-340 — Borah & Sarma (2022): Judi in birth and marriage ritual

**Citation:** Dadul Borah and Pranjal Sarma (2022), “Religious Practices Among the Dimasas in Dima Hasao District of Assam with Special Reference to Their Daikho Systems and Rituals Concerning Birth, Marriage and Death,” *Research Journal of Philosophy & Social Sciences* 47(2), pp. 255–272. DOI: 10.31995/rjpss.2022.v47i02.030.  
**Full text:** https://anubooks.com/uploads/session_pdf/1666773560RJPSS%20Vol.47%20No.2%20Sept.%202022%20%2830%29.pdf

### What it records

- The study says it examined religious practices in four selected Dima Hasao villages: Semkhor, Wadrengdisa, Dima Hading and Donjenraji.
- In the Nanadihonba birth ritual, the midwife places puffed rice and drops of rice beer called **Judi** on a fingertip into the newborn's mouth; Judi is also included in the feast.
- During the Laothailamba marriage-proposal custom, homemade rice beer **Judi** is presented in dried bottle-gourd containers called Laothai to the bride's elder relatives.
- Judi is also mentioned in the wedding reception and other ceremonial contexts.

### Limits

The four villages are the study's religious-practice field sites, not a beverage production survey. The article does not identify which village's households brewed Judi, name a producer, specify ingredients or describe the preparation process. These are ritual-use records, not production-locality records. The newborn ritual is documented as a cultural practice; it is not medical advice or evidence of safety for infant alcohol exposure.

## Cross-source synthesis and terminology controls

1. **Judi is not merely an unverified spelling variant.** It is independently documented as a named homemade rice beer in the 2022 ritual study and as a named rice wine in Longmailai's 2024 food taxonomy. The exact beverage category varies by source; preserve both source descriptions.
2. **Jugap is a distinct named category in Longmailai (2024).** This may help explain the earlier SRC-336 “jugap” residual fermented mass and SRC-049 “Zukap” fermented rice mass, but resemblance alone is insufficient. The former is printed as the name of a rice beer in Longmailai; the latter two are described as process-stage fermented rice masses. Do not merge.
3. **Juharo is described as a rum-like rice drink by Longmailai.** This aligns broadly with sources describing Juharo/Juharao as a stronger/distilled drink, but the paper does not explicitly document distillation. Retain the analogy and process uncertainty.
4. **Bose et al. (2018, SRC-338) prints “Jumai/Judi” together.** Longmailai (2024) distinguishes Judi from Jugap and Juharo but does not discuss Jumai. This does not resolve whether Jumai and Judi are synonyms, variants or a typographic grouping in SRC-338.
5. **Ju / Zou / Judima remain separate lexical records.** The 2022 Judima paper says “Ju” means beer and “Dima” is the tribe name; this is an etymological gloss, not proof that all products containing “Ju” share a single recipe. Sarma (2017) quotes Bordoloi et al. (1987) for Dimasa rice beer called “zou.” Keep “zou” as a historical broad label, not a synonym merge.

## Archive decisions

- Register Longmailai (2024) as SRC-339 and Borah & Sarma (2022) as SRC-340.
- Preserve source-native spellings and categories: Judi, Jugap, Juharo; and Judi as the ritual rice beer in SRC-340.
- Record Humao as a starter association for the three categories in SRC-339 only.
- Do not assign the 14–15 Cachar archive villages or the four Dima Hasao ritual-study villages to beverage production.
- Do not merge Judi/Jumai, Jugap/Zukap/jugap (process stage), Juharo/Juhara/Juharao, or Zou/Ju/Judima.
- Next priority: inspect the Bodo and Dimasa Heritage Digital Archive's actual food and heritage-dictionary records, where the paper says entries may include alternate spellings, translations, audio, source, location and cultural narratives. Preserve any item-level cultural protocols and attribution; do not republish protected community material without permission.
