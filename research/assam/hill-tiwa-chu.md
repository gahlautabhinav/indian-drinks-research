# Hill-Tiwa Chu — research dossier

**Record ID:** ASM-023-HTC  
**Community:** Hill-Tiwa  
**Region:** Hamren subdivision, West Karbi Anglong (the 2014 source uses the historical district label Karbi Anglong)  
**Classification:** Rice-based alcoholic beverage; a sometimes-distilled form is mentioned but its identity remains unresolved  
**Evidence status:** One peer-reviewed ethnobotanical study documents the community-level tradition and ten named study villages; it does not map Chu preparation to each village or establish current production.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-23

## Summary

Teron and Borthakur (2014) document Hill-Tiwa ethnobotanical knowledge based on fieldwork conducted from 2007 to 2012 in the Hamren subdivision of the then Karbi Anglong district. Their study describes locally brewed rice beer called **Chu** as a common Hill-Tiwa alcoholic drink. The authors explicitly distinguish Hill-Tiwa plant-use patterns from those of Plains-Tiwas, so this archive gives Hill-Tiwa Chu a separate provisional record rather than merging it with Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu.

The paper says starter cakes are prepared from rice and plant ingredients. It names **Croton joufra** leaves as an ingredient and says **Amomum koenigii** or **Acacia pennata** may sometimes be used as substitutes. It also reports that some beer is distilled in a crude still. The article does not fully define whether the distilled product is the same Chu, a later process stage, or a separate local preparation. No separate distilled beverage record is created until this boundary is clearer.

## Named study villages

The methods section names ten study villages:

- Umdoba
- Morten
- Khumrei Khora
- Ulukunchi
- Umswai
- Boramli
- Khyabat
- Bor Rongkhoi
- Ghelani
- Tumpreng

These are ethnobotanical study locations, not a village-by-village production census. The article describes Chu at the Hill-Tiwa community level; it does not establish that every village listed prepares the same drink or the same starter formula.

The study-era administrative description is Hamren subdivision of Karbi Anglong district. The Government of Assam identifies West Karbi Anglong as the district formed from the former district's Hamren subdivision, with Hamren as headquarters. The archive uses **West Karbi Anglong (source-era district: Karbi Anglong)** for current normalization while retaining the original source-era wording. Individual village revenue boundaries have not been independently checked.

## Names and identity boundaries

- **Hill-Tiwa Chu:** retained as its own provisional canonical record because the source distinguishes Hill-Tiwa and Plains-Tiwa plant-use patterns.
- **Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu:** maintained separately under ASM-015-TZU, supported by studies in Morigaon and Nagaon districts.
- Similar Roman-script spelling does not establish identical product identity, starter composition, fermentation conditions or cultural use.
- **Distilled form:** the source reports that some beer is distilled in a crude still, but does not define its name or whether it is identical to the fermented Chu. This remains unresolved.

## Preparation and ingredients

The paper describes starter cakes made from rice and plant ingredients. Croton joufra leaves are named; Amomum koenigii and Acacia pennata are reported as occasional substitutes. These are source-reported details, not a universal recipe for all Hill-Tiwa households. The article does not supply a village-by-village ingredient table for Chu, so no such attribution is inferred here.

## Cultural context

The article describes Chu being served during **Wanchoa** and documents Hill-Tiwa plant use in socio-religious life. This supports a broad cultural association, not a claim that every household or village serves the same drink in the same way.

## Geographic confidence and current status

- **Historical study region:** Hamren subdivision, source-era Karbi Anglong district.
- **Named study villages:** ten villages listed above.
- **Current district normalization:** West Karbi Anglong, based on Assam government district information.
- **Village-level production:** not established for each named village.
- **Current practice:** requires fresh, locality-specific confirmation. Fieldwork from 2007–2012 is not proof of production in 2026.

## Research ethics and evidence limits

The article records community knowledge gathered with verbal consent from community elders. Any follow-up should use informed consent, acknowledge knowledge holders, respect restrictions on cultural knowledge, and avoid presenting published plant-use statements as permission to commercialize local knowledge.

The paper's plant-use and ethnobotanical statements are documentation of reported practices, not clinical evidence of medical efficacy or safety.
