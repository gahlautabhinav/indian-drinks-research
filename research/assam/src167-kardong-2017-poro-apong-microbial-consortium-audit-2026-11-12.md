# SRC-167 full-text audit: Po:ro Apong household starter versus laboratory consortium

**Research date:** 2026-11-12  
**Source:** Kardong, D.; Pegu, B. K.; Ghose Guha, B. (2017), *Current Trends in Pharmaceutical Research* 4(2):6–17.  
**DOI:** Not located in the inspected article.  
**Full text:** https://oldweb.dibru.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Article_21.pdf  
**Existing source ID:** SRC-167 (no duplicate source created).

## 1. Research question and product identity

The article explicitly investigates fermentation of Mising **Po:ro apong / Saimod**. It is not a generic study of all Apong forms. The experimental question is whether a selected laboratory microbial consortium, with or without a herbal ingredient mixture, can reproduce the fermentation behaviour of a household-made starter (HMS).

## 2. Materials and design

The authors collected HMS from Mising households in unspecified Mising-populated localities. No named village, district, household count, sample count or biological replicate structure is reported.

The MC consisted of:
- one amylolytic *Mucor* sp.;
- one amylolytic *Saccharomycopsis* sp.; and
- one alcohol-fermenting *Saccharomyces* sp.

All three are described as isolates from HMS. The article gives genus-level identities only. LMSP combined the MC with a powdered herbal concoction and laboratory-grade starch at a 1:3 ratio, made into a paste with distilled water and sterilized before inoculation. LMSN was the corresponding non-inoculated preparation. HMS was the household-made starter comparator.

Initial microbial loads printed in Table 3: LMSP 1.03 × 10³ ± 215; MC 1.7 × 10³ ± 267; HMS 5.49 × 10⁷ ± 874; LMSN is shown as a dash. The endpoint is 144 hours (six days).

**Important unit caution:** 3.1 ml/100 g fermented mash is not an ABV percentage. Do not compare it directly with % v/v values in other Apong papers.

## 4. What can be concluded

Within this experiment, HMS produced the highest reported ethanol and titratable acidity, lowest pH and reducing sugar, and a distinct fruity smell. The selected MC and LMSP did not reproduce the HMS result. The authors suggest that microbial diversity and relative loads may matter; the experiment does not establish the contribution of any individual plant species.

## 5. Limitations and unresolved inconsistencies

- The paper reports only the 144-hour endpoint, not a longitudinal fermentation time series.
- Replicate number/type behind the ± values is not specified clearly in the accessible methods; statistical reporting is insufficient to reconstruct the design.
- Abstract says 13 herb ingredients; results and Table 1 report 14 species. Retain this discrepancy.
- Isolates are identified only as *Mucor* sp., *Saccharomycopsis* sp. and *Saccharomyces* sp.; no species or accession numbers should be inferred.
- Household HMS and sterilized laboratory starch-based preparations are not fully equivalent experimental substrates.
- No village or household provenance is supplied.
- Traditional medicinal interpretations in the herb table are not clinical evidence.
- The reference to Kardong et al. (2012) SRC-136 does not establish identical samples, starter material or a continuous experiment.

## 6. Archive decision

SRC-167 remains the existing source record. This audit adds a more precise interpretation of its experimental design, endpoint values and limitations to the Apong microbiology crosswalk. It does not justify a new source ID, locality, microbial taxon, standard recipe or canonical process change.
