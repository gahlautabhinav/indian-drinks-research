# SRC-105 — biochemical sample study of five Assam rice beverages

**Source:** Das, A. J.; Khawas, P.; Miyaji, T.; Deka, S. C. (2014). “HPLC and GC-MS analyses of organic acids, carbohydrates, amino acids and volatile aromatic compounds in some varieties of rice beer from northeast India.” *Journal of the Institute of Brewing*, 120(3), 244–252. https://doi.org/10.1002/jib.134  
**Audit date:** 2026-10-23  
**Evidence type:** Primary laboratory analysis with a small, historical multi-state sample set.

## 1. Assam sample crosswalk

The paper reports collection of nine rice-beer samples across Assam, Nagaland, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh during November 2010–February 2011. Five sample codes are labelled as Assam beverages:

| Sample code | Source-reported beverage | Community label | Collection locality | Alcohol (% as reported) | pH |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| AsB1 | Jou-bishi | Bodo | Gossaigaon, Assam | 4.33 ± 0.07 | 4.22 ± 0.04 |
| AsB2 | Lao-pani | Adivasi | Gossaigaon, Assam | 3.99 ± 0.03 | 4.56 ± 0.02 |
| AsB3 | Hor-alank | Karbi | Diphu, Assam | 5.09 ± 0.24 | 4.72 ± 0.01 |
| AsB4 | Xaj-pani | Ahom | Sibsagar, Assam | 4.12 ± 0.01 | 4.39 ± 0.01 |
| AsB5 | Apong | Mising | Lakhimpur, Assam | 4.77 ± 0.04 | 4.31 ± 0.08 |

The authors also measured organic acids, carbohydrates, amino acids and volatile/semi-volatile aromatic compounds using HPLC and GC-MS. Lactic acid was reported as predominant among measured organic acids across the sample set; chemical profiles varied between samples.

## 2. Interpretation and caveats

- **These are sample measurements, not canonical specifications.** Each named Assam beverage/locality appears as one sample code in Table 1. Analytical triplicates and reported standard deviations describe laboratory replicates, not independent households or a representative survey.
- **The data are historical.** Samples were collected in 2010–2011; they do not establish current brewing, prevalence or contemporary alcohol concentration.
- **Locality resolution is limited.** Gossaigaon, Diphu, Sibsagar and Lakhimpur are collection localities/district-level labels; the paper does not provide a named producing village for these samples in Table 1. Place-register rows therefore state sample collection, not village production.
- **Names are preserved as printed.** “Jou-bishi”, “Lao-pani”, “Hor-alank”, “Xaj-pani” and “Apong” are retained as source-reported labels. The Apong label alone does not identify Nogin versus Po:ro; Lao-pani is not automatically merged with every Haria/Haaz tradition.
- **Do not adopt the paper’s broad safety conclusion.** A small chemical-characterisation study cannot establish that alcohol consumption is safe for people generally. Sample-specific chemical findings are not clinical evidence of benefit or safety.
- **Nutrient presence is not a health outcome.** Detecting amino acids, carbohydrates, organic acids or volatile compounds does not establish therapeutic effect or overall nutritional superiority.
