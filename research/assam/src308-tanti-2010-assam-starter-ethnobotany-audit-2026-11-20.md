# SRC-308 — Tanti et al. (2010) Assam starter ethnobotany audit

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Source:** Bhaben Tanti, Lisha Gurung, Hridip K. Sarma and Alak K. Buragohain (2010), “Ethnobotany of starter cultures used in alcohol fermentation by a few ethnic tribes of Northeast India,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 9(3), July 2010, pp. 463–466.  
**Official full text:** https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9774/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20463-466.pdf

## 1. Retrieval and study design

The complete four-page article was recovered from the official NIScPR Online Periodical Repository. It reports a study of starter cultures used by 11 ethnic communities in six Northeast Indian states: Assam, Manipur, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh. The methods describe visits to remote villages, structured/semi-structured questionnaires, discussions with village chiefs and local men and women, and prior informed consent.

The paper reports 440 participants overall (308 men and 132 women); 242 participants (approximately 55%) supplied information for all methods. The latter complete-information group comprised 134 men and 108 women. These are whole-study totals, not Assam-only or Dimasa-only counts. The article does not publish an Assam-specific respondent breakdown, community-level sample sizes, village names, district allocations or sample-to-locality mapping.

The authors state that plant specimens were identified using references and Botanical Survey of India, Shillong herbarium specimens, and that voucher specimens were deposited in the Gauhati University Department of Botany herbarium. No voucher numbers, collection dates, collectors' numbers or plant-to-voucher crosswalk are provided in the article.

## 2. Assam starter and beverage associations in Table 1

The article's Table 1 reports the following:

| Community label | State | Substrate | Starter name (printed) | Liquor name (printed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adivasi | Assam | Rice | Dabai | Haria |
| Dimasa | Assam | Rice | Hamao | Zu |
| Ahom | Assam | Rice | Xajar Pitha | Xajpani |
| Mishing | Assam | Sticky rice | Apong kusure | Apong |
| Bodo | Assam | Rice | emao | Zu |
| Karbi | Assam | Rice | Thap | Arak |

The paper therefore uses **Zu for both Dimasa and Bodo**, while assigning different starters (Hamao and emao). This is a source-specific table entry. It does not establish that the two Zu products are identical, nor does it explain the relationship between the Dimasa Zu label and Judima.

The Dimasa starter is printed as “Hamao” here. Other papers use “Humao”; preserve the spelling in the source record and do not silently standardise it.

## 3. Assam botanical observations

The article reports 23 plant species across all 11 communities. The following are the Assam-specific observations relevant to this archive.

### Dimasa
Table 2 lists:
- *Hedyotis diffusa* Willd. (printed name; Rubiaceae), local name “Banjaluk”.
- *Solanum ferox* L. (Solanaceae), local name “Katahibengana”.

The article does not provide a detailed Dimasa starter-cake preparation sequence in the results prose. These taxa should be recorded as source-reported Dimasa starter-associated plants, not as a complete recipe or as a verified universal ingredient list for Judima/Humao.

### Bodo
The results prose describes Zu as a rice beer prepared using starter cakes called emao. It says the cakes are made from husked rice and leaves of “Atrocarpus integrifolia” (printed spelling), with wild herbs including *Clerodendrum viscosum* and *Polygonum hydropiper*. The likely botanical spelling correction for “Atrocarpus” is not silently applied in the transcription; retain the printed form in quotations and flag it as an apparent typographical error. Table 2 lists the corresponding plant names/local names in the Bodo section.

### Adivasi
The prose describes Haria preparation with dry leaves of *Nicotiana tabacum* and *Polygonum hydropiper*. These are source-reported ingredients; the paper does not provide named Assam villages or a full household process.

### Ahom
The prose states that seeds of “Datura metal” are added to Ahom rice beer, Xajpani. Table 2 prints “Datura metal” and also lists *Zanthoxylum nitidum*, *Clerodendrum viscosum*, *Adenanthera lucidor* and *Solanum viarum* within the Ahom group. These spellings are transcribed as printed. Do not silently correct “metal” to *metel* or “lucidor” to another taxon without checking herbarium vouchers or an authoritative taxonomic source.

### Karbi
The prose says leaves of *Dryopteris* sp. are added during rice-beer fermentation. Table 1 identifies the starter as Thap and liquor as Arak. The paper does not give a complete Thap cake recipe in the extracted prose.

### Mishing
The article describes rice-flour starter cakes mixed with dry powdered plants and additional *Piper longum* seeds. It distinguishes Nagin-apong (half-boiled sticky rice mixed directly with Kusure starter) and Poro-apong (rice husk added). Table 2 lists multiple Mishing-associated plant taxa. The article does not provide locality-level or household-specific allocation.

**Scope caution:** Table 2's 23-species total covers all 11 communities across six states. It must not be reported as 23 Assam species or as a Dimasa-only list.

## 4. Critical cross-source interpretation: Hamao / Humao / Zu / Judima

This source is important because it records Dimasa Hamao as the starter and Zu as the liquor. Later Dimasa-focused sources in the archive describe Humao/Umhu as a starter associated with Judima. SRC-304 (2011) gives a generic starter account and uses Humao in a Dimasa context; SRC-306 (2018) explicitly describes Emao as Bodo and Humao as Dimasa; SRC-307 (2018) analyses five Assam Humao starter samples but does not disclose community or district provenance.

None of these observations by itself resolves whether:
- Zu is a distinct Dimasa beverage label, a broader category, a synonym used by some informants, or a source/translation issue;
- Judima is a distinct product name within a larger Dimasa Zu category;
- Hamao and Humao are simply spelling variants or reflect distinct local forms;
- the Dimasa plants listed in 2010 are still used, or were used at the unnamed study sites.

Do not merge the 2010 Dimasa Zu into ASM-001-JUD (Judima), do not create a new canonical beverage row solely from this one table, and do not attach the 2010 Dimasa plant list to every later Humao recipe.

## 5. Limitations and transcription cautions

1. No Assam village, district, household, producer, collection date or beverage-sample locality is named.
2. No Assam-specific respondent count is reported; 440 and 242 are whole-study totals.
3. Voucher specimens are said to be deposited at Gauhati University, but voucher IDs and plant-to-voucher mapping are absent.
4. Several scientific names appear to contain typographical or taxonomic issues: “Atrocarpus integrifolia”, “Datura metal”, “Piper nigram”, “Adenanthera lucidor”. Preserve printed forms and resolve only through voucher/catalogue evidence.
5. The paper sometimes describes plants added to the starter and sometimes ingredients added during beverage fermentation. Do not assume every plant in Table 2 is a component of the starter cake in the same sense.
6. The paper's general statement that some plants are believed to add intoxicating properties is not experimental evidence of pharmacological effect or safety.

5. Keep the archive's existing Bodo Zu/Jou records separate from Dimasa Zu and from other communities' Zu/Zao/Chu products.

**Conclusion:** SRC-308 provides an unusually valuable early primary ethnobotanical record and a concrete herbarium acquisition lead. Its most important contribution is the Dimasa Hamao–Zu pairing and the two Dimasa plant taxa, but missing villages and voucher identifiers prevent a defensible locality or botanical crosswalk. The archive therefore records the source and opens a targeted terminology/voucher gap rather than creating a premature beverage record.
