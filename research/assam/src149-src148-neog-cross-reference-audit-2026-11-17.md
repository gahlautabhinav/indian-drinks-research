# SRC-149 cross-reference audit: Neog's 2015 record and SRC-148

**Research date:** 2026-11-17  
**Purpose:** Test whether the recovered 2014 Mising Apong article co-authored by Mridula Neog can clarify the missing 2015 *INTUIT* article or its associated UGC minor research project.  
**Decision:** Treat SRC-148 as a related, distinct primary article and acquisition lead. Do not substitute its data for SRC-149 or infer that it is the UGC MRP report.

## 1. Direct author overlap established

Kakojan College's official faculty profile for Mridula Neog lists the 2015 publication:

“Biochemical investigation of Fermented Rice Product of Mishing Tribe of Jorhat District,” *Intuit*, Vol. II, ISSN 2319-2097.

The same profile separately lists a UGC-sponsored Minor Research Project titled “Ethno-biological Investigation and Quality Assessment of Fermented Rice Products of Mishing Tribe of Jorhat District of Assam.”

The recovered SRC-148 article, “Mishing’s Apong – An Intangible Cultural Heritage of Assam,” names Babita Phukan Borkotoky, **Mridula Neog**, and Iswar Chandra Barua as authors. This confirms author overlap between the two research records. It does not confirm shared sample batches, shared datasets, the full author list of SRC-149, or a direct article-to-project relationship.

## 2. What SRC-148 actually documents

The accessible digital issue prints the article on pages 59–62 of *The Exalted Edition / IJHEPS*, Issue 7. The issue masthead states January 2014, while the page 2 running header contains January 2015 and the FlipHTML5 host says it was published 17 January 2015. Retain the printed masthead year (2014) in SRC-148 while preserving the date conflict; do not infer that SRC-149 belongs to this issue or journal.

SRC-148's methods report:
- Repeated field survey in Mising-dominated areas of Jorhat district.
- Prepared Epop/Epop pitha and Apong samples collected mostly from five named villages: **Sirum, Budubari, Boloma, Bhomoraguri and Arunamukh**.
- Plant collection in April–June of 2011 and 2012.
- Voucher specimens prepared and preserved in the Kakojan College Herbarium, with identification support from Assam Agricultural University, Jorhat, and BSI Eastern Circle, Shillong.
- Microbial culture and morphology-based identification, plus biochemical analyses including Kjeldahl protein, Soxhlet fat, Anthrone carbohydrate, distillation/hydrometry alcohol, paper chromatography of free amino acids and pH measurement.

The article reports 51 starter-plant species in 39 genera and 31 families, but the table image/text is not reliably transcribed in this pass. No reconstructed plant inventory is created.

The paper distinguishes:
- Nogin Apong: tightly cooked rice, usually 4–8 Epop cakes/kg, 3–4 days in pots.
- Po:ro Apong: rice-husk/hay ash mixed into cooked rice before starter addition, 15–20 days in pots, then water poured through a banana-leaf-lined conical bamboo frame and filtrate collected.

Its abstract/results report up to 5% alcohol for both varieties, more carbohydrate/protein/fat and higher calculated calorific value in Nogin than Po:ro, and approximately 11 free amino acids in each. These are the study's reported results, not a universal product specification or safety/health conclusion. Its microbial results are culture-method-dependent and do not establish absence of bacteria in all Mising Apong.

The five villages are described as the main sample-collection sites; the article does not allocate individual beverage types, starter plants or analytical batches to each village. Do not create five village-specific beverage production claims from this study.

## 3. How SRC-148 changes the SRC-149 retrieval strategy

The author overlap and shared Jorhat research subject make these requests more focused:

1. Ask Mridula Neog whether the 2015 *INTUIT* article is a separate analysis of the same 2011–2012 field collection used in SRC-148, a later sample set, or an unrelated project output.
2. Ask whether SRC-149 appeared in *INTUIT* Vol. II (2013), Vol. III (2014), or Vol. IV (2015), and request the issue masthead, contents page and article first/last pages.
3. Ask whether the UGC MRP is the funding source acknowledged in SRC-148, whether it is the same project listed in the faculty profile, and whether a final report, sample register, plant/voucher annexure or biochemical tables survive.
4. Ask for the Kakojan College Herbarium voucher accession register for the 2011–2012 Mising Apong collections. The article says vouchers were deposited but supplies no accession identifiers in the accessible text.
5. Ask whether the five villages listed in SRC-148 were the complete collection set for both Nogin and Po:ro, or only the main sites, and whether site-specific sample metadata exist.

A shared author, district and research theme are a strong lead for document acquisition, but are not proof that the 2014 and 2015 papers share the same dataset or that the 2015 paper is a project report.

## 4. Evidence boundaries retained

- SRC-148 remains a separate article and is not duplicated as SRC-149.
- No new beverage, locality, producer, sample or village row is added.
- No plant taxa are reconstructed from the unreadable table.
- SRC-149's author list, correct *INTUIT* volume/year, pages, beverage identity, sample count, methods, numerical results and relationship to the UGC MRP remain unresolved.
- ASM-GAP-065 remains High/Open.
