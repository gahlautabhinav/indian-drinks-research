# SRC-358 — Sarmah, Pegu & Sanong (2022): Dhansiri field study and Judima sample provenance audit

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026

## Citation
Puja Sarmah, Bhaba Kumar Pegu and James Sanong (2022), “Documentation of Traditional Method of Preparation and Characterization of Judima, a Traditional Rice Beer of Dimasa Tribe of Assam and its Comparison with Old Preserved Judima,” *Biosciences Biotechnology Research Asia* 19(4), pp. 1133–1138. DOI: 10.13005/bbra/3062.
Full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366784741_Documentation_of_Traditional_Method_of_Preparation_and_Characterization_of_Judima_a_Traditional_Rice_Beer_of_Dimasa_Tribe_of_Assam_and_its_Comparison_with_Old_Preserved_Judima

## Exact locality and fieldwork
- Study area: Dima Hasao district, Assam.
- The authors state that the district had 695 villages and that **Dhansiri village** was selected for the field survey.
- Fieldwork took place in February 2022.
- The team conducted semi-structured, face-to-face interviews in the local language with 100 local people: 65 female and 35 male, ages 28–67; signed consent is reported.
- Freshly prepared and old preserved Judima samples were collected from a family in the same village. The family is described by the authors as well-known for preparing Judima over many generations and renowned for authenticity.

## Evidence classification
**Dhansiri is a confirmed Judima field-survey locality and a direct beverage sample/preparation locality.** This is stronger than merely reporting a cultural association or applicant address.

However:
- The family is not named.
- The paper does not say how many households in Dhansiri brew Judima.
- It does not provide a producer census or village-wide prevalence.
- Both fresh and preserved samples appear to derive from the same family; the text does not establish independent sampling across producers or households.
- The 100-person interview sample must not be mistaken for 100 brewers or 100 producer households.

Recommended archive relations:
- Dhansiri → Judima field survey (February 2022; 100 respondents): **supported**.
- Dhansiri → fresh and old preserved Judima samples collected: **supported**.
- Dhansiri → one unnamed family described as a multigenerational/authentic preparer: **supported, author-attributed**.
- Dhansiri → named producer identity: **not established**.
- Dhansiri → village-wide production prevalence / producer count: **not established**.

## Preparation account: source-specific, not universal
The article describes:
- Humao as the starter cake.
- Thempra / *Acacia pennata* bark as a cake ingredient.
- Bairing or Bora rice as preferred rice.
- Rice fermentation in earthen or bamboo vessels, with time varying by season.
- Other plant parts/taxa reported during field study: *Piper betle*, *Acacia pennata*, *Buddleja asiatica* and *Hedyotis scandens*.

These are the authors' documented account. The paper does not establish that every Dimasa household uses the same combination, quantities or sequence. Preserve this as one locality-linked recipe record and compare it against the existing Humao/Umhu/Emao variation gap rather than replacing it.

## Laboratory and interpretation cautions
- The abstract gives alcohol content in mg/ml, while Table 1 and the discussion report alcohol percentage as % v/v. This is an internal unit inconsistency requiring clarification from authors or raw data.
- The abstract says the differences are not statistically significant; table p-values are close to 0.05. Do not overstate fresh-versus-old differences.
- Triplicate analytical determinations are not equivalent to independent production batches or independent producer samples.
- The article's broad nutritional, medicinal and health-oriented discussion is not clinical evidence and should not be repeated as a health claim.
- The paper's sample provenance is a single unnamed family in Dhansiri; the 100 interview respondents do not expand the beverage sample to 100 households.

## Relationship to existing locality evidence
- Dibarai (SRC-351): about 50 respondents in a 2019 snowball-sampled Judima-focused household/community survey; raw-material collection and cultural association are discussed, but the paper does not enumerate brewing households.
- Tereh (SRC-003): The Locavore's 2023 report identifies Khimkho Hojai brewing Judima for her household.
- Dhansiri (SRC-358): 2022 field survey plus fresh/old beverage samples from one unnamed multigenerational preparer family.

These are different evidence types and should not be collapsed into a single measure of village production prevalence.

## Archive decision
Register SRC-358. Add Dhansiri as a field-survey and beverage-sampling/preparation locality with the explicit one-family limitation. Do not add a named producer or producer-count edge. Keep ASM-GAP-001 Open pending the GI producer list, official map, Statement of Case and independent producer/village corroboration.
