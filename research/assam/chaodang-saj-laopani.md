# Chaodang Saj / Laopani — research dossier

**Record ID:** ASM-014-CHS  
**Community:** Chaodang  
**Region:** Jorhat district, Upper Assam  
**Classification:** Fermented rice-based alcoholic beverage  
**Evidence status:** Core tradition documented; exact village names and current locality-by-locality practice remain unresolved.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-20

## Summary

A 2021 field study by Girish Gogoi documents preparation of **Saj or Laopani** among the Chaodang community of Upper Assam. The study says its information came from 80 traditional knowledge holders across 10 randomly selected villages in Jorhat district. The accessible abstract does not name those villages; the archive therefore records Jorhat district as the documented fieldwork area, not as a complete production map.

The source describes cooked rice inoculated with locally prepared starter cakes called **Su-pitha / Modor-pitha**. It records Bora (sticky rice), Chakuwa rice and ordinary rice as materials used. The paper reports 81 plant species from 47 families associated with starter-cake preparation in its study sample. This is a source-specific inventory, not a universal recipe for all Chaodang households.

## Names and boundaries

- **Saj** and **Laopani** are the names used for the Chaodang beverage in Gogoi (2021).
- **Su-pitha / Modor-pitha** refer to starter-culture cakes, not to the finished beverage.
- Laopani also appears in broader Assamese and Northeast Indian beverage writing. Similar naming alone does not establish that Chaodang Saj/Laopani is identical to Ahom, Bodo or other communities' beverages.
- The archive retains a single provisional Chaodang record while keeping cross-community equivalence unresolved.

## Ingredients and preparation

The study reports cooked rice and a traditionally prepared starter cake. Bora, Chakuwa and ordinary rice are named. It documents starter-cake preparation using plant materials and rice, but the exact ingredients and proportions vary in the source's community knowledge accounts.

Do not convert the paper's 81-species inventory into a fixed recipe: plants and preparation practices can vary by locality, household, season and availability. This dossier does not prescribe a fermentation time because a reliable universal duration has not been established from the accessible source text.

## Cultural context

Gogoi (2021) reports that Saj/Laopani is part of Chaodang cultural life and is prepared for Bihu, ritual functions, birth ceremonies and marriage ceremonies. These are reported contexts from the study, not a claim that every household or locality follows the same practice today.

The source describes the beverage as healthy and an energy booster; these are reported traditional beliefs and should not be presented as clinically established health effects.

## Geographic evidence

- **Jorhat district, Assam:** documented fieldwork area.
- **Ten randomly selected villages:** included in the study, but not named in the accessible abstract.
- **Current production status:** requires fresh, locality-specific confirmation. The existence of a 2021 ethnographic study is not proof that all sampled households still brew the beverage.

## Geographic context: do not confuse community distribution with beverage production

The article's introduction cites a 1962 government socio-economic survey of the Chaodang community in the Jorhat subdivision and a 2015 Assamese-language history of Jorhat. It describes an older settlement context of 42 Chaodang villages in the then subdivision, with 21 said to be close to one another, and mentions Chaodang communities elsewhere in Upper Assam, including Golaghat, Sivasagar, Lakhimpur and Dibrugarh.

These are **historical community-distribution leads**, reported through references cited by Gogoi (2021). They are not a current settlement census, and they do not establish that Saj/Laopani was prepared in all 42 villages or in each other district named. Do not add those places as confirmed beverage-production localities on this evidence alone.

The article PDF is discoverable online, but the accessible landing-page abstract does not name the ten sampled villages. The next extraction attempt should inspect the article body, tables, maps and figure captions. If those still do not identify the villages, retain the gap as unresolved rather than guessing.

## Evidence assessment

**Well supported within source scope**
- Chaodang community association.
- Saj / Laopani terminology in the study.
- Su-pitha / Modor-pitha starter terminology.
- Use of Bora, Chakuwa and ordinary rice in the reported practice.
- Fieldwork with 80 knowledge holders across 10 villages in Jorhat district.
- Reported cultural contexts including Bihu, ritual, birth and marriage ceremonies.

**Needs further verification**
- Names and coordinates of the ten villages.
- Which named starter plants are used in each village and household.
- Current production and intergenerational transmission.
- Local pronunciation and preferred written forms of Saj, Laopani, Su-pitha and Modor-pitha.
- Whether any Chaodang-specific process or product is locally distinguished from other beverages also called Laopani.
- Household-specific fermentation conditions and equipment.

## Research ethics

Treat published plant inventories as evidence of documented knowledge, not as permission to universalize or commercialize community knowledge. Any new fieldwork should use informed consent, acknowledge knowledge holders and respect restrictions on ceremonial or otherwise sensitive information.
