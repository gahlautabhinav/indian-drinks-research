# South Salmara-Mankachar district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for traditional fermented or alcoholic beverage evidence explicitly associated with present-day South Salmara-Mankachar district, including Goalpariya/Deshi, Koch-Rajbongshi, Mech, Rabha and other local community contexts.  
**Decision:** No qualifying beverage place row added. The district remains an evidence gap, not a district with no beverage tradition.

## Sources and findings reviewed

### 1. Official district profile — community context, not beverage evidence

The Government of Assam district profile describes the district's mixed culture, identifies Deshi/Goalpariya-speaking communities, and notes that Deshi groups trace ancestry to local indigenous groups including Koch, Rajbongshi, Mech, Jogi and Rabha. It also notes three tea gardens. The page does not document a named traditional beverage, brewing practice, producer or village-level beverage observation. [Official district profile](https://southsalmaramankachar.assam.gov.in/about-district/district-glance).

The draft District Survey Report similarly describes local language/community context and economic conditions, but the reviewed excerpt does not document a beverage practice. [Draft District Survey Report](https://southsalmaramankachar.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/public_utility/SSM%20DSR%20Report.pdf).

**Decision:** Use as administrative/community context only. Ancestry, community presence and tea-garden counts do not establish beverage preparation or consumption.

### 2. Assam-wide beverage literature

Saikia and Bori (2020) describe rice-beer traditions among several Assam communities, including Bodo and Rabha, but the accessible abstract does not allocate a drink or field site to South Salmara-Mankachar. [Article page](https://archives.palarch.nl/index.php/jae/article/view/3776).

The 2019 chapter *Natural Beverages of Assam and its Ethno Medicinal Value* discusses multiple community traditions at state level without identifying a South Salmara-Mankachar locality in the accessible abstract. [Publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/pii/B9780128166895000031).

### 3. Rabha and southern Assam research

Rabha Choko/Jonga mod studies located in this pass identify Goalpara field sites, not South Salmara-Mankachar. The 2024 and 2026 southern Assam beverage studies use district frames centred on Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi/Karimganj, not this district. [Rabha study](https://environmentandecology.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MS23.pdf); [southern Assam study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11459095/).

**Decision:** Do not transfer Goalpara fieldwork or southern Assam samples to South Salmara-Mankachar.

## Archive treatment

- No place row added.
- No beverage record or source-register entry added from broad community or administrative descriptions.
- Matrix remains at zero place rows.
- Preserve local community/linguistic context as a search lead, not as beverage evidence.

## Remaining research questions

1. Do Goalpariya/Deshi, Koch-Rajbongshi, Mech or Rabha community-held sources document a named beverage in present-day South Salmara-Mankachar?
2. Can historical Goalpara district ethnographies be mapped to villages now in this district?
3. Are there local-language theses, oral histories or household studies that document preparation, starter ingredients and current practice?

**Conclusion:** No source reviewed in this pass establishes a traditional beverage locality in present-day South Salmara-Mankachar. This bounded search result is not evidence that the district lacks beverage traditions.
