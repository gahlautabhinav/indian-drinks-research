# Mising Apong microbiology reconciliation: source, stage and isolate audit

**Research date:** 2026-11-10  
**Scope:** SRC-136, SRC-148, SRC-152, SRC-165 and SRC-167. This is a provenance reconciliation, not a census of the Mising microbiome.

## Executive finding

These five sources do not describe one interchangeable microbial community. They sample different material stages, use different methods and identify organisms at different taxonomic levels.

- **Starter cake / Epop:** SRC-148 reports three Saccharomyces species plus one Mucor and one Rhizopus; SRC-165 lists three Mising Apop-pitha isolates of Pediococcus pentosaceus; SRC-152 reports unnamed amylolytic/alcohol-fermenting isolates; SRC-167 uses three genus-level isolates in an experimental consortium.
- **Apong-labelled material:** SRC-165 separately lists Lactobacillus casei TEZU262 for “Apong, Mising”. It is not resolved as Poro or Nogin and cannot be mapped to a named household or district.
- **Mash / finished beverage:** SRC-136 gives total microbial-load counts for Epop, ripe mash and finished Po:ro, but no taxa for those counts. SRC-167 compares home-made and experimental mash at 144 hours. Neither is a taxonomic succession dataset.

## 1. Source-by-source evidence

### SRC-136 — Kardong et al. (2012)

Reported total microbial loads:
- Epop starter: 549 × 10^7 cfu/g
- Ripe mash / Po:ro arouk: 414 × 10^7 cfu/g
- Finished product: 8,700 ± 6.332 cfu/mm³

These are total-count measurements, not species/isolate identifications. The units differ (cfu/g versus cfu/mm³), and the paper does not establish a controlled longitudinal series with matched aliquots and comparable enumeration protocols. Do not convert them into a survival curve. The paper's separate 16-name Epop plant list was compiled through villager-expert interaction and literature survey; it is not proof that each plant came from the three households supplying finished samples.

### SRC-148 — Borkotoky, Neog & Barua

The reproduced article reports three Saccharomyces species, one Mucor and one Rhizopus from starter culture. It also reports no growth of Enterobacteriaceae or LAB on the culture plates used.

Limits:
- The accessible article text does not expose the full taxonomic table, species names for the three Saccharomyces, isolate codes or accessions.
- Repeated survey and collection mostly from five named Jorhat villages: Sirum, Budubari, Boloma, Bhomoraguri and Arunamukh. No household/informant denominator or village-by-isolate matrix is given.
- “No growth” applies only to selected media/incubation conditions. It does not prove universal absence of LAB or Enterobacteriaceae.
- The printed issue masthead says January 2014, while institutional/digital metadata points to 2015; source register retains printed issue year and notes the discrepancy.

### SRC-152 — Kardong UGC project report (2007–2009)

The accessible five-page Dibrugarh University report states:
- Three Epop samples from different, unnamed places.
- Seven pure isolates with amylolytic properties.
- Six isolates with alcohol-fermenting properties.
- Partial characterisation codes AY-3 (also amy-3), AY-1(19), AY-4 (Big cell) and FY-10.
- AY-3 + AY-4 + FY-10 proposed as a likely consortium for Po:ro/Saimod solid-state fermentation.

No species-level names, technical annexure, full isolate table, locality list or assay protocols are present in the accessible copy. AY/FY codes cannot be assigned to taxa reported by SRC-148 or SRC-167. The proposed consortium is not a validated household starter.

### SRC-165 — Das et al. (2019): direct Mising rows in Table 1

The open-access primary article's Mising rows are:

| Material label | Organism | Isolate | NCBI accession |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apop-pitha, Mising | Pediococcus pentosaceus | TEZU481 | KT273331 |
| Apop-pitha, Mising | Pediococcus pentosaceus | TEZU482 | KT273330 |
| Apop-pitha, Mising | Pediococcus pentosaceus | TEZU486 | KT273328 |
| Apong, Mising | Lactobacillus casei | TEZU262 | KR781613 |

Methods describe LAB isolation on MRS agar and identification by 16S rRNA sequencing. Samples came from households of different tribal communities in Assam, but there is no named village or household-to-isolate crosswalk. “Apong” is not subtype-resolved as Poro or Nogin. The combined table heading refers to rice beer/starter cake, so the exact stage for the Apong-labelled row remains cautious; the Apop-pitha rows are explicit starter-cake labels.

The paper's prose says three L. casei strains, while Table 1 lists four L. casei rows across all communities. For Mising, Table 1 explicitly lists one L. casei Apong isolate and three P. pentosaceus Apop-pitha isolates. Preserve these as four separate source rows. In-vitro acid/bile tolerance, antibiosis and antioxidant results do not establish clinical probiotic efficacy, food safety or health benefits from consuming Apong.

### SRC-167 — Kardong, Pegu & Ghose Guha (2017)

The paper compares traditional home-made starter (HMS) with laboratory-made starter/consortium material for Po:ro/Saimod. The laboratory consortium contains:
- Mucor sp.
- Saccharomycopsis sp.
- Saccharomyces sp.

At 144 hours, HMS mash is reported at pH 4.78; reducing sugar 17 ± 0.12 mg/g; titratable acidity 0.22 ± 0.01% lactic acid; ethanol 3.1 ± 0.02 ml/100 g mash. The lab consortium is an experimental comparator, not a customary household recipe. No named village or household-level sample provenance is given. Six days is not equivalent to the full household maturation period reported in SRC-136.

Do not assume SRC-167's Mucor and Saccharomyces are the same isolates as SRC-148's organisms. Saccharomycopsis is an additional genus and must not be substituted for Rhizopus.

## 2. Cross-source reconciliation

| Dimension | Supported | Not supported |
|---|---|---|
| Saccharomyces | SRC-148 reports three species in Epop; SRC-167 reports Saccharomyces sp. in its lab consortium | Same species/isolates across papers |
| Mucor | Genus-level reports in SRC-148 and SRC-167 | Same isolate or household provenance |
| Rhizopus | One species reported in SRC-148 | Presence/absence in other studies |
| Saccharomycopsis | Genus-level isolate in SRC-167 consortium | Occurrence in SRC-148 or mapping to SRC-152 codes |
| LAB | SRC-165 lists Mising P. pentosaceus and L. casei; SRC-148 reports no LAB growth on its selected plates | Universal presence or absence in all Epop |
| Total counts | SRC-136 reports counts for starter, mash and finished liquid | Direct stage-to-stage comparison or taxonomic succession |
| AY/FY project codes | SRC-152 reports activity counts and partial codes | Species mapping to SRC-148 or SRC-167 |

## 3. Why SRC-148 and SRC-165 are not contradictory

SRC-148's LAB-negative result is method-bound. SRC-165 deliberately isolates LAB using MRS agar supplemented with CaCO3 and bromocresol purple. Different media, sampling and isolation targets can yield different recoveries. The archive should say that separate studies recovered different organisms under different protocols—not that Mising starter categorically lacks LAB or categorically contains the same LAB in every household.
