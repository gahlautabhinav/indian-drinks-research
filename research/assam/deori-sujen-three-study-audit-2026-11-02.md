# Deori Sujen: five-study starter, locality and process audit — 2026-11-02

## Executive finding

Five studies now document Deori Sujen starter culture under overlapping but not identical names: **Mod pitha** (SRC-009, 2007), **Chuzae-pitha** (SRC-010, 2010), **Sujen pitha / Chuzae-pitha / Mod pitha** (SRC-012, 2023), and Mod pitha in a two-village Dibrugarh account (SRC-131, 2023). The publications support overlapping terminology, but do not establish universal synonymy across all Deori territorial groups (khel), districts or households.

The five source-specific plant inventories contain 32, 15, 13, 19 and 21 listed taxa respectively. SRC-018 is a review article with a section-specific Tinsukia field-derived account; its locality and sampling details are less complete than the other studies. They should not be collapsed into a single canonical recipe. Simple normalized binomial comparison gives five overlapping names between SRC-009 and SRC-010, seven between SRC-009 and SRC-012, and six between SRC-010 and SRC-012. A few of these comparisons depend on correcting obvious source typos (e.g. “Coitus speciosus” vs Costus speciosus; “Drimaria cordata” vs Drymaria cordata), so overlap counts are candidate name matches, not voucher-confirmed identity.

## 1. Source and field-site evidence

The fourth source, SRC-131, is documented in the addendum below; its 19-taxon table is a separate Dibrugarh study inventory.

### SRC-009 — Deori et al. (2007)

“Ethnobotany of Sujen—a local rice beer of Deori tribe of Assam,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 6(1):121–125.

- Fieldwork: 2003–2004.
- Named villages: Sunpura Deori gaon, Sadiya (Tinsukia); Kololuwah (Dibrugarh); Rajabari (Sivasagar).
- Researchers interviewed villagers who regularly prepared Sujen and observed the entire preparation process.
- The authors say methods were the same across the three villages, but the number of plant species used varied from family to family.
- The combined table lists 32 Mod pitha plants, without allocating each taxon to an individual village.
- Plant identification was undertaken with consultation of the Regional Herbarium Assam and Botanical Survey of India, Eastern Circle.
- The article's statement that more than 100 plants were used “in olden days” is a recollection, not a 100-plus-species inventory.

The 32-row source-faithful transcription is `sujen-mod-pitha-32-species-src009.csv`.

### SRC-010 — Baruah et al. (2010)

“Plants used in the preparation of starter culture of rice-beer by the Ahom, Deori and Mishing tribes of Dhemaji District, Assam, India,” *Pleione* 4(2):288–293.

- Fieldwork: October 2009–January 2010.
- Deori field site: Borbam Deori gaon, Bordoloni block, Dhemaji.
- Informants: two older women in the selected Deori village.
- Deori starter is explicitly named **Chuzae-pitha**; 15 taxa are listed.
- Plant specimens were preserved in the Darrang College Herbarium (DCH), and accession codes appear in the article.
- This is the strongest voucher-linked, named Deori starter inventory among the three reviewed sources, but it is a small single-village historical study.

The 15-row transcription is `sujen-chuzae-pitha-15-species-src010.csv`.

### SRC-012 — Duarah et al. (2023)

“Medicinal plants used in rice beer starter culture by different tribes in Assam: an ethnobotanical survey,” *International Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences and Research* 14(6):3012–3027.

- Survey districts: Sibsagar, Nagaon and Bongaigaon.
- Authors state Deori, Mising and Ahom data were collected in Sibsagar; prior information was used to identify the relatively few households who prepare starter culture.
- Deori Table 3 lists 13 taxa under the heading “Sujen pitha, Chuzae-pitha, or Mod pitha”.
- The accessible paper does not name the Deori villages, report a Deori-specific sample size or allocate taxa to particular villages.
- Methods include semi-structured questionnaires, discussions with experienced women, village/forest walks and plant collection/authentication with an Assam Agricultural University taxonomist.

The 13-row transcription is `sujen-mod-pitha-13-species-src012.csv`. Printed names are retained, including obvious taxonomic/transcription concerns, rather than silently corrected.

## 2. Terminology: Mod pitha and Chuzae-pitha

Evidence supports that authors use both names for the Deori Sujen starter:
- SRC-009 uses Mod pitha.
- SRC-010 uses Chuzae-pitha at Borbam Deori gaon, Dhemaji.
- SRC-012 explicitly groups “Sujen pitha, Chuzae-pitha, or Mod pitha” in its Deori table heading.

This is publication-level evidence of overlapping terms, not proof that all Deori speakers use them interchangeably. Local usage may vary by locality, household or Deori khel. Preserve each source's term and request community/language review before normalizing the archive's canonical starter label.

## 4. Process and seasonal observations

### SRC-009 (2007; historical field observations)

- Mod pitha: cleaned plant parts sun-dried; 3–5 kg rice soaked about two hours; rice and plant materials ground together; 2–3 old starter cakes used as inoculant; sticky paste formed into small cakes.
- Cakes dried above the kitchen fireplace for a couple of weeks and stored in a small earthen pot; paper reports storage of unused starter for 2–3 months.
- Sujen: cooked rice mixed with powdered Mod pitha and covered with leaves; fermentation in an earthen vessel.
- Reported fermentation: 3–4 days in summer, about one week in winter.
- The article reports historical finished-beverage storage observations (up to one month in winter and 2–3 weeks in summer before souring). These are not food-safety recommendations and must not be presented as safe storage periods.

### SRC-010 (2010; Dhemaji village account)

- Starter-making seasons reported as September–October and February–March.
- Plant materials and softened rice are powdered separately, mixed with a powdered parent starter, shaped into cakes and dried in sunlight or over mild kitchen fire.
- Clay pitchers and hearth-side storage are described.
- These are the authors' observations from the selected village and two informants, not a current statewide calendar.

### SRC-012 (2023; comparative survey)

- Also reports two starter preparation periods: September–October and February–March.
- The paper does not provide a Deori village-specific fermentation or storage schedule in the accessible text.
- Its ethnomedicinal columns record reported community beliefs, not demonstrated clinical effects.

## Addendum — two named Dibrugarh field sites (SRC-131; 2023)

A further primary field account was located during the 2026-11-02 continuation pass:

Bharali, Anup Jyoti; Deori, Chinmoyee; Das, Jitu (2023), “A Study on the Use of Fermented Rice Beverage (Sujen) by the Deoris of Brahmaputra Valley: Socio-Cultural Values and Health Related Issues,” *PARIPEX – Indian Journal of Research* 12(11), November 2023. Canonical source ID: SRC-131.

The paper names two Dibrugarh field villages: **Kalalowa Adarsha Deori Village** and **Madhupur Adarsha Deori Janajati village**. It identifies residents of both as Tengaponiya division and describes Sujen preparation and ritual/cultural use. The paper does not state fieldwork dates, informant count, number of producer households, or village-specific plant allocation. These are therefore named contemporary publication field sites, not quantified present-day production censuses. Two locality records ASM-PL-110 and ASM-PL-111 were added with those limitations.

The paper's Table of starter plants lists 19 taxa, transcribed without silent correction to `sujen-dibrugarh-19-plants-src131.csv`. The table includes potentially problematic source-reported entries (for example, *Ananas comosus* with “bark” as the part used and *Pteridium aquilinum* with “fronds /roots”). These are preserved as published and must be checked against author notes, vouchers and community review. The 19 taxa are not allocated between the two villages.

Its source-specific Sujen account describes rice cooked with husk (tuh), cooled on a bamboo dola, mixed with Mod pitha, covered with banana leaves and bihlongoni leaves, then transferred to an earthen koloh vessel for fermentation. It reports approximately one week to full fermentation. It also records a belief that citrus contact spoils the preparation; this is a reported belief, not a verified chemical effect or food-safety instruction. The paper does not give a current seasonality calendar.

This fourth inventory reinforces the need to preserve separate source lists. It does not establish that the 19 plants are used in both villages, that the list is current, or that all Deori divisions use this recipe. The new source was added to the register as SRC-131; ASM-GAP-009, -010 and -011 were updated; the canonical Sujen record now includes the two named Dibrugarh sites and explicitly retains the lack of producer counts and village-level plant mapping.

## Addendum 2 — SRC-018: Tinsukia Sujen table and its relationship to the 2007 inventory (2026-11-02)

Bhuyan and Baishya (2013), “Ethno medicinal value of various plants used in the preparation of traditional rice beer by different tribes of Assam, India,” *Drug Invention Today* 5(4):335–341, is explicitly labelled a **Review Article** in its front matter. However, the Sujen subsection states that “this study was carried out in Tinsukia district” and the acknowledgements thank village women of Tinsukia district for sharing information about rice-beer preparation and starter plants. This is sufficient to treat the Sujen subsection as containing a field-derived account, but the article does not identify the village(s), survey dates, informant count, interview/collection protocol, or specimen vouchers. Its reference 9 is Deori, Begum and Mao (2007), but that citation alone does not establish that Table 4 is copied from the 2007 study.

Table 4 lists 21 taxa. A source-faithful transcription is stored in `sujen-tinsukia-21-species-src018.csv`. Eight exact binomial names overlap with the combined 32-taxon SRC-009 inventory: *Allium sativum*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Capsicum annuum*, *Melastoma malabathricum*, *Oryza sativa*, *Saccharum officinarum*, *Scoparia dulcis* and *Solanum torvum*. The overlap is only a comparison of published names against the combined SRC-009 table, which pools three villages without village-level taxon allocation. It does not establish duplicate data, shared specimens, or independent sampling.

The 2013 paper’s Tinsukia village(s) could overlap with **Sunpura Deori gaon, Sadiya**, already documented in SRC-009, but the paper does not name the site and the relationship cannot be resolved from the accessible article. A district-level, unnamed study-scope record ASM-PL-112 was therefore added, not a new named village. The source register entry SRC-018 was amended to distinguish its review classification from the field-derived content in this subsection.

The article reports a preparation sequence of overnight soaking of rice; grinding plant parts with soaked rice into dough; forming round cakes around 4 cm in diameter and drying them on a bamboo sieve; boiling rice, cooling it, adding Mod pitha, sealing in an earthen pot with banana leaves and fermenting for 4–5 days. It reports storage of Sujen in earthen pots for 1–2 months at room temperature. These are source-reported historical descriptions, not current universal practice or food-safety recommendations.

**Interpretive status:** retain SRC-018 as a field-derived Tinsukia district account embedded in a review article; do not count it as a confirmed independent village survey until authors clarify the field site and relationship to SRC-009. ASM-GAP-009, -010 and -011 remain open.
