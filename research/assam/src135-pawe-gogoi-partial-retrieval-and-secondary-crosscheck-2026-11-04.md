# SRC-135 Pawe & Gogoi (2013) — partial retrieval audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-04. Existing source ID: SRC-135.

## Retrieval status

The citation is corroborated by the Botanical Survey of India scientist bibliography and by Mili & Sundriyal (2023). The ResearchGate page for the original article is marked full-text available, but only a partial introductory preview was accessible; opening the publication/PDF returned HTTP 403. The preview distinguishes Nogin Apong from Poro Apong, says rice-husk and rice-straw ash are mixed with cooked rice for Poro, and states that the paper documents Poro preparation plus Apob/natural-yeast-cake plants with botanical names, families, vernacular names and plant parts.

The complete four-page article, full plant table, methods, named field localities, informant/sample design and detailed process remain inaccessible. No new plant or village rows are justified from the partial preview.

## Secondary cross-check — SRC-144

Basumatary et al. (2023), “Ethnic fermented foods and beverages of North-Eastern India: A comprehensive review,” European Chemical Bulletin 12(10), 13119–13145, DOI 10.48047/ecb/2023.12.10.9192023.25/08/2023, was retrieved as an author-uploaded review. Its Apong section attributes a detailed Poro process to Pawe et al. (2013) and Handique & Deka (2016): a summer variant with rice-husk/straw ash, starter ratio around 1:45, earthen Koloh, 4–5 days above a fireplace, then water addition and cloth filtration; and a winter variant with 100 g starter per 3 kg cooked rice, 30–35°C, 8–9 days, and mash called poro arouk. These are secondary multi-source summaries, not verified primary-text extractions. Do not assign every step to SRC-135 alone.

The review separately lists 17 Apong starter plants and attributes that list to Gogoi, Dutta & Mondal (2013), not Pawe & Gogoi. It is a distinct source/list and must not be merged with the Pawe & Gogoi Apob table. Preserve printed forms such as “Drymeria cordata”, “Phogocanthus thyrsiflorus”, “Ipoemea sp” and “Cyclosorus exlensa” pending primary table/voucher checks.

## Process discrepancies

- SRC-136 (Kardong et al. 2012) describes 100 g powdered Epop per 3 kg cooked rice, about 20 days at 30–35°C, ripe mash called Po:ro arouk, then warm-water extraction through a bamboo Ta:suk.
- SRC-144 reports different seasonal variants, including 4–5 days in summer and 8–9 days in winter.
- SRC-005 (Mili & Sundriyal 2023) describes ash addition and uses distillation wording in its introduction, unlike SRC-136's Ta:suk extraction.
- Distillation, cloth filtration and Ta:suk extraction are not interchangeable. Preserve source-specific accounts until primary text and process diagrams are checked. Medicinal claims are not clinical evidence or safety evidence.
