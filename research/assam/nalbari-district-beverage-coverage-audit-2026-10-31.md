# Nalbari district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for traditional beverage evidence explicitly associated with present-day Nalbari district, particularly Bodo, Rabha and other community traditions.  
**Decision:** No qualifying Nalbari place row added. The district remains an evidence gap, not a district with no beverage tradition.

## Sources and findings reviewed

### 1. Basumatary and Barmahalia (2025), *The Use of Rice-Beer in the Bodo Society*

Gauhati University's publication record identifies a 2025 article by Rangina Basumatary and Faguna Barmahalia, in volume 11, issue 1, published by Nalbari Commerce College. The accessible record provides the title and bibliographic metadata but no abstract, field methods, village list or study district. [Gauhati University record](https://gauhati.ac.in/publications/the-use-of-rice-beer-in-the-bodo-society/).

**Important distinction:** The publisher is Nalbari Commerce College. That does not establish that the article's research was conducted in Nalbari district or that it documents a Nalbari beverage locality.

**Decision:** High-priority full-text retrieval lead, but not place evidence until the article's methods and locality data are inspected.

### 2. Statewide Assam rice-beer literature

Saikia and Bori (2020) discuss rice-beer traditions among several Assam communities, including Bodo and Rabha, but the accessible abstract does not map any beverage to Nalbari. [Article page](https://archives.palarch.nl/index.php/jae/article/view/3776).

The 2017 paper *Rice Beer and Local Communities of Assam* describes fieldwork in several districts, including Udalguri, Kamrup, Goalpara, Morigaon, Tinsukia-Dibrugarh and others; Nalbari is not named in its stated fieldwork list. [Full article PDF](https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jhss/papers/Vol.%2022%20Issue7/Version-2/C2207021316.pdf).

The 2019 chapter *Natural Beverages of Assam and its Ethno Medicinal Value* provides broad community-level discussion but no Nalbari field locality in the accessible abstract. [Publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/pii/B9780128166895000031).

**Decision:** These sources remain state-level or other-district context; no Nalbari production claim.

### 3. Rabha beverage literature

The 2013 review describes Rabha Jonga mod/Kecha mod and notes broad Rabha settlement areas, but the cited field locality for the relevant ethnobotanical material is Dhupdhora, Goalpara district. The 2010 primary article also concerns Rabha rice beer in Goalpara district. Neither establishes a Nalbari production locality. [2013 article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0975761913000720); [2010 article](https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9773/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20459-462.pdf).

**Decision:** Do not transfer Goalpara observations or broad Rabha distribution claims to Nalbari.

## Archive treatment

- No place row added.
- No beverage record added from statewide community descriptions.
- Matrix remains at zero place rows.
- Record the 2025 Bodo society article as a full-text retrieval lead, not a Nalbari field study, until its research geography is verified.

## Remaining research questions

1. Does Basumatary and Barmahalia (2025) identify any Nalbari villages or field sites in its full text?
2. Are there Bodo, Rabha or Tea-tribe/Adivasi village-level beverage studies in present-day Nalbari?
3. Can local-language sources identify beverage names, preparation practices and current localities?

**Conclusion:** No source reviewed in this pass establishes a traditional beverage locality in present-day Nalbari. The 2025 Bodo article is a promising retrieval lead, but its publisher location must not be mistaken for a field site. This is a bounded search result, not evidence of absence.
