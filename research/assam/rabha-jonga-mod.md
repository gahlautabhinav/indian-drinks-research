# Jonga-mod / Choko — Rabha community research dossier

- **Archive ID:** ASM-007-JON
- **Primary Assam locality lead:** Dhupdhora, Goalpara district
- **Community:** Rabha
- **Type:** Traditional fermented rice-based alcoholic beverage
- **Starter terminology:** Bakhor, Surachi, Phap (as reported in different sources)
- **Evidence status:** Beverage and starter-culture tradition documented; exact equivalence of all names and current village distribution require community verification.

## What the sources establish

Deka and Sarma (2010) document plant materials used in preparing rice-beer starter cakes among the Rabha community of Goalpara district, Assam. Bhuyan and Baishya (2013) describe the beverage as **Jonga mod or Kecha mod**, and identify **Surachi or Phap** as starter-cake names. Their review gives **Dhupdhora, Goalpara district** as the locality for the Jonga-mod ethnobotanical account.

A separate study by Bhattacharjee and colleagues, published in 2023, examines **Choko/Chokot** among Rabha people in North Bengal. Its samples were collected in February 2022 from rural houses in **Rasikbill Rabha basti, Cooch Behar district, West Bengal**. It describes the starter as **Bakhor or Phap**. This is valuable cross-border comparative evidence, but it does not prove that every Rabha community in Assam uses the same name or recipe.

A 2024 review uses Jonga-mod and Choko in related discussions. Bhattacharya and Deka (2024) separately report a **Janga** sample prepared by Rangdani Rabha and collected in Goalpara district, as well as sticky-rice beer samples associated with Pati Rabha (Baksa and Bongaigaon) and Koch Rabha. These are analytical sample descriptions; the paper does not establish exact villages or prove that Janga is synonymous with Jonga-mod. The Assam Tourism page additionally lists **Matha Mod** and **Roshi Mod**, but provides no recipe, locality or source trail. A government-hosted report uses **Ruhi** for a wooden extractor in a Rabha Kesa/Jonga mod preparation, so Ruhi should not be added as a beverage name without Hajang-specific confirmation. The archive therefore retains one provisional canonical record for the broader research family while preserving every name as reported and explicitly flagging synonym status for further verification.

## Names and starter culture

Names found in the current source set include:
- Jonga mod / Jonga-mod
- Kecha mod
- Choko / Chokot
- Bakhor
- Surachi
- Phap

These are not all the same type of term: the first group are beverage names; the second group are starter-culture names. Some sources connect the beverage names, but the archive does not claim exact equivalence across localities without community/language evidence.

## Starter ingredients and process evidence

Published accounts describe a rice-and-plant starter cake. The 2010 Goalpara research and the 2013 review list plant ingredients, while the 2023 Chokot study investigates the starter microbiologically. The plant inventories vary between accounts and should be stored source by source rather than merged into a universal Rabha starter recipe.

The 2023 study reports bacterial genera including *Bacillus*, *Arthrobacter*, *Lactobacillus*, *Ilyobacter*, *Clostridium* and *Lactococcus* in the sampled starter culture. These are results from one study and sampling context; they are not a universal microbial profile for every Rabha household or beverage.

Historical descriptions mention cooked rice, a powdered starter, a covered fermentation vessel and subsequent straining. The exact sequence, vessel arrangement and duration should be attributed to the relevant paper rather than treated as a single standard method.

## Locality evidence

- **Dhupdhora, Goalpara, Assam:** identified in the Jonga-mod section of Bhuyan and Baishya (2013), drawing on earlier ethnobotanical work.
- **Rasikbill Rabha basti, Cooch Behar, West Bengal:** directly sampled for Chokot starter and beverage in February 2022 by Bhattacharjee et al. (2023).

The West Bengal locality is included as cross-border comparative context. It should not be treated as proof that the same names, ingredients or process apply to all Rabha villages in Assam. The archive does not yet provide a current village-by-village distribution map.

## Cultural and contemporary status

Research describes this as a traditional household beverage with dietary and socio-cultural significance. The 2023 study documents Rabha women involved in starter preparation in its sampled locality. Claims about medicinal benefits found in traditional accounts are recorded as beliefs or reported uses, not as established clinical effects.

## Research gaps

1. Verify with Rabha-language/community sources whether Jonga-mod, Kecha-mod, Choko and Chokot are synonyms, regional variants or related but distinct preparations.
2. Map current production villages in Goalpara and other Rabha-inhabited districts of Assam.
3. Compare Bakhor, Surachi and Phap terminology and plant inventories by locality.
4. Follow up the 2022 Cooch Behar sampling to document whether starter-making knowledge remains concentrated among a small number of households.
5. Record present-day practices separately from historical descriptions and laboratory results.

## Research pass — 2026-10-22: Matha Mod and Roshi Mod

### Source audit

The official Assam Tourism cuisine page and the 2022 *Aporup Assam* publication list **Matha Mod** and **Roshi Mod** as Rabha beverages. Both sources are broad tourism summaries: they do not provide a named village, a preparation description, a starter-culture name, or a citation trail for either term. Their repetition should not be counted as independent ethnographic confirmation.

The accessible 2010 primary ethnobotanical study by Deka and Sarma documents Rabha rice-beer and starter-cake plant knowledge in Goalpara district, but its accessible metadata and abstract do not identify Matha Mod or Roshi Mod as separately documented products. The 2013 review uses **Jonga mod / Kecha mod** and **Surachi / Phap** starter names, with a Dhupdhora locality lead. The 2022–2024 analytical studies add source-specific evidence for Koch Rabha Mod, Pati Rabha sticky-rice beer, and Rangdani Rabha Janga; these do not establish synonymy with Matha Mod or Roshi Mod.

### Decision

- Keep **Matha Mod** and **Roshi Mod** as unverified names under ASM-GAP-039.
- Do not add separate canonical beverage rows or merge them with ASM-007-JON.
- Keep Rangdani, Koch and Pati Rabha evidence distinct by subgroup and source.
- Next useful evidence: a Rabha-language/community-authored account or a primary study that explicitly connects either name to a named settlement, recipe/process, starter and community-preferred meaning.

The present desk-research result is a **bounded negative finding**: no preparation-level corroboration was found in the sources reviewed in this pass. It does not prove that the beverages do not exist or are no longer made.


## Cross-community field study (2026): Rabha comparison and limits

This adds a recent research lead beyond older Goalpara literature and the cross-border West Bengal Choko/Chokot study. ## Full-text extraction pass — Das, Rabha & Das (2021)

**Source:** Utpala Das, David Rabha and Jugabrat Das, “Traditional Methods of Preparation of Choko: A Fermented Rice Beer by the Rabha Tribe of Goalpara District, Assam,” *Environment and Ecology* 39(2), 455–459; received 4 March 2021, accepted 20 May 2021, published 10 June 2021. Full text: https://environmentandecology.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MS23.pdf

### Fieldwork design
The authors state that fieldwork ran from August 2019 to January 2020 in five remote villages of Goalpara district. They interviewed 150 older, experienced participants and conducted focus-group discussions in each village, with particular attention to women aged 18–60 who were involved in preparation. The article does not name the five villages in the accessible text; do not invent village records from this district-level description.

### Source-specific starter and equipment
The article identifies Phap as the starter cake and also calls it Bakhor. It describes the starter ingredients as Phap jibra (*Scoparia dulcis*), Pan-chung (*Artocarpus heterophyllus*) and Mai (*Oryza sativa*). These three ingredients are the paper's stated core ingredients, not a complete universal formula for all Rabha groups.

The described equipment includes:
- **Jonga:** earthen pitcher, reported capacity 4–5 kg
- **Janthi:** bamboo filter, identified as *Bambusa vulgaris*
- **Rai:** banana leaves, identified in the paper as *Musa balbisiana*
- **Kur:** bamboo rope
- **Hanger:** a small piece of charcoal
- **Mai:** rice

### Process as documented by the authors
The authors describe cooking the rice, cooling it on a bamboo mat, mixing it with powdered Phap, placing the mixture in the Jonga, sealing the vessel with banana leaves and charcoal, then filtering the fermented mixture through Janthi. Their account reports a 5–6 day fermentation interval; it also cites Deka and Sarma (2010) for a seasonal account of 4–5 days in summer and 7–8 days in winter. These are historical source-specific observations, not safety guidance or a universal schedule.

### Cultural and evidence cautions
The paper associates Choko with Baikho puja, engagement and marriage contexts. It repeats traditional beliefs about health benefits; these must be described as reported beliefs rather than demonstrated clinical effects. The article says knowledge was concentrated among older people, but its sample does not establish the present-day status of every Rabha settlement.

### Terminology decision
This source uses **Choko** as the finished beverage and **Phap/Bakhor** as starter names. It does not prove that Jonga-mod, Kecha-mod, Choko and West Bengal Chokot are all exact synonyms. Preserve each source's wording and keep the canonical record ASM-007-JON until community- and locality-specific evidence resolves the names.

### Next evidence needed
1. Identify the five study villages from author correspondence or supplementary field materials.
2. Compare the source's three named starter ingredients against the ten-species inventory in Deka & Sarma (2010) and the seven-species report in Duarah et al. (2023); do not treat the different counts as direct contradictions without checking sampling and ingredient criteria.
3. Extract the Rabha-specific results from the 2026 comparative study (SRC-091) before using it to revise names or geography.

### Findings
1. The headline counts (10, 3, 7, and 41 across three communities) are not directly comparable: they refer to different sampling frames, table definitions and scopes. The 2021 article's three ingredients are presented as core ingredients, while the 2023 table explicitly lists seven Phap entries and the 2026 total pools three communities.
2. There is a clear shared signal for *Artocarpus heterophyllus* and *Scoparia dulcis* across the focused Choko paper and the older literature. The 2023 Phap table also includes *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, but does not list *Scoparia dulcis* in its seven-row table. This may reflect locality or practice variation, not necessarily a contradiction.
3. *Musa balbisiana* is recorded in the 2023 table as a covering material, while the 2021 Choko paper describes banana leaves as a sealing material. These are overlapping uses of banana leaves, but they do not prove identical taxonomic identification or the same plant part nomenclature across studies.
4. The 2010 count discrepancy (10 reported in some secondary descriptions versus 11 visible entries in a review's Rabha list) remains open until the primary table is checked row-by-row. Do not present a definitive number for the 2010 inventory in dashboard summaries yet.
5. The 2026 study documents Rabha research in Goalpara and Kamrup but gives only 10 Rabha informants and no named villages in the reviewed methods. Do not add place-level rows from district labels alone.
6. Ethnomedicinal uses and reported health benefits are traditional claims, not clinical efficacy evidence. Duarah et al. explicitly notes that efficacy evidence for the plants used in starter cultures is lacking.

### Terminology decision
Retain the current canonical record ASM-007-JON as a research grouping for now, but treat each name as a source-specific label: **Jonga mod / Kecha mod** (Deka & Sarma literature), **Choko** (Das et al. 2021; Talukdar et al. 2026), **Phap / Bakhor / Surachi** (starter names in different sources), and **Chokot/Chokoth** (cross-border Rabha comparison). The current evidence does not establish that all finished-drink names are exact synonyms, nor that starter names are interchangeable across all Rabha subgroups.

## Primary-source verification of the 2010 plant count — 2026-10-23

The original Deka & Sarma (2010) article has now been checked directly. Its Results section explicitly says **ten plant species** were used in Rabha rice-beer starter cakes, and Table 1 contains ten entries: *Ananas comosus*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Calotropis gigantea*, *Capsicum frutescens*, *Clerodendrum viscosum* (printed as “Cleodendrum”), *Dennstaedtia scabra*, *Ochthochloa coracana*, *Plumbago indica*, *Saccharum officinarum* and *Scoparia dulcis*. The eleven-item secondary list previously noted included *Sida rhombifolia*, which is not in the primary Table 1. For the 2010 study, use the primary table's ten entries; retain the review's extra *Sida rhombifolia* as an unverified secondary-source discrepancy, not part of the confirmed 2010 list.

The original article also explicitly states that the starter cake is called **bakhor, surachi or phap**, while the finished rice beer is called **choko or jonga-mod** among the Rabhas of Goalpara. This is direct primary-source support for treating *Choko* and *Jonga-mod* as alternate names in that study's context. It does **not** establish that *Kecha-mod*, West Bengal *Chokot/Chokoth*, *Matha Mod* or *Roshi Mod* are synonyms in all places or subgroups.

## Cross-border Choko/Chokot evidence — 2026-10-23

The 2023 paper by Ghosh et al., “Biochemical and Metagenomic sketching of microbial populations in the starter culture of ‘Chokot’, a rice-based fermented liquor of Rabha Tribe in North Bengal, India,” explicitly describes “Choko/Chokot” as a rice-based Rabha beverage found in North Bengal and Assam, with variation associated with the Bakhor/Phap starter culture. Its samples were collected in February 2022 from rural Rabha households in Rashik Bill Rabha Basti, Cooch Behar district, West Bengal; the study also collected three bottles of freshly prepared Chokot. This is strong evidence that the paired labels Choko/Chokot are used in the research literature for the related Rabha rice-based beverage across the two regions. It is not, by itself, proof that every locality's recipe or product is identical.

The article reports measured pH around 4.3 ± 0.57 and alcohol content 12.66 ± 1.15% for its sampled Chokot. These are sample-specific analytical results, not general specifications or safety guidance. The study focuses on starter microbiota and does not establish equivalence with the Goalpara term Kecha-mod, nor with Matha Mod/Roshi Mod.

## Name crosswalk audit: Kecha-mod, Matha Mod and Roshi Mod — 2026-10-23

### Evidence by name
- **Jonga-mod / Choko:** Deka & Sarma (2010) explicitly calls the Goalpara Rabha finished drink “Jonga mod or Kecha mod” in its section heading and calls it “choko or jonga-mod” in the preparation text. The wording varies within the published literature, so retain the original phrasing by passage rather than silently forcing a single exact synonym set.
- **Kecha-mod:** The 2010 primary article and the 2013 review by Bhuyan & Baishya use “Jonga mod or Kecha mod” for Rabha rice beer. This is reasonable evidence of paired naming in the Goalpara-focused literature, but neither source demonstrates that every Rabha subgroup or locality uses the names identically.
- **Choko / Chokot:** Ghosh et al. (2023) use the paired form “Chokot” in the title and refer to Choko/Chokot for a Rabha rice-based beverage across North Bengal and Assam. The study's sampled West Bengal locality is Rashik Bill Rabha Basti, Cooch Behar district. This supports a cross-border related-name relationship, not proof of identical recipes in all localities.
- **Matha Mod / Roshi Mod:** The Assam Tourism cuisine page lists both names as Rabha beverages but gives no recipe, starter, district, subgroup, or source citation for either. No preparation-level primary corroboration was identified in this pass. They remain unlinked labels/leads, not aliases of Jonga-mod/Choko and not separate canonical beverage records yet.

### Starter inventory caveat from the 2023 survey
Duarah, Kalita & Mishra (2023) reports seven entries in Table 6 for the Rabha Phap starter: *Musa balbisiana*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Xanthium strumarium*, *Centella asiatica*, *Hydrocotyle sibthorpioides*, *Sphaerostepheras unitus* (source spelling) and *Clerodendrum indicum*. However, their cross-community Table 7 marks *Scoparia dulcis* as not used by Rabha, consistent with its absence from Table 6; this differs from the 2010 Goalpara inventory, where *Scoparia dulcis* is listed. The authors explicitly state that plant use varies by community and area and that some plants have become less available. Preserve this as a locality/sample difference, and retain the source's raw botanical spellings and family-label inconsistencies until taxonomic review.
