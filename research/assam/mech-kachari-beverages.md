# Mech Kachari beverage names: Haaz, Photika, Jau and Zu

**Status: research lead only; no canonical beverage entries created.**

## What is documented

A 2023 review table, *Biochemical Indices and Consumption Pattern of Traditional Alcoholic Beverages by Tribal Communities of North-East India*, lists the Mech Kachari community with the starter terms **Haaz pitha, Zu and Bakhar**, the beverage names **Haaz, Photika and Jau**, and the districts **Udalguri, Kokrajhar and Goalpara**. This is a useful discovery lead for expanding the Assam archive beyond the better-described traditions already catalogued. [SRC-062](https://www.foodandnutritionjournal.org/volume11number2/biochemical-indices-and-consumption-pattern-of-traditional-alcoholic-beverages-by-tribal-communities-of-north-east-india-a-review/)

## What cannot yet be concluded

The table does not make the mapping between each starter term and each beverage name sufficiently clear. It does not establish that Haaz, Photika and Jau are synonyms, variants of one drink, or three distinct products. Nor does it establish whether every name refers to an alcoholic finished beverage, an intermediate product, or a wider category. The listed districts are broad community-distribution context, not proof of current production in every district or village.

Do not merge these names with Bodo Jou, Mising Haaz/Nogin Apong, Hmar Zu, or Tiwa Zu/Chu based on spelling alone. Similar names across communities are leads for linguistic comparison, not proof of shared product identity.

## Research plan

1. Follow the review's cited references to the underlying primary studies and inspect the exact community labels and methods.
2. Seek community-authored or primary ethnographic sources that use Mech Kachari language terms directly.
3. Record each product separately only when the evidence links a name to a defined preparation or finished beverage.
4. Add village-level coverage only when a source explicitly identifies the village and what was documented there.
5. Treat medicinal or health claims as ethnographic reports unless independently tested; do not infer clinical benefit.
6. Consult knowledge holders respectfully and do not publish private or restricted ceremonial knowledge without permission.
