# Hojai district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for traditional fermented or alcoholic beverage evidence explicitly associated with present-day Hojai district, including historical Nagaon labels and Bodo, Tiwa, Rabha, Tea-tribe/Adivasi and other community contexts.  
**Decision:** No qualifying Hojai place row added. The district remains an evidence gap, not a district with no beverage tradition.

## Sources and findings reviewed

### 1. Statewide Assam beverage studies

Saikia and Bori (2020) describe rice-beer traditions among several Assam communities, including Bodo and Rabha, but the accessible abstract provides no Hojai locality or district allocation. [Article page](https://archives.palarch.nl/index.php/jae/article/view/3776).

The 2019 chapter *Natural Beverages of Assam and its Ethno Medicinal Value* discusses beverages among Bodo, Karbi, Ahom, Deori, Rabha and Mising communities, but its accessible abstract does not identify Hojai as a field locality. [Publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/pii/B9780128166895000031).

**Decision:** State-level context only; no Hojai production claim.

### 2. Multi-district ethnobotanical beverage compendium

Borah et al. (2021) report field investigations in 32 villages across six Assam districts, involving 202 respondents. The accessible abstract and article landing-page record reviewed in this pass do not establish that any of those villages were in Hojai or identify a Hojai community/product pairing. [Article record](https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451).

**Decision:** Do not infer Hojai inclusion from the fact that it is an Assam district or from the study's multi-district scope. A full locality list and historical district crosswalk would be required.

### 3. Southern Assam beverage research

Yumnam et al. (2024) study Zeme Naga, Dimasa Kachari, Hmar, Karbi and Tea-tribe beverage cohorts, with the article's study frame and district distribution centred on Cachar, Hailakandi and Karimganj/Sribhumi. Hojai is not identified as a study locality. [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11459095/).

Yumnam, Nath and Sharma (2026) report 49 household visits across Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi; the abstract does not include Hojai. [Publisher abstract](https://iubmb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bab.70113).

**Decision:** Do not extend southern Assam sample evidence to Hojai.

### 4. Historical district-name issue

Hojai's present district identity requires attention when searching older records filed under Nagaon. However, no reviewed source in this pass supplied a named village now within Hojai and a corresponding beverage observation. Historical district membership alone is insufficient to create a place record.

## Archive treatment

- No place row added.
- No beverage canonical record or source-register entry added from statewide summaries alone.
- Matrix remains at zero place rows and records the historical Nagaon crosswalk as an open research task.
- Keep Hojai open for village-level, local-language and community-held evidence.

## Remaining research questions

1. Which villages now in Hojai were included in older Nagaon district ethnographic or ethnobotanical studies?
2. Do local Tea-tribe/Adivasi, Bodo, Tiwa or other community sources document named beverage practices in Hojai?
3. Can a source jointly establish the present-day locality, community and beverage name?

**Conclusion:** No source reviewed in this pass establishes a traditional beverage locality in present-day Hojai. This bounded search result is not evidence that local beverage traditions are absent.
