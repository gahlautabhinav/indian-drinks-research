# Hor-alank and Hor-arak — Karbi community, Assam research dossier

- **Archive IDs:** ASM-005-HAL (Hor-alank); ASM-006-HAR (Hor-arak)
- **State:** Assam
- **Community:** Karbi
- **Core distinction:** Hor-alank is the fermented rice beverage; Hor-arak is the distilled form obtained from Hor-alank.
- **Starter culture:** Thap
- **Evidence status:** Core distinction and historical process/cultural descriptions are documented; current locality-by-locality production remains incomplete.

## Evidence-backed overview

Teron’s 2006 paper, *Hor, the traditional alcoholic beverage of Karbi tribe in Assam*, describes Karbi Hor-alank as a fermented rice beverage prepared using the locally made starter culture thap. The distilled form is called Hor-arak. The paper reports first-hand knowledge gathering during field surveys of rural Karbi Anglong in 2003–2005 and describes starter preparation, local substitutes and adulterants, cultural uses, and the traditional still known as Bhot as well as improved stills.

A 2012 survey of rice-beer preparation provides a second account of Hor-alank and thap and cites work in the Diphu sub-division of Karbi Anglong. A separate section of the literature reports a study in Kathkatia village, Silonijan. These are research locality leads, not proof that these are the only villages where the drinks are prepared or that historical practice remains unchanged.

## Hor-alank — ASM-005-HAL

Hor-alank is the fermented form. Teron (2006) describes cooked rice being fermented with thap. A 2012 survey describes rice being cooled, mixed with powdered thap, and fermented in stages; it reports study-specific durations that vary with season. Those timings should not be generalised into a universal method.

## Hor-arak — ASM-006-HAR

Hor-arak is the distilled form derived from Hor-alank. Teron (2006) documents a traditional still called Bhot and also discusses improved stills. The repository treats this as a distinct beverage record because distillation is a defining process difference. Current equipment and present-day practice need updated, locality-specific evidence.

## Thap starter culture

The 2006 paper describes thap preparation using leaves of Marthu, identified as *Croton joufra*, and water-soaked rice, with material from a previous starter batch. It also reports other plants as substitutes, including Ku-eng (*Amomum corynostachyum*) and Themra (*Acacia pennata*) in the article's account. Other surveys list additional plant ingredients. The archive does not merge all published plant lists into one universal recipe; each must remain tied to its study and locality.

Historical papers report starter cakes being dried and stored. Such reports are descriptions of traditional practice, not modern food-safety validation. Ethnomedicinal uses mentioned in older literature are recorded only as reported beliefs/practices, not as proven medical treatments.

## Locality evidence and limits

- **Kathkatia, Silonijan, Karbi Anglong:** a 2013 article section reports a Horlang study in this village.
- **Diphu sub-division / rural Karbi Anglong:** the 2012 survey and Teron’s 2006 paper provide broader district/subdivision research leads.
- **West Karbi Anglong:** the district government’s cultural overview describes Hor and mentions Horpo in Chojun ritual context. This is useful cultural context, not a production-method study or evidence for every village.

The archive does not yet have a verified current village-by-village map for either form.

## Cultural context

Teron (2006) reports Hor-alank in traditional marriage and worship contexts, and Hor-arak in social occasions including death ceremonies and guest hospitality. The Government of West Karbi Anglong also describes Hor as integral to Karbi culture and mentions Horpo in Chojun. Because Horpo appears as a related but distinct term in the official cultural overview, it is not merged with Hor-alank or Hor-arak without stronger evidence.

## Cross-source terminology audit — SRC-096 (2026-10-23)

Tanti et al. (2010; SRC-096) lists the Karbi pairing as “Thap / Arak” in its comparative table. This abbreviated table entry should not be interpreted as evidence that the fermented product is named only Arak, nor as a reason to merge the fermented and distilled records. Teron (2006; SRC-013) gives the clearer process sequence: Thap is the starter, Hor-alank is the fermented rice beer, and Hor-arak is distilled from Hor-alank. The archive therefore retains ASM-005-HAL and ASM-006-HAR as separate beverage records. SRC-096 remains useful for the reported starter association, but its shorthand beverage label is annotated rather than treated as a complete product taxonomy.

## Research gaps

3. Compare thap ingredient lists across studies while preserving each study's geography and botanical identifications.
4. Document contemporary changes in traditional Bhot still use and any improved equipment, if community sources support the description.
5. Verify ceremonial uses and differences between fermented and distilled forms with Karbi-language and locality-specific sources.

## Editorial rule

Keep Hor-alank and Hor-arak distinct, attribute process and plant lists to their sources, and distinguish historical ethnography from current practice.

### Comparative field study lead: SRC-061

Borah et al. (2021) include Karbi communities in a multi-community ethnobotanical field study covering 32 villages across six Assam districts and 202 respondents overall. The abstract reports distillation among the studied communities except Ahom, which is broad sample-level evidence rather than a universal statement about every Karbi household or locality. Exact village attribution and the paper's community-specific tables should be verified before importing plant lists or process details. [SRC-061](https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451)

### Methodological scope of the 2021 multi-community study

The full-text methods section specifies fieldwork from **October 2019 to March 2020** in 32 villages across **Baksa, Biswanath, Golaghat, Lakhimpur, Sivasagar (printed as Sibasagar) and Sonitpur**. Its 202 respondents covered Ahom, Bodo, Karbi and Mising communities. The main text does not map each Karbi observation to an individual village, so these six districts are the overall study area, not a verified six-district distribution of Hor or Hor-arak. [SRC-061](https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451)

### Additional locality trail: SRC-065

Bhuyan and Baishya (2013) provide a named locality/informant trail involving **Kathkatia and Silonijan** in the discussion of Karbi rice-beer and starter-plant knowledge. This is useful alongside the older Karbi ethnographic references, but the review is not a current village census and the exact mapping of each locality to Hor-alank versus Hor-arak needs verification against the underlying source. The place lead is tracked as `ASM-PL-059`; do not infer that both drinks are currently produced throughout either locality. [SRC-065](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dit.2013.09.002)
