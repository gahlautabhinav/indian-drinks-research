# SRC-150 — Gogoi (2021): Chaodang Su-pitha and Saj/Laopani full-text audit

## Bibliographic record
Girish Gogoi (2021). “Traditional knowledge on preparation of starter culture cakes and rice-beers by Chaodang community of upper Assam, India.” *Emergent Life Sciences Research* 7(1):63–75. DOI: 10.31783/elsr.2021.716375. Published online 23 June 2021. Publisher PDF: https://www.emergentresearch.org/uploads/38/13084_pdf.pdf.

## Field design and locality
- Fieldwork: January–June 2020.
- Ten randomly selected villages in Baghchung Development Block, Jorhat subdivision, Jorhat district.
- Eight key informants per village (mainly experienced older men and women), 80 total.
- Prior informed consent obtained from knowledge holders and village heads / Gaon Panchayat presidents.
- Named villages: Habungia Gaon, Jamuguri Gaon, Kumar Chaodang Gaon, Borbheta Gaon, Kebang Gaon, Baghmoria gaon, Timtimia Gaon, Lojonia Gaon, Ujani Nonoi Gaon and Khoriachuk Gaon.
- The author says village GPS coordinates were recorded and mapped in Figure 1, but accessible text does not expose a coordinate table. No coordinates inferred from the map.
- The author reports 50 of 81 plants common across all ten villages; 31 vary by village, and one village used up to 61. No species-by-village matrix is published.

## Beverage and starter identity
The study documents Chaodang community Saj / Laopani and starter cake Su-pitha / Modor-pitha. Do not automatically merge this with Ahom Sajpani, Xajpani or other Assamese Laopani traditions. The article says the Chaodang method is similar to Ahom Sajpani and Deori Sujen but describes its own process and names.

## Starter-cake method
- Plants collected from homestead gardens, tea gardens, private forests, roadsides, riversides, agricultural fields and natural forests.
- Amblovenatum opulentum is sun-dried 1–2 days in summer and 3–4 days in winter; other species are reportedly not dried because drying makes them harder to grind with the Dheki.
- Normal Sali rice soaked 3–4 hours, drained for one hour, then mixed with washed plant materials.
- Mixture pounded in wooden Dheki, sieved first through bamboo and then finer metal sieve, repeatedly until powder.
- Water added to make paste; round cakes about 2.5 cm diameter shaped.
- Each new cake overlaid with powder from a selected old starter cake as inoculum.
- Cakes covered with paddy straw above and below and kept four days in summer / six days in winter.
- After yeast growth, cakes air-dried 2–3 hours, then smoke-dried on bamboo frame/sieve 1–2 m above kitchen fire for 2–3 days.
- Cakes reportedly storable for 2–3 months.

## Saj / Laopani process
- Rice varieties: Bora (sticky), Chakuwa, normal Sali, or a mixture. Bora rice described as cooked in boiling-water steam; all are cooled on banana leaves for about one hour.
- Crushed Su-pitha mixed at 3–5 cakes per kg cooked rice and covered with banana leaves.
- Initial fermentation: 1–2 days in summer / 3–4 days in winter.
- Mixture transferred to smoked and dried earthen pot for another 5–7 days.
- About 1.5 L clean water per kg rice added; after 12 hours first extraction is ready and called Rohi mod.
- About 1 L more water per kg residual fermented mass added and mixed; second extraction is Choka mod, described as white, more turbid and lower in alcohol.
- Residual mash reportedly fed to pigs.
- Citrus plants are kept away during preparation because their acids are believed to sour the beer.
- Sensory descriptions vary by rice type. No numeric alcohol assay is provided in this article.

## Limitations and caution
- One district-level field study, not a statewide Chaodang census.
- 81 taxa are aggregate inventory; only 50 reportedly common to all ten villages and 31 vary. No per-village plant matrix is published.
- No herbarium voucher accession numbers or full taxonomic authentication dataset are given.
- No standardized quantitative recipe or measured plant mass ratios.
- No laboratory alcohol concentration, microbial profile or nutritional analysis reported.
- Health, medicinal, energy-booster and no-side-effect claims are not clinical evidence and are not adopted as verified health claims.
- Cross-community plant-count comparisons should be checked against cited originals; the paper's Mising count of 16 does not replace Mising-specific inventories in other studies.
