# Kardong thesis and 2011 Epop papers — acquisition audit

**Research date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Recover primary documentation behind Devid Kardong's Mising Po:ro/Sai-mod microbiology and biochemical research. This is an acquisition/provenance audit; it does not add new beverage localities, recipes or production claims.

## New institutional confirmation: 2011 PhD thesis

A CSIR–North East Institute of Science and Technology (NEIST) institutional publication/achievement PDF lists the following PhD award:

- **Researcher:** Dr Devid Kardong
- **Thesis title as printed:** “Studies on Microbiological and Biochemical aspects of Chaimod(PO:RO): A Traditional Alcoholic Beverage of Mishing Tribes of Assam.”
- **University:** Dibrugarh University
- **Year:** 2011
- **Guide:** Dr T. C. Bora
- **Institutional source:** https://neist.res.in/publication/are11123.pdf

This independently corroborates that the thesis was awarded in 2011 and preserves its exact title spelling, including “Chaimod(PO:RO)”. It is bibliographic evidence only: the NEIST PDF does not contain the thesis, abstract, methods, results, tables, appendices or repository identifier. It must not be cited as evidence for microbial taxa, fermentation measurements or village sampling.

## Related project report: SRC-152

Dibrugarh University's institutional PDF for Kardong's UGC Minor Research Project (F. No. 32-617/2006(SR)) records a project period of 1 April 2007–31 March 2009. The accessible copy contains the administrative final-report form and a short Annexure-I summary.

The summary reports:
- Three Epop starter-culture samples from unspecified places.
- Seven pure amylolytic isolates (described collectively as bacteria and fungi/yeasts).
- Six isolates with alcohol-fermenting activity.
- Partial characterisation of AY-3 (also printed “amy-3”), AY-1(19), AY-4 (“Big cell”) and FY-10.
- AY-3 + AY-4 + FY-10 proposed as a likely suitable consortium for Saimod/Po:ro solid-state fermentation.

The report does **not** expose species identities for those codes, named collection localities, sample-level results, detailed culture protocols or raw assay tables. Do not map the codes to organisms described in later papers without a direct cross-reference.

Official report: https://www.dibru.ac.in/pdf/Devid-Kardong-Report.pdf

## Related but separate 2017 experiment: SRC-167

Kardong, Pegu & Ghose Guha (2017), “Evaluation of Efficacy of the Microbial Consortium for Fermentation of Alcoholic Rice Beverage Amongst Mising Tribe of Assam, India,” *Current Trends in Pharmaceutical Research* 4(2), is available from Dibrugarh University's archive:

https://oldweb.dibru.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Article_21.pdf

The article's experimental consortium is described as *Mucor* sp., *Saccharomycopsis* sp. and *Saccharomyces* sp. isolated from home-made starter (HMS). The article does not provide a demonstrated isolate-code crosswalk to AY-3, AY-4 or FY-10 from SRC-152. Therefore, do not state that the 2017 consortium is the same consortium proposed in the 2007–09 project report.

At 144 hours, the article reports HMS ethanol of 3.1 ± 0.02 ml/100 g fermented mash, pH 4.78, titratable acidity 0.22 ± 0.01% lactic acid and reducing sugar 17 ± 0.12 mg/g. It reports less ethanol/acidity in the laboratory-made consortium than in HMS. These are experimental endpoint measurements, not a household fermentation schedule or a full time-series.

## Acquisition result and next actions

**What is newly verified:** the exact 2011 thesis title, awarding university, year and guide are independently confirmed by an institutional NEIST document.

**What remains unavailable:** the thesis full text; the complete technical annexures and raw tables for SRC-152; the full SRC-139 and SRC-140 journal articles; and any explicit crosswalk connecting SRC-152 isolate codes with the 2017 consortium strains.

Recommended document requests to Dibrugarh University Central Library / Department of Life Sciences:

- *Bulletin of Life Sciences* Vol. XVI, 2011, including the Kardong et al. Epop mould paper.

Do not create new village, household or beverage-production rows from these institutional bibliographies. Keep SRC-152, SRC-139, SRC-140 and SRC-167 as distinct research records unless recovered primary material demonstrates overlap.
