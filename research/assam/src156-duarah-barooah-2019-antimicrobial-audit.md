# SRC-156 — Duarah & Barooah (2019): antimicrobial screening of Zanthoxylum and Drymaria

Citation: Radali Duarah and Mridula Saikia Barooah (2019), “Antimicrobial potential of different crude extracts of zanthoxylum nitidum (roxb.) and Drymaria cordata against selected human pathogens,” *International Journal of Chemical Studies* 7(6):2683–2685. Received 17 September 2019; accepted 20 October 2019. Full text: https://www.chemijournal.com/archives/2019/vol7issue6/PartAR/7-5-452-883.pdf

## Provenance
- Laboratory: Department of Biotechnology, Gauhati University.
- Plant materials: leaves of *Zanthoxylum nitidum* (Roxb.) and *Drymaria cordata*, randomly collected from nearby fields and forest in Sibsagar district with help from local people.
- Authentication: Dr Iswar Chandra Baruah, Principal Scientist, Assam Agricultural University, Jorhat.
- Samples washed, dried at 40°C for 12 hours, ground; 5 g plant powder extracted in 20 ml water, ethanol or methanol for two days, filtered, evaporated to one-fourth volume and stored at 4°C.
- Test organisms: *Salmonella typhi*, *Escherichia coli*, *Listeria monocytogenes* (printed/OCR text is corrupted), *Shigella* and *Serratia*. The paper says strains were obtained from “National Centre for Microbial research (NCMR), Pune”; retain this institutional name as printed pending verification.
- Agar-disc-diffusion on Mueller-Hinton agar, 5 mm paper discs, 12 h at 37°C, solvent negative controls, triplicate experiments and mean inhibition zones. Antibiotic comparators included chloramphenicol, ciprofloxacin and ampicillin.

## Critical limitations
1. The article's abstract claims activity against all selected bacteria, but tables include zero-valued cells. Preserve both the author summary and table-level results.
2. Units are inconsistent: methods describe disc loading at 10, 100 and 250 μl; results prose calls these concentrations 10, 100 and 250 mg/ml. Do not resolve or convert.
3. The source reports triplicates and means, but no standard deviations, confidence intervals or inferential tests.
4. No strain accession numbers, plant voucher IDs, named villages, informant count or village-level starter-use matrix.
5. The tables' text extraction interleaves portions of Table 2 with narrative; values above require visual checking against the PDF page image before any machine-readable dataset is created.
6. This is an in-vitro crude plant-extract assay, not a test of finished Apong, rice beer or Epop starter; it establishes no clinical efficacy, beverage safety or health benefit.

## Relationship to Duarah's later work
SRC-156 predates SRC-155 (2020 four-plant abstract), the AAU 2020–21 thesis abstract, SRC-154 (2023 NSI abstract) and SRC-151 (2023 ethnobotanical survey). *Drymaria cordata* is an exact shared source string. *Zanthoxylum nitidum* resembles “Xanthozylum nitidium” (SRC-155) and “Zanthoxylum nitidium” (AAU thesis abstract); preserve each spelling and do not treat them as voucher-confirmed synonyms. Sibsagar district overlaps the later survey's broad geography, but same specimens or datasets are not established. Do not count these as independent replications without the full thesis and lab records.

## Handling
Register as a distinct full-text laboratory study, not an ethnobotanical survey or finished-beverage assay. Record only district-level collection provenance (Sibsagar), with no invented village points. Keep numeric values out of the beverage dataset. Linked audits: SRC-154, SRC-155 and the Duarah 2020–2023 chronology.


## Scope clarification

The 2019 article itself does **not** explicitly identify either plant as a rice-beer starter ingredient, and its stated selection basis is information from local people about medicinal properties. The association of these two plants with rice-beer starter culture is explicit in the authors' separate 2020 conference abstract (SRC-155), not in the 2019 article. Accordingly, SRC-156 should be classified primarily as a Sibsagar-collected medicinal-plant antimicrobial study by the same research team, and only cross-referenced to the rice-beer starter research programme as a later author-linked association. Do not use SRC-156 alone to add either plant to an Apong/Epop ingredient inventory or locality matrix.
