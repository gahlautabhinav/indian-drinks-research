# SRC-049 — NIPCCD Assam Dimasa Judima entries: Xumai, Zukap and rice-colour variants

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026  
**Source:** National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD), Regional Centre, Guwahati, *An Exploratory Study on Indigenous Foods among the Tribals of North Eastern Region*, Assam chapter, printed pp. 102–103 (PDF text lines 3219–3250). The institute's research index identifies the report as 2022.  
**Source URL:** https://www.spniwcd.wcd.gov.in/archive/download/2zq4zt

## 1. What was recovered

The Assam chapter contains two consecutive Dimasa beverage entries:

- **2.2.73 — “Maiju hanger judima (Red rice beer)”**: red rice, printed as “maiju hanger mairung,” plus Xumai yeast cake.
- **2.2.74 — “Judima (White rice beer)”**: sticky rice printed “maijumairung” mixed with a non-sticky rice variety, plus Xumai yeast cake.

Both entries describe Xumai preparation as thembra powder mixed with rice flour at a printed 1:15 ratio, kneaded with water, covered with powdered yeast cake, and sun-dried for 4–5 days. The cooked rice is mixed with powdered Xumai and kept covered in a dark room, undisturbed, for three days. The fermented rice is called **“Zukap”**; the liquor extracted from Zukap is called **Judima**. Both are served with roasted chicken, fish or pork.

## 2. Research-design limits

The report's general methodology says women heads of family were selected as respondents through contacts with CDPOs, Anganwadi workers, Gaon Burhas, village leaders and women's groups; personal interview schedules and photographs were used. The executive summary reports 89 indigenous foods collected from eight Assam tribes, including Dimasa.

However, the Dimasa beverage entries do not name the respondent, village, district, interview date, number of households or sample/batch. The wider report's Assam scope cannot be used to assign these recipes to a named Dima Hasao locality. These entries are source-level recipe documentation, not a producer census or current prevalence estimate.

The report cover does not state a publication year; the NIPCCD institutional index identifies the report as 2022. Retain that distinction in bibliographic metadata.

## 3. Terminology and process comparison

### 3.1 Xumai versus Humao/Umhu

The NIPCCD report prints **Xumai** for the Dimasa starter. Other Dimasa sources use Humao/Umhu (SRC-001, SRC-003, SRC-138, SRC-304, SRC-336). This is a potentially related starter term, but the NIPCCD text does not provide a linguistic explanation or explicitly equate Xumai with Humao. Keep the forms source-faithful; do not merge them in the authority layer yet.

### 3.2 Zukap versus jugap

NIPCCD calls the fermented rice mass before extraction **Zukap**, from which Judima liquor is extracted. SRC-336 (Tamang et al. 2016) prints **“jugap”** for the residual rice mass after liquid Judima has been collected, and reports that this residual mass is distilled to make Juharo.

The near-identical spellings and adjacent process context suggest a plausible lexical relationship, but the source roles are not identical in the printed accounts:
- NIPCCD: Zukap = fermented rice mass; Judima is the extracted liquor.
- SRC-336: jugap = residual mass after Judima liquid collection; Juharo is distilled from that residual mass.

Do not normalize Zukap and jugap as identical terms or treat them as proof of one distillation protocol without Dimasa-language or producer confirmation.

### 3.3 Red versus white rice Judima

The report documents red-rice and white-rice preparations separately, but both are called Judima and use the same described Xumai/three-day fermentation sequence. This is evidence for two source-described rice-input variants, not sufficient evidence for two distinct beverage entities. Retain the rice variant as a recipe attribute, not a separate canonical beverage.

### 3.4 Ingredient spelling

The report says “thembra powder (local herbs)” but does not give a botanical binomial in these recipe entries. Do not silently assign *Acacia pennata* or *Albizia myriophylla* based only on the spelling. Compare against source-specific botanical records separately.

## 4. Archive decisions

1. Keep SRC-049 as the canonical source record; SRC-180 is only a duplicate alias and must not be counted as an independent source.
2. Add both red-rice and white-rice Judima entries to source-level evidence.
3. Preserve Xumai, Zukap, maiju hanger mairung and maijumairung as printed.
4. Do not create village-level locality edges, respondent identities, a new canonical beverage entity, or a universal recipe.
5. Leave Xumai–Humao and Zukap–jugap as unresolved lexical crosswalk candidates.
6. Seek the original Dimasa-language names and process sequence from a named producer/knowledge-holder before merging terminology.
