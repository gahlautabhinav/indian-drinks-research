# SRC-315 — Bhuyan & Baishya (2013): seven-community Assam rice-beer ethnobotany audit

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Citation:** Biman Bhuyan and Krishna Baishya (2013), “Ethno medicinal value of various plants used in the preparation of traditional rice beer by different tribes of Assam, India,” *Drug Invention Today* 5(4):335–341. DOI: 10.1016/j.dit.2013.09.002.  
**Publisher page:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0975761913000720  
**Full text inspected:** Scribd-hosted seven-page copy of the published article: https://www.scribd.com/document/544151929/BHUYAN-Biman-BAISHYA-Krishna-Ethno-medicinal-value-of-various-plants-used-in-the-preparation-of-traditional-rice-beer-by-different-tribes-of-Assam  
**Source classification:** Publisher labels this a Review Article. The body nevertheless contains locality-specific accounts and thanks named informants. Treat as a mixed review/ethnobotanical synthesis; the precise division between newly collected field information and material synthesized from earlier literature is not transparent.

## 1. Scope and source-quality assessment

The article reports plant uses associated with seven communities:
- Bodo
- Karbi
- Ahom / Tai-Ahom
- Deori
- Rabha
- Mising
- Sonowal Kachari

The article does not provide a standalone Methods section describing field dates, number of interviews, sampling design, plant collection protocol, botanical authentication, voucher numbers or herbarium deposit details. It also does not provide a complete village-by-village or informant-to-plant crosswalk.

The acknowledgement thanks local informants and identifies seven locality groupings:
1. Kokrajhar district — Bodo account; no village named.
2. Tinsukia district — Deori account; no village named.
3. Kathkatia village, Silonijan, Karbi Anglong — Karbi account.
4. Baragharia village, Golaghat — Ahom account.
5. Dhupdhora, Goalpara — Rabha account.
6. Jokai area, Dibrugarh — Sonowal Kachari account.
7. Barmukuli village, Dhemaji — Mising account.

The article's narrative identifies the same account locations. These are source-reported locations, not a current census of producers. No village row should be created for the Bodo or Deori accounts because no village is named.

Important distinctions:
- “Jou bishi” is the source's compound Bodo beverage name; the source does not establish that “Bishi” alone is a separate canonical drink.
- “Horlang” should not be silently normalized to Hor-alank or equated with the distilled Hor-arak.
- “Haj”, “Haj pani” and “Koloh pani” are the Ahom terms in this source. This source does not resolve all variants appearing in other papers.
- “Jonga mod” and “Kecha mod” are presented as alternative names by the article; community confirmation is still needed before treating them as universal synonyms.
- The Mising account says generic “Apong”; it does not identify Noggin/Nogin Apong or Poro/Po:ro Apong. Do not assign this source's process to either specialized variant.
- Rohi is explicitly assigned to Sonowal Kachari in this source.

## 3. Source-specific process notes

### Bodo — Jou bishi
- Starter: Angkur.
- Rice grains soaked approximately 5–6 hours.
- Plant parts washed and ground with soaked rice; a little water added to make dough.
- Round cakes approximately 5 cm diameter and 1 cm thick; overlaid with powdered Angkur, covered with paddy straw and dried 4–5 days.
- For the drink, rice is boiled, cooled and mixed with Angkur; mixture held overnight, a little water added, then fermented in an earthen pot covered with banana leaves for at least three days; fermented mass mixed with water and strained.

### Karbi — Horlang
- Starter: Thap.
- Rice soaked approximately one day.
- Plant parts washed and ground with rice; flat cakes approximately 6 cm diameter and 0.5 cm thick; overlaid with powdered Thap, placed on bamboo sieve and dried 3–4 days.
- Rice boiled, cooled and mixed with Thap; held two days, then a little water added and fermentation continued 2–4 days.
- The article does not describe distillation in this Horlang section. It should not be used to overwrite the separate Hor-arak record.

### Ahom — Haj / Haj pani / Koloh pani
- Starter: Vekur pitha.
- Rice soaked overnight; plant parts washed and ground with rice into paste; ovule-shaped balls approximately 3 × 4 cm, placed on banana leaves and dried 3–5 days.
- Rice half-cooked and cooled, mixed with Vekur pitha, placed in sealed earthen pot and fermented 3–5 days; fermented mass diluted with a little water and strained.
- Field account: Baragharia village, Golaghat district.

### Deori — Sujen
- Starter: Mod pitha.
- Rice soaked overnight; plant parts washed and ground with rice into dough; round cakes approximately 4 cm diameter, placed on bamboo sieve and dried.
- Rice boiled and cooled, mixed with Mod pitha, sealed in earthen pot with banana leaves and fermented 4–5 days; fermented mass mixed with water and strained.
- The paper claims Sujen can be stored in earthen pots for 1–2 months at room temperature. This is an unvalidated source-specific storage statement, not a safety recommendation.
- Field account: Tinsukia district, village unspecified.

### Rabha — Jonga mod / Kecha mod
- Starter: Surachi or Phap.
- Rice soaked 2–4 hours; plant parts washed and ground with rice into paste; round/flat cakes dried on paddy straw.
- Cooked rice cooled, mixed with powdered starter and put in earthen pot. A bamboo-net cylinder is placed just above the mixture, the pot sealed with banana leaves.
- Fermentation reported as 4–5 days in summer and 7–8 days in winter; fermented mass mixed with a little water to obtain Jonga mod.
- Field account: Dhupdhora, Goalpara.

### Mising — Apong
- Starter: Apop pitha.
- Rice soaked 3–4 hours; plant parts washed and ground with rice into dough; ovule-shaped cakes approximately 3 × 6 cm dried in sun.
- Rice cooked and cooled; Apop pitha added; before fermentation the earthen pot is fumigated over a fireplace until blackened; pot sealed with banana leaves.
- Fermentation reported as 4–5 days; fermented mass mixed with a little water and filtered.
- Field account: Barmukuli village, Dhemaji district.
- This is generic Apong in the article. It is not a Poro Apong ash/Ta:suk process and is not specifically identified as Noggin Apong.

### Sonowal Kachari — Rohi
- Starter: Saoul pitha.
- Rice soaked overnight; plant parts washed and ground with rice into paste; ovule-shaped balls dried on banana leaves for 3–5 days.
- Rice half-cooked and cooled, mixed with Saoul pitha, placed in earthen pot sealed with banana leaves and fermented 3–5 days; fermented mass strained to obtain what the article calls “strong liquor Rohi”.
- Field account: Jokai area, Dibrugarh district.

## 4. Plant tables — important transcription cautions

The article includes one plant table for each named beverage. These tables combine the plant part reportedly used in the starter with a separate column of ethnomedicinal uses. Do not mistake every medicinal-use plant part in the final column for the plant part used in the starter.

Selected printed examples:
- Bodo Jou bishi: *Clerodendrum viscosum* (leaves, roots), *Oryza sativa* (rice grain), *Scoparia dulcis* (leaves), *Xanthium strumarium* (whole plant).
- Karbi Horlang: *Acacia pennata* (printed part “barks”), *Artocarpus heterophyllus* (leaves), *Croton joufra* (leaves), *Oryza sativa*, *Phlogacanthus thyrsiflorus* (leaves), *Solanum torvum* (leaves).
- Ahom Haj: *Centella asiatica*, *Cinnamomum bejolghota*, *Cissampelos pareira*, *Clerodendrum viscosum*, *Croton caudatus*, *Hydrocotyle sibthorpioides*, *Lygodium flexuosum*, *Naravelia zeylanica*, *Oryza sativa*, *Pteridium aquilinum*, *Piper nigrum*, *Sida rhombifolia*, *Smilax perfoliata*.
- Deori Sujen: includes *Allium sativum*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Ananas comosus*, *Alstonia scholaris*, *Alternanthera sessilis*, *Capsicum annuum*, *Cinnamomum bejolghota*, *Centella asiatica*, *Costus speciosus*, *Lygodium flexuosum*, *Melastoma malabathricum*, *Mussaenda roxburghii*, *Naravelia zeylanica*, *Oryza sativa*, *Pteridium aquilinum*, *Saccharum officinarum*, *Scoparia dulcis*, *Solanum torvum*, *Thunbergia grandiflora*, *Zanthoxylum nitidum*, *Zingiber officinalis*.
- Rabha Jonga mod: *Ananas comosus*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Calotropis gigantea*, *Capsicum annuum*, *Clerodendrum viscosum*, *Oryza sativa*, *Piper nigrum*, *Saccharum officinarum*, *Scoparia dulcis*, *Sida rhombifolia*.
- Mising Apong: *Ananas comosus*, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, *Cinnamomum tamala*, *Centella asiatica*, *Costus speciosus*, *Drymeria cordata* (printed spelling), *Hydrocotyle sibthorpioides*, *Lygodium flexuosum*, *Oldenlandia corymbosa*, *Oryza sativa*, *Phlogacanthus thyrsiflorus*, *Piper nigrum*, *Pteridium aquilinum*, *Saccharum officinarum*, *Vitex negundo*.
- Sonowal Kachari Rohi: *Centella asiatica*, *Clerodendrum viscosum*, *Corchorus olitorius*, *Naravelia zeylanica*, *Oryza sativa*, *Pteridium aquilinum*, *Sida rhombifolia*.

These are printed source records, not taxonomically verified modern determinations. The tables contain obvious or suspected inconsistencies:
- Rabha *Ananas comosus* lists “Bark” as the part used, while its medicinal-use text refers to tender leaves.
- Karbi *Acacia pennata* lists “Barks” while the text is unclear.
- Deori *Ananas comosus* lists “Bark”, with medicinal-use prose referring to leaves/roots.
- Deori *Zingiber officinalis* is printed with family “Zingiberaceae” and part “Bulb”; do not silently change the part to rhizome.
- Mising “Drymeria cordata” should be preserved as printed until taxonomic verification.
- “Lycopodiaceae” is printed for *Lygodium flexuosum* in several tables; family placement requires taxonomic correction only with a documented taxonomic authority.
- Table text and PDF extraction contain line wrapping and typographical errors; preserve original wording in the source layer and record normalized taxonomy separately with evidence.

The article's ethnomedicinal column describes traditional-use claims. It is not clinical efficacy evidence, and some claims involve potentially hazardous plants. No plant should be recommended for ingestion based on this article.

## 5. References and dependence on earlier literature

The reference list includes:
- Saikia, Tag & Das (2007), “Ethno botany of foods and starters beverages among the rural farmers of Tai Ahom of North Lakhimpur district, Asom,” *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 6:126–132.
- Ghosh & Das (2004), preparation of rice beer by tribal inhabitants of tea gardens in Terai, West Bengal (not an Assam source).
- Jeyaram et al. (2008), molecular identification of yeasts in Hamei, Manipur (not an Assam beverage source).
- Roy et al. (2004), fermented foods/beverages in Uttaranchal (not Assam).
- Singh & Singh (2006), Yu of Meitei communities, Manipur (not Assam).
- Deka & Sarma (2010), traditionally used herbs in Rabha rice beer, Goalpara, Assam.
- Deori, Begum & Mao (2007), ethnobotany of Deori Sujen, Assam.
- Dung et al. (2006), Vietnamese rice wine starter functionality (not an Assam ethnobotanical source).
- Tamang et al. (2007), LAB in Hamei and Marcha (not an Assam-specific source).

This creates an important evidence-dependence issue:
- The Ahom account overlaps with Saikia et al. 2007.
- The Rabha account overlaps with Deka & Sarma 2010.
- The Deori account overlaps with Deori et al. 2007.
- Some microbial and starter-culture claims are drawn from non-Assam or non-beverage-specific sources.

Therefore SRC-315 is useful for crosswalks and locality leads, but overlapping claims should not be counted as independent corroboration until the underlying primary paper is checked.

**Conclusion:** SRC-315 is a useful bridge source across seven Assam communities and supplies several valuable locality leads. Its review classification, missing methods and overlap with earlier primary studies mean it should not be counted as seven independent primary field surveys. The strongest next step is a primary-source reconciliation of its Ahom, Deori and Rabha sections.
