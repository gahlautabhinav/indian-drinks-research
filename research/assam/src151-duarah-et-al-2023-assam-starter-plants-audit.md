# SRC-151 — Duarah, Kalita & Mishra (2023): Assam rice-beer starter plants; related AAU thesis abstract

## Bibliographic record
Radali Duarah, Pratap Kalita and Angkita Mishra (2023). “Medicinal plants used in rice beer starter culture by different tribes in Assam: An ethnobotanical survey.” *International Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences and Research* 14(6):3012–3027. DOI: 10.13040/IJPSR.0975-8232.14(6).3012-27. Published 1 June 2023. Full HTML text: https://ijpsr.com/bft-article/medicinal-plants-used-in-rice-beer-starter-culture-by-different-tribes-in-assam-an-ethnobotanical-survey/?view=fulltext.

Related institutional thesis abstract: Radali Duarah, “Bioactivity of medicinal plants used in traditional rice beer starter cultures of Assam,” listed in Assam Agricultural University, *Post Graduate Thesis 2020–21*, Community Science / Food Science and Nutrition, pp. 350–351; major adviser Dr. Mridula Saikia Barooah. AAU compilation PDF: https://aau.ac.in/assets/files/thesis/vol-VIII-2020-21.pdf. The accessible compilation exposes the abstract, not the full thesis.

## Survey design and geographic scope
- The 2023 article reports a semi-structured ethnobotanical survey in selected rural villages of Sibsagar, Nagaon and Bongaigaon districts.
- District selection was purposive, based on the communities predominantly residing there.
- Community allocation reported: Sibsagar for Mising, Deori and Ahom; Nagaon for Tiwa; Bongaigaon for Bodo and Rabha.
- Data collection involved village heads, elderly men and women, experienced local women, group discussion, village walks and forest transects; prior informed consent is stated.
- Plant specimens were collected, dried, pressed and preserved as herbarium material; identification/authentication is attributed to a taxonomist in the Department of Agronomy, Assam Agricultural University, Jorhat.
- The accessible methods do not state the total number of villages, informants, households or samples. Do not infer these.
- The article's wording “six major communities” is not perfectly reflected in its count summary: it gives Mising 36, Ahom 27, Deori 13, Tiwa 20, and Bodo and Rabha seven each. It reports 51 distinct plants overall. Treat the article's reported totals as reported, not as a fully reproducible union calculation.

## Mising Apong / starter culture findings
- The Mising section identifies the beverage as Apong and the starter as Opo Pitha / Apob.
- It distinguishes Nogin Apong from Poro Apong; the latter is described as cooked rice with ash made from rice husk and rice straw and is also called Chai mod.
- The article reports 36 plants in the Mising starter inventory for Sibsagar district.
- Table 1 presents local names, botanical names, family, parts used, reasons for use in starter preparation and other medicinal claims.
- The publisher PDF was inspected visually (pages 3015–3016). It physically prints rows 22 and 23 twice, so the table contains 38 displayed row instances but 36 distinct numbered records. The duplicate instances are exact repeats and are retained as a documented source-layout anomaly; the deduplicated 36-entry transcription is committed at `src151-duarah-et-al-2023-mising-36-plants.csv`. The PDF also contains inconsistent botanical spellings and taxonomy (for example, source forms “Ipmoea pentaphylla,” “Caesapinia bondac,” “Casealpinia boduc,” “Sphaerostepheras unitus,” “Zanthoxylum nitidum,” “Rubus hexagynous”); family names and vernacular spellings also vary. Preserve source forms; do not silently correct.
- The Mising table's plant-use descriptions include flavour, bitterness, sweetness, stickiness, concentration/strength and other stated purposes. These are reported local explanations, not independently validated functional mechanisms.
- Article's general method section refers to a figure and says methods are similar for most communities; it does not provide a detailed, independently reproducible Mising-specific recipe in the accessible text. Do not borrow process parameters from other Apong studies and attribute them to this survey.
- No Mising village-by-village species matrix or voucher accession numbers are exposed in the article.

## Related AAU thesis abstract (2020–21)
The AAU thesis compilation lists Radali Duarah's thesis “Bioactivity of medicinal plants used in traditional rice beer starter cultures of Assam,” in Community Science / Food Science and Nutrition, with Dr. Mridula Saikia Barooah as major adviser. The abstract reports:
- Semi-structured survey of experienced local men and women in Sibsagar, Nagaon and Bongaigaon.
- Community counts: Mising 36, Ahom 27, Deori 13, Tiwa 20, Bodo and Rabha seven each; 51 plants overall.
- Twelve selected plants for laboratory screening: *Curcuma longa*, *Sphaerostepheras unitus*, *Polygonum hydropiper*, *Drymaria cordata*, *Psidium guajava*, *Cinnamomum bejolghota*, *Zanthoxylum nitidium*, *Elsholtzia blanda*, *Rubus hexagonus*, *Vitex negundo*, *Justicia gendarussa* and *Scoparia dulcis*.
- Abstract reports proximate composition, aqueous/ethanolic extraction, ABTS and DPPH antioxidant assays, phytochemical measures, FTIR and antimicrobial assays against five named bacteria. It reports no inhibition against *Shigella* in the abstract.
- These assays concern selected plant samples/extracts, not the safety, efficacy or clinical effects of Apong or other finished beverages. No therapeutic claim is adopted here.
- Because the thesis abstract and 2023 article share author Radali Duarah, adviser and overlapping plant counts, they are likely related outputs of a research programme, but the accessible materials do not establish whether the article reproduces the thesis dataset exactly. Keep them cross-referenced, not counted as independent field surveys without full-text comparison.

## Distinction from the 2018 Neog presentation lead
Kakojan College's faculty profile separately records that Mridula Neog presented a paper at an ICSSR-sponsored national seminar on 27–28 April 2018 titled “Qualitative Phytochemical Analysis of two Pteridophytic Plant species used traditionally by Tribes of Assam in Starter Culture preparation of rice beer fermentation.” This is a presentation record only. No proceedings, abstract, full paper, plant identities or analytical results were located in this retrieval pass. Do not infer that the two pteridophytes are the same as any fern taxa in the 2023 Mising inventory or the 2020–21 thesis abstract. This lead remains open as a separate retrieval task.

## Relationship to existing Assam sources
- The 36-plant Mising list is a Sibsagar-district survey and should not be merged into the Jorhat-only plant list in SRC-148, the multi-community table in SRC-061, the Sivasagar Apong starter list in SRC-146, or the 48-plant Majuli inventory in SRC-084.
- Repeated botanical names across sources are evidence of reported overlap only; source spelling, identification, parts and locality attribution must be reconciled individually.
- The article's Mising beverage description is not a substitute for the source-specific Nogin / Po:ro process accounts in SRC-136, SRC-145, SRC-147 and SRC-148.

## Limitations
- No informant, household, village or specimen counts in accessible methods.
- No village-by-taxon matrix, voucher accession list or reproducible detailed Mising process recipe.
- The publisher PDF itself repeats rows 22–23; after deduplicating those exact repeated entries, the 36 distinct numbered records are transcribed in the committed CSV. The table's row 18–19 botanical-name/family columns appear unusually populated and are preserved as printed, with a note rather than an attempted correction.
- Botanical names and families contain typographical or taxonomic inconsistencies. No silent normalization.
- Medicinal claims are community-reported beliefs or statements in the paper; laboratory antioxidant or antimicrobial assays do not establish clinical benefit, beverage safety or efficacy.
- The AAU thesis is available here only as an abstract in an institutional thesis compilation, not the full thesis.
- The 2018 Neog conference presentation remains a separate, unverified bibliographic lead.
