# Garo Chu — Assam

**Archive ID:** `ASM-022-CHU`  
**Status:** Provisional canonical lead; village-level identity and current practice unverified.  
**Community:** Garo  
**Type:** Reported fermented rice beverage  
**Geographic confidence:** Low. The Assam Tourism page names the beverage but does not give a district or village.

## What sources support

The Assam Tourism Development Corporation lists **Chu** as a rice beer prepared by the Garo community. A 2022 Assam Tourism publication independently repeats the Chu label in a broader community-beverage list. These are useful tourism discovery leads, but neither source supplies a village attribution, recipe or underlying ethnographic citation. [SRC-047](https://assamtourism.gov.in/cuisine.php) [SRC-057](https://publications.assamtourism.gov.in/publications/files/Aporup_Assam_2022.pdf)

A 2024 review of Northeast Indian fermented beverages describes Garo **Chu** as a cooked-rice beverage made with a starter cake called **Wansi**, fermented in a sealed vessel with a bamboo sieve called **dekeli** for liquid collection, in a dark warm place for around 5–7 days. These are review-level process details and should not be treated as a universal or village-verified recipe. [SRC-055](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/7769743)

A 2017 broad survey of Assam rice beer includes fieldwork in Kamrup for Garo communities and uses the term **Su** for Garo beverage in its discussion. It does not establish that Su and Chu are synonyms or provide a village-level process description for Chu. [SRC-054](https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jhss/papers/Vol.%2022%20Issue7/Version-2/C2207021316.pdf)

A 2021 peer-reviewed article documents **Chubitchi** among Garo communities in Meghalaya. It notes Garo populations in adjacent plains of Kamrup and Goalpara, Assam, but its product-specific study concerns Meghalaya. The Assam Chu record must therefore remain separate from Chubitchi unless direct community or linguistic evidence establishes a relationship. [SRC-056](https://doi.org/10.1186/s42779-021-00102-7)

## Process summary (secondary-source report only)

The 2024 review reports cooked rice and Wansi starter, a sealed fermentation vessel and a dekeli bamboo sieve; it gives 5–7 days as a reported fermentation period. The underlying Assam village and household are not identified in the reviewed account. Do not infer a universal rice variety, starter formulation, alcohol strength, equipment set or present-day production area from this description.

## Cultural and geographic context

Garo Chu is named in an Assam tourism overview, but specific ritual uses, seasonal practices, current village production and community-preferred terminology remain to be verified from Assam-based sources. General Garo cultural practices documented in Meghalaya should not be transferred to Assam without explicit evidence.

## Open questions

- Which Garo-inhabited villages in Assam currently prepare Chu, and which district/block are they in?
- Is **Chu** the preferred local name, and how is it pronounced/written in Garo?
- What rice varieties, starter ingredients and vessel/sieve terminology are documented by local knowledge holders?
- Does **Su** refer to the same beverage, a broad category, or a different preparation?
- Is there any community-confirmed relationship between Assam Chu and Meghalaya Chubitchi?
- Which cultural occasions and current transmission practices can be documented with consent?

These questions are tracked in `ASM-GAP-043`. Until primary locality-level evidence is available, keep the record provisional and do not merge it with Chubitchi or a general Garo Su label.

## Research pass — 2026-10-22: Assam locality and name-boundary audit

### New evidence checked
- Sarma (2017) explicitly says the study's primary fieldwork included **Kamrup for Garo communities**. This is a defensible Assam fieldwork lead, but the methods section does not name the Garo village or demonstrate that the interviewees prepared a beverage specifically called Chu. The paper uses **Su** in its Garo discussion; this alone does not prove Su and Chu are interchangeable. [SRC-054]
- The 2024 review gives a secondary-source process description for Chu—cooked rice, Wansi starter, a sealed vessel and dekeli bamboo sieve, with 5–7 days reported—but the accessible account does not identify an Assam village or household. [SRC-055]
- The Garo-English dictionary digitized by the University of North Texas library has separate lexical entries for **Chubitchi** (described as strong, undiluted rice beer) and **Chubok** (Garo rice beer). This is a useful warning against assuming every similar-looking “Chu-” name is identical. The dictionary entry does not establish the relationship among Chu, Su, Chubok and Chubitchi, nor does it attribute an Assam preparation locality. [External lead: UNT Digital Library, *Kubidik: A Garo-English-Assamese Dictionary*, p. 125; https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc2062332/m1/125/]
- The 2021 Chubitchi study is explicitly about Garo communities in **Meghalaya**; mentioning Garo populations in Assam's Kamrup and Goalpara plains is not evidence that its Chubitchi sample came from Assam. [SRC-056]

### Finding
The best current geographic lead is **Garo fieldwork in Kamrup**, not a verified village where Chu is currently made. The reviewed sources do not resolve whether Chu and Su are alternate names, broad category terms or different preparations. Nor do they justify merging Assam Chu with Meghalaya Chubitchi. Keep all relationships provisional and avoid presenting the review's process as an Assam household recipe.

### Next evidence target
Prioritise a Garo-language/community source that names the Assam village and uses the beverage term in context. Record locality, local spelling/pronunciation and product-specific process separately; do not infer present-day production from the 2017 fieldwork alone.


## Research pass — 2026-10-22: locality and name-boundary audit

Sarma (2017) states that the study's primary fieldwork included Kamrup for Garo communities, but the methods section does not name the Garo village or show that a particular informant prepared a beverage specifically called Chu. Its use of the term **Su** in the Garo discussion does not establish that Su and Chu are synonyms. [SRC-054]

The 2024 review describes Chu as cooked rice fermented with a starter called **Wansi**, using a sealed vessel and **dekeli** bamboo sieve, and reports 5–7 days; this remains secondary-source information without a named Assam household or village. [SRC-055] A digitized Garo-English dictionary has separate entries for **Chubitchi** and **Chubok**, reinforcing that similar-looking names should not be merged without linguistic/community evidence. The dictionary does not resolve the relationship among those names, Assam Chu or Su. https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc2062332/m1/125/

**Finding:** Kamrup is a historical fieldwork lead, not a verified current Chu-production locality. Keep Chu, Su, Chubok and Meghalaya Chubitchi separate until a Garo-language source or community knowledge-holder confirms their relationships. Do not transfer the Meghalaya Chubitchi process to Assam Chu.

**Next evidence target:** Find a Garo-language or community-led source that names an Assam village, gives the local spelling/pronunciation and links the name to a specific preparation. Current production remains unverified.
