# SRC-110 — Zao/Zou and Ndhuihi in the 2024 southern Assam metagenomic study

**Citation:** Yumnam, H., Hazarika, P., & Sharma, I. (2024). “Metagenomic insights into traditional fermentation of rice-based beverages among ethnic tribes in southern Assam, Northeast India.” *Frontiers in Microbiology*, 15, article 1410098. https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2024.1410098

## Scope and sample design
The study collected 15 traditionally prepared beverage samples across five community cohorts, three samples per cohort: Zeme Naga, Dimasa Kachari, Hmar, Karbi and Tea tribes. Commercial market beverages were excluded. Samples were collected before distillation. The study used 16S rRNA V3–V4 sequencing to characterize bacterial communities, alongside pH and electrical conductivity measurements.

## Zeme Naga beverage name discrepancy
- The introduction calls the Zeme Naga beverage **Zao** and its starter **Ndhuihi**.
- The sample-size/methods section also calls the product **Zao**.
- Table 1 instead labels the beverage **Zou** for Zeme Naga and cites Atungbou (2020) for preparation conditions.
- The study does not explicitly reconcile “Zao” and “Zou,” and it does not equate either with Nduijao or its starter Nduiyi from Chakrabarty (2017; SRC-109).

**Atlas decision:** retain Zao/Zou as source-specific variants requiring verification and do not merge with Nduijao/Nduiyi. The community labels differ in the two sources (Zeme Naga in SRC-110; Jeme Naga in SRC-109), and the starter spellings differ (Ndhuihi vs Nduiyi). Similarity in spelling is not proof of synonymy or difference; community/author clarification and the cited Atungbou (2020) source are the next checks.

## Geography and characterization
The paper describes the study as southern Assam and Table 2 indicates the Zeme Naga cohort distribution as Cachar in the study context. It does not provide a named village for the Zao/Zou samples in the accessible sample description, so this supports a district-level research lead only, not a village production record.

For the Zeme Naga cohort sample, Table 3 reports pH 3.5 ± 0.24 and electrical conductivity 0.8 ± 0.09 S/cm as printed. These are study-specific measurements and not universal specifications. The paper uses 16S bacterial sequencing; it should not be cited as a yeast/fungal survey. Its general health-benefit framing is not clinical evidence of benefit from these specific drinks.

## Other beverage cohorts
The paper also names Judima (Dimasa Kachari; starter humao), Zu (Hmar; starter Chol), Hor Alank (Karbi; starter thap), and Haria (Tea tribes; starter dawai). These are useful source-specific cross-checks for existing Assam records, but the study's 15 samples do not establish statewide prevalence or full village distribution.

## Follow-up
1. Retrieve Atungbou (2020), cited by the 2024 paper for Zeme Naga preparation conditions.
2. Verify with community-led or primary sources whether Zao and Zou are alternate spellings and how Ndhuihi relates, if at all, to Nduiyi.
3. Do not merge ASM-026-NDJ with any Zao/Zou record until identity is resolved.
