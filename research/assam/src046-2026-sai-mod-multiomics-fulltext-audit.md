# SRC-046 full-text audit — 2026 Sai Mod multi-omics study

**Audit date:** 2026-11-09  
**Source:** Das, Rohit; Medhi, Madhu Chandana; Tamang, Buddhiman (2026). “Microbial diversity and its links to retinol pathways and aroma compounds in ethnic fermented rice beverages of Assam.” *AMB Express* 16, article 91. DOI: 10.1186/s13568-026-02062-0.  
**Full text:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13568-026-02062-0  
**Existing source ID:** SRC-046 (no duplicate source created)

## 1. Relevance and locality

The study examines four Assam rice beverages: Black Rohi Modh (BR), Rohi Modh (RH), Jou Bidwi (JOU), and Sai Mod (SM). Its introduction explicitly describes Sai Mod, also called Po:ro Apong, as a Mising beverage made using Epop. The SM sample is reported from Jamuuri Panchali, Dhemaji district, where preparation is described as rare. This is a named sample locality, not evidence of district-wide prevalence or a current producer cluster.

Other beverage samples were reported from Pukia, Dhemaji (BR and RH) and Kaljar, Baksa (JOU). They belong to other community beverage contexts and must not be merged into the Mising Apong profile.

## 2. Sample design and independence

The methods say 12 samples represented four beverage types, but also state that each beverage was collected only once as an authentic household batch. All quantitative measurements were performed in triplicate technical replicates derived from single fermentation batches. The authors explicitly characterize the dataset as a case-study dataset.

**Interpretation:** independent biological evidence is one authentic household batch per beverage type. The 12-sample wording must not be interpreted as 12 independent households or fermentation events. Technical triplicates estimate analytical repeatability, not household-to-household variation.

## 3. Sai Mod findings

### Physicochemical and antioxidant measurements

- HPLC ethanol: 4.28 ± 0.14% v/v for SM.
- The GC–MS/MS results section separately reports 4.99% ethanol for SM. Preserve both method-specific figures; the paper does not reconcile the difference.
- Group-level pH across the four beverages: 4.1–4.5; titratable acidity: 0.58–0.72% lactic acid. The main-text range does not identify the exact SM values.
- SM DPPH: 46.84 ± 1.39%.
- SM FRAP: 96.93 ± 1.46 µmol/mL.

Antioxidant results are in-vitro chemical assay measurements, not evidence of clinical benefit.

### Culture-dependent isolates

Across all four beverages, the study reports 68 LAB and 55 yeast isolates. For SM, it reports 16 LAB and 11 yeast isolates. Reported SM LAB identifiers include:
- *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* (SM1)
- *Levilactobacillus brevis* (SM6)
- *Lentilactobacillus hilgardii* (SM3, SM4, SM5)

Reported SM yeast identifiers include:
- *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (SM1Y, SM2Y)
- *Saccharomyces paradoxus* (SM4Y)
- *Wickerhamomyces anomalus* (SM3Y)

These are culture-derived isolates; they do not by themselves represent the full beverage microbiome.

### Shotgun metagenomics

The metagenomic analysis reports *Saccharomyces* as the most abundant genus in SM (54.85%) and *Lentilactobacillus* at 28.29%. These are sequence-derived relative-abundance estimates, not viable-cell counts. Do not conflate these results with the isolate list.

The article reports 1,785 species across all four beverage metagenomes. Given the single-batch-per-beverage design, species-level calls and rare-taxon claims require cautious interpretation.

### Metabolomics and predicted functions

SM is reported to contain 113 detected metabolites, with serine, aromatic acids, and disaccharides including cellobiose and trehalose highlighted. The paper also reports predicted retinol-related metabolic pathways. Functional gene annotation indicates potential metabolic capacity; it is not direct measurement of retinol/vitamin A production, bioavailability, or human health effects.

## 4. Reconciliation flags

1. **12 samples vs four batches:** the article's 12-sample statement coexists with one authentic household batch per beverage and technical triplicates. Do not count technical replicates as independent biological observations.
2. **Ethanol values:** HPLC gives 4.28 ± 0.14% v/v for SM; GC–MS/MS results report 4.99%. Preserve both and seek author clarification before choosing a definitive value.
3. **Taxonomy and viability:** culture isolates and shotgun metagenomic assignments are separate evidence streams. DNA detection does not prove viability; isolate counts do not describe all organisms present.
4. **Small independent n:** statistical tests, ordinations and microbial–metabolite correlations are exploratory and batch-specific. The article itself acknowledges low power from only four independent beverage samples.
5. **Retinol:** predicted pathways and annotated genes do not establish direct vitamin A production or nutritional/clinical effects.
6. **Cross-study comparability:** do not pool this study's Sai Mod values with SRC-087 or SRC-136. They use different samples, locations, methods and process stages; no synthetic time series or universal Apong range is justified.

## 5. Archive decision

This is a deeper audit of already registered SRC-046, not a new source ID. Its useful Mising-specific contribution is a named Sai Mod collection locality (Jamuuri Panchali, Dhemaji), a one-household-batch multi-omics snapshot, culture-derived isolate labels, and the unresolved method-specific ethanol discrepancy. No production map pin beyond the source's sample locality, prevalence estimate, or general recipe claim is justified.
