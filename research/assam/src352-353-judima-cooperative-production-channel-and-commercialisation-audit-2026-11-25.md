# SRC-352/SRC-353 — Judima cooperative production channel and commercialisation evidence audit

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026

## SRC-352 — Chatterjee (2024), “Brewing Heritage”

**Citation:** Priyadarshini Chatterjee, “Brewing Heritage,” *Open*, 12 July 2024.  
**Full text:** https://openthemagazine.com/art-culture/brewing-heritage

### Source findings

The article identifies the **Judima Traditional Brewers' Industrial Co-operative Society Ltd (JTBICS)** as one of the cooperatives through which Judima is bottled and sold. It quotes Sarbojit Thaosen, described as a member and stakeholder of JTBICS, who says:
- Judima is prepared by local Dimasa women.
- The cooperative has its own standards.
- Only batches meeting those standards are bottled.

It also attributes a source-specific account of the rice mix (maiju/sticky rice, non-sticky everyday rice and Bairing), starter cake with rice and *Acacia pennata* bark (Thembra) plus other wild herbs, and fermentation in bamboo or earthen containers. Thaosen says fermentation typically takes about 24 hours in summer and longer in winter; the article does not give a quantified winter duration.

### Evidentiary limits

This is journalistic reporting based on a named cooperative stakeholder, not an audited cooperative record or technical product specification. It does not disclose:
- the cooperative's registration number, registered office or operating village;
- member count or individual producer list;
- number of supplying households or women producers;
- batch rejection rate, annual output, capacity or sales;
- GI authorised-user status of the cooperative or its individual members;
- whether all cooperative supply is made in one village or distributed across Dima Hasao.

Do not map JTBICS to Dibarai, Tereh or any other village without documentary evidence.

## SRC-353 — Goswami & Sarma (2023), “Women, Work, and Wine”

**Citation:** Upasana Goswami and Abhishruti Sarma, “Women, Work, and Wine: Shifting Cultures of Brewing in Northeast India,” *The Jugaad Project*, Vol. 5, No. 2 (2023).  
**Full text:** https://www.thejugaadproject.pub/home/brewing

### Source findings

The paper discusses Judima within a regional analysis of home-based brewing and commercialisation. It explicitly identifies women’s cooperatives as one channel through which Judima production takes place, alongside household brewing. It discusses the gendered division of labour and the transition from domestic production to commercial manufacturing.

The Judima-specific cooperative reference supports the existence of a collective production/marketing channel, but the accessible text does not name the cooperative or identify its registered office, producer villages, members or output. The article's field vignettes about Shergaon, West Kameng (Arunachal Pradesh) and West Karbi Anglong relate to other brews and must not be reassigned to Judima.

### Evidentiary limits

This is an academic interpretive paper on gender, work and brewing transitions across Northeast India, not a producer census or Judima cooperative registry. Do not use its regional observations as a Judima producer count or village list.

## Archive decisions

1. Register SRC-352 and SRC-353 as cooperative/commercialisation sources.
2. Add a cooperative production/marketing entity node for JTBICS only if the archive's entity model supports organisations; do not create a village edge.
3. Keep household production, cooperative supply, bottling/marketing and GI authorised-user registration as separate relation types.
4. Do not infer that JTBICS is the GI applicant, registered proprietor, authorised user or the same organisation as YADEM or Dimajik Hosom.
5. Preserve the source-attributed process descriptions; do not treat them as the GI's registered process specification.
6. Next research task: identify JTBICS legal registration record, registered office, date, member list and Part B authorised-user status through official Assam cooperative records and GI registry records. If these cannot be accessed, preserve the gap.

No contact has been made with JTBICS or any individual producer.
