# SRC-168 full-text audit — Mising fermented foods and Apong process account

**Audit date:** 2026-11-09  
**Source:** Pegu, Bhaba Kumar; Sarmah, Puja; Yein, Jelshing; Sanong, James (2022). “Study on a Few Traditional Fermented Food Practices of Mising Community of Dhemaji District, Upper Assam, India.” *Asian Journal of Biological and Life Sciences* 11(3):840–843. DOI: 10.5530/ajbls.2022.11.112.  
**Full text:** https://www.ajbls.com/sites/default/files/AsianJBiolLifeSci-11-3-840.pdf  
**Source ID:** SRC-168

## 1. Publication metadata and version discrepancy

The PDF masthead and final citation identify the article as *Asian Journal of Biological and Life Sciences*, 11(3), September–December 2022, pages 840–843. The repository landing page currently displays an August 2026 publication date and labels the issue as 2026, despite showing the DOI containing 2022 and the article's original received/accepted dates (17 September 2022 / 13 November 2022). This audit uses the PDF's issue masthead and citation year (2022); the repository metadata discrepancy is retained rather than silently changing the original citation.

## 2. Fieldwork and locality limits

The paper states that an extensive field survey was conducted in villages and rural areas of Dhemaji district for three months, September–December 2021. It says households mainly involved in manufacturing fermented food were selected. However, it does not report:
- village names;
- number of villages;
- number of households, respondents or informants;
- sampling frame or selection procedure beyond targeting producers;
- beverage-specific household counts;
- a locality-to-product matrix.

Consequently, Dhemaji district is the defensible geographic scope. Do not add village production pins or estimate prevalence. The discussion's assertion that every Mising household prepared the foods/beverages is not supported by a reported representative sample or denominator.

## 3. Apong process account

The paper identifies two Mising rice-beer types, printed as “Pr: ro Apong” and “Noggin Apong,” and says their fermentation processes differ. It reports:
- Epop powder applied to cooked rice;
- material stored in a closed earthen jar, sealed with straw;
- earthen vessels placed in a smoke-reached loft;
- an approximately 15-day period during which the preparation is described as chemically fermented and ready for brewing;
- for Po:ro, cooked rice is spread on a bamboo-stick sheet (Dola), cooled/saturated, then kneaded with ash from charred straw (Amrong) and husk (ampe);
- Nogin is described as using selected fresh/sticky rice, naming Ampi-abun and Lie-ambun (glutinous rice);
- Po:ro is described as brown/black and Nogin as creamy/white;
- the source names Ponyok for the Po:ro ingredient/residue and Arug for the Nogin ingredient/residue, and says these materials are used as pig/fish feed in rural areas.

The process prose is short and its sequence is not fully clear. In particular, it is not sufficiently explicit whether the 15-day loft period applies identically to both beverage variants, nor does it give separate summer/winter durations. Do not treat 15 days as a universal or controlled fermentation endpoint. The paper's terminology for Ponyok/Arug should be preserved as printed and checked with community knowledge-holders before assigning exact process meanings.

## 4. Evidence quality and cross-source comparison

This is a short communication with a brief process narrative, not a microbiology or fermentation-kinetics study. It reports no:
- Epop plant inventory or voucher specimens;
- microbial isolates, sequencing, or microbial-load measurements;
- pH, ethanol, acidity, metabolite or safety analyses;
- batch replication or time-course sampling.

Its 15-day description should be kept separate from SRC-136's approximately 20-day process account, SRC-147's seasonal timing, SRC-148's distinct Po:ro/Nogin durations, SRC-161's Majuli descriptions, and the 144-hour laboratory endpoint in SRC-167. The sources differ in locality, recipe, season, process stage and endpoint. They do not form one standardized recipe or fermentation timeline.

## 5. Archive decision

SRC-168 is registered as a 2022 short communication, not a 2026 new study. Its contribution is a Dhemaji-district process account naming Po:ro and Nogin variants, Epop, ash ingredients, rice types, vessels, loft storage and Ponyok/Arug terminology. It adds no village names, household denominator, prevalence estimate, plant list, microbial evidence or biochemical measurements.

Do not treat the repository landing-page year (2026) as the publication year without explanation; cite the original PDF issue (2022) and retain the metadata discrepancy in the register.
