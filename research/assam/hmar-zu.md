# Hmar Zu — Assam

**Record:** `ASM-018-HZU`  
**Classification:** fermented rice-based alcoholic beverage  
**Research status:** documented in southern Assam research; exact sample villages and locality-specific variants remain unresolved

## Summary

Hmar Zu is a traditional rice-based fermented beverage associated with Hmar communities. Southern Assam research distinguishes it from other community beverages by its starter culture, **Chol**. The 2026 field article identifies the bark of **Zangzu**, botanically identified in that article as *Acacia pennata*, as an ingredient in Chol. A 2024 microbiome study included three Hmar Zu samples among 15 traditional beverage samples from five community cohorts, collected before distillation.

This entry is deliberately separate from **Tiwa Zu/Chu** (`ASM-015-TZU`) and **Zeme Naga Zao/Zou** (`ASM-017-ZAO`). Similar-looking names do not establish a shared recipe, starter or cultural meaning.

## Ingredients and process reported

The 2026 southern Assam study describes Hmar starter culture Chol as a mixture of rice grains and bark from the plant called Zangzu (*Acacia pennata*), shaped into round or flat cakes and dried. Cooked rice is cooled and mixed with starter, then fermented in a covered vessel and filtered.

The 2024 study gives a 3–5-day range for Hmar Zu depending on seasonal conditions. Its wider description mentions earthen pots covered with banana leaves or sacks, but the exact Hmar-specific vessel and cover should be confirmed rather than assumed to be universal. The 2024 samples were taken before distillation; this record therefore documents the fermented beverage, not a separate distilled product.

## Geography

The 2024 study describes Hmar Zu in its southern Assam study, covering Cachar, Hailakandi and Karimganj (now Sribhumi); its table associates Hmar population in the study context with Cachar and Karimganj. The 2026 field study covers Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi and reports interviews across 49 households from five communities. The reviewed passages do not identify the precise villages from which the Hmar Zu samples were collected.

INTACH has a cultural-practices report on Hmar communities in Dima Hasao, but this regional evidence does not by itself prove current Zu production in every Hmar locality. No village names have been inferred for this record.

## Cultural context

The 2026 study reports that traditional rice beverages have distinct ritual, social and medicinal functions within each studied community. An official Mizoram cultural-history narrative mentions Zu in a Chapchar Kut origin story. That is useful regional context, but it should not be treated as proof that the same festival practice or interpretation applies uniformly to Hmar communities in Assam.

Reported medicinal uses are cultural/ethnographic claims, not clinical evidence.

## Evidence cautions

- **Supported:** Hmar Zu is documented as a fermented rice beverage; Chol is the starter named in southern Assam studies; the 2026 article associates Chol with rice and Zangzu (*Acacia pennata*).
- **Unresolved:** exact village distribution; Hmar subcommunity and language-specific variants; current household production by locality; whether a distinct distilled product has a locally recognized name.
- Do not conflate Hmar Zu with Tiwa Zu/Chu or Zeme Naga Zao/Zou.
- The 2024 microbiome study describes sampled products; it does not prove health benefits or safety.
- Do not transfer Mizoram festival narratives directly to all Hmar communities in Assam.

## Open research tasks

1. Inspect supplementary sample metadata for the 2024 study and detailed locality information in the 2026 paper.
2. Review the INTACH report on Hmar cultural practices in Dima Hasao for village-specific context.
3. Seek community-authorized sources documenting Hmar Zu terminology and variation by village.
4. Investigate any claimed distilled forms separately, using local names and direct process evidence.
5. Record current production only when a source distinguishes contemporary practice from historical description or research sampling.
