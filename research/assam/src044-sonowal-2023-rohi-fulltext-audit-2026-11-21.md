# SRC-044 — Sonowal (2023): Sonowal Kachari Rohi full-text audit

**Audit date:** 21 November 2026  
**Citation:** Ripunjoy Sonowal (2023), “Traditional Rice Beer Fermentation Technology of the Sonowal Kachari Tribe of Assam, India,” *Antrocom Online Journal of Anthropology* 19(1):605–613.  
**Full text:** https://antrocom.net/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/sonowal-rice-beer-fermentation-sonowal-kachari-assam.pdf

## 1. Field provenance

The article reports fieldwork in three Sonowal Kachari villages in Dibrugarh district:
- Boiragimath Kachari gaon
- Niz-Manncota
- Dainijan

Selected informants who regularly prepare Rohi were interviewed and the preparation process was observed. The article does not state the number of informants, exact field dates, household/batch counts, village-specific plant-use allocation or herbarium voucher accession numbers. Plant identifications are described as being made with reference to standard local-flora literature, including Dutta (1985).

The article mentions that Sonowal Kacharis also inhabit other Assam districts. Those broader settlement statements are not evidence that Rohi was sampled or produced in every such district.

## 2. Beverage names and identity

The article calls the Sonowal Kachari rice beer **Rohi**, and also reports the names **Jhul mod** and **Madhopurukhor rokh**. It does not establish whether these are exact synonyms, regional variants or names for different serving/preparation forms. Preserve them as source-reported labels pending community confirmation.

The starter is called **Mod pitha**, also **Saul pitha** in the preparation section. Do not merge these with similarly named starter cakes in other communities without evidence.

## 3. Starter plant inventory

The discussion reports 11 wild-harvested plant species used as key ingredients in Mod pitha. Table 1 actually contains 11 entries, one of which is *Oryza sativa* (rice grains), so it should not be represented as 11 wild herbs in addition to rice.

The paper prints “Ptridium aquilinum”; do not silently correct this to *Pteridium aquilinum*. The likely intended taxon requires botanical verification. No voucher numbers are supplied in the accessible article.

The article describes plant material collection, washing and one day of sun-drying, followed by grinding with soaked local rice. Two or three old Mod pitha cakes are added as inoculum. The dough is shaped into cakes around 4 cm in diameter and 1 cm thick, placed on paddy straw or banana leaves, covered with straw and Bihlongoni fern, and dried above the kitchen fireplace on a Dhua sang for about two weeks. Surplus cakes are stored in a Tekele earthen pot or bamboo tube; the paper reports storage for at least one year. These are source-reported observations, not a food-safety recommendation.

## 4. Rohi fermentation process

The article identifies sticky/glutinous Bora rice as the preferred substrate:
1. Wash and soak rice overnight.
2. Drain and steam-cook it without overcooking.
3. Spread the cooked rice on banana leaves over a Dola and cool in open air.
4. Crush Mod pitha on a Pota and mix the powder into cooled rice. The paper gives a ratio of 1 kg rice to 1 starter cake.
5. Compact the mixture in an earthen Koloh.
6. Close with banana leaves or a plate and wrap with clean white cloth.
7. Clean the Koloh with ash and water, then dry/fumigate it over the hearth using rice-straw smoke.
8. Ferment undisturbed at room temperature for at least five days; the paper gives about one week in winter.
9. Filter through a Sakoni net. The slightly yellowish filtrate is Rohi.

The abstract gives a 4–5-day period, while the detailed process says at least five days and about one week in winter. Preserve this internal difference rather than selecting one number as universally correct. The paper describes further dilution and re-filtration as an option.

The article reports sensory observations and storage claims but does not provide measured ethanol concentration, microbial characterization, finished-beverage safety testing or toxicological analysis.

## 5. Equipment and process vocabulary

- **Mod pitha / Saul pitha:** starter cake
- **Dheki:** traditional foot-operated grinder
- **Saloni:** round bamboo sieve
- **Soriya:** wide bowl for mixing starter dough
- **Dola:** large round bamboo tray
- **Dhua sang:** hanging bamboo rack above the fireplace
- **Tekele:** small earthen storage pot
- **Soru:** rice pot
- **Pota:** stone mortar and pestle
- **Koloh:** earthen fermentation pot
- **Sakoni:** filtration net

## 6. Cross-source comparison and archive safeguards

- **SRC-045 (2026):** reports four different Dibrugarh study villages—Chaharikota, Teporguri, Bhoralibari and Meslou—and a five-entry Table 1 (including rice). Its process distinguishes Rohi, the first yellowish filtrate, from Sepa, the whitish pressed liquor from the water-diluted residue. SRC-044's three villages and 11-entry table must not be combined with SRC-045's four villages or plant list as though they were one survey.
- **SRC-315 (2013):** reports a Sonowal Kachari Rohi account from Jokai, Dibrugarh, using the starter name Saoul pitha. It is a separate locality-specific account, not a substitute for the named SRC-044 villages.
- **SRC-312 (2021):** uses Rohi mod for the first extraction in the Chaodang Saj process. Shared wording does not establish that Chaodang Rohi mod and Sonowal Kachari Rohi are the same beverage.

No new canonical beverage or village records are warranted by this audit. Existing **ASM-019-ROH** is enriched with the three SRC-044 villages and the source-specific plant/process details.

## 7. Open evidence gaps

1. Informant count and fieldwork dates are not reported.
2. No village-to-plant or household-to-batch matrix is supplied.
3. No voucher accession numbers are published.
4. “Ptridium aquilinum” requires verification against original plant material or author notes.
5. The 11-entry SRC-044 table and five-entry SRC-045 table need a source-by-source comparison; differences may reflect location, reporting scope or practice variation.
6. The relationship among Rohi, Jhul mod and Madhopurukhor rokh needs community-led terminology confirmation.
7. Current production prevalence is not established by this historical field account.

**Conclusion:** SRC-044 is a useful source with three named Dibrugarh study villages, an 11-entry Mod pitha table, a detailed starter-drying sequence and a Rohi fermentation process. Its value is stronger locality and process documentation—not a universal recipe, a village-level plant map or proof of present-day prevalence.
