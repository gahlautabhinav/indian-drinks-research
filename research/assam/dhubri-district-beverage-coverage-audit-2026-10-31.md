# Dhubri district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**Scope:** Targeted search for traditional beverage evidence explicitly associated with present-day Dhubri district, including Rabha, Bodo/Kachari, Garo and other community traditions.  
**Decision:** No qualifying Dhubri place row added. The district remains an evidence gap, not a district with no beverage tradition.

## Sources and findings reviewed

### 1. Assam-wide traditional beverage summaries

Saikia and Bori (2020) discuss rice-beer traditions among Bodo, Mishing, Sonowal, Deori, Rabha and Dimasa communities at state level. The accessible abstract does not assign a beverage or field locality to Dhubri. [Article page](https://archives.palarch.nl/index.php/jae/article/view/3776).

The 2019 chapter *Natural Beverages of Assam and its Ethno Medicinal Value* similarly describes beverage practices among several Assam communities but does not identify Dhubri as a study locality in the accessible abstract. [Publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/pii/B9780128166895000031).

**Decision:** Retain as state-level context only; no Dhubri production claim.

### 2. Rabha Choko / Jonga mod sources

Das, Rabha and Das (2021) document Choko preparation among Rabha communities in five remote villages of Goalpara district, with fieldwork from August 2019 to January 2020. The article explicitly identifies Goalpara as its study district. [Full article PDF](https://environmentandecology.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MS23.pdf).

Deka and Sarma (2010) likewise study Rabha rice-beer starter herbs in Goalpara district. The source does not identify a Dhubri field locality. [Full article PDF](https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/9773/1/IJTK%209%283%29%20459-462.pdf).

**Decision:** Do not transfer Goalpara field observations to Dhubri. Historical administrative boundaries and broad Rabha settlement descriptions require a named village and a defensible boundary crosswalk before any Dhubri row can be added.

### 3. Wider Assam beverage research

The 2024 southern Assam metagenomic study concerns Zeme Naga, Dimasa Kachari, Hmar, Karbi and Tea-tribe beverage cohorts and gives a southern Assam study frame; it does not identify Dhubri as a sample district. [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11459095/).

The 2021 multi-district ethnobotanical compendium reports 32 villages across six Assam districts, but the reviewed abstract does not name Dhubri among them. The accessible record is not enough to attribute any of its samples to Dhubri. [Article record](https://doi.org/10.13057/biodiv/d220451).

**Decision:** Do not infer Dhubri inclusion or community/product attribution from statewide or multi-district study summaries.

## Archive treatment

- No place row added.
- No beverage record or source-register entry added solely on the basis of broad statewide summaries.
- The district matrix remains at zero place rows and records the search limitations.
- Keep Dhubri open for village-level and community-led research.

## Remaining research questions

1. Do present-day Dhubri village studies document traditional rice beer or other fermented beverages among local communities?
2. Can historical Goalpara-era ethnographies be mapped to named villages that now fall within Dhubri district?
3. Are there community-held accounts or local-language sources that identify beverage names, preparation practices and current localities?

**Conclusion:** No source reviewed in this pass establishes a beverage-making or consumption locality in present-day Dhubri. This is a bounded literature-search result, not evidence that Dhubri communities lack traditional beverages.


## 2026-11-13 follow-up — historic Goalpara crosswalk and Rabha Kesa mod

### Administrative chronology

The official Dhubri district history (SRC-181) states that present-day Dhubri was one of the civil subdivisions of erstwhile Goalpara district, and that Goalpara was divided into four districts in 1983, including Dhubri. It also records the 2016 separation of South Salmara-Mankachar from Dhubri. This establishes why pre-1983 "Goalpara district" references require a village-level crosswalk before they can be assigned to either current Goalpara or Dhubri. It does not establish which old Goalpara beverage-study villages fall inside present Dhubri.

### Additional Rabha beverage source

The NIPCCD Regional Centre, Guwahati report *An Exploratory Study on Indigenous Foods among the Tribals of North Eastern Region* (2022; SRC-049) documents **Kesa mod/Jonga mod** as a Rabha rice beer. It describes local rice (mai), herbal yeast cake (bakhar), fermentation for three days, extraction using a wooden implement called ruhi, and dilution with water to produce kesa mod. The institute's research-report index identifies the item as "Study on Indigenous Foods 2022".

However, the Rabha recipe entry gives no named village, district, fieldwork date, informant count or site-to-recipe allocation. It is valuable community/product/process context but cannot be used as Dhubri evidence or as a locality record.

### Recheck of Rabha field studies

- **SRC-102 (Das, Rabha & Das 2021):** full text specifies five remote villages in Goalpara district, fieldwork August 2019–January 2020, and 150 older experienced participants. The five villages are not named. The paper notes Dhubri as the western boundary of Goalpara in its district description; that boundary statement is not a claim that any study village was in Dhubri.
- **Deka & Sarma 2010:** the Rabha starter-herb study identifies Dhupdhora, Goalpara district. It does not provide a Dhubri village or a present-Dhubri boundary crosswalk.
- **SRC-124 (Talukdar et al. 2026):** the Rabha component is explicitly located in Goalpara and Kamrup districts; its study-area list does not include Dhubri. Its community-level Choko descriptions must not be extended to Dhubri.
- The 2024 North-East fermented-beverage review and statewide beverage summaries provide no Dhubri field locality.

### Decision and next evidence required

No Dhubri place row is added. The district remains at zero verified beverage-specific place rows. New **ASM-GAP-077** tracks the need for (1) named sites and original field registers for historical Goalpara Rabha studies, (2) official village-code/boundary crosswalks, and (3) current Dhubri community-led or published evidence identifying a beverage, locality and producer/household. Keep Kesa mod/Jonga mod as a Rabha community beverage lead; do not equate every source's Jonga mod, Choko and Kesa mod without further terminology work.

This is a bounded source and geographic-resolution result, not evidence that Dhubri lacks traditional beverages.
