# SRC-097 — Chakrabarty, Sharma & Tamang (2009): retrieval and cross-source audit

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Source ID:** SRC-097  
**Target citation:** J. Chakrabarty, G. D. Sharma & J. P. Tamang, “Substrate utilisation in traditional fermentation technology practiced by tribes of North Cachar Hills district of Assam,” *Assam University Journal of Science & Technology: Biological Sciences* 4(1):66–72.

## 1. Retrieval outcome

The full article was **not recovered** in this audit. The source-register URL, `https://www.bilaspuruniversity.ac.in/PDF/ResearchPublications/163.pdf`, was tested directly. The domain redirected the request to the university home page rather than serving the PDF, and a direct file download failed. This is a failed retrieval route, not proof that the article is unavailable elsewhere.

A 2021 bibliography hosted by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research's KIRAN portal cites the paper as 2009, volume 4(1), pages 66–72. An official academic CV for Prof. G. D. Sharma also lists Chakraborty, Sharma and Tamang (2009), with the same title, journal, volume and pages. Some later bibliographies instead cite 2010 for the same volume and pages. The archive retains 2009 as the primary year because it is supported by the official CV and earlier citations, while recording the year discrepancy rather than silently normalising it.

## 2. What the accessible abstract supports

The indexed abstract excerpt identifies the following as familiar North Cachar Hills alcoholic beverages:

- **Judima** — rice-based alcoholic beverage
- **Zunak** — rice-based alcoholic beverage
- **Deku(j)ao** — rice-based alcoholic beverage; the abstract spelling is rendered as “dekujao” in one indexed version
- **Juharo** — distilled liquor

It also broadly describes the use of locally available agricultural and livestock products in traditional fermented foods and beverages. The abstract excerpt does **not** provide product-specific recipes, starter compositions, fermentation times, analytical results, sample counts, named villages, community assignments or present-day prevalence. No such details have been inferred from it.

## 3. Cross-source interpretation

### 3.1 Saphak and Sathu are not beverage names in the evidence recovered

The 2009 abstract excerpt groups “saphak” and “satu” among fermented products prepared from pork fat, not among the rice-based alcoholic beverages. This is consistent with Das & Deka (2012; SRC-305), which explicitly describes Saphak as fermented pork fat among Hrangkol and Sathu as fermented pork fat among Vaipei. This supports the archive's correction: do not expand the 2011 extracted table string “Saphak Zunak” into a confirmed compound beverage name.

### 3.2 Zunak remains a distinct beverage record

SRC-097 independently supports Zunak as a named North Cachar Hills rice-based alcoholic beverage. The 2011 chapter (SRC-304) separately describes Zunak as the residual fermented rice and Zuhing as the liquid draining from the vessel. Because the 2009 full text and original 2011 printed table have not been inspected together, the community attribution and relationship between Zuhing/Zuhning remain unresolved. Keep Zunak separate; do not merge it with Saphak or automatically treat Zuhing as a synonym.

### 3.3 Juharo and Deku(j)ao

The 2009 abstract supports Juharo as a distilled liquor and Deku(j)ao as a rice-based alcoholic beverage at district level. The 2011 chapter (SRC-304) provides a process account for Juharo and a separate process account for Dekuijao/Dekujiao. These are cross-source leads, not proof that the later process description is identical to the 2009 observations. Preserve spelling variants and source attribution.

### 3.4 Judima cross-source lead

The 2014 Chakrabarty, Sharma & Tamang article (SRC-098) is independently available in full text. Its abstract says the researchers selected Humao starter and Judima beverage for study, documented producer/consumer knowledge through observation and interviews, and analysed 21 samples across five product groups: Tuaithur, Tuairoi, Honoehingrain, Humao and Judima. It reports microbial identification and nutritional analysis. The abstract does not expose named villages or a Judima-only sample count. This later paper strengthens the Judima research trail but does not recover the 2009 article or validate all four 2009 beverage mentions.

## 4. Source-quality and archive decisions

- SRC-097 remains a **bibliographic/abstract-level source**, not a full-text-audited source.
- The source-register record now explicitly states the failed PDF retrieval and the 2009/2010 citation-year discrepancy.
- ASM-GAP-125 now includes the failed direct PDF route, year-variant reconciliation and the expanded beverage list in the indexed abstract.
- No new beverage, method, community or place rows were created from abstract-only evidence.
- No named locality or present-day production claim is inferred.

**Audit conclusion:** The 2009 abstract corroborates the district-level names Judima, Zunak, Dekujao and Juharo, and helps prevent Saphak/Sathu from being misclassified as alcoholic beverages. The full paper remains unrecovered; all finer-grained claims remain open.
