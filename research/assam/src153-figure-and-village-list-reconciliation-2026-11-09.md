# SRC-153 — Figure 1 and village acknowledgement reconciliation

**Audit date:** 2026-11-09  
**Source:** Mili, R. & Sundriyal, R.C. (2023), “ÉPOP (traditional starter culture): a complex composition of plant resources prepared by the Misings of Assam, Northeast India”, *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 10:47. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2  
**Primary full text:** https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2.pdf  
**Related gap:** ASM-GAP-068

## 1. Methods statement

The article says the field survey covered **15 randomly selected villages** in Sivasagar district. It then identifies **Majorbari and Ligiribari** as two representative villages selected for in-depth study. The text describes interviews with knowledgeable women in those selected villages and direct observation of plant collection and ÉPOP preparation. It does not publish a complete village-by-village sampling register or allocate each plant taxon to each village.

## 2. Figure 1 visual inspection

Publisher PDF page 4 (Figure 1) shows a Sivasagar district map with **13 visible, unlabeled black point symbols** in the study area. The symbols have no village labels or coordinate table. Their plotted positions cannot be reliably assigned to named villages from the figure alone.

## 3. Acknowledgements

The acknowledgements name 13 villages whose residents supported field visits:

- Disangmukh group: Majorbari, Ligiribari, Afala, Balama, Charaipora.
- Rajabari group: Dolopa, Balikur, Samukjan, Kotiari, Milanpur.
- Charaideo group: Gumutha, Ramnagar, Balikuthi.

This is 13 named villages. The article does not explicitly state that this acknowledgement list is the complete 15-village sample frame. Nor does it state that each acknowledgement village corresponds one-to-one with a point symbol in Figure 1.

## 4. Reconciliation and research decision

There is a visible documentary mismatch:
- Methods: 15 randomly selected villages.
- Figure 1: 13 visible, unlabeled point symbols.
- Acknowledgements: 13 named villages.

The equality of the two 13-counts is suggestive, but insufficient to prove that the 13 acknowledgements are the complete mapped subset or that exactly two of the 15 villages are missing. The two in-depth villages are explicitly Majorbari and Ligiribari; the article does not provide the full sampling frame, marker labels or coordinates.

**No village-level beverage-production coverage rows were created from this reconciliation.** These are fieldwork/acknowledgement leads for an ÉPOP starter-culture study, not a producer census or proof that every named village independently produces Apong. The existing district-level Sivasagar evidence remains the appropriate geographic resolution until the authors' field register, map coordinates or village-level matrix is obtained.

## 5. What this adds to ASM-GAP-068

The next request to the authors should ask for:
1. the complete list of all 15 randomly selected villages;
2. the 13 Figure 1 point coordinates and their village-name crosswalk;
3. clarification of whether the 13 acknowledgement villages are the same as the plotted points;
4. the village-by-taxon / field-notebook matrix and any voucher accession register;
5. clarification of whether the 31-taxon inventory is pooled across all 15 villages or otherwise sampled.

The plant inventory remains an aggregate study result; it must not be represented as a uniform recipe or as the plant composition of every Mising household.

## 6. Related new-source check

A separate 2026 paper by Mili et al. reports voucher specimens deposited at NIF-India, Gandhinagar under NIFG/2023/1–125. Its stated scope is a broader trees-outside-forests / floristic inventory across Assam, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh. It does **not** identify those vouchers as the ÉPOP specimens from the 2023 study, and it does not supply the missing ÉPOP village-by-taxon register. The two voucher systems must not be conflated.
