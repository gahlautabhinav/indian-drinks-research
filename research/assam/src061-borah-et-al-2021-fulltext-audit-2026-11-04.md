# SRC-061 — Borah et al. (2021) full-text re-audit for Mising Apong evidence

**Audit date:** 2026-11-04  
**Article:** Borah, D.; Gogoi, T.; Sarma, J.; Borah, P. J.; Gohain, B.; Mili, C.; Upadhyaya, A.; Basumatary, J.; Neog, K.; Wangpan, T.; Tangjang, S. (2021). “Compendium of plants used for preparation of traditional alcoholic beverages by four major ethnic communities of Assam, northeast India.” *Biodiversitas* 22(4):2019–2031. DOI: 10.13057/biodiv/d220451.  
**Full text:** https://smujo.id/biodiv/article/download/8097/4751/40267

## 1. Study design and geographic coverage

- Field investigations were conducted October 2019–March 2020 across 32 villages in six Assam districts: Baksa, Biswanath, Golaghat, Lakhimpur, Sivasagar (paper spelling: “Sibasagar”) and Sonitpur.
- The study reports 202 respondents, mostly older people and women, selected through purposive stratified sampling and voluntary participation; data collection used participatory rural appraisal, semi-structured questionnaires and personal interviews.
- Plant specimens were collected with respondents, identified using floras and regional herbarium comparisons, and submitted to the Herbarium of Rajiv Gandhi University, Arunachal Pradesh.
- The paper reports 129 plant species, 107 genera and 54 families across four communities (Ahom, Bodo, Karbi and Mising). These are combined study totals, not a Mising-only count.
- The main text does not disclose the complete list of 32 villages or map each beverage/community to a district. Do not infer that every community or beverage was present in all six districts or create unsupported locality rows.

## 2. Mising beverage and starter identification

Table 2 associates the Mising community with “Poro apong, Sai mod” and the starter culture “Apoppitha”. This is the paper's community-level association; the table does not allocate each Mising beverage to every surveyed district.

## 3. General starter and rice-beer method

The article's general starter-cake description says:
- plant parts are washed and used fresh or dried; drying is described as smoke- or sun-drying for a couple of days;
- rice grains are soaked approximately 2–6 hours, rinsed and ground;
- dried plant material is ground separately and mixed with rice powder;
- a small amount of finely powdered previously prepared starter is added as inoculum;
- small round cakes are formed, dried and stored above the fireplace;
- leaves of *Thelypteris parasitica* (L.) Tardieu, *Musa balbisiana* Colla and paddy hay are mentioned as materials used to keep starter balls free from pests/moisture and increase storage longevity.

The article gives a general rice-beer fermentation period of 5–9 days depending on season and climate. This is not stated as a universal Mising-specific fermentation time and must not replace the separate Po:ro description below.

## 4. Po:ro Apong-specific process

The article separately describes Po:ro Apong:
- paddy husk and dried paddy straw are partially burnt to collect ash;
- the ash is mixed with powdered starter cake and boiled rice;
- the mixture is placed in a heavily fumigated earthen pot, sealed with straw and leaves of *Thelypteris parasitica* (L.) Tardieu;
- fermentation usually takes one to two weeks, depending on season, climate and starter quantity;
- the cone-shaped bamboo basket Ta:suk is lined internally with banana leaves and a paddy-straw pack (Amrong) at the base;
- the fermented mixture (boiled rice + ash) is poured into Ta:suk; water is poured from above to extract soluble components; the filtrate is collected as Apong.

The paper separately describes distillation of fermented rice beer among three communities (not Ahom), including a generic apparatus and a process that may take about four hours. The distillation section is not explicitly labelled as a Po:ro Apong stage. Do not conflate the Po:ro Ta:suk filtration account with the separate generic distillation account.

## 5. Relationship to SRC-135 Pawe & Gogoi (2013)

- SRC-061 Table S1 cites Pawe & Gogoi (2013) among previous records for several plant entries. This is evidence that the 2021 authors considered the 2013 article relevant to their plant-record synthesis.
- Table S1 contains 129 combined community entries and interleaved taxon/community labels. This audit does not transcribe a Mising-only plant inventory from the PDF table because the extracted table layout interleaves community codes, taxa, vernacular names, plant parts, collection numbers and previous references. A separate row-by-row extraction with visual page-image checking is required before assigning any taxon to Mising.
- The 2021 article therefore does not replace the inaccessible primary SRC-135 paper and cannot be used to claim that a specific Table S1 plant was in the Pawe & Gogoi inventory unless the row's previous-record field explicitly names that source.

## 6. Cross-source process comparison

| Source | Po:ro process/time | Extraction |
|---|---|---|
| SRC-136 Kardong et al. (2012) | About 20 days at 30–35°C; ash-assisted rice/Epop | Ta:suk |
| SRC-147 Pegu et al. (2013) | 6–8 days warm; 12–15 days cold; ash-assisted | Ta:suk water percolation |
| SRC-061 Borah et al. (2021) | Usually 1–2 weeks; ash + starter + rice | Ta:suk filtration |
| SRC-084 Borah (2026) | 10–15 days in summer; ash-assisted | Ta:suk water filtration |

These accounts converge on ash-assisted Po:ro preparation and Ta:suk extraction, but report different time windows and details. Keep each as a source-specific observation; do not average fermentation periods or assume a single standardized protocol.

## 7. Archive decisions

1. SRC-061 is already registered; this audit adds a full-text process/geography extraction.
2. No new beverage rows are needed: Po:ro Apong is already represented.
3. No village-level place rows are created because the full village list and community-to-village allocation are not provided in the reviewed main text.
4. No Mising-only plant CSV is created from the extracted Table S1 layout until each row can be visually verified.
5. Preserve *Thelypteris parasitica* as printed in SRC-061. Do not equate it with *Cyclosorus extensa* or other Rukji names from different papers without taxonomic voucher reconciliation.
6. Keep the generic distillation section distinct from Po:ro Ta:suk filtration.

**Conclusion:** SRC-061 is a substantial multi-community field study and a useful independent Po:ro process account. It supports a 32-village/six-district combined study design, a combined 129-species inventory, Mising Apoppitha nomenclature, and ash-assisted Po:ro/Ta:suk extraction. It does not provide a village-resolved Mising-only dataset in the reviewed main text.
