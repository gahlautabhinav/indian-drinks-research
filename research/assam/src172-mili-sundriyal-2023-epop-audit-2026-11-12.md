# SRC-172 — Mili & Sundriyal (2023): Sivasagar ÉPOP 31-taxon inventory audit

**Research date:** 2026-11-12  
**Source ID:** SRC-172 (new; source register checked before assignment)  
**Citation:** Rajiv Mili & R. C. Sundriyal (2023), “ÉPOP (traditional starter culture): a complex composition of plant resources prepared by the Misings of Assam, Northeast India,” *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 10, article 47. DOI: 10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2. Open-access PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2.pdf

## 1. Study design and provenance

- Study district: Sivasagar, Assam.
- Methods report an extensive field survey in 15 randomly selected Mising villages; two representative villages, Majorbari and Ligiribari, were then selected for in-depth study.
- Knowledgeable women were interviewed formally and informally using a semi-structured questionnaire; researchers observed ingredient collection and starter preparation.
- The acknowledgements additionally name villagers/locations including Majorbari, Ligiribari, Afala, Balama and Charaipora of Disangmukh; Dolopa, Balikur, Samukjan, Kotiari and Milanpur of Rajabari; and Gumutha, Ramnagar and Balikuthi of Charaideo. This list names 13 localities, not the full 15 villages in the methods. Do not assume it is a complete sampling list.
- Specimens were identified with help from BSI Arunachal Station, Itanagar, compared with regional herbaria ARUN and ASSAM and regional floras. The paper does not publish voucher accession numbers or a taxon-by-village specimen matrix.
- Data on ingredients, source land use and collection times were gathered through interviews, group discussions and direct/participant observation. No respondent count is given in the article's methods.

## 2. Inventory transcription

Table 1 records **31 plant taxa across 30 genera and 22 families**, comprising 27 angiosperms and four pteridophytes. The source-faithful table transcription is research/assam/src172-mili-sundriyal-2023-epop-31-taxon-inventory.csv.

The inventory records source scientific name, family, local name, habit, plant part, collection period and relative collection dependency in forest, home garden, agricultural field and fallow land. The article's symbol key is: * = low, ** = medium, *** = high, – = nil. The CSV maps these to Low/Medium/High/– while retaining the source's categorical meaning; it does not convert the symbols to numerical scores.

The article reports forest as the largest source category (35.53%), followed by fallow land (23.68%), agricultural fields (21.05%) and home gardens (19.74%). It reports leaf as the most used plant part (69.23%), followed by young shoot (20.51%) and whole plant (10.26%). Collection was most often in the afternoon (51.61%). These are article-level percentages; they are not abundance, harvest volume or ecological population estimates.

## 3. Taxonomic and transcription cautions

The table and narrative contain source spelling variants or unresolved identifications. Preserve the table string and do not silently substitute accepted names:

- Table 1 prints “Alstonia shcolaris”; narrative prose prints *Alstonia scholaris*.
- Table 1 prints “Melia azadirach”; retain as printed.
- Table 1 prints “Albizzia lucida” and family Mimosaceae; retain as printed.
- Table 1 prints “Cinnamomum tamala” with an unusual author citation.
- *Neolitsea* is identified only as *Neolitsea* sp.
- *Selaginella* is identified only as *Selaginella* sp.
- Some life-form labels (e.g. *Dracaena trifasciata* and *Saccharum officinarum* as shrubs) are transcribed as the authors' classifications, not independently corrected.

The CSV contains 31 data rows and retains the source's botanical strings. It is an inventory transcription, not a taxonomic authority crosswalk.

## 4. Variant attribution and relationship to other Mising studies

The article discusses both Poro Apong and Nogin/Noggin Apong as Mising beverage types, but Table 1 is a general ÉPOP starter inventory. It does **not** assign individual taxa to Poro versus Nogin. Do not relabel this 31-taxon list as Poro-only or Nogin-only.

Keep the records separate:

- SRC-148 (Borkotoky et al., 2014 issue): reports 51 Epop plant species, but its table remains inaccessible as searchable text and no subtype allocation is provided.
- SRC-170 (Pawe & Gogoi, 2013): explicitly Poro Apob, 28 taxon-level names, including two Thelypteris names sharing a numbered row.
- SRC-171 (Sarma & Devi, 2016): 11 dicot taxa explicitly for Nogin/Nogjin Epop; not a full dicot/monocot inventory.
- SRC-172 (Mili & Sundriyal, 2023): 31 taxa in general ÉPOP from Sivasagar, without variant-specific allocation.

The 2023 paper is a distinct field study and is not a transcription or replacement of the 2014/2016/2013 inventories. Similar names across lists are candidates for a later authority-backed crosswalk only; no canonical plant records or source-identity merges are created here.

## 5. Process details captured by this source

The paper describes ingredient collection before flowering; washing and sun-drying for 3–4 days; crushing plant material; soaking glutinous rice for 2–3 hours; mixing plant ingredients with soaked rice at a reported 1:5 ratio; forming 20–25 g cakes; sprinkling ground old starter containing yeast strains onto new cakes; preparing two large stock cakes (GAI); applying bracken (*Cyclosorus extensa*) to reduce unwanted fungal infestation; drying in sun and over fire for at least seven days; and storing in a cool, dry place, preferably an earthen container. Fresh starter cultures are reported storable for up to six months.

These are source-specific process descriptions. The 1:5 ratio is recorded as stated by the authors; the paper does not clearly define the measurement basis in the extracted passage, so do not reinterpret it as a mass ratio without checking the original figure/context.

## 6. Limitations and archive decisions

- No exact respondent count, voucher accession numbers, complete 15-village list, taxon-by-village matrix or Po:ro/Nogin ingredient allocation.
- Table 1 has a 31-taxon total but does not provide a voucher number for each row.
- The authors' narrative claims about harmlessness or medicinal uses are not clinical, toxicological or food-safety evidence.
- The table's collection dependency categories are qualitative star ratings, not quantitative abundance or harvest pressure.
- No plant abundance, recipe proportion per taxon, microbial identification, or finished-beverage composition is established by this botanical inventory.
- No canonical plant records or accepted-name merges created.
