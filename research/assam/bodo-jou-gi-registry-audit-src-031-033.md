## What this establishes

The three names are separate registered GI entries, each with its own application and certificate number. The registry’s displayed geographical-area field is “Assam.” This metadata should be transcribed as the registry’s field value, not interpreted as proof that production is uniformly distributed across every Assam district or village.

The registry pages list original Statement of Case PDFs, maps and producer lists. The accessible search/page output exposed the document names and dates but did not expose a usable PDF payload in this pass. Consequently, the following have **not** been extracted or verified here:
- product-specific definition and ingredient/substrate requirements;
- required or characteristic processing steps, including whether any distillation is specified for Jou Gwran;
- exact GI production-area boundary from the map/case statement;
- named producer list and any locality-level evidence it provides;
- the wording used to distinguish Jou Gishi from Maibra Jou Bidwi and broader published terms such as Jou bishi, Joubidwi, finai and Joufinai.

Authorised-user addresses displayed on the registry pages are leads for further verification, not a representative production map. They should not be converted into beverage-production place rows without corroboration.

**Next research move:** keep the Bodo Jou product specifications unresolved until the primary PDFs are readable; separately use Judima's official Journal 131/case statement as a template for what evidence fields to extract (product definition, raw materials, process, geographic boundary, producer/association details). ## New institutional cross-check: CTIL/IIFT GI study (SRC-115)

An institutional report prepared by Dr Annamma Samuel under the Centre for Trade and Investment Law, Indian Institute of Foreign Trade, provides a useful independent cross-check: [full report PDF](https://ctil.org.in/docs/WCP_Study/_%20A%20STUDY%20ON%20THE%20APPLICATION%20AND%20COVERAGE%20OF%20GEOGRAPHICAL%20INDICATIONS%20FOR%20INDUSTRIALMANUFACTURED%20GOODS%20UNDER%20INDIA%E2%80%99S%20FREE%20TRADE%20AGREEMENTS%20(FTAs).pdf).

### Findings with page-level anchors
- **Pages 49–50:** the report places Bodo Jou Gwran, Bodo Jou Gishi and Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi in Class 32 and describes the class as beers/alcoholic beverages/wines/spirits. Judima is listed separately in Class 33, “except beers.” This clarifies the registry-class difference as a formal goods-classification distinction, but does not prove chemical composition or recipe.
- **Pages 51–52, Table 4.4:** the report lists the Bodo Traditional Brewer’s Association as registered proprietor for all three Bodo Jou entries and records **no registered authorised users** in its December 2024 snapshot. It also records no registered authorised users for Judima in that table.
- **Pages 70–71:** a qualitative stakeholder discussion involving the Bodo Traditional Brewer’s Association, Assam Agricultural University and local brewers says the beverages are brewed mainly by women in household settings; it notes research-funding/infrastructure constraints, issues around authorised-user registration and regulatory restrictions. This is institutional stakeholder synthesis, not a representative household survey and not a village-level production map.
