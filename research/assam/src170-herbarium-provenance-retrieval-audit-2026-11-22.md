# SRC-170 — BSI herbarium provenance and retrieval-route audit

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026  
**Source under review:** Pawe, Dimbo; Gogoi, Rajib (2013), “Ethnobotany of Poro Apong or Chai Mod — A local rice beer of Mishing tribes of Assam,” *NeBIO* 4(2):46–49.  
**Purpose:** Determine what the public record establishes about herbarium confirmation versus specimen deposition, identify official retrieval routes, and prepare a precise archival enquiry. No contact has been made.

## 1. Evidence distinction: identification is not deposition

The article states that plant material was collected and processed using standard herbarium methodology, initial identification was made using literature, and final confirmation was made at the Botanical Survey of India herbarium in Shillong (ASSAM) and Itanagar (ARUN).

The article does **not** publish:
- a statement that voucher specimens were deposited at either herbarium;
- collector names or collector numbers;
- herbarium accession/catalogue numbers;
- collection dates;
- specimen images or determination slips;
- a taxon-by-voucher crosswalk;
- a statement that all 28 taxa were represented at both herbaria.

Therefore, the archive must describe the article as reporting **identification confirmation at ASSAM and ARUN**, not as proving deposition or current custody of 28 vouchers. It is possible that material was examined, retained, deposited, returned, or distributed; the published text does not resolve which.

## 3. Targeted enquiry protocol

Send separate enquiries to ASSAM and ARUN, because the paper does not say which taxa were checked at which centre. Attach the article citation and the source-faithful 28-row inventory. Ask the institution to search both accession registers and any historical determination/identification records.

### Questions to ask each herbarium

1. Did the herbarium receive, accession, temporarily examine, or otherwise process plant specimens associated with Pawe & Gogoi (2013), “Ethnobotany of Poro Apong or Chai Mod,” *NeBIO* 4(2):46–49?
2. Is there any accession, collector number, field-book reference, determination slip, loan/return record, or correspondence associated with either author and the seven named localities?
3. If specimens exist, can the herbarium provide accession number, verbatim label name, current determination, collector, collection date, locality, and image access conditions?
4. If no accession is found, can the institution distinguish “no matching accession located” from “no record of identification consultation”?
5. Are there unaccessioned working sheets, duplicate sets, exchange records or historical registers that could contain the material?
6. If the herbarium cannot search without collector details, what minimum additional information should be requested from the authors?

### Search keys to provide

- Authors: Dimbo Pawe; Rajib Gogoi
- Publication year: 2013
- Journal: *NeBIO* 4(2):46–49
- Title: “Ethnobotany of Poro Apong or Chai Mod — A local rice beer of Mishing tribes of Assam”
- Study districts: Lakhimpur and Sonitpur, Assam
- Named villages: Dhunaguri; Badati; Bongalmora; Chamaguri Temona; Luitmukh; Kathalkuchi; Rajbari
- Plant table: 28 taxon-level names; the two *Thelypteris* taxa are both printed under table serial 25.
- Do not search only by the modern candidate spellings. Include all verbatim source spellings from the inventory, because a historical register may preserve the paper's original determination.

## 4. Author-side request

If both herbaria cannot identify material from the article citation and village names alone, ask the authors for:
- field/collection dates;
- collector name(s);
- field numbers and notebook references;
- which taxa were submitted to ASSAM and which to ARUN;
- whether specimens were deposited or only examined for identification;
- accession numbers or determination slips;
- any specimen photographs or duplicate-set information;
- clarification of the two *Thelypteris* taxa under table serial 25 and the skipped serial 26.

The BSI scientist bibliography lists the Pawe & Gogoi 2013 article under Rajib Gogoi's publications:
- https://bsi.gov.in/rc-scientist-profile/hi?rcu=132

A BSI scientist profile PDF has historically listed Dr Rajib Gogoi at the Itanagar centre, but staff postings and contact details can change. Use the current institutional office route above rather than relying on old personal contact details:
- https://bsienvis.nic.in/writereaddata/s53.pdf

## 5. Suggested archive status language

Use this exact evidence status until a response is received:

> **Herbarium identification reported; voucher deposition/accession not established.** Pawe & Gogoi (2013) state that plant identifications were finally confirmed at BSI Shillong (ASSAM) and Itanagar (ARUN). The article does not identify which taxa were checked at which herbarium and does not provide collector numbers, accession numbers or a voucher list. Whether specimens were deposited, retained, or only examined remains unresolved.

Do not label the records “voucher verified,” “deposited at ASSAM,” or “deposited at ARUN” without documentary confirmation.

## 6. Decision and next action

- No new source ID or plant record is justified.
- Do not alter the 28-row source transcription or the taxonomic authority crosswalk.
- Keep the seven locality names as study sites only; no plant-by-village distribution can be inferred.
- The next evidence-producing action is a response from ASSAM, ARUN, or the authors. Until then, this remains an open archival provenance gap.
