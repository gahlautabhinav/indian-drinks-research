# Bajali district beverage-coverage audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-31  
**District:** Bajali, Assam  
**Outcome:** No beverage-specific place record accepted; district remains an evidence gap.

## Administrative crosswalk

The 2025 Bodo ethnomedicinal study explicitly states that Bajali was formerly part of erstwhile Barpeta district. Historical Barpeta records therefore need village-level boundary crosswalks before they can be assigned to current Bajali. A former district label alone is insufficient.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Bodo community ethnomedicinal survey (2025) — excluded from beverage records

Basumatary, Maina; Nath, Madhushmita; Boro, Mijing; Das, Barnali; Nath, Namita (2025), “A quantitative ethno-botanical study of traditional medicinal plant use among the Bodo community of Bajali district, Assam, India,” *Ethnobotany Research and Applications* 32, article 60. Publisher: https://ethnobotanyjournal.org/index.php/era/article/view/7474

The abstract reports semi-structured/open-ended discussions with local healers in seven Bodo-dominated villages and records botanical names, vernacular names, medicinal applications, plant parts, preparation and routes of administration. The stated scope is traditional medicinal plant use, not beverage production. No Jou preparation, starter-culture recipe, beverage producer or beverage locality is established by the abstract.

### 2. Three-village Bajali ethnomedicinal study (2026) — excluded from beverage records

Das, Hirakjyoti; Chanu, Nongmaithem Randhoni; Saha, Ashmita (2026), “Investigating traditional ethnobotanical knowledge and the use of medicinal plants in rural communities in Assam's Bajali district,” *Explore* 22(4), 103420. DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2026.103420. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42019408/

The abstract reports 94 informants and fieldwork from September 2024 to January 2025 in Doloigaon, Bamakhata and Muguria, cataloguing 101 medicinal plant species. The stated subject is rural medicinal plant use; it does not document traditional beverages or starter-culture preparation. Do not transfer its plant inventory to Jou or any other beverage.

### 3. General Bodo beverage evidence — not Bajali-specific

Existing registered sources describe Bodo Jou and related products at community, Bodoland or other district levels (including SRC-123 and existing Jou dossiers). The reviewed material does not establish that any named Bajali village currently produces a specific Bodo beverage. A general statement that Bodo people prepare Jou is not sufficient to assign a district-specific place row.

## Decision

- Keep Bajali at zero beverage-specific place rows.
- Preserve the seven-village and three-village ethnomedicinal research as leads for future community contact only; do not add their plant taxa to beverage ingredient records.
- Do not reassign historical Barpeta beverage evidence to Bajali without named village crosswalks.
- This is an evidence gap, not evidence that Bajali lacks beverage traditions.

## Next research route

Search village-specific community histories and beverage documentation for Doloigaon, Bamakhata, Muguria and the seven Bodo-dominated villages named in the 2025 paper. Obtain the full article/supplement for the village list, then seek direct beverage evidence independently. Keep medicinal and beverage knowledge systems separate unless a source explicitly connects a particular plant and process to a named beverage.


## 2026-11-13 — targeted follow-up: named ethnomedicinal sites are not beverage sites

The next-step search was carried out against the named village leads from the 2025 Bodo ethnomedicinal survey and the 2026 three-village medicinal-plant paper. No qualifying source connecting Jou/Joubidwi/Joufinai/Jougwran preparation to a named current Bajali village was found in this pass.

### SRC-176 — full-text verification of the 2025 Bodo ethnomedicinal survey

Basumatary, Nath, Boro, Das & Nath (2025), *Ethnobotany Research and Applications* 32:60, DOI 10.32859/era.32.60.1-29. The accessible full text confirms:
- Fieldwork was conducted March 2022–March 2024 in seven Bodo-dominated Bajali villages: **Chenglimari, Duramari, Mwithabari, Jamguri, Bharegaon, Sonapur and Kathalmurighat**.
- The study interviewed **64 informants** using semi-structured and open-ended discussions about herbal medicinal practices.
- It records **131 medicinal plant species** across 63 families and 116 genera.
- Identification was supported by the Gauhati University Botanical Herbarium (GUBH), photographs and botanical references.
- The stated objectives, methods, tables and results concern medicinal applications, plant parts and therapeutic use. The paper does not document Jou preparation, beverage consumption, starter-cake recipes, fermentation or beverage producer households.

One potential false-positive was explicitly checked: *Musa balbisiana* has high medicinal Use Value and Relative Frequency of Citation in this paper, but that does not make it a Jou starter ingredient. Other sources' use of Musa in Bodo starter cultures cannot be imported into this paper's medicinal dataset or allocated to these seven villages.

### SRC-177 — 2026 three-village medicinal study

Das, Chanu & Saha (2026), *Explore* 22(4), article 103420, DOI 10.1016/j.explore.2026.100763. The publisher abstract reports **94 informants** and fieldwork from September 2024 to January 2025 in **Doloigaon, Bamakhata and Muguria**, documenting medicinal plants. Full text was not accessible in this pass, so no table-level or taxonomic claims are made. The accessible abstract does not document traditional beverage preparation, Jou starter culture or beverage production.

### SRC-175 — administrative boundary context

The 2011 Barpeta District Census Handbook includes both **Bajali Revenue Circle** and **Bajali CD Block** within the then-current Barpeta district (map/index and village-directory contents). This supports the existing warning that historical “Barpeta” source labels cannot be automatically assigned to the present-day Barpeta or Bajali districts. A village-level administrative crosswalk is required. The handbook is administrative context, not beverage evidence.

### Village-level targeted search and disposition

Targeted searches using the named sites and terms Jou, Jou bishi, Joubidwi, Joufinai, Jougwran and Bodo rice beer did not produce a reliable beverage-specific record for any of the ten villages named across SRC-176 and SRC-177. General Bodo beverage sources still describe Jou at community/Bodoland/Kokrajhar or other regional scopes; none of the retrieved records attributes production to these Bajali sites. The seven villages in SRC-176 and three villages in SRC-177 therefore remain **ethnomedicinal research leads only**.

**Decision:** Bajali remains at zero beverage-specific place rows. No village rows or beverage ingredient rows were added. New gap ASM-GAP-075 tracks direct beverage evidence and community-confirmed producer/locality mapping. This is a scoped search result, not evidence of beverage absence.
