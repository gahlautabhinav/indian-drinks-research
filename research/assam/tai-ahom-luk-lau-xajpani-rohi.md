# Tai-Ahom beverages: Luk-Lau, Xajpani, Nam-Lao and Rohi

**State:** Assam  
**Community:** Tai-Ahom / Ahom (preserve source-specific labels)  
**Record type:** Terminology and process audit  
**Status:** Research in progress; do not create separate canonical beverage entries until distinctions are verified.

## Executive summary

Sources use several names for traditional Ahom rice beverages: **Luk-Lao**, **Nam-Lao**, **Xajpani/Xaj-paani**, and, in one recent heritage magazine, **Rohi**. These labels do not appear consistently across sources. Some describe Luk-Lao as rice beer and Nam-Lao as a diluted form; others use Xajpani or Kolohpani for the traditional drink. A 2025 magazine calls Rohi concentrated Xajpani and labels it Luk-Lao, but this is a popular heritage source and requires corroboration before the archive treats the names as strict synonyms or separate products.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Assam Tourism, *Aporup Assam* (2022 edition)

The tourism publication identifies “LUK-LAU” as the Ahom drink, but gives no beverage-specific recipe, starter-culture name, fermentation period or clear distinctions among related names.

Source: https://publications.assamtourism.gov.in/publications/files/Aporup_Assam_2022.pdf

**Evidence value:** Useful name-discovery source; insufficient for detailed product classification.

### 2. Saikia & Das (2007), Tai-Ahom ethnobotany in North Lakhimpur

The paper *Ethnobotany of foods and beverages among the rural farmers of Tai Ahom of North Lakhimpur district, Asom* describes processing techniques and plant materials associated with a traditional rice beer it calls **Sajpani (Tai) / Laopani (Assamese)**. This is a potentially valuable locality-specific source for the broader beverage family, but terminology should be quoted exactly from the paper and not automatically equated with Luk-Lau without examining the full text.

ResearchGate record: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237772274_Ethnobotany_of_foods_and_beverages_among_the_rural_farmers_of_Tai_Ahom_of_North_Lakhimpur_district_Asom

**Evidence value:** Scholarly ethnobotanical study with locality specified in its title; promising for process detail and starter ingredients.

### 3. Bhuyan & Baishya (2013), Ahom Haj/Haj pani/Koloh pani

This review states that the Ahom prepare a rice beer called **Haj pani or Koloh pani**, and identifies **vekur pitha** as its starter cake. Its field study for this entry was carried out in Baragharia village, Golaghat district. The article describes starter plants, but its terminology differs from Luk-Lau and Xajpani in other sources. Treat these as a documented naming discrepancy, not as proof of synonymy.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dit.2013.09.002  
Article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0975761913000720

**Evidence value:** Useful source for Ahom starter-culture and locality leads; product-name relationship unresolved.

### 4. Saikia & Bori (2019), broad secondary account

The article “A Study on the Use of Traditional Alcoholic Drinks in Tribal Communities of Assam” uses **Somrokh / Lukolau / Xaj-paani** in a traditional Ahom origin narrative. It does not by itself establish that all names are technically identical in modern community practice.

PDF: https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR1907E44.pdf

**Evidence value:** Secondary source for historical/cultural naming; verify its underlying citations before using it as primary evidence.

### 5. *Shree Exclusive*, “Essence of Assam” (2025)

This magazine labels **Xaj Pani / Nam-Lao** as an Ahom heritage brew and describes **Rohi / Luk-Lao** as a sweeter, more concentrated offering. The wording suggests a relationship between diluted and concentrated preparations, but the article does not provide enough method detail or primary references to settle the nomenclature.

PDF: https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/52307e8db3d777978ee056e115ebb6e134ccfb1b.pdf

**Evidence value:** Recent popular heritage source; treat the Rohi relationship as a hypothesis pending stronger corroboration.

### 6. Journal of Ethnic Foods (2025 online / 2026 issue), Bodo methodology paper

A scholarly review of Bodo beverage methodology describes Xajpani/Kolohpani as the traditional Ahom drink and names **Vekur-pitha** as its starter. This supports Xajpani/Kolohpani and Vekur-pitha as a scholarly reported pairing, but it is not a dedicated field study of Luk-Lau/Rohi distinctions.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-025-00286-2

**Evidence value:** Useful corroboration of Ahom beverage and starter terminology; does not resolve all aliases.

## Key research questions

1. Inspect the full text of Saikia & Das (2007) to extract the recipe, plant taxa, local names, starter preparation, and exact localities.
2. Compare it with Bhuyan & Baishya (2013)'s Baragharia, Golaghat account and record whether the beverage is named Haj pani, Koloh pani or another term in the original table.
3. Find a Tai-Ahom community-authored or language source that explains the literal meanings and usage contexts of Luk-Lao, Nam-Lao, Xajpani, Rohi and Sajpani.
4. Verify whether Rohi is a concentrated fraction, a later fermentation stage, or a separate preparation; do not infer this solely from a magazine description.
5. Determine whether these names refer to fermented rice liquid, a diluted serving, or a distilled product. The 2021 ethnobotanical compendium reports that distillation was observed in studied communities except the Ahom, which cautions against classifying these Ahom products as distilled spirits without specific evidence.
6. Keep locality-specific recipes separate; do not merge different starter plant lists into a universal recipe.

## Canonical-record decision

**No new canonical beverage entries created in this pass.** Maintain this as an unresolved terminology family. A future record should distinguish product, serving/dilution state, and starter culture rather than treating all names as interchangeable.
