# SRC-127 audit — regional fermented foods review (2026)

**Source:** Sarma, Dipasri; Islam, Jasmin; Sharma, Daisy (2026). “Various Fermented Foods of Northeastern Region and Their Functional Roles and Impact on Gut Microbiota: A Review.” *Asian Journal of Dairy and Food Research*. First online 15 October 2026. DOI: https://doi.org/10.18805/ajdfr.DR-2498. Publisher: https://arccjournals.com/journal/asian-journal-of-dairy-and-food-research/DR-2498.

## Scope
The publisher page describes a secondary review of fermented foods from Northeast India, including cereal-based fermented beverages. The stated search window is January 2000 through March 2026, with final search on 15 March 2026, using Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Google Scholar and Wiley Online Library, plus reference-list screening. It names Apong and Xaj-pani among examples of rice/millet-based beverages.

This is not a new Assam field study, beverage-specific experiment or locality census. Its archive value is bibliographic: it can help cross-check citations and identify follow-up literature.

## Evidence controls
1. Do not use this review to add production places, producer records, recipes, ingredient-to-beverage links or community attributions without checking the cited primary source.
2. General claims about probiotics, gut microbiota and health effects do not establish clinical benefit for any named Assamese beverage. The review itself says well-designed clinical studies are needed.
3. Lists of molds, yeasts and lactic acid bacteria must not be treated as universal starter composition for Apong, Xaj-pani or every Assamese rice beverage. Trace species-level claims to the actual primary study and its samples.
4. The publisher disclaimer says the authors do not guarantee completeness or accuracy. Use this review as a reference-finding aid, not as a substitute for primary evidence.
5. Its search cutoff is 15 March 2026 and therefore cannot include later publications.

## Assam-specific value and follow-up
The rice/millet beverage discussion names Apong and Xaj-pani and cites earlier starter-culture work, including Borah et al. (2021) on plants used in traditional alcoholic beverages of four major ethnic communities of Assam. Check the existing archive record for that paper rather than creating a duplicate. Next, compare the review's Assam table and beverage-related citations with registered primary sources, noting citation errors or unsupported generalizations.

## Decision
SRC-127 is a secondary bibliographic lead only. No new canonical beverage rows, ingredient links or locality records are warranted from this review alone. It does not close a named Assam research gap or change the state-transition decision; Assam remains in progress.


## 2026-10-29 — Assam citation and internal-consistency cross-check

The publisher's full HTML text was reviewed for Assam beverage references and compared against already-registered sources. This pass identified useful leads but also several reasons not to treat the review's summary tables or organism lists as verified archive facts.

### Citation traceability

- **Borah et al. (2021)**, “Compendium of plants used for preparation of traditional alcoholic beverages by four major ethnic communities of Assam, northeast India,” is already registered as **SRC-061**. The review cites it for complex starter communities and functional potential, but the compendium's plant-use documentation should not be converted into a universal recipe or a beverage-specific ingredient link without checking its community/beverage-level entries.
- **Narzary et al. (2016)**, “A study on indigenous fermented foods and beverages of Kokrajhar, Assam, India,” is already registered as **SRC-075**. It is a district-scoped study, not evidence for all Assam. Use its original scope and sampling design rather than generalizing the review's summary.
- The review's displayed references have a numbering jump in the accessible HTML (items 1–7 then 13 onward, with further missing ranges). This may be a publication or HTML-extraction issue; do not label it a confirmed authoring error without checking the formatted PDF. It does, however, make the HTML reference list insufficient for a complete reproducible bibliography on its own.

### Internal consistency and citation-trace flags — 2026-10-29 follow-up

Do not treat SRC-061 as evidence for metagenomic findings. The review may have intended a different Borah paper or the citation may be mismatched; resolve against the formatted PDF and the intended primary paper before accepting the claim.

The accessible publisher page includes a “Download Article” label but exposes no usable PDF link in the text view inspected during this pass. The table headings are present, but table rows remain unavailable in extracted text. The primary sources SRC-061 and SRC-075 are already registered; the latter is an original Kokrajhar field survey of fermented foods/beverages, not a metagenomic paper.

### Internal consistency and overgeneralization flags

1. The narrative calls **Apong** an Assam beverage in the cereal-beverage section, but later places Apong among Arunachal Pradesh foods. These may reflect distinct traditions/name usage, but the review does not explain the distinction. Keep Assam and Arunachal Pradesh records separate unless the cited primary sources support a specific equivalence.
2. The review lists Apong, Xaj/Xaj-pani, Jou, Chubitchi and Zutho together with generalized starter organisms (molds, yeasts and LAB). This is regional framing, not proof that every named beverage has all listed organisms or a shared starter recipe.
3. The publisher HTML contains headings such as “Table 1. Fermented foods of Assam” but the accessible text extraction does not expose the actual table rows. Consequently, the claimed Assam table could not be independently audited row by row from this view.
5. Health and gut-microbiota passages repeatedly acknowledge that human clinical evidence is limited or still needed. Statements about probiotic potential, antioxidant activity or functional benefits must remain hypotheses or study-specific findings unless supported by direct beverage-specific human evidence.

No new beverage or locality rows were added. SRC-127 remains a secondary review and citation-discovery aid. Next: obtain the typeset PDF or author-supplied copy; identify the intended primary citation behind the metagenomics sentence; recover the Assam table rows and trace each row to its cited primary source. Reconcile name/community/geography conflicts before any canonical data changes. The review does not close any existing Assam gap or pass the state-transition gate.
