# SRC-148 — Borkotoky, Neog & Barua (2014 issue) primary-page audit: sampling geography and microbiology method

**Research date:** 2026-11-12  
**Existing source:** SRC-148 — no new source ID created  
**Article:** Babita Phukan Borkotoky, Mridula Neog & Iswar Chandra Barua, “Mishing’s Apong – An Intangible Cultural Heritage of Assam,” *The Exalted Edition / IJHEPS*, January 2014, Issue 7, pp. 59–62. The digital flipbook was uploaded/published by its platform on 17 January 2015; retain the printed issue date while noting the platform date discrepancy.

## 1. Sampling geography and dates

The article’s methods state that repeated surveys were carried out in Mising-dominated areas of **Jorhat district, Assam**. Prepared starter culture (Epop/Epop-pitha) and prepared Apong were collected for laboratory study **mostly from five villages**:

- Sirum
- Budubari
- Boloma
- Bhomoraguri
- Arunamukh

Plant samples were collected in April, May and June of 2011 and 2012. The wording “mostly from five villages” does not establish that every microbial or beverage sample came from every named village, nor does it give a sample count or village-to-sample matrix. Do not create five village-level microbial occurrence records or pin individual isolates to these villages.

## 2. What material was cultured

The methods explicitly state that microorganisms involved in rice fermentation were isolated **from Epop** by serial dilution. This is starter-cake evidence, not a direct microbial census of finished Apong liquid.

The reported culture conditions were:

- Enterobacteriaceae: violet red bile glucose agar (HiMedia M581), 30°C for 48 hours.
- Lactic acid bacteria: MRS agar supplemented with 1% calcium carbonate; plates incubated at 30°C for 24–72 hours.
- Yeasts and moulds: Sabouraud dextrose agar, pH 5.6, supplemented with streptomycin 10 µg/mL; 28°C for 48 hours.
- Total viable cells: plate count agar (HiMedia M091A), 30°C for 48 hours.

Yeasts were identified using colony and microscopic appearance plus germ-tube test; moulds by colony/microscopic appearance and reproductive structures. The authors say organisms were identified “at least to the group or lowest possible genera.” The article's accessible text does not provide a readable isolate-by-isolate strain register, accession numbers or molecular sequence identifications.

## 3. Reported organisms and limits

The abstract and results report three species of *Saccharomyces* and one species each of *Mucor* and *Rhizopus* from the starter culture. The accessible text does not expose the taxon names below genus, isolate codes, or a table mapping each organism to a village or starter batch. Do not infer species names from the genera, or link these organisms to SRC-167's genus-level Po:ro consortium.

The paper describes both Nogin and Po:ro preparation processes, but the microbiological subsection reports organisms isolated from Epop generally. It does **not** state that the *Saccharomyces*, *Mucor* or *Rhizopus* findings are specific to Po:ro or to Nogin. Retain this as Mising Epop/Apong starter-culture evidence with subtype unresolved.

The authors report no growth of Enterobacteriaceae or lactic acid bacteria on the selected media and infer that fermentation was free from bacterial involvement. This conclusion is stronger than the method can establish: the result is limited to the sampled Epop material, the selected media, incubation conditions and the organisms recoverable under them. It is not proof that bacteria are absent from all Epop, fermenting mash or finished Apong. It also does not negate later LAB recovery in SRC-165, which used a distinct study/sample set and culture workflow.

The reported viable yeast-and-mould count is 118 × 10^7 cells/mL of “original culture sample.” It is a source-specific count for the tested starter culture; it is not subtype-resolved, species-resolved or a finished-beverage count.

## 4. Process context is not microbiological allocation

The article describes Nogin as boiled rice fermented with starter, with Apong accumulating after 3–4 days. For Po:ro, ash from specially burnt rice husk and hay is mixed with cooked rice before starter addition; the mash is kept 15–20 days in a conical bamboo frame lined with banana leaves, then drinking water is poured through and the filtrate collected. These descriptions establish process distinctions but do not connect the five reported microbial taxa to either subtype.

## 5. Archive decision

- Preserve Jorhat district and the five named villages as the study's broad sampling frame, with “mostly” and no village-to-sample allocation.
- Preserve April–June 2011 and 2012 as plant-collection months/years; do not assume the same dates apply to all microbial samples unless the paper says so.
- Classify the organisms as Epop starter-culture findings, not finished-drink findings.
- Keep Po:ro/Nogin attribution unresolved.
- Keep the three *Saccharomyces* species and one *Mucor* and *Rhizopus* species at the level reported; do not invent binomials or isolate IDs.
- Treat “no bacterial growth” as a media- and condition-bound negative result, not general bacterial absence.
- Do not create new source IDs, taxa, isolate records, village-specific microbial records or canonical recipes from this paper.
