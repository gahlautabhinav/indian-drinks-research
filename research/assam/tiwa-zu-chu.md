# Tiwa Zu / Chu — research dossier

**Record ID:** ASM-015-TZU  
**Community:** Tiwa (Lalung in some literature; use the community's preferred self-identification where possible)  
**Region:** Nagaon and Morigaon districts, Assam; wider distribution not yet mapped  
**Classification:** Rice-based fermented alcoholic beverage; some Hill-Tiwa ethnobotanical literature also mentions distillation, but the relationship to the Zu/Chu described in plains-Tiwa studies is unresolved.  
**Evidence status:** Beverage and starter-culture tradition documented by multiple sources; current locality-by-locality production and exact boundaries of Zu versus Chu require further verification.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-20

## Summary

Tiwa community sources describe a rice-based alcoholic beverage named **Zu** or **Chu**, prepared with a starter culture called **Bakhor**. A 2020 article, *Traditional Rice Beer of the Tiwa Community: An Analytical Exposition*, reports fieldwork in Boaropujia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat in Nagaon and Morigaon districts. It describes interviews with knowledgeable residents, particularly people involved in making the beverage, and reports plant ingredients used in Bakhor.

A 2023 ethnobotanical survey by Duarah, Kalita and Mishra independently lists Tiwa rice beer as Zu/Chu and its starter as Bakhor. It says its Tiwa starter-culture survey was conducted in Nagaon district and records 20 plants for the selected Tiwa sample. The survey's plant counts are study-specific; they should not be combined into a single universal recipe.

## Names and identity

- **Zu / Chu:** both forms appear in literature for the Tiwa beverage. The sources reviewed do not establish whether the spellings reflect dialect, transliteration, or different local preparations. They are retained as reported variants for now.
- **Bakhor:** starter culture/cake name, not a beverage synonym.
- **Hill-Tiwa Chu is kept separate for now.** Teron and Borthakur (2014; SRC-038) studied Hill-Tiwa ethnobotany in the Hamren subdivision of the then Karbi Anglong district, with fieldwork in ten named villages during 2007–2012. They describe Chu as a common Hill-Tiwa alcoholic drink made using rice-and-plant starter cakes, name Croton joufra leaves as a starter ingredient and Amomum koenigii or Acacia pennata as occasional substitutes, and report that some beer is distilled in a crude still. The paper does not map each beverage/process detail to each village or fully define the distilled product. Similar spelling alone is not enough to merge Hill-Tiwa Chu with Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu.

## Ingredients and preparation

The literature describes rice-based fermentation using a locally prepared plant-and-rice starter cake. The 2020 Tiwa article reports around 19 plant species used in Bakhor in its fieldwork; the 2023 survey records 20 plants in its selected sample. These totals are not contradictory proof of one fixed inventory: they come from different studies, and local practice can vary.

The 2020 study names field localities as Boaropujia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat, spanning Nagaon and Morigaon districts. Exact village-to-district assignment and present-day production status should be checked against the article's full text and local confirmation. Do not treat reported preparation times or plant lists as universal.

### Extracted starter-plant inventory (SRC-078)

Das (2016) lists 19 plant taxa associated with Bakhor preparation in the five-village Morigaon survey. The transcription is preserved in [`data/tiwa_bakhor_plant_inventory.csv`](../../data/tiwa_bakhor_plant_inventory.csv), including vernacular names, source spellings, any plant part named in the table, and the source's reported use or preparation role. The table's botanical names, author citations and some family labels contain spelling/identification issues; the CSV deliberately preserves the raw source strings rather than silently correcting them. It is an extraction layer, not a taxonomically validated checklist. The article's medicinal statements are reports of traditional beliefs or uses and are not evidence of clinical efficacy or safety.

## Cultural context and reported health claims

The sources describe Zu/Chu as part of Tiwa food and social life. The 2020 article reports traditional beliefs that the drink or starter has medicinal properties, including use in connection with human and cattle ailments. These are ethnographic reports of community beliefs, not clinical evidence of safety or efficacy. This archive does not endorse alcoholic beverages as treatment.

The 2023 survey also reports a medicinal concoction called **Juguli** given to bulls in some Tiwa contexts. Juguli is not automatically a synonym for Zu/Chu; it is recorded as a related ethnoveterinary practice lead only.

## Geographic evidence

- **Morigaon district:** Das (2016) explicitly identifies five villages—Borchila, Ga-khujuwa, Kahua-ati, Gokhai-khonda and Dalang-ghat—as sites for an August–December 2015 field survey on Tiwa rice-beer preparation and Bakhor starter plants. This is the clearest village-level evidence in the current source set, but it is historical and does not establish current production.
- **Nagaon and Morigaon:** Goswami et al. (2020) lists Boaropujia/Boarpurpia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat in a study spanning both districts. The accessible text does not clearly map every locality to a specific district; do not force an assignment.
- **Nagaon district:** Duarah et al. (2023) describes its selected Tiwa starter-culture survey as taking place in Nagaon district, but the source record used so far does not provide a village-by-village production map.
- **Separate Hill-Tiwa study area (not mapped to this canonical Plains-Tiwa record):** Teron and Borthakur (2014; SRC-038) report fieldwork during 2007–2012 in Umdoba, Morten, Khumrei Khora, Ulukunchi, Umswai, Boramli, Khyabat, Bor Rongkhoi, Ghelani and Tumpreng in the Hamren subdivision of the then Karbi Anglong district. The study describes Hill-Tiwa Chu but does not establish that every named village prepares the same product or that the sometimes-distilled form is identical to Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu. Current district assignments should be checked against later administrative boundary changes.
- **Current production:** not established comprehensively. Published fieldwork demonstrates documented practice at the time of research, not current brewing in every named locality.

## Evidence assessment

**Documented**
- Tiwa association with rice-based Zu/Chu.
- Bakhor as the starter-culture name.
- A 2020 field study with named locality leads in Nagaon and Morigaon districts.
- A 2023 ethnobotanical survey identifying Nagaon as its Tiwa study area and recording a sample-specific starter-plant inventory.

**Unresolved**
- Whether Zu and Chu represent transliteration variants or distinct local products.
- Exact village-to-district assignments and current household production.
- Local variation in Bakhor ingredients, rice types, equipment, fermentation duration and storage.
- Whether the distilled drink mentioned in Hill-Tiwa literature is the same product or a distinct preparation.
- The local role and terminology of Juguli, documented separately from the beverage.

## Research ethics

Treat starter-plant lists as source-specific documentation, not as a universal recipe or permission to commercialize community knowledge. New fieldwork should use informed consent, credit knowledge holders and respect any ceremonial or otherwise restricted information.

## Follow-up research pass — 2026-10-23

### New finding: Hill-Tiwa Chu must remain analytically separate from Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu

The full text of Teron & Borthakur (2014), *Ethnobotanical appraisal of the Hill-Tiwas of Assam, India* (*Pleione* 8(1): 109–119), confirms a Hill-Tiwa field study conducted from 2007 to 2012 in ten villages in the Hamren subdivision of then-Karbi Anglong district:

- Umdoba
- Morten
- Khumrei Khora
- Ulukunchi
- Umswai
- Boramli
- Khyabat
- Bor Rongkhoi
- Ghelani
- Tumpreng

The article describes Chu as a locally important alcoholic rice beverage, including use in traditional rituals. It reports starter cakes made from rice and plant ingredients. The source-specific primary plant is *Croton joufra* Roxb.; *Amomum koenigii* and bark of *Acacia pennata* are described as occasional substitutes. It also reports that some beer is distilled in a crude still.

**Interpretation:** This is direct evidence for a Hill-Tiwa Chu tradition and its broad study-area footprint, but it does not establish that each of the ten villages used every reported ingredient or distilled the beverage. Nor does it prove that the distilled product is identical to the Plains-Tiwa beverage recorded as Zu/Chu. Do not create a synonym link or merge process descriptions without better evidence.

### Plains-Tiwa comparison

Das's 2015 field survey, published as “Medicinal plants used traditionally for the preparation of rice beer by the Tiwa tribe of Morigaon district of Assam, India,” explicitly names five Morigaon villages: Borchila, Ga-khujuwa, Kahua-ati, Gokhai-khonda and Dalang-ghat. It describes the starter cake as *bakhor* and lists approximately 19 plant species. These village-level data are a separate Plains-Tiwa evidence set; they must not be mixed with the ten Hill-Tiwa study villages.

The 2023 multi-community ethnobotanical survey reports 20 plants used in Tiwa starter culture. The difference between 19 and 20 should be treated as source/sampling variation pending a taxon-by-taxon reconciliation, not as an error to silently normalize. Reported traditional medicinal uses are not clinical evidence.

### Geographic and record decisions

1. Keep canonical record **ASM-015-TZU** for the Plains-Tiwa tradition as currently scoped.
2. Keep the Hill-Tiwa Chu evidence as a distinct comparison lead until the name/process boundary is community-verified; do not silently create a new canonical beverage merely because the article uses the same romanized word.
3. Record the ten Hill-Tiwa villages as **study villages**, not as proof that every village currently produces Chu.
4. Keep the five Das (2015) Plains-Tiwa villages separate from the five localities reported by Goswami et al. (2020), whose individual district assignments remain unclear in the accessible source.
5. Next steps: reconcile starter-plant lists from Das (2016 publication), Goswami et al. (2020), and Duarah et al. (2023); check the original article table against accepted botanical names; seek locality-specific current-production evidence.

## New current-practice lead — urban-adjacent Plains-Tiwa Zu (2025 fieldwork)

A recently surfaced study by Janarddan Deka and Nishika Dey, “Brewing Continuity in an Urban Tiwa Settlement: Production, Cost, Women's Work, and the Cultural Meaning of Zu in Betkuchi Lalung Gaon, Assam,” provides a more recent fieldwork lead than the 2015–2020 village surveys. The journal abstract reports fieldwork from approximately 25 January to 10 February 2025, combining household survey, observation, interviews, case studies and group discussion in 25 of 37 recorded households. The settlement is identified as Betkuchi Lalung Gaon in Kamrup Metropolitan, Assam.

The abstract describes Zu-making as an ongoing household and cultural practice: women prepare or obtain Bakhor starter cakes, process and cool rice, add starter, monitor fermentation, filter the drink, assess quality and manage household sales. It associates Zu with hospitality, ancestral offerings, festivals and life-cycle rites, and reports changes related to limited space, plant availability, education and shifting preferences. Treat these findings as one settlement case study, not a statewide generalisation.

**Economic data caveat:** the abstract reports input costs of ₹480 for a five-kilogram batch while the stated yield and price imply gross receipts of ₹200–300. Its monthly earnings figure cannot therefore be treated as verified profit without checking the full paper and reconciling the arithmetic.

**Geographic decision:** add Betkuchi Lalung Gaon as a documented 2025 fieldwork locality for Plains-Tiwa Zu. This updates the evidence base but does not prove production in all 37 households or all nearby Tiwa settlements.

Source: Deka, J. & Dey, N. (2025), *Brewing Continuity in an Urban Tiwa Settlement: Production, Cost, Women's Work, and the Cultural Meaning of Zu in Betkuchi Lalung Gaon, Assam*. Journal page: https://nrjss.in/social-science ; DOI listed by the journal: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22629564

### Updated synthesis
- Plains-Tiwa Zu/Bakhor is documented across multiple study periods and localities, including a 2025 urban-adjacent settlement case in Kamrup Metropolitan.
- The recent case adds current-practice, labour and household-economy evidence that is absent from the earlier starter-plant inventories.
- Hill-Tiwa Chu remains a distinct comparison lead pending community-verified product boundaries; similar romanized names do not establish synonymy.
