# Tiwa Zu / Chu — research dossier

**Record ID:** ASM-015-TZU  
**Community:** Tiwa (Lalung in some literature; use the community's preferred self-identification where possible)  
**Region:** Nagaon and Morigaon districts, Assam; wider distribution not yet mapped  
**Classification:** Rice-based fermented alcoholic beverage; some Hill-Tiwa ethnobotanical literature also mentions distillation, but the relationship to the Zu/Chu described in plains-Tiwa studies is unresolved.  
**Evidence status:** Beverage and starter-culture tradition documented by multiple sources; current locality-by-locality production and exact boundaries of Zu versus Chu require further verification.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-20

## Summary

Tiwa community sources describe a rice-based alcoholic beverage named **Zu** or **Chu**, prepared with a starter culture called **Bakhor**. A 2020 article, *Traditional Rice Beer of the Tiwa Community: An Analytical Exposition*, reports fieldwork in Boaropujia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat in Nagaon and Morigaon districts. It describes interviews with knowledgeable residents, particularly people involved in making the beverage, and reports plant ingredients used in Bakhor.

A 2023 ethnobotanical survey by Duarah, Kalita and Mishra independently lists Tiwa rice beer as Zu/Chu and its starter as Bakhor. It says its Tiwa starter-culture survey was conducted in Nagaon district and records 20 plants for the selected Tiwa sample. The survey's plant counts are study-specific; they should not be combined into a single universal recipe.

## Names and identity

- **Zu / Chu:** both forms appear in literature for the Tiwa beverage. The sources reviewed do not establish whether the spellings reflect dialect, transliteration, or different local preparations. They are retained as reported variants for now.
- **Bakhor:** starter culture/cake name, not a beverage synonym.
- Some Hill-Tiwa ethnobotanical literature describes rice beer called Chu and notes that some beer is distilled in a crude still. The available evidence does not establish that this distilled product is identical to every Zu/Chu described in the plains-Tiwa field studies. Do not create a separate canonical distilled record until a source clearly defines the product boundary.

## Ingredients and preparation

The literature describes rice-based fermentation using a locally prepared plant-and-rice starter cake. The 2020 Tiwa article reports around 19 plant species used in Bakhor in its fieldwork; the 2023 survey records 20 plants in its selected sample. These totals are not contradictory proof of one fixed inventory: they come from different studies, and local practice can vary.

The 2020 study names field localities as Boaropujia, Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat, spanning Nagaon and Morigaon districts. Exact village-to-district assignment and present-day production status should be checked against the article's full text and local confirmation. Do not treat reported preparation times or plant lists as universal.

## Cultural context and reported health claims

The sources describe Zu/Chu as part of Tiwa food and social life. The 2020 article reports traditional beliefs that the drink or starter has medicinal properties, including use in connection with human and cattle ailments. These are ethnographic reports of community beliefs, not clinical evidence of safety or efficacy. This archive does not endorse alcoholic beverages as treatment.

The 2023 survey also reports a medicinal concoction called **Juguli** given to bulls in some Tiwa contexts. Juguli is not automatically a synonym for Zu/Chu; it is recorded as a related ethnoveterinary practice lead only.

## Geographic evidence

- **Nagaon district:** selected Tiwa starter-culture survey in Duarah et al. (2023); localities named by the 2020 paper include Raha, Kahua-ati, Neeli and Dalang-ghat in a study covering Nagaon and Morigaon.
- **Morigaon district:** named in the 2020 paper's fieldwork description; precise village mapping requires full-text verification.
- **Current production:** not established comprehensively. Published fieldwork demonstrates documented practice at the time of research, not current brewing in every named locality.

## Evidence assessment

**Documented**
- Tiwa association with rice-based Zu/Chu.
- Bakhor as the starter-culture name.
- A 2020 field study with named locality leads in Nagaon and Morigaon districts.
- A 2023 ethnobotanical survey identifying Nagaon as its Tiwa study area and recording a sample-specific starter-plant inventory.

**Unresolved**
- Whether Zu and Chu represent transliteration variants or distinct local products.
- Exact village-to-district assignments and current household production.
- Local variation in Bakhor ingredients, rice types, equipment, fermentation duration and storage.
- Whether the distilled drink mentioned in Hill-Tiwa literature is the same product or a distinct preparation.
- The local role and terminology of Juguli, documented separately from the beverage.

## Research ethics

Treat starter-plant lists as source-specific documentation, not as a universal recipe or permission to commercialize community knowledge. New fieldwork should use informed consent, credit knowledge holders and respect any ceremonial or otherwise restricted information.
