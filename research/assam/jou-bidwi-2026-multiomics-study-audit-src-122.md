# Jou Bidwi multi-omics study audit — SRC-122

**Audit date:** 2026-10-24  
**Source:** Das, Rohit; Medhi, Madhu Chandana; Tamang, Buddhiman (2026), “Microbial diversity and its links to retinol pathways and aroma compounds in ethnic fermented rice beverages of Assam,” *AMB Express* 16, article 91. [Open-access article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13568-026-02062-0).

## Evidence extracted

- The study used integrated metagenomic sequencing and GC–MS/MS-based metabolomics to examine four beverage categories: Jou Bidwi (JOU), Sai Mod (SM), Black Rohi Modh (BR), and Rohi Modh (RH).
- It reports twelve samples overall. For each beverage, the authentic material was obtained from a single household batch; laboratory triplicates are technical replicates, not independent batches or households.
- The Jou Bidwi sample was collected at **Kaljar, Baksa district, Assam**. This is a useful, source-attributed locality lead for one observed study batch, not proof of general production prevalence or a complete distribution map.
- The paper reports ethanol around **5.5% v/v for the Jou Bidwi sample** and pH values across all four beverages of 4.1–4.5. These are study-specific measurements and must not be copied into a universal beverage specification.
- The authors report associations between microbial composition and metabolite profiles. Functional annotation of retinol-related pathways is described as microbial metabolic potential; it is not evidence that the beverage directly supplies vitamin A or confers a clinical benefit.

## Identity and scope cautions

The paper uses the name “Jou Bidwi”; the GI registry has a separate registered product named **Bodo Maibra Jou Bidwi** (application 970). The study excerpt does not establish that its sampled household product conforms to the GI statement of case, the named Maibra rice specification, or any legally defined GI boundary. Keep the scientific beverage sample and the GI product linked as related evidence only, not asserted synonyms.

## Research use

1. Add Kaljar, Baksa as a source-supported study-sampling locality lead only if the place register can retain the source's explicit relation (“sample collected here”), distinct from a verified general production site.
2. Preserve ethanol and pH as sample-level observations with method/source context, not as canonical beverage attributes.
3. Use the paper as a technical comparison point for future process/name reconciliation, but do not close ASM-GAP-026: it does not provide the official GI Statement of Case, amended map, or producer list.

## Limitations

One batch per beverage limits generalisation. The study is valuable scientific evidence for the samples analysed, but not a representative household or district survey, and it is not a legal GI specification.
