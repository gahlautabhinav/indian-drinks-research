# ASM-GAP-075 — Bajali Bodo beverage locality evidence audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-19  
**Question:** Is there evidence that a named Bodo community/household in present-day Bajali district prepares or consumes a named Jou form, and can the practice be assigned to a named locality?

## Finding

**No beverage-specific Bajali village is verified by the sources reviewed. Keep ASM-GAP-075 Open. Do not add a beverage-place row.**

The research corpus does now contain ten named villages described as Bodo-dominated or Bodo-community study sites in Bajali:
- **SRC-176 (2025):** Chenglimari, Duramari, Mwithabari, Jamguri, Bharegaon, Sonapur and Kathalmurighat. This is a 2022–2024 ethnomedicinal survey of 64 informants and 131 medicinal plants.
- **SRC-177 (2026):** Doloigaon, Bamakhata and Muguria. The accessible abstract reports 94 informants and 101 medicinal plant species from September 2024–January 2025; the full text was not accessible in this audit.

Neither source documents Jou/Jou bishi/Joubidwi/Joufinai/Jougwran, Amao/Angkur starter culture, beverage preparation, consumption, producer households or beverage batches. These ten villages remain **ethnomedicinal study sites only**, not beverage production sites.

## Source-by-source assessment

### 1. Basumatary et al. (2025), SRC-176

The publisher's full article describes primary data collection in seven Bodo-dominated Bajali villages and reports 64 informants and 131 medicinal plant species. Its stated purpose and data are ethnomedicinal: plant names, plant parts, applications and healthcare practices. The presence of *Musa balbisiana* in the medicinal inventory is not evidence that it was used in Amao or any beverage starter. No beverage process, beverage consumption or starter-culture evidence is supplied.

### 2. Das et al. (2026), SRC-177

The publisher abstract reports a survey in Doloigaon, Bamakhata and Muguria, with 94 informants and 101 medicinal plants. Its stated subject is native medical practices and cultural legacy. The accessible abstract does not link any named village or informant to a Jou form or starter culture. Treat all claims as abstract-level until the full paper is acquired.

### 3. Deka et al. (2025), SRC-066

This is the strongest recent primary beverage-process source reviewed for Bodo Jou forms, but its fieldwork is in rural Kokrajhar-area localities around Kokrajhar headquarters: Amlaiguri, Gendrabili, Jalpaiguri and Banshigaon. It reports 50 interviews over April–July 2022 and October–November 2023. It describes Joubidwi, stored Joufinai and fruit-based/distilled Jougwran forms, plus Amao starter. It does **not** sample Bajali. Do not transfer its process or locality claims to Bajali.

### 4. Earlier Bajali sub-division ethnobotanical work (2015), SRC-296

Kalita et al. report a medicinal-plant survey in the former Bajali sub-division, Barpeta district, from December 2013 to December 2014. The paper names Bagapara, Balipara, Bamunkuchi, Bangaon, Barbang, Belona, Bhogpur, Dubi, Dumuria, Garemari, Gobindapur, Jalikhata, Kharadhara, Muguria, Pipla, Raipur and Titka among places where most plants were collected. It also says multiple communities, including Boro, lived in the villages. It does not allocate Boro respondents to particular villages and does not discuss Jou or starter culture. This is historical ethnobotanical/geographic context only.

### 5. Bajali bamboo study (2010), SRC-295

Sarma et al. report fieldwork in Bajali sub-division in 2008, including 17 sample plots across 15 villages and 258 respondents. A discussion paragraph notes that bamboo culms are used in Assam as containers for storing home-brewed rice beer. This is a general Assam-wide statement in a bamboo-use discussion; it is not a Bajali-specific observation, does not identify Bodo brewers, and does not identify a beverage name, village, household or producer. It cannot close the gap.

## Administrative boundary caution

Bajali was a sub-division of Barpeta before becoming a separate district by Assam Government notification dated 12 January 2021. Therefore, older records labelled “Barpeta district” or “Bajali sub-division” require a village-level boundary crosswalk before being assigned to the present-day district. Do not automatically equate all historic Barpeta records with current Bajali.

## What would close the gap

1. A consent-based interview or published observation that explicitly connects a named Bajali village and a Bodo household/producer to a named Jou form.
2. Community-preferred spellings and distinctions among Jou, Jou bishi, Joubidwi, Joufinai, Jou Gishi and Jou Gwran, with local starter terms (Amao/Angkur/Emao) recorded without assuming synonymy.
3. At least one source-specific process/batch record: rice or fruit substrate, starter, preparation/storage/distillation stages, vessel names, season/date and whether the account is current or historical.
4. A village-level administrative crosswalk for older “Bajali sub-division, Barpeta district” evidence.

**No author or community contact has been made.** No personal details from individual respondents have been transcribed. The medicinal plant inventories have not been copied into beverage ingredient records.
