# SRC-007 — Yein et al. (2022): Majuli Mising Apong 21-entry Epop inventory audit

**Source:** Jelshing Yein, Bhaba Kumar Pegu, James Sanong, Nirupam Raj Khanikar & Biswajit Pegu (2022), “Traditional Ethnomedicinal Practice in the Homemade Alcoholic Beverage of Mising Community of Majuli District, Upper Assam,” *Biosciences Biotechnology Research Asia* 19(2):497–500. DOI: 10.13005/bbra/3003. Open PDF: https://www.biotech-asia.org/pdf/vol19no2/BBRA_Vol_19_No_2_p_497-500.pdf .

## Field design and source scope

- The article says a field survey was conducted in Majuli district during September–December 2021 and that rural households involved in manufacturing homemade alcoholic beverages were the primary information source.
- It says plants were collected from nearby villages and forests and that more than 20 villages were visited. No full village list, respondent/informant count, plant-by-village matrix or herbarium voucher accessions are provided.
- The methods call the survey “three months” while giving the date range September–December 2021. Retain this as an internal duration discrepancy; do not silently resolve it.
- The article discusses both Po:ro Apong and Nogin Apong, but Table 1 is a general Epop starter-culture list. It does not allocate individual entries to either variant.

## Table 1 transcription

All 21 plant entries from Table 1 were transcribed into `src007-yein-2022-21-entry-inventory.csv`, preserving the printed scientific name, family, Mising local name, plant part and ethnomedical-use wording. The table includes rice (*Oryza sativa*) as an entry. Source spelling and labels are retained, including “Centala acciatica,” “Hydrocotyl rotundifolia(Roxb);”, “Occimum sunctum,” the family label “Laurels” for *Cinnamomum verum*, “Dogbanes” for *Rauvolfia serpentina*, and the assignment of *Vitex negundo* to Lamiaceae. These require separate authority review before any normalization.

A table-level ambiguity exists for *Lygodium japonicum*: the plant-part column says leaves, while the ethnomedical-use text says root and leaves. The *Zingiber officinale* row says “Bulb”; that is retained as printed, not silently rewritten to rhizome. The *Swertia chirata* part field prints “Leaves and Burk.” The *Rauvolfia serpentina* local-name cell wraps across lines; it is transcribed as “Arachoritita / Sarpagandha.”

## Ethnomedicinal claims: evidence boundary

The “Ethno medical used” column records traditional-use statements attributed to villagers. It is not clinical evidence that the plants treat the named conditions, and it does not establish dose, safety, efficacy, interactions or suitability for self-medication. Archive these as reported traditional knowledge only.

## Comparison with existing Mising inventories

- SRC-170 (Pawe & Gogoi 2013): Poro-specific Apob list, 28 taxon-level entries, Lakhimpur/Sonitpur.
- SRC-171 (Sarma & Devi 2016): 11 dicot entries explicitly for Nogin/Nogjin Epop, Sonitpur; not a complete mixed-flora recipe.
- SRC-172 (Mili & Sundriyal 2023): 31 general ÉPOP taxa, Sivasagar; no Poro/Nogin allocation.
- SRC-007 (Yein et al. 2022): 21 general Epop entries, Majuli; no Poro/Nogin allocation.
- SRC-148 (Borkotoky et al. 2014): reports 51 general Epop taxa in Jorhat; individual Table 1 entries remain unavailable from searchable text/image extraction.

These inventories differ by geography, field period, inclusion criteria and variant attribution. Do not sum them or call them one standard recipe. No household prevalence or plant-by-village claims can be derived from the overlap alone.

## Source limitations

- No botanical vouchers or accession numbers disclosed.
- No respondent count or named village list despite “more than 20 villages.”
- No plant-to-village allocation.
- No Po:ro versus Nogin allocation for Table 1 taxa.
- Internal “three months” versus September–December date-range discrepancy.
- Several source spellings, family labels and plant-part entries need authority verification.
- Traditional medicinal statements are not clinical efficacy or safety evidence.

## Source-ID reconciliation and table-image verification (2026-11-13)

The article's DOI (10.13005/bbra/3003), title, authors and year were already registered as SRC-007. SRC-173 was a duplicate source-ID registration, not a second study. The 21-row expanded transcription has therefore been merged into the canonical SRC-007 inventory at `research/assam/src007-yein-2022-21-entry-inventory.csv`; SRC-173 is retained in the source register as an alias only and must not be counted independently.

The original publisher PDF page 499 (Table 1) was checked against the earlier reduced transcription. The expanded transcription preserves the printed forms that the earlier file had normalized or mistyped: row 6 `Hydrocotyl rotundifolia(Roxb);` (spacing and semicolon as printed); row 8 part used `Leaves and Burk` (not “Leaves and bark”); and row 15 `Occimum sunctum` (no trailing “L.” in the table). The table's family for Psidium guajava is Myrtaceae. The article's narrative list of “21 plant families” is not treated as a verified count of unique families. The table remains a general Epop inventory and does not allocate taxa to Po:ro versus Nogin. No plant-by-village, voucher or household assignments were added.
