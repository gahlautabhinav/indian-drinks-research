# SRC-153 — Sivasagar ÉPOP field-matrix and herbarium voucher retrieval audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-19  
**Parent gap:** ASM-GAP-068  
**Primary source:** Mili, R. & Sundriyal, R.C. (2023), “ÉPOP (traditional starter culture): a complex composition of plant resources prepared by the Misings of Assam, Northeast India,” *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 10:47, DOI 10.1186/s42779-023-00214-2.

## 1. What the primary article actually says about fieldwork

The publisher PDF's Methods section states:
- An extensive survey covered **15 randomly selected villages in Sivasagar district**.
- Two representative villages, **Majorbari and Ligiribari**, were then selected for in-depth study.
- Knowledgeable women from the selected villages were interviewed formally and informally using a semi-structured questionnaire.
- Researchers observed collection of plant ingredients from natural habitats and documented the species, place and time of collection, land use, starter preparation and use.
- Collected specimens were identified with assistance from taxonomists at the Botanical Survey of India (BSI), Arunachal Station, Itanagar.
- The authors say a herbarium was prepared by comparing specimens with regional herbaria at ARUN and ASSAM and consulting regional floras.
- The species composition, source of collection and preparation were observed during practice; the plant species used to prepare cakes were recorded in a field notebook.
- Qualitative interview, group-discussion, direct-field and participant-observation material was compiled and tabulated.

The Methods section does **not** publish the fieldwork dates, total interviewee/informant count, number of households, interview schedule, full village register, plant-by-village matrix, collector numbers, voucher accession numbers, or a named repository where the study's own vouchers were deposited. The phrase about comparing specimens with ARUN and ASSAM herbaria does not itself establish that the newly collected study specimens were deposited at either herbarium.

## 2. Village-name reconciliation: a useful lead, not a 15-village register

The Acknowledgements name villagers from 13 settlements, grouped as follows:
- **Disangmukh:** Majorbari, Ligiribari, Afala, Balama and Charaipora.
- **Rajabari:** Dolopa, Balikur, Samukjan, Kotiari and Milanpur.
- **Charaideo:** Gumutha, Ramnagar and Balikuthi.

The paper separately identifies Majorbari and Ligiribari as the two in-depth villages. Figure 1 (PDF page 4) shows 13 unlabeled study-area point symbols, whereas Methods state 15 randomly selected villages. The 13 named acknowledgements and 13 plotted symbols are suggestively concordant, but the article never says that the acknowledgement list is the full 15-village sample frame, does not label the map points, and does not explain the two-village difference.

**Geographic caution:** the Methods call the study area Sivasagar district, while the acknowledgements separately name three Charaideo settlements. Do not silently reassign the latter to the 15-village survey or assume all 13 acknowledgement settlements were survey sites. They may have supported field visits in another capacity. No village-level Apong/ÉPOP production record or taxon occurrence should be created from acknowledgements alone.

## 3. Voucher and taxonomic evidence

The article states that plant specimens were identified with BSI Arunachal Station taxonomist assistance, naming Dr. Ritesh Kr. Chowdhury in the acknowledgements. It also states that a herbarium was prepared by comparison with regional herbaria ARUN and ASSAM. It does not list any specimen voucher number, field book number, collector number, barcode, herbarium accession, or deposited-sheet location.

The BSI official page for the Arunachal Pradesh Regional Centre confirms that ARUN is its regional herbarium at Itanagar and that it holds approximately 36,000 angiosperm and pteridophyte specimens. Its public institutional page gives the centre's contact route. This establishes ARUN as a real and relevant institutional acquisition route, **not** that SRC-153's specimens are among its holdings. The BSI Indian Virtual Herbarium describes label-level metadata fields (including collector, collection number, date and locality) and search by species, collector, collection number, state and country; however, no SRC-153-specific voucher was identified in this pass.

The 31-entry SRC-153 transcription remains source-faithful. Preserve source spellings and taxonomic uncertainty; do not silently harmonise *Selaginella* sp. to a species or equate *Cyclosorus extensa* with a modern synonym without specimen evidence. The four pteridophyte records in the inventory are not evidence of four village-specific occurrences.

## 4. Data-availability and field-notebook implications

The publisher article's “Availability of data and materials” statement is **“Not applicable.”** The Methods nevertheless say that plant-cake species were recorded in a field notebook and that field observation, interviews and group discussions were compiled. Therefore, the original field notebook and associated specimen labels/registers are the most direct potential source for the missing matrix; no public supplementary dataset is identified by the article.

This is a targeted acquisition lead rather than proof that the notebook or vouchers remain extant. No email or phone contact has been made.

## 5. Exact retrieval request

Write to corresponding author **Rajiv Mili** and request, for SRC-153:
1. The complete list of all 15 randomly selected survey villages, with official/local spellings and block/area association.
2. Clarification whether the 13 acknowledgement settlements are the 15-village survey frame, field-visit support sites, or a partly overlapping set; identify the two additional village names if applicable.
3. Identification of the 13 symbols in Figure 1, and an explanation of the 15-village / 13-map-symbol discrepancy.
4. The plant-by-village matrix: each of the 31 source taxa, vernacular name, plant part, whether used in ÉPOP, observed/confirmed village(s), informant or household count where shareable, and collection land-use category.
5. Fieldwork dates, number of knowledgeable women interviewed, household count, questionnaire/field schedule and any consent/benefit-sharing documentation suitable for archival access.
6. Field notebook extracts or anonymised transcriptions supporting the 31 plant entries and collection-source percentages.
7. Collector names, field numbers, specimen label data, determination slips, voucher accession numbers, and the exact herbarium (ARUN, ASSAM, author-held reference collection, or other) in which each study specimen was deposited.
8. Whether the plant materials were all collected from the two in-depth villages or across the 15-village survey, and which observations underpin the forest/home-garden/agricultural-field/fallow-land dependency marks.
9. Confirmation of the botanical determination and source spelling for each taxon, especially genus-level *Selaginella* sp. and the fern named *Cyclosorus extensa* in the cake-protection description.

**Institutional route:** BSI Arunachal Pradesh Regional Centre, Itanagar (ARUN), official centre page: https://bsi.gov.in/rc-page/en?rcu=127%2C35. Ask the centre to search its accession register and herbarium sheets by author/project, Mising/Sivasagar locality, collector(s), and the taxonomist named in the paper. A parallel enquiry to BSI Eastern Regional Centre, Shillong (ASSAM) may establish whether the paper's comparison material or any new specimens were deposited there. The ARUN page's general holdings are not evidence of SRC-153-specific custody.

## 6. Evidence safeguards

- Keep 15 villages (Methods), 2 in-depth villages (Methods), 13 acknowledgement settlements, and 13 unlabeled map symbols as four distinct facts.
- Do not infer which two villages are missing from the acknowledgement list.
- Do not treat Majorbari and Ligiribari as the only survey sites; they are explicitly the two in-depth sites.
- Do not treat Charaideo acknowledgement villages as confirmed ÉPOP-production sites or as members of the 15-village Sivasagar survey without author confirmation.
- Do not infer that ARUN/ASSAM hold the study's own voucher sheets from the wording “herbarium was prepared by comparing them with regional herbaria”.
- No village-by-taxon occurrence, new beverage-place row, voucher accession or fieldwork date is added in this audit.
