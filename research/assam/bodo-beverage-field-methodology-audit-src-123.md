# Bodo beverage field-methodology audit — SRC-123

**Review date:** 2026-10-25  
**Source:** Anamika Kalita Deka, Sunshri Basumatary and Dibakar Chandra Deka (2025), “Methodology of ethnic cereal-based and fruit-based beverages of Bodo community of Assam,” *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 12, article 23. [Open-access full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-025-00286-2).

## Fieldwork and geographic scope

The authors report interviews with 50 people (35 men and 15 women) during summer 2022 and winter 2023. The methods section names Amlaiguri, Gendrabili, Jalpaiguri and Banshigaon as rural survey villages, described by distance and direction from Kokrajhar district headquarters. These names are evidence of the survey's fieldwork context, but the article does not allocate every beverage preparation described later to each village individually. Therefore, do not create four product-specific production-place claims from the methods list alone.

## Findings relevant to the Bodo beverage taxonomy

- **Amao:** the paper describes a rice-and-plant starter culture, with named plant ingredients including Anaros (jackfruit), Kanthal (jackfruit), Dongphangrakeb (*Scoparia dulcis*), Thalir (*Musa balbisiana*) and Mwkwna (*Clerodendrum viscosum*); it also notes that Kuser (*Saccharum officinarum*) leaves may sometimes be added. The paper reports sun-drying starter cakes for 2–3 days and storage near fireplaces, with a stated storage life of 6 months to 1 year. Treat these as source-reported practices, not a universal fixed formula.
- **Joufinai:** described as a stored rice-based beverage distinct from direct-fermented Joubidwi. Cooked Mairong rice is cooled, mixed with Amao to form Jumai, rested overnight on banana leaves, then transferred to a vessel for fermentation/storage. The paper discusses storage intervals including 3, 6, 10 and 12 months and reports colour changes with age. It should remain a distinct canonical beverage record from Joubidwi.
- **Kanthal-Jougwran:** described as a fruit-based distilled beverage made from jackfruit cloves (excluding seeds) mixed with Amao and fermented for up to three days before distillation.
- **Kola-Jougwran:** described as a fruit-based distilled beverage made with mashed bananas and Amao, fermented for 3–4 days before distillation.
- The paper describes a four-vessel distillation arrangement using locally named components. These process descriptions provide useful terminology leads for the existing Jou Gwran audit.

## Critical distinction: fruit-based Jougwran versus GI Bodo Jou Gwran

This article explicitly distinguishes cereal-based and fruit-based preparations, including Kanthal-Jougwran and Kola-Jougwran. The GI registry has a registered product named **Bodo Jou Gwran** (application 968), but the registry metadata alone does not show whether its specification covers fruit-based variants, rice-based variants, or both. Do not merge these product concepts or attach fruit-based process claims to the registered GI product until the official Statement of Case and amendments are readable.

## Research implications

1. Use this source to sharpen the name/process comparison among Joubidwi, Joufinai and fruit-based Jougwran.
2. Preserve the four named villages as fieldwork locations only unless another source links a named beverage process to a specific village.
3. Compare the reported Amao ingredients and process with the separate 2026 multi-omics Jou Bidwi paper (SRC-122) and the community heritage source (SRC-119), noting differing scopes and evidence types.
4. Keep ASM-GAP-026 open: this peer-reviewed article is valuable secondary process evidence but is not the GI Statement of Case, amended map or official producer list.

## Source limitations

The article is a peer-reviewed ethnographic/methodological report, not the legal GI specification. It reports customary beliefs about medicinal plants; those beliefs should not be repeated as demonstrated clinical effects. Its named survey villages are not by themselves evidence that every product described is currently made in every village.
