# Hajang Jatha Kasa and Ruhi — Assam research note

**Status:** Open identity and locality question; no canonical beverage record created.  
**Community label in the source lead:** Hajang (spelled “Hajang” on the Assam Tourism page; other sources may use “Hajong”).  
**Geographic confidence:** Low. The tourism page names the community but gives no district, village, recipe or fieldwork details.

## What is currently supported

The Assam Tourism Development Corporation's “Culinary Treasures of Assam” page lists **Jatha Kasa and Ruhi** as beverages consumed by the Hajang community. This is useful as a discovery lead, but the page does not cite its underlying evidence or provide preparation details. It does not establish whether Jatha Kasa and Ruhi are separate products, alternate names, or locally restricted terms. [SRC-047](https://assamtourism.gov.in/cuisine.php)

The reviewed sources use similar words in unrelated or not-yet-connected contexts:

- **Ruhi as a beverage:** an IGNOU-hosted educational chapter describes “Indian Ruhi” as a fermented-rice beverage in Nagaland/eastern hill regions. It does not identify a Hajang community or an Assam village. Do not attribute its process or reported alcohol content to Hajang Ruhi. [SRC-053](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/11353/5/Unit-12.pdf)
- **Ruhi as an implement:** a government-hosted overview uses Ruhi in the context of extracting liquid during a Rabha Kesa/Jonga-mod preparation. This is described as a tool, not established as the name of a drink. [SRC-049](https://www.spniwcd.wcd.gov.in/archive/download/2zq4zt)
- **Haj / Haj pani:** a separate Assam rice-beer review uses Haj pani / Koloh pani for a Tai-Ahom beverage and reports Baragharia village, Golaghat district in that section. This spelling resemblance is not evidence of a relationship to Hajang Jatha Kasa or Ruhi. [SRC-022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dit.2013.09.002)

These records should remain separate unless community-preferred terminology and source context establish a connection.

## What is not established

- Whether Jatha Kasa and Ruhi are two distinct Hajang beverages.
- The preferred Hajang-language spellings, pronunciation, meanings, and any distinction between beverage names and implements.
- Any named Assam district, block, village, household or current production site.
- Ingredients, rice variety, starter-culture name, fermentation or distillation process, equipment, seasonal pattern, serving practices or ceremonial contexts.
- Whether the tourism page reflects present-day practice, a historical source, or a broad regional label.

## Research protocol and next steps

1. Find Hajang/Hajong-language ethnographies, historical accounts and community-authored sources that explicitly name Jatha Kasa or Ruhi.
2. Record the exact citation, publication date, spelling as printed, named community/subcommunity, district, village and whether the author observed preparation or merely repeated a label.
3. Seek consent-based confirmation from Hajang knowledge holders before assigning ingredients, methods or cultural meanings; do not publish restricted ceremonial knowledge without permission.
4. Only create separate canonical beverage entries when evidence distinguishes the products. Until then, retain the pair as an unresolved research lead under `ASM-GAP-038`.
