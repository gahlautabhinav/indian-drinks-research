# Nalbari district beverage-coverage audit — 14 November 2026

## Decision

**No Nalbari beverage-specific place row is added.** Nalbari remains at zero place rows in the district matrix. This is an evidence-coverage decision, not evidence that traditional beverages are absent.

## 1. Bodo Jou sources

### SRC-184 — Barmahalia & Basumatary (2025)

The full PDF of *Indigenous Knowledge on Jou Fermentation: An Analysis in the Bodo Society* was retrieved (Journal of Anthropological and Archaeological Sciences 11(1), DOI 10.32474/JAAS.2025.11.000354). It provides useful community-level documentation of Emao starter preparation and source-specific Jou forms: Jou Gisi/Jou Bidwi, Maibra Jou and Jou Gwran/Sereb. The article says it uses primary information from aged Bodo community members and secondary literature.

**Geographic limitation:** the methods do not identify the sampled district, village names, fieldwork dates or informant count. Thelemara, Sonitpur appears in a narrow comparison about adding rice husk to cooked rice; it is not clearly identified as the paper's sampling site. The article does not identify Nalbari. It is therefore registered as community/process context only, with no Nalbari place row.

A second 2025 article, *The Use of Rice-Beer in the Bodo Society*, by Rangina Basumatary and Faguna Barmahalia is listed by Gauhati University as published in a peer-reviewed journal by Nalbari Commerce College. The accessible university record provides bibliographic metadata but no full text, field methods or locality schedule. **Nalbari Commerce College is the publisher, not proof of Nalbari fieldwork.** It remains a retrieval lead and has not been treated as an independent locality source.

## 2. Bodo/Rabha household liquor evidence — excluded from Nalbari attribution

### SRC-186 — Rabha (2023), food-security thesis

The full Assam Agricultural University PhD thesis, *An Assessment of Food Security Condition among Selected Tribals of Bodoland Territorial Region of Assam*, includes household liquor preparation/use as a food-security variable. Full methods resolve its actual geography:

- Study districts: **Udalguri and Baksa**;
- Blocks: Udalguri, Bhergaon, Goreswar and Tamulpur;
- 20 named villages (10 in each district);
- 400 household respondents: 204 Bodo and 196 Rabha.

The results report whether respondents used foodgrain for liquor preparation. This is useful contextual evidence of household practice in the sampled BTR communities, but it is **not Nalbari fieldwork**. Nalbari is mentioned as a district adjoining Baksa in the geographical description, not as a selected district or study site. The thesis does not provide a named traditional beverage or starter-culture inventory in the relevant results. No Nalbari place record is justified.

## 3. Administrative history and boundary risk

### SRC-185 — Census of India 2011 District Census Handbook, Nalbari

The official handbook states that Nalbari was carved out of erstwhile Kamrup district in 1985. Its certified district/revenue-circle map identifies adjoining Baksa, Barpeta and Kamrup and marks part revenue circles under Baksa. This reinforces two separate cautions:

1. A historical source labelled only “Kamrup” cannot be reassigned to Nalbari without its named village and a source-era/current boundary crosswalk.
2. A locality or revenue-circle reference associated with old Nalbari geography cannot automatically be assigned to present Nalbari if it was subsequently transferred to Baksa.

The handbook is an administrative reference, not a beverage source. Its 2011 village directory is a useful starting point for crosswalk work but not a substitute for later boundary notifications.
