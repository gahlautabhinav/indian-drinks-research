# Cultural ecology and conservation study audit — SRC-124

**Review date:** 2026-10-25  
**Source:** Simi Talukdar, Darpa Saurav Jyethi, Barasa Sarma et al. (2026), “Cultural ecology and conservation of traditional beverage practices among selected communities of Assam, Northeast India,” *Discover Food* 6, article 376. [Open-access article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44187-026-01101-w).

## Design and geographic scope

The article reports fieldwork during February 2023–2024 across six districts: Bodo participants/sites in Kokrajhar and Baksa, Rabha in Goalpara and Kamrup, and Ahom in Tinsukia and Dibrugarh. It describes 30 key informants in total (10 per community), identified using snowball sampling, and methods including semi-structured interviews, focus groups, participant observation and plant specimen collection/identification.

The authors report 41 plant species across 29 families, with 85% described as wild-sourced. This is a comparative study of selected communities and sites, not an exhaustive statewide inventory or representative estimate of beverage practices across Assam. The 41-species total is pooled across Bodo, Rabha and Ahom; it must not be copied as a Bodo-only plant inventory.

## Bodo-relevant findings and caveats

- The general-method section describes Jou as rice fermented with a starter it calls “angkur,” with fermentation in earthen pots for five to seven days and possible distillation for a stronger variant. Elsewhere the article refers to a fermented herbal cake as “Bakhar” among Bodo and Rabha, while the 2025 Bodo-specific study (SRC-123) describes Amao. Preserve these source-specific terms; the new article does not establish that angkur, Bakhar and Amao are interchangeable labels in every locality or product.
- The paper discusses plants used in beverage preparation and broader ritual/ecological roles. Names appearing in its abstract/discussion include *Scoparia dulcis*, *Zingiber officinale* and *Musa* species, but a pooled cross-community list is not evidence that each plant belongs in every Bodo starter or every beverage. For ingredient updates, require table-level community/product attribution and compare with source-specific inventories already in the archive.
- It describes harvesting practices, sacred groves/Thans, restrictions on uprooting and community norms. These should be presented as reported practices among the sampled informants, not universal rules for all Bodo, Rabha or Ahom people.
- The article discusses medicinal beliefs and potential biomedical relevance. This audit does not treat those claims as demonstrated health benefits of consuming the beverages.

## Quantitative-index consistency flag

The article defines Fidelity Level (FL%) as the ratio of informants independently citing a plant for a specific use to those citing any use, multiplied by 100, i.e. a percentage. However, it reports values including 3.78 for *Musa paradisiaca*, 3.25 for *Zingiber officinale* and 2.4 for *Artocarpus heterophyllus*. These values are not interpretable as percentages under the stated formula without clarification of scaling, table notation or a reporting error. Record the discrepancy as a source-quality flag; do not reuse these FL values quantitatively until the underlying table/calculation is checked. The reported CKSI values (e.g. 0.68) are a separate index and should not be conflated with FL.

## Use in the Assam archive

1. Add this as a comparative ethnobotanical source, not as a product-specific recipe authority.
2. Use Kokrajhar/Baksa as district-level study scope for the Bodo arm, not as proof of every product's presence in every village.
3. Keep starter-culture names source-attributed; do not automatically merge Amao, angkur and Bakhar.
4. Only map individual plant taxa into the Bodo beverage dossier after extracting table rows with their stated community, plant part, role and beverage attribution.
5. Keep ASM-GAP-026 open: the study is not the GI Statement of Case, amended map or official producer list.

## Limitations

The sample is small and purposively/snowball selected; geographic detail is at district and study-site level in the accessible text. Its pooled plant inventory, broad process description and internally unclear FL scale cannot replace the detailed Bodo-specific study or primary GI documents.
