- **GI status:** Official registry lists Judima as registered; verify current record before publication.

## Current evidence-backed summary
Judima is a traditional rice-fermented beverage associated with the Dimasa community. Research describes rice varieties including Bairing and Bora and a traditional starter known as Humao/Umhu. Some accounts report Acacia pennata (Thembra) in starter preparation. Published sources describe household variation, fresh and preserved forms, and cultural uses in festivals and some life-cycle contexts.

## Locality leads
- **Dibarai, Haflong:** appears in the GI applicant address; do not treat an applicant address alone as proof of household production.
- **Dhansiri:** locality in a published field study.
- **Tereh:** locality in an ethnographic account describing a household brewer.

These leads do not establish that Judima is confined to these places or made in every village of the district.

## Production notes
Published descriptions discuss cooking rice, cooling it, mixing it with the starter, fermentation in covered vessels, and collecting liquid from the fermented material. One source reports a fermentation duration of roughly five to seven days, but this must not be presented as a universal recipe or safety validation.

## Cultural notes
Sources report Judima in festivals, hospitality and some marriage, birth and mourning-related contexts. These are source-specific accounts and should not be generalised to every Dimasa household or locality.

## Open questions

2. Which Humao/Umhu ingredients and steps vary by locality?
3. What is the community-verified etymology of Judima?
4. How has GI recognition affected household brewers and commercialisation?
5. Which practices remain current, and which are historical or changing?
