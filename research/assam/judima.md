# Judima — Assam research dossier

- **Archive ID:** ASM-001-JUD
- **State:** Assam
- **Primary district:** Dima Hasao
- **Community:** Dimasa
- **Type:** Traditional fermented rice beverage
- **Starter:** Humao / Umhu
- **Evidence status:** Core identity documented; geographic coverage incomplete
- **GI status:** Official registry lists Judima as registered; verify current record before publication.

## Current evidence-backed summary
Judima is a traditional rice-fermented beverage associated with the Dimasa community. Research describes rice varieties including Bairing and Bora and a traditional starter known as Humao/Umhu. Some accounts report Acacia pennata (Thembra) in starter preparation. Published sources describe household variation, fresh and preserved forms, and cultural uses in festivals and some life-cycle contexts.

## Locality leads
- **Dibarai, Haflong:** appears in the GI applicant address; do not treat an applicant address alone as proof of household production.
- **Dhansiri:** locality in a published field study.
- **Tereh:** locality in an ethnographic account describing a household brewer.

These leads do not establish that Judima is confined to these places or made in every village of the district.

## Production notes
Published descriptions discuss cooking rice, cooling it, mixing it with the starter, fermentation in covered vessels, and collecting liquid from the fermented material. One source reports a fermentation duration of roughly five to seven days, but this must not be presented as a universal recipe or safety validation.

## Cultural notes
Sources report Judima in festivals, hospitality and some marriage, birth and mourning-related contexts. These are source-specific accounts and should not be generalised to every Dimasa household or locality.

## Open questions
1. What is the verified village/block distribution?
2. Which Humao/Umhu ingredients and steps vary by locality?
3. What is the community-verified etymology of Judima?
4. How has GI recognition affected household brewers and commercialisation?
5. Which practices remain current, and which are historical or changing?

## Editorial rule
Treat this as a living research dossier. Preserve uncertainty, add source IDs to claims, and distinguish traditional knowledge from modern commercial production.


## Additional historical evidence — North Cachar Hills studies

Two additional sources now broaden the historical evidence base for the Dima Hasao / North Cachar Hills tradition without expanding the locality map:

- **SRC-097 — Chakrabarty, Sharma & Tamang (2009):** the indexed full-text abstract lists Judima, Zunak and Dekujao among rice-based alcoholic beverages familiar in the North Cachar Hills district. Preserve these spellings as the authors printed them; the abstract alone does not establish whether the names are synonyms, subtypes, or distinct beverages, and it does not map each to a named village.
- **SRC-098 — Chakrabarty, Sharma & Tamang (2014):** the full-text article identifies Judima as the selected fermented beverage and Humao as a dry mixed amylolytic starter in a study of traditional knowledge, production, consumption, microbial profiles and nutritional value. The authors report observation and interviews with producers and consumers. The study is selective, not a district-wide census.

These sources support a **historical district-level literature association**, not a new village-level production claim or proof of current prevalence. Do not merge Zunak or Dekujao into Judima without a dedicated name-boundary investigation. The 2014 article's nutritional and microbial analyses should not be presented as clinical health evidence.

## SRC-098 study-design clarification (2026-10-23)

The full-text abstract of Chakrabarty, Sharma and Tamang (2014) specifies a **selected-product characterization study**, not a district-wide survey: the authors selected five product categories—wet Tuaithur, dry Tuairoi, Honoheingrain, the dry mixed amylolytic starter Humao, and the beverage Judima—and report 21 samples across those five categories. The study combines producer/consumer interviews and observation with microbial and nutritional analysis. Its district-level framing supports historical association with North Cachar Hills (now Dima Hasao), but the accessible abstract does not supply village-by-beverage counts or establish current prevalence. The 21 samples must not be misreported as 21 Judima samples. [Source: SRC-098; full text](https://www.jnu.ac.in/Faculty/scgarkoti/tks/pdf/7.TFB_Traditional-Food-_-Beverage.pdf).
