# SRC-351 — Jamal, Upadhyay & Bajaj (2022): Dibarai household-survey scope and Judima locality classification

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026

## Citation

Shahid Jamal, Aakash Upadhyay and Amrita Bajaj (2022), “Judima the Traditional Rice Wine and Dimasa Community: A Geographical Review of Dima Hasao,” *Indi@logs* 9, pp. 77–97. DOI: 10.5565/rev/indialogs.205.  
Accessible full text: https://www.academia.edu/113154469/Judima_the_Traditional_Rice_Wine_and_Dimasa_Community_a_Geographical_Review_of_Dima_Hasao  
Journal landing page: https://revistes.uab.cat/indialogs/article/view/v9-jamal-upadhyay-bajaj

## Fieldwork extraction

- Abstract states a household survey using snowball sampling in Dima Hasao district in 2019.
- Methods section (printed p.79 / PDF page corresponding to journal page 79) explicitly says the primary survey was conducted in **Dibarai village** in November 2019 and approximately **50 respondents** were surveyed.
- Respondents were aged 20–80 and described as involved in the daily practice of collecting raw materials from nearby forest to earn their livelihood.
- Interviews were short informal interactions, approximately 10–20 minutes.
- The article does not identify respondent names, household IDs, number of brewing households, number of producers, gender breakdown, or a village list beyond Dibarai.
- The fieldwork's direct subject was community perceptions/cultural association with Judima and GI-related identity, not a producer census or a structured production-process survey.

## Locality classification

**Dibarai is a confirmed field-survey locality for a Judima-focused household/community study.** It is not yet a verified producer-household locality based on this paper alone: the 50 respondents are said to collect forest raw materials, but the accessible methods do not state that all or any specified number brewed Judima themselves. The article discusses Dibarai Mahila Samiti and YADEM as festival organisers, but this does not prove that every organisation member is a producer.

Use the following archive edges:
- Dibarai → Judima-focused household/community survey (2019; approx. 50 respondents): **supported**.
- Dibarai → forest raw-material collection linked to respondents' livelihoods: **supported, source-attributed**.
- Dibarai → named Judima producer or identified brewing household: **not established by this paper**.
- Dibarai → GI applicant/organisation address and festival activity: supported by separate official/journalistic sources, but keep as different relation types.
- Dibarai → village-wide Judima production prevalence: **not established**.

## Why this matters

Dibarai is a second important locality in the Judima documentary record, but it must not be treated as a duplicate of Tereh's direct household-production evidence. The Locavore's 2023 report explicitly documents Khimkho Hojai brewing Judima for her household in Tereh village. Jamal et al. documents a Dibarai household survey, not the same evidentiary claim.

The paper's limitations section says primary data collection was challenging and male participants were preferably interviewed because female participants were not comfortable in the interviewer's presence. This should be represented as a limitation reported by the authors, not as a statement about women's willingness to participate in all research settings.

## Source quality notes

1. Peer-reviewed journal article with accessible full text.
2. The article predates Judima's GI registration and therefore describes GI status as pending; do not use it for current GI status.
3. The article includes broad nutritional/health assertions and alcohol pricing that are not independently validated by this fieldwork; do not import these as product standards or health evidence.
4. The preparation flowchart is explicitly adapted from Arjun et al. and is not a separately observed household protocol from the Dibarai survey.
5. The paper contains demographic and district-level statistics that should not be confused with its Judima respondent sample.

## Archive decision

Register as SRC-351. Add Dibarai as a field-survey locality and organisational/GI context node where relevant, but do not add a producer or production-household edge from this source. Keep Tereh as the current direct named household-production locality evidence from SRC-003. Further producer evidence for Dibarai requires the original GI producer list, named producer testimony, field observation or another source explicitly linking a household/producer in Dibarai to brewing Judima.
