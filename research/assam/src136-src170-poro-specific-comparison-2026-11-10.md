# Direct comparison of two Poro-specific Mising Epop/Apob studies

**Date:** 10 November 2026  
**Sources:** SRC-136 (Kardong et al. 2012) and SRC-170 (Pawe & Gogoi 2013).  
**Purpose:** compare two primary studies that explicitly concern Po:ro/Poro Apong and provide starter-plant lists. This is not a universal recipe or a pooled ethnobotanical census.

## 1. Source design and evidence scope

### SRC-136 — Kardong et al. (2012)

- *Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge* 11(3):499–504; full text is available through ResearchGate and bibliographic metadata is corroborated by the NIScPR Online Periodical Repository.
- The authors collected finished Po:ro Apong from local Mising households in Dibrugarh district for biochemical analysis. Three beverage samples are described as having broadly similar taste and texture; results are reported as mean of three samples/observations.
- The paper says at least 16 herbs are generally used and that the number may vary from place to place within the Mising community.
- Important method distinction: the names of starter plants were listed by interacting with a “villager expert” and through literature survey. The paper does not say that all 16 plants were individually collected and authenticated from the same households that supplied the three finished beverage samples.
- No village names, informant count, plant vouchers, herbarium accessions or plant-by-household matrix are supplied.

### SRC-170 — Pawe & Gogoi (2013)

- *NeBIO* 4(2):46–49; full article text is readable on the ResearchGate page.
- The authors report work in Mising-dominated localities in Lakhimpur and Sonitpur districts and name seven selected villages: Dhunaguri, Badati, Bongalmora, Chamaguri Temona, Luitmukh, Kathalkuchi and Rajbari.
- They report interviews with villagers who regularly prepare Poro, observation of the preparation method, plant collection and identification confirmation at BSI Shillong (ASSAM) and Itanagar (ARUN).
- Table 1 is explicitly titled as plants used for Poro Apong preparation. It contains 27 visible numbered serials (1–25, 27, 28), with two Thelypteris names in serial 25. This yields 28 taxon strings; serial 26 is absent.
- The paper does not provide field dates, informant count, voucher accession numbers or a plant-by-village matrix.

## 2. Inventory comparison

- SRC-136: 16 listed starter taxa/records.
- SRC-170: 28 taxon strings from 27 visible table serials.
- Exact lexical overlap after removing parenthetical author citations and punctuation and comparing the first two scientific-name tokens: **2** — *Piper nigrum* and *Piper longum*.
- The direct crosswalk is stored at `src136-src170-poro-specific-epop-crosswalk-2026-11-10.csv`. It preserves the source strings and separately marks likely spelling variants, genus-only entries and distinct taxa.

### Important apparent similarities that are not exact matches

1. **Scoparia dulcis / Scoperia dulcis:** likely a spelling variant, but not an exact match. Verify source image and accepted-name authority; no automatic merge.
2. **Centala acciatica / Centella asiatica:** likely a typographic/transcription error in SRC-136. It may represent the same intended name as SRC-170, but specimen identity is not demonstrated.
3. **Hydrocotyl rotundifolia / Hydrocotile javanica:** different epithets; do not merge.
4. **Lygodium japonicum / Lygodium flexuosum:** distinct species; do not merge.
5. **Selaginella sp. / Selaginella semicordata:** genus-level identification cannot be promoted to the species-level record.
6. **Costus speciosus / Coctus speciosa:** likely spelling error in SRC-170, but preserve the published string until source-image and authority review.
7. **Zanthoxylum hemiltonianum / Zenthoxylum nitidum:** different epithets; the likely misspelling in the former does not make it equivalent to the latter.
8. **Pteridium sp. / Thelypteris parasitica and T. dentata:** do not equate these fern genera. SRC-136 identifies only a genus-level Pteridium record.

The crosswalk is deliberately conservative. It does not attempt to collapse every probable typo or nomenclatural synonym into one canonical plant identity.

## 3. Process comparison: both describe filtration, not distillation

| Feature | SRC-136 (2012) | SRC-170 (2013) |
|---|---|---|
| Rice/starter description | About 100 g powdered Epop per 3 kg cooked rice | 4–5 crushed Apob cakes added to the ash/rice mixture; Apob cake recipe starts with 2–4 kg rice |
| Ash | Rice-straw and rice-husk ash mixed with warm cooked rice | Rice-straw and rice-husk ash produced by firing straw/husk with partly cooked rice; ash mixed with cooked rice |
| Fermentation | About 20 days at 30–35°C | 7–12 days depending on season, sometimes more than a month |
| Vessel / closure | Clean, dry container, preferably earthen pot, plugged with dry straw or fresh Pteridium fern leaves | Fermented rice packed into bamboo basket, covered with banana leaves; article describes the storage container in its process account |
| Extraction | Warm water poured over fermented mash in bamboo Ta:suk wrapped internally with banana or Canna leaves | Water poured through banana-leaf/straw-lined V-shaped bamboo Tasuk; re-filtering until liquid changes from black to brown |
| By-product | Remaining mash used as pig/cattle fodder | Filtered residue is separated from Poro liquid; article gives its own local terminology |

**Interpretation:** both studies describe a fermented mash followed by water-assisted Ta:suk filtration. Neither of these two primary process descriptions establishes distillation as the Poro finishing step. Their reported fermentation durations differ substantially and must remain source-specific; the papers do not supply a harmonized experimental design that explains the difference.

## 4. SRC-136 biochemical evidence — keep the sample and assay limits visible

The 2012 article reports analysis of three finished Poro samples, with mean values/standard deviations where applicable:

- pH: 4.06 ± 0.1
- titratable acidity: 0.53% as lactic acid
- total carbohydrate: 46.62 ± 4.3 mg/ml
- reducing sugar: 3.33 ± 0.2 mg/ml
- total protein: 1.05 ± 0.06 mg/ml
- free amino acids: 2.43 ± 0.12 mg/ml
- ethanol: 7.52–18.5% v/v
- amylase: 2.38 ± 0.06 U ml⁻¹ min⁻¹; protease and lipase not detected under the study's methods
- microbial load: Epop 549 × 10⁷ cfu/g; ripe mash 414 × 10⁷ cfu/g; finished product 8,700 ± 6.332 cfu/mm³.

The microbial counts use different units and methods for starter/mash versus finished liquid; they are not a directly comparable time series. The three finished samples are a small, historical sample set, not a statewide product specification. The authors' ethnomedicinal interpretations are reported beliefs, not clinical evidence. The article itself raises concerns about possible microbial contamination and calls for characterization; do not use the pH or counts as food-safety certification.

## 5. Archive decision and next evidence needed

- Keep both sources and inventories separate. SRC-136's 16-plant table is an informant-plus-literature list; SRC-170's 28-taxon table is a Poro-specific list supported by reported field collection and herbarium confirmation.
- Do not average their plant counts, infer prevalence or assume that every plant in either table was used by every surveyed household.
- Do not create plant-village edges: neither paper supplies a plant-by-village matrix.
- Prioritize obtaining SRC-170 voucher accessions and the missing field metadata; for SRC-136, seek the underlying informant/literature provenance for each of the 16 plant names and any original voucher sheets.
- Retain process duration and ingredient proportions as source-specific, not universal.
- No canonical plant, village or beverage records were created or merged in this comparison.
