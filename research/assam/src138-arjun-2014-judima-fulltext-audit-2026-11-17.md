# SRC-138 — Arjun et al. (2014) Judima preparation and biochemical study: full-text extraction audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-17  
**Source:** Arjun, J.; Verma, A. K.; Prasad, S. B. (2014), “Method of preparation and biochemical analysis of local tribal wine Judima: an indigenous alcohol used by Dimasa tribe of North Cachar Hills District of Assam, India,” *International Food Research Journal* 21(2):463–470.  
**Scope:** Extract the accessible full-text copy, distinguish field observations from laboratory sample data, and identify internal inconsistencies. This is a primary study text reproduced on Academia.edu; Cotton University’s official faculty bibliography independently lists the same citation and links the journal-hosted PDF. The publisher PDF timed out in this retrieval session, so the Academia reproduction is used for text extraction, not claimed as the publisher-hosted original.

## 1. Field survey and sample design

- The paper says the field survey used semi-structured questionnaires, personal visits to homes, workplaces and wine-selling markets, and local-language interviews about Judima preparation and preservation.
- It reports **100 participants: 79 women and 21 men**, aged **30–60 years**. It states that participants were informed about the project and signed prior consent forms.
- It says the authors visited rural areas where Judima was prepared and observed/documented the process.
- **No village, revenue village, town, household count, producer count, sample collection date, batch ID, number of Judima beverage samples, or mapping of interviewees to samples is disclosed in the accessible paper text.** Do not create village/place rows or infer that all 100 interviewees were producers.
- The paper does not explain whether the 100 interview participants and the laboratory beverage sample(s) are the same people/households or how the beverage was selected.

## 2. Preparation account — retain the paper's internal timing difference

The paper describes the Judima starter as **umhu/humao**.

- Rice cake: glutinous rice (*Oryza glutinosa* Lour.) is soaked, ground with pieces of thempra/thembra bark, identified as *Acacia pennata*, and mixed with water into a paste.
- New cakes are reported as 5–7 cm in radius and 80–100 g. Powder from 15–20 g of an old cake is sprinkled over a new cake. The new cake is kept for at least 24 hours and sun-dried until hard; the paper says it can then be stored for months.
- The text mentions leaves/twigs of *Piper betle*, *Buddleja asiatica* and *Hedyotis scandens* as flavour/variation ingredients, alongside *Acacia pennata* bark in the starter account. The paper's plant collection section later lists leaves of all four species; its results discuss leaves/twigs/bark usage. It does **not** map each plant part to a named village, household or individual batch.
- Beverage preparation: boiled rice is cooled, mixed with powdered humao; one large cake is said to be sufficient for 5 kg rice. The mass is placed in a large container covered with jute gunny bags. The method section says fermentation takes **5–7 days**, after which yellowish juice emerges and can be diluted with water and filtered.
- **Timing discrepancy:** the abstract says Judima fermentation usually takes about **4–5 days** at room temperature in earthen/aluminium pots, while the detailed preparation method says **5–7 days** in a large container covered with jute bags. These are not reconciled. Preserve both as separate statements; do not standardize to one duration or merge the vessel descriptions into a single universal recipe.

## 3. Plant collection and vouchers

The paper lists four plant species collected from “different locations” in North Cachar Hills district:

1. *Piper betle* — leaves
2. *Buddleja asiatica* — leaves
3. *Hedyotis scandens* — leaves
4. *Acacia pennata* — leaves (the preparation narrative separately mentions its bark)

The paper states that the plant materials were identified/authenticated by Dr J. Arjun of Lumding College and that voucher specimens **JA-1, JA-2, JA-3 and JA-4** were deposited in the Department of Zoology, Lumding College. It does not supply a voucher-to-species crosswalk in the accessible text, herbarium accession numbers beyond those labels, collection coordinates, collection dates, collector-specific field numbers, or plant-by-locality distribution. Do not infer the JA-number-to-species order from the order of the list unless the original voucher register is recovered.

## 4. Analytical methods actually described

- Ethanol: decanted Judima centrifuged at 5,000 rpm for 25 minutes at 4°C; supernatant used. Chromic-acid colorimetric method, with a 1% ethanol standard curve; absorbance read at 600 nm.
- Turbidity: absorbance/optical density at 640 nm against water.
- Total carbohydrate: Anthrone method, glucose standard 1.0 mg/ml, absorbance at 550 nm.
- Total protein: Folin–phenol/Lowry method.
- Free amino acids: method attributed to Lee et al. (2003).
- Antioxidant activity: DPPH radical scavenging assay, Judima concentrations 30, 60, 90, 120 and 150 μl; absorbance at 517 nm.
- Plant extracts: 50 g plant material dried at 38–40°C, extracted by maceration in 2 L 70% methanol for seven days with daily agitation; TLC uses silica gel F254, chloroform:methanol 8:2, iodine visualization.
- PBMC cytotoxicity: paper states PBMCs were collected from mice, 1×10^6 cells/ml seeded in 24-well plates, treated with different Judima concentrations for 12 hours, then MTT assay. Ethanol 50% and 100% were comparators. This is an in-vitro mouse-cell assay, not a human clinical safety study.
- The methods section does **not** provide a clear protocol for the microbial-load counts that appear in Table 2. It also does not provide a clear beverage batch count, independent batch sampling plan, or detailed beverage-sample provenance.

## 5. Reported Judima values — source-specific, not a product specification

Table 2 reports:

| Parameter | Reported value |
|---|---:|
| pH | 5.1 |
| Total carbohydrate | 32.43 ± 2.7 mg/ml |
| Total protein | 0.97 ± 0.18 mg/ml |
| Free amino acids | 3.21 ± 0.21 mg/ml |
| Ethanol | 16 ± 2% (v/v) |
| Total solids | 0.007 mg/ml (as printed) |
| Optical density against water | 0.003 |

The paper also reports microbial-load values in the same table: starter culture 632 × 10^7 cfu/g; first day 732 × 10^7 cfu/g; third day 545 × 10^7 cfu/g; fifth day 422 × 10^7 cfu/g; ripe/after filtration 278 × 10^7 cfu/g. **The microbial assay method, organism identities, culture media, replicate design and sample-to-stage linkage are not sufficiently described in the methods text.** Preserve these as table-reported counts with a major methods caveat, not as verified microbial ecology.

The article says determinations were repeated three times and values are mean ± SD; it also describes 100 field interviewees. It is unclear whether n=3 means technical replicates, independent beverage batches, or three experimental groups. The table note says data are from “three different experimental groups,” but the sampling design is not described. Do not treat n=3 as three independent household batches.

## 6. Interpretation and evidence limits

- The paper reports a single Judima biochemical profile, but does not identify the village or household source of the beverage sample. The 100-person survey cannot be treated as the sample count for the laboratory results.
- The reported 16 ± 2% v/v ethanol is a result of this paper's assay and sample; it is not a universal Judima strength, GI specification, or current market value.
- The paper's statements that Judima has antioxidant activity and negligible short-term cytotoxicity are limited to its DPPH and mouse-PBMC in-vitro experiments. They do not establish human health benefits, safety, medicinal efficacy, or that drinking Judima prevents or treats disease. The article's broad health language should not be repeated as a verified health claim.
- The four plants and voucher labels are useful evidence of a documented plant-collection effort, but the study does not disclose a village-level inventory or link each taxon to each sampled starter/batch.
- No direct comparability is assumed with SRC-001's Dhansiri household study, SRC-002's Dibarai study, SRC-003's Tereh household account, or SRC-148's Mising Apong work. Their communities, sites, sampling units and recipes differ.

## 7. Archive decisions

- **SRC-138 full-text extraction is substantially completed** from the accessible full-text reproduction; publisher-hosted PDF retrieval remains a separate archival task.
- **ASM-GAP-061 is partially resolved but should remain Open.** The article's preparation and biochemical tables are extracted, but exact locality, beverage sample/batch count, collection dates, sample-to-household linkage, and microbial methods remain missing.
- Update SRC-138's evidence summary and caveats; do not create new beverage-production localities or plant occurrence rows.

## 8. Author and institution acquisition route (2026-11-19)

### Author identity and publication-year discrepancy

A publicly accessible professional CV identifies the paper's first author as **Dr Jashodeb Arjun**. It records him as Associate Professor, Department of Zoology, Lumding College from September 1986 to August 2021, then Associate Professor at Silapathar Science College from September 2021 to March 2023, and Professor, Department of Zoology, Royal Global University from April 2023 onward. The CV lists a public professional email: **jashodeb@gmail.com**. It also lists the Judima paper but gives the year as **2012**, whereas the Cotton University faculty bibliography, NEHU Annual Report 2013–14 and journal citation give **2014** (IFRJ 21(2):463–470). This is a bibliographic discrepancy, not evidence of a separate 2012 paper; retain 2014 as the journal citation and record the CV's 2012 entry as unresolved until the author or issue masthead confirms it.

The Lumding College NIRF 2022 submission independently lists Jashodeb Arjun among its Zoology faculty, with joining date 02-09-1987 and current-institution status for the reporting period. His CV's 1986 start date differs by about one year; this employment-date discrepancy is not material to the specimen evidence, but is preserved rather than reconciled by inference.

### Lumding College collection and library route

The college's current official website lists a Central Library with OPAC and reprographic services, four current journal subscriptions, 30 bound volumes of periodicals, and a named librarian (Ms Pritishmita Thakuria). It also publishes the general institutional contact lumdingcollege@yahoo.in and helpline 03674-263375. A separate Digital Library landing page links NDLI, N-LIST, e-PG Pathshala and Shodhganga, but currently displays zero publication records. These facilities establish an appropriate institutional enquiry route; they do **not** prove that the JA-1–JA-4 specimens, old laboratory books, or the 2014 journal issue remain in the college's custody.

The current Zoology Department page lists its present faculty but not Jashodeb Arjun, consistent with his CV's departure in 2021. It does not list a public departmental herbarium or voucher catalogue. Therefore address the first request to Dr Arjun as the paper's author and separately copy the college Principal / Zoology HOD / Librarian for a search of departmental and library records. No email or phone call has been made in this research pass.
