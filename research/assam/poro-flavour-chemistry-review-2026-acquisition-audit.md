## 2. What the accessible abstract actually establishes

The abstract says the review brings together literature concerning:
1. phytochemicals from plants used in OPOB/Epop starter culture;
2. fermentative microbial communities; and
3. ash derived from rice straw/husk.

It highlights Maillard reactions and ash-mediated flavour chemistry and discusses secondary metabolites that **may** contribute to flavour and sensory properties. It frames gaps in mechanism and reproducibility and proposes directions for standardisation.

These are the review's stated synthesis aims and mechanistic framing. The abstract does **not** report a new PO:RO batch experiment, a volatile-compound profile, GC-MS/GC-IMS data, sensory-panel results, controlled ash comparison, or causal test of Maillard chemistry. The phrase “data generated during this study” in the publisher metadata cannot be read as proof of an experimental dataset: the item is explicitly classified as a review, and the manuscript/supplementary files are unavailable for inspection.

## 4. Important distinctions for future extraction

When the full review becomes available, every chemistry statement should be tagged as one of:
- **Direct PO:RO measurement:** compound or property measured in a defined PO:RO sample/batch, with analytical method and sample provenance.
- **Starter-material measurement:** compound measured in an individual plant, plant powder or Epop/OPOB cake; not automatically present in finished beverage.
- **Microbial capability evidence:** metabolite production by an isolate or laboratory culture; not automatically produced in traditional PO:RO.
- **Analogy/extrapolation:** evidence from sake, rice wine, beer, other fermented foods, model systems or generic Maillard literature.
- **Hypothesis/proposed mechanism:** plausible explanation not directly tested in PO:RO.
- **Sensory report:** descriptive or panel observation, with panel design and sample identity stated.

For each claim, capture the review page, exact cited reference number, primary source DOI, sample type, locality, analytical method, measured endpoint and whether the primary paper actually supports the wording.
