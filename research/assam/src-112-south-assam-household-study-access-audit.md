# SRC-112 — South Assam household study: access and evidence audit

## Citation
Yumnam, H., Nath, S., & Sharma, I. (2026). “Brewing Traditions of Northeast India, South Assam: Exploring Rice Beer's Cultural Significance and Indigenous Craftsmanship.” *Biotechnology and Applied Biochemistry*, 73(3), 1501–1511. First published online 29 December 2025. https://doi.org/10.1002/bab.70113

## Why this source matters
This is a recent ethnographic study directly focused on rice-beer practices among five southern Assam communities: Dimasa Kachari, Hmar, Zeme Naga, Karbi and Tea tribes. The abstract reports fieldwork from May–October 2023, 49 households across Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi, and interviews with older participants aged 50–70 years, with informed consent.

## What can be verified from accessible publisher metadata
- Five community cohorts are named: Dimasa Kachari, Hmar, Zeme Naga, Karbi and Tea tribes.
- The abstract identifies the study geography as Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi (the district names as stated in the article).
- It reports that preparation methods and starter-culture variation were documented.
- Plant species named in the abstract are *Acacia pennata*, *Glycyrrhiza glabra* and *Croton joufra*; “Ndhui gei” is reported as unidentified.
- The paper frames beverages as having social, ritual and medicinal roles. These are reported cultural practices/beliefs, not clinical proof of therapeutic benefit.
- The publisher page says underlying information is available from the author on a justifiable request.

## Access and evidence limitations
The publisher page accessible in this audit exposed the abstract and references but not the full article's methods, tables, figures or sample-level details. It therefore does **not** support a verified allocation of the 49 households across communities/districts, exact villages, per-community beverage spellings, plant-to-starter mappings, or individual recipes. Do not use the abstract's general statements to assign the three named plant species to any particular beverage or community.

The article cites Atungbou (2020) for Zeme Zou and cites earlier work on other community beverages. It is a promising lead for resolving the Zeme terminology crosswalk, but the accessible material does not independently establish whether Zao/Zou, Nduijao and Nduizou are equivalent names or whether Ndhuihi, Nduiyi and nduihi denote the same starter.

## Atlas handling decision
- Register as a new source because the 49-household fieldwork is distinct from Yumnam et al. (2024)'s 15-sample microbiome study.
- Do not add a new canonical beverage record or village-level place row based on the abstract alone.
- Keep the 2024 microbiome study separate: it used three samples per community and bacterial 16S rRNA sequencing; this 2025/2026 ethnographic article reports interviews and household fieldwork.
- Request full text and/or author-provided sample tables to crosswalk each beverage, starter plant, locality and community. Respect consent and community authority when seeking confirmation.
