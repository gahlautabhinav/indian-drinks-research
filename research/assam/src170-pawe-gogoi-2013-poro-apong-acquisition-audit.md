# SRC-170 — Pawe & Gogoi (2013), Po:ro Apong / Chai Mod acquisition audit

## Bibliographic identity

Dimbo Pawe and Rajib Gogoi (2013), “Ethnobotany of Poro Apong or Chai Mod — A local rice beer of Mishing tribes of Assam,” *NeBIO: An International Journal of Environment and Biodiversity* 4(2):46–49.

- ResearchGate publication record: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/publication/270577488_Ethnobotany_of_Poro_Apong_or_Chai_Mod-A_local_rice_beer_of_Mishing_tribes_of_Assam
- Independent bibliography corroboration: Botanical Survey of India / Rajib Gogoi bibliography and BSI annual report list the same author pair, title, journal, volume, issue and pages.
- ResearchGate preview labels the article “Full-text available” and gives January 2013 metadata. The linked “View” endpoint returned HTTP 403 in this retrieval pass.

## What the accessible preview supports

The ResearchGate abstract/preview states:

1. The Mising community prepares two different kinds of rice beer: **Nogin Apong** and **Poro Apong**. It describes Poro as different from the common/popular Nogin preparation.
2. The Poro preparation combines cooked rice and other ingredients with ash made from rice husk and rice straw; the preview says the ash contributes to the brown colour.
3. The article's stated subject is ethnobotanical observations on Poro Apong.
4. The preview says the paper provides plant species used in preparing **Apob** (starter/“natural yeast cake”) for Poro, with botanical names, families, vernacular names and plant parts.

This is useful evidence for a source-specific distinction between Poro and Nogin and for the existence of a Poro-focused Apob plant table. It is not a substitute for the full table or methods.

## Retrieval and source-integrity findings

- The linked ResearchGate “View” endpoint failed with **403 Forbidden**. No article pages, plant rows, locality or field methods were recovered.
- The historical journal domain `nebio.in` currently redirects to an unrelated gambling/SEO page. Treat the current domain as compromised or repurposed; do not rely on it for article downloads or journal authenticity.
- The BSI bibliography and a later peer-reviewed article's reference list corroborate the bibliographic record, but neither supplies the missing primary table.
- The accessible preview does not expose field dates, district/village names, informant count, sampling design, plant voucher identifiers or exact process sequence.
- The preview repeats a medicinal/mineral rationale for the ash. This is a source-reported traditional/author claim, not a validated clinical, nutritional or safety conclusion.

## Archive decision

- Register as **SRC-170**, clearly labelled as a primary ethnobotanical article with only bibliographic and abstract-preview evidence recovered.
- Add it to the Po:ro Apong source list as a variant-focused source lead. Do not add it as an independent plant inventory yet.
- Do not create locality records or assign any plant taxa. No plant names were exposed in the accessible preview.
- Preserve the distinction between the 2013 Poro-focused paper and general Apong/E’pob inventories. Do not assume its table is interchangeable with the 2023 Sivasagar ÉPOP table or the 2026 Majuli E’pob inventory.
- Keep ASM-GAP-060 open: the complete Apob table and exact locality remain unresolved.
