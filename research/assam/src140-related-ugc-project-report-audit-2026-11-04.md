# SRC-140 related project report audit — 2026-11-04

## Document retrieved

D. Kardong, *Isolation and Biochemical Characterization of Potential Amylolytic and Alcohol Producing Yeast Strains from Yeast Cake (Epope) Used in the Preparation of Po:ro (Sai-mod), a Traditional Alcoholic Rice Beverage of Mising Tribe of Assam, India*. Dibrugarh University, Department of Life Sciences. UGC Minor Research Project F. No. 32-617/2006(SR), final report.

Public institutional PDF: https://www.dibru.ac.in/pdf/Devid-Kardong-Report.pdf

## What the accessible report establishes

- The project period is 1 April 2007 to 31 March 2009.
- The report describes Saimod (Po:ro) as a Mising homemade rice beverage and Epop/Epope as the plant-based starter culture used to ferment cooked rice.
- Three starter-culture samples from different places were collected.
- The summary reports seven pure bacterial/fungal isolates with amylolytic properties and six isolates with alcohol-fermenting properties.
- Candidate isolates named for a possible consortium are AY-3 (amy-3), AY-4 (Big cell) and FY-10.
- The report characterises the beverage fermentation as solid-state fermentation.
- The administrative report states that paper writing was still underway / results were being prepared for publication at the time of submission.

## Critical distinction from SRC-140

The Dibrugarh University publication bibliography separately lists a 2011 article titled “Amyolytic activity of mold isolated from Epop used by the Mising tribe of Assam, India in preparation of alcoholic Rice beer” in *Bulletin of Life Sciences*, Vol. XVI. The retrieved UGC project report is related antecedent research, not the journal article itself. It cannot be treated as the full text or as proof that every result and method in the report appeared in that 2011 paper.

## What remains unknown

The accessible report does not provide enough detail to verify the three sample localities, isolate species identities, complete assay protocols, isolate-level numerical results or a complete community-level Epop recipe. Do not infer these from the project title or short summary. The 2011 article's page range and full text remain outstanding.

## Research implication

This is a useful new primary institutional record for the history and laboratory investigation of Epop/Po:ro fermentation. It strengthens the evidence base for the existence of a 2007–2009 Dibrugarh University project and its isolate-screening results, while leaving ASM-GAP-062 open because the two 2011 journal articles have not been retrieved in full.
