# SRC-337 — Terangpi et al. (2013): Acacia pennata bark and Dimasa Judimah/Juharao context

**Audit date:** 22 November 2026  
**Source:** Reena Terangpi, Ratan Basumatary, A. K. Tamuli and R. Teron (2013), “Pharmacognostic and Physicochemical evaluation of stem bark of *Acacia pennata* (L.) Willd., a folk plant of the Dimasa tribe of Assam,” *Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry* 2(2), printed pp. 134 onward.  
**Full text:** https://www.phytojournal.com/vol2Issue2/Issue_july_2013/18.1.pdf

## 1. Beverage-relevant findings

The paper's introduction identifies *Acacia pennata* stem bark as a culturally important Dimasa material used “since time immemorial” as a substrate for preparing the rice starter cake Humao. It describes dried bark of “Themra” (*Acacia pennata*) pounded and mixed with pre-soaked ground rice, moistened and shaped into round flat cakes, sun-dried for 2–5 days, then used to ferment cooked rice into a beverage printed as “Judimah.” It adds that a spirit liquor called “Juharao” is sometimes produced by distillation of Judimah using a crude still.

This is useful independent corroboration of the *Acacia pennata* / Themra / Humao / Judimah association and of a distinct distilled product name. It does not resolve the separate *Albizia myriophylla* / “themra” report in SRC-335 or the precise Juharo still charge.

## 2. Botanical specimen provenance — precise boundary

The article distinguishes two plant-material streams:
- Stem bark for pharmacognostic analysis was collected from a local market in January 2013.
- The plant with flowers was collected from Dhansiri Reserved Forest, Assam; the authors state its botanical identity was confirmed by the first and fourth authors and that the specimen was compared with a voucher specimen deposited in the Department of Life Science and Bioinformatics, Assam University, Diphu Campus.

The article does not give a voucher number, accession, collector number, label locality or image. The statement about a comparator voucher supports the authors' botanical identification procedure; it does not establish that the market bark used in laboratory testing came from the same individual plant, nor that a voucher of the actual Humao starter material exists. Do not claim a deposited voucher for a beverage batch or a village-level starter occurrence.

## 3. Process and study limitations

- The article is primarily a pharmacognostic and physicochemical study of bark, not a new beverage-production field survey.
- It supplies no named Dimasa producer, Judimah batch, household count, production village, sample collection date for the beverage or recipe prevalence.
- “Juharao” is described as distillation of Judimah with a crude still; the feedstock state is not specified. Do not infer whether the collected liquid, fermented solids or both are charged to the still.
- The authors state that further work on grain character as microbial substrate and evaluation of starter-cake microflora is necessary to assess toxicity and quality. The paper therefore does not establish microbial safety or finished-beverage safety.
- The article's bark physicochemical measurements are not alcohol content, beverage chemistry or evidence of therapeutic efficacy.

## 4. Cross-source relevance

- SRC-001, SRC-002 and SRC-003 describe *Acacia pennata* / Thembra-Thempra in Humao/Judima accounts, each with its own locality and design limits.
- SRC-335 reports *Albizia myriophylla* / “themra” in local liquors including Judima and Juharo, but its taxon-to-voucher mapping remains unresolved.
- SRC-337 independently supports the *Acacia pennata* tradition in a plant-focused paper, but does not settle whether SRC-335 describes a separate local variant, a different vernacular mapping or a taxonomic error.
- SRC-304 describes a filtrate-distillation process for Juharo; SRC-336 describes distillation of the residual rice mass (“jugap”); SRC-337 says only that Judimah is distilled. Keep the three source-specific process accounts separate.

## 5. Archive decision

1. Register as SRC-337.
2. Record *Acacia pennata* / Themra / Humao as the paper's own botanical and starter account.
3. Preserve Judimah and Juharao as the printed beverage spellings.
4. Do not create a new locality or canonical recipe; Dhansiri Reserved Forest is the plant collection site, not a named beverage-production locality.
5. Do not treat the comparator voucher as an accession for the market bark or Humao material.
6. Retain the *Albizia* versus *Acacia* divergence and Juharo distillation-feedstock question as unresolved.
