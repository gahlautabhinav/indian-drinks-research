## 1. Full-text recovery and field design

The complete four-page article is publicly readable on ResearchGate, including the methods, full plant table, process description and acknowledgements. The PDF is author-uploaded by Rajib Gogoi (uploaded 8 January 2015); the article itself is *NeBIO* Vol. 4, No. 2, April 2013, pages 46–49.

Methods (printed page 46 / extracted lines 160–209) say the ethnobotanical survey covered Mishing-dominated areas of Lakhimpur and Sonitpur districts. Seven selected villages are explicitly named:
1. Dhunaguri
2. Badati
3. Bongalmora
4. Chamaguri Temona
5. Luitmukh
6. Kathalkuchi
7. Rajbari

Villagers who regularly prepare Poro Apong were interviewed and the complete preparation method was observed and documented. Plants used for Apob cakes were collected and processed using standard herbarium methodology. Initial identification used literature; final confirmation was made at Botanical Survey of India herbarium Shillong (ASSAM) and Itanagar (ARUN).

**Not reported:** survey/collection dates; number of informants or households; village-by-village allocation of informants, plants or recipes; collector numbers; herbarium accession numbers; specimen images or a voucher list. The acknowledgement thanks village residents collectively but does not identify them or their localities.

## 2. Table 1 serial-number audit — 28 taxa accounted for

The paper states that 28 plant species were identified. Table 1 has serial numbers 1–25 and 27–28; serial 26 is absent. This does not indicate a missing taxon:

- Serials 1–24 each contain one botanical taxon (24).
- Serial 25 contains **two separately named taxa**: *Thelypteris parasitica* and *Thelypteris dentata* (2).
- Serials 27 and 28 each contain one taxon (2).
- Total = **28 botanical taxa**.

Thus the published 28-species total is fully accounted for by the printed names. Serial 26 is a numbering omission/formatting error unless the authors indicate otherwise. Do not create an invented 26th taxon or leave the inventory count at 27.

## 3. Botanical transcription and verification cautions

Preserve source spellings in the raw transcription. Several names appear to contain orthographic or taxonomic issues and should be reconciled against current botanical databases and, ideally, the physical herbarium vouchers:

- *Artocarpus heterophylla* (likely intended *Artocarpus heterophyllus*; verify source/voucher).
- *Capsicum annuam* (likely *Capsicum annuum*).
- *Coctus speciosa* (likely *Costus speciosus*).
- *Hydrocotile javanica* (likely *Hydrocotyle javanica*).
- *Leucus plukenetii* (likely *Leucas plukenetii*).
- *Scoperia dulcis* (likely *Scoparia dulcis*).
- *Zenthoxylum nitidum* (likely *Zanthoxylum nitidum*).
- Thelypteridaceae / Aspidiaceae family attribution for the two *Thelypteris* species should be checked against the voucher determination and current family treatment.
- *Coffea bengalensis* and other less familiar printed names require voucher-based verification rather than silent replacement.

Do not overwrite the verbatim CSV fields. Store normalized accepted names and confidence separately, with authority/database and access date.

## 4. Process observations — source-specific only

The article describes two phases: Apob starter-cake preparation and Poro Apong brewing.

Apob: plant parts are dried 1–2 days; 2–4 kg rice is soaked 2–3 hours, ground with dried plant parts, sieved and formed into cakes; cakes are covered with *Thelypteris parasitica* and *T. dentata* leaves and rice straw, then dried above a kitchen fireplace for 3–5 days.

Poro: cooked rice is mixed with ash made by burning rice straw and husk; 4–5 crushed Apob cakes are added; the mash is packed in a bamboo basket/vessel and fermented for 7–12 days depending on season, sometimes over a month. The product is extracted by water filtration through a V-shaped bamboo Tasuk lined with banana leaves and straw, with repeated filtration until the liquid turns brown.

The described extraction is filtration/water percolation, not a documented distillation stage. Retain this as the process reported in this source, not a universal statement for all Mising Poro Apong.

## 6. Archive decision

- Retain the seven named localities as study sites in Lakhimpur/Sonitpur only; no village-by-village production prevalence is claimed.
- Keep the inventory count at 28 taxa, with two taxa represented under serial 25.
- Treat serial 26 as a numbering omission, not an unknown plant.
- Keep survey dates, informant count and voucher/accession identifiers as open metadata gaps.
- No new place rows or beverage records are justified by this metadata audit.
