# SRC-311 — Boro et al. (2022): non-Saccharomyces yeasts from Emao and rice-beer trials

**Audit date:** 20 November 2026  
**Citation:** Nitesh Boro, Ashis Borah, Rajib L. Sarma and Diganta Narzary (2022), “Beer production potentiality of some non-Saccharomyces yeast obtained from a traditional beer starter emao,” *Brazilian Journal of Microbiology* 53(3):1515–1531. DOI: 10.1007/s42770-022-00765-7. PMID 35488168; PMCID PMC9433491.  
**Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9433491/

## 1. Sample provenance

The authors report collecting a traditionally prepared, **one-month-old Emao starter** aseptically from a Boro village named **Boragari, Dotoma, Assam**. The district is not specified in this paper. The sample was stored dry at room temperature and processed for microbial isolation within one month.

This overlaps in village-name wording with SRC-309 (Narzary et al. 2021), whose Table 1 lists **Boragari, Dotoma, Kokrajhar** as one of six Emao starter collection sites. The 2022 SRC-311 paper does not independently state Kokrajhar, and does not say it is the same household, batch or sample as the 2021 study. Do not transfer the district or merge the sample records until confirmed.

The sample is a starter cake, not a finished Jou sample. The article's traditional Emao control in fermentation comparisons is also not described as a separately sourced village sample; it is a positive control prepared using 2 g Emao suspended in 25 mL sterile water.

## 2. Culturable fungal diversity

Fungi were isolated on Sabouraud dextrose agar supplemented with tetracycline. Fifty distinct colonies were picked from mixed-culture plates. Ten representative morphotypes were selected for molecular identification and SSBF assays.

The ten representative morphotypes were assigned to the following species and strain codes:

| Species | Isolate codes |
|---|---|
| *Hyphopichia burtonii* | Hbur-FI38, Hbur-FI44, Hbur-FI47, Hbur-FI68 |
| *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* | Scer-FI51 |
| *Wickerhamomyces anomalus* | Wano-FI52 |
| *Candida carpophila* | Ccar-FI53 |
| *Mucor circinelloides* | Mcir-FI60 |
| *Saccharomycopsis malanga* | Smal-FI77, Smal-FI84 |

This is a culture-dependent selection from one starter sample. The 50 colonies are not a complete inventory of the starter microbiome, and the ten morphotypes are not ten independent household samples. The article's abstract reports four non-Saccharomyces strains with SSBF capacity: Hbur-FI38, Hbur-FI44, Ccar-FI53 and Smal-FI77.

## 3. Simultaneous saccharification and beer fermentation (SSBF)

The authors screened representative isolates for the ability to liquefy rice substrate through simultaneous starch saccharification and fermentation. For the laboratory assay:

- 180 g dehusked glutinous rice, identified as the local variety Maibra, was washed and autoclaved at 15 psi for 15 minutes.
- Each pure-culture inoculum was prepared from 2 g fungal biomass suspended in 25 mL sterile distilled water.
- Rice substrate was cooled to approximately 35°C before inoculation.
- Bottles were kept in a dark, dry place at 25 ± 2°C and monitored weekly for 28 days.
- Liquefaction was used as the positive SSBF criterion.

Four non-Saccharomyces strains—Hbur-FI38, Hbur-FI44, Ccar-FI53 and Smal-FI77—showed SSBF capacity. The abstract reports their rice beers contained approximately 7–10% (v/v) ethanol.

## 4. Scaled-up rice-beer comparison

The authors then performed a larger fermentation experiment using five strains (Hbur-FI38, Hbur-FI44, Ccar-FI53, Smal-FI77 and Smal-FI84) alongside an Emao control. Do not mistake the five strains included in the scaled-up comparison for the four strains explicitly identified as SSBF-positive in the abstract.

The scale-up used 6 kg of dehusked non-glutinous rice, local variety Aijong, divided into six approximately 1 kg portions. Each portion was transferred into a 5-L plastic fermentation jar. The jars were kept at 25 ± 2°C for 28 days, with samples taken after 14, 21 and 28 days when liquefaction was observed.

At day 28:
- Emao control reached 17.7% (v/v) ethanol.
- Hbur-FI38 and Hbur-FI44 produced approximately 10% (v/v).
- Ccar-FI53 produced approximately 9% (v/v).
- Smal-FI77 produced approximately 7% (v/v).

The individual-strain beers generally had lower alcohol and energy values than the Emao control. These are controlled laboratory trials, not a description of how households traditionally brew Jou.

## 5. Nutritional, functional and sensory findings

The study measured physicochemical and nutritional properties, including total carbohydrate, total reducing sugars, protein, free amino nitrogen, free amino acids, pH, titratable acidity, total phenols, ascorbic acid, DPPH radical-scavenging activity, reducing power, total antioxidant activity and colour.

The authors report:
- Both *H. burtonii* strains stood out for higher ascorbic acid, phenol and antioxidant measurements, together with improved sensory profile and lower alcohol/energy than the Emao control.
- Energy-value reductions for Hbur-FI38, Hbur-FI44, Ccar-FI53 and Smal-FI77 were reported as 45.02%, 45.5%, 51.77% and 55.94%, respectively, relative to Emao.
- The total titratable acidity ranged from 0.56 ± 0.03% TAE (Hbur-FI44) to 1.44 ± 0.1% TAE (Smal-FI84).
- A five-person sensory panel was used. The paper reports the H. burtonii beers and Emao in the “good” category; Ccar-FI53 and Smal-FI77 as “commercially acceptable”; and Smal-FI84 as “deficient” under the cited score interpretation.
- Sensory scores are based on five panellists who had prior experience consuming traditional rice beer. This is a small exploratory panel, not a representative consumer study or market validation.

The article reports triplicate measurements for most physicochemical parameters and five sensory observations. Its correlations and PCA describe relationships within this experimental dataset; they should not be read as causal or universal properties of Bodo Jou.

## 6. Important interpretation boundaries

1. **Starter, not finished beverage provenance:** Boragari, Dotoma is the Emao starter collection site. It is not proof that a specific finished Jou subtype was collected or produced there.
2. **District not given in SRC-311:** SRC-309 gives Kokrajhar for its Boragari, Dotoma sample. SRC-311 says only Assam. Preserve this distinction.
3. **One sample only:** The paper does not establish the prevalence of these strains across all Bodo starters or villages.
4. **Culture bias:** Plating on one medium and choosing morphologically distinct colonies cannot reveal the full starter community.
5. **Strain-level claims:** Results apply to the named laboratory isolates under the stated experimental conditions, not every member of their species.
6. **No health or safety certification:** Antioxidant assays and sensory results do not establish health benefits. Reduced ethanol does not make a product risk-free. The study does not certify the strains or finished beers for food safety or commercial use.
7. **Traditional practice versus engineered process:** Sterilised rice, pure inocula, controlled jars and scheduled sampling are research conditions, not a traditional household recipe.

## 7. Cross-source relationship

- **SRC-309 (2021):** Six Bodo Emao starter sites, including Boragari, Dotoma, Kokrajhar; six starter samples pooled for metagenomic sequencing.
- **SRC-310 (2022):** One Emao starter sample from Rajapanichanda, Rani, Kamrup; fungal isolation and pure-culture rice fermentations. Do not confuse this with the Boragari sample.
- **SRC-311 (2022):** One-month-old Emao starter from Boragari, Dotoma, Assam (district not stated); non-Saccharomyces yeast SSBF and scaled-up trials.

These are distinct papers and distinct reported sample descriptions. Similar author teams and recurring place names do not prove that they share a physical sample or household.

## 9. Next actions

1. Verify the district and administrative location of the SRC-311 Boragari, Dotoma village using author confirmation and reliable local administrative records.
2. Ask whether the 2021 and 2022 Boragari starter samples were collected from the same village, household or producer, and whether either paper used a retained sample.
3. Retrieve the supplementary DOCX files and preserve the isolate identification and sensory questionnaire details as separate evidence.
4. Keep the four SSBF-positive isolates distinct from the five strains included in the scale-up comparison.
5. Seek independent community-level documentation of current finished Jou production before mapping this starter collection locality as a beverage-production site.

**Conclusion:** SRC-311 adds a second functional fermentation study of Bodo Emao and a further sample explicitly associated with Boragari, Dotoma. It strengthens the strain-level evidence for direct rice fermentation by selected non-Saccharomyces yeasts, while leaving the sample's district and relationship to the 2021 Boragari collection unresolved.
