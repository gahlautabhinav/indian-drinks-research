## 2. What the accessible abstract-level record reports

- The authors visited rural areas where Judima was prepared and observed/documented the preparation process.
- Fermentation is summarized as occurring at room temperature in earthen/aluminium pots and taking approximately 4–5 days to complete.
- The study assayed carbohydrate, protein, free amino acids and alcohol percentage.
- It also assessed antioxidant activity of Judima and plants used in its preparation.
- A short-term cytotoxicity test on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) is described, with 50% and 100% ethanol used as comparators.
- The abstract characterizes the beverage as containing carbohydrate, protein and free amino acids and reports high antioxidant activity, but the underlying numerical results and assay details were not accessible in the abstract.

These points are recorded only at abstract level. They must not be expanded into exact recipe, chemical values or health conclusions without the primary paper.

## 3. What remains unknown

The accessible record does not establish:
- named village(s), field dates, household count or producer selection;
- number of batches/samples and whether samples were independent;
- rice variety, starter name, botanical ingredient list or ingredient proportions;
- exact vessel type and fermentation monitoring conditions;
- biochemical assay methods, units, calibration, replicate counts or statistical tests;
- numerical results in the paper's tables and figures;
- PBMC donor/sample design, exposure concentrations, controls, endpoints or detailed results.

No locality row, new ingredient, process-method row or numeric biochemical value is added from this partial record.

## 4. Comparison with existing Dimasa evidence

The reported 4–5-day fermentation summary is not identical to the 5–7-day account documented for one Dhansiri household in SRC-001. SRC-001, SRC-002 and SRC-003 each have distinct locality and evidence scopes. Do not average these timings or present one standardized Dimasa recipe. SRC-138's exact field locality and method must be extracted before it can be compared at household or village level.

## 5. Evidence boundary

Antioxidant assays and short-term PBMC cytotoxicity screening are laboratory endpoints. They do not establish clinical benefit, safe consumption, beverage-level bioavailability or a health claim. No medical or food-safety inference is made here.
