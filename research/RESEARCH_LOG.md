## 2026-10-20 — Apong research pass
- Added separate provisional canonical records for Noggin Apong and Poro Apong/Saimod to avoid conflating the non-distilled and distilled forms described in the literature.
- Added Mising community, place leads, production-stage records, cultural-context record, source records SRC-005 to SRC-008, and research gaps.
- Key locality evidence includes a 2023 ÉPOP starter-culture study with in-depth work in Majorbari and Ligiribari, Sivasagar district; additional literature leads relate to Dhemaji, Majuli and Lakhimpur, but not all are village-specific.

- Editorial caution: the 31-plant ÉPOP inventory and customary rules are study-specific, not universal claims about all Mising villages.

## 2026-10-20 — Sujen research pass
- Added Sujen as ASM-004-SUJ, associated with the Deori community, and recorded Mod pitha and Chuzae-pitha as starter terminology rather than beverage synonyms.
- Added historical village-level research leads: Sunpura Deori gaon (Sadiya/Tinsukia), Kololuwah (Dibrugarh), Rajabari (Sivasagar), and Borbam Deori gaon (Dhemaji).
- Added source records SRC-009 to SRC-012, preparation-stage records, cultural context and open research gaps.
- The 2007 ethnobotanical paper reports 32 plant species in its three-village study; a 2010 Dhemaji study reports 15 plant species in its Deori starter-culture sample. These are study-specific inventories, not a single universal recipe.
- Next planned case: Karbi Hor-alank and Hor-arak, keeping fermented and distilled forms distinct.

## 2026-10-20 — Karbi Hor research pass
- Added distinct canonical records for Hor-alank/Horlang (fermented beverage) and Hor-arak (distilled form), rather than treating them as synonyms.
- Added thap starter-culture records, the traditional Bhot still reference, Karbi community context, locality leads and research gaps.
- Key source: Teron (2006), *Hor, the traditional alcoholic beverage of Karbi tribe in Assam*, Natural Product Radiance 5(5), 377–381. The paper reports fieldwork during 2003–2005 in rural Karbi Anglong.
- Supplementary sources include a 2012 Northeast India starter-culture survey, a 2024 Frontiers in Microbiology study of southern Assam fermented beverages, and the Government of West Karbi Anglong cultural overview.
- Editorial caution: starter ingredients, ceremony associations and equipment descriptions are source- and locality-specific; historical medicinal-use claims are not clinical evidence.
- Next planned case: Rabha Jonga-mod / Choko, with beverage-name and starter terminology reconciliation.
## 2026-10-20 — Rabha Jonga-mod / Choko research pass
- Added one provisional canonical record for Jonga-mod, preserving Jonga mod, Kecha mod, Choko and Chokot as source-reported names without assuming complete equivalence.
- Added Dhupdhora (Goalpara, Assam) as a historical research locality and Rasikbill Rabha basti (Cooch Behar, West Bengal) as a separate cross-border comparative locality from a 2022 sample.
- Added source records SRC-017 to SRC-020, starter/fermentation/microbiology method records, community and cultural context, and research gaps.
- Core sources: Deka & Sarma (2010) on Rabha starter herbs in Goalpara; Bhuyan & Baishya (2013) review; Bhattacharjee et al. (2023) on Chokot starter microbiology; Loying et al. (2024) review.
- Editorial caution: starter plant lists and microbiology are study-specific; traditional medicinal-use claims are not clinical evidence. Cross-border Chokot research is comparative context, not proof of identical practice in Assam.
- Next planned case: Tai-Ahom Xajpani/Kolohpani, checking beverage names and starter terminology.

## 2026-10-20 — Tai-Ahom Xajpani / Kolohpani research pass
- Added provisional canonical record ASM-008-XAJ, preserving Xaj pani, Koloh pani and Haj pani while marking broader Haaj/Laopani equivalence as unresolved.
- Added starter-culture and fermentation method records, Tai-Ahom community context, cultural-practice notes and research gaps.
- Added source records SRC-021 to SRC-026, including a 2012 preparation survey, 2016 starter-culture metagenomics study, 2020 six-district Xaj-pitha sampling study and secondary cultural/ethnobotanical reviews.
- Geographic note: the 2020 paper reports Xaj-pitha starter sampling across Tinsukia, Dibrugarh, Sivasagar, Jorhat, Lakhimpur and Dhemaji. These are starter-sampling localities, not proof that every site currently produces Xajpani. Titabar (Jorhat) is a distinct 2016 household sampling locality; Baragharia (Golaghat) remains a secondary-source locality lead pending primary-source corroboration.
- Editorial caution: starter plant lists and microbial profiles are study-specific; distinguish starter names from beverage names, and do not treat reported medicinal use as clinical evidence or historic process durations as safety guidance.
- Next planned case: Bodo Jou/Jou bishi, beginning with local terminology and distinctions among fermented forms and starter terms.


## 2026-10-23 — Tiwa Zu / Chu locality and process distinction pass
- Reopened the Hill-Tiwa primary ethnobotanical study by Teron & Borthakur (2014), confirming ten study villages in Hamren subdivision: Umdoba, Morten, Khumrei Khora, Ulukunchi, Umswai, Boramli, Khyabat, Bor Rongkhoi, Ghelani and Tumpreng.
- Extracted the Hill-Tiwa Chu starter details: rice-and-plant starter cakes; *Croton joufra* as the reported primary plant, with *Amomum koenigii* and *Acacia pennata* reported as substitutes; some beer reportedly distilled in a crude still.
- Cross-checked against Das's Plains-Tiwa survey of five named Morigaon villages and the 2023 multi-community survey. The 19-vs-20 starter-plant counts are retained as source-specific inventories pending taxonomic reconciliation.
- Editorial decision: keep Hill-Tiwa Chu distinct as an unresolved comparison to Plains-Tiwa Zu/Chu; study villages do not prove current production in each village, and the distilled product is not assumed to be the same canonical beverage.
- Next: reconcile the three starter-plant inventories and source metadata before changing canonical record counts.
